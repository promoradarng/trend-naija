Court remands journalist Daniel Ojukwu in Abuja prison over cybercrime charges

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu has been remanded in prison in Abuja on a 12-count cybercrime charge linked to his reports about a presidential aide, pending a bail hearing. The decision has renewed concerns about press freedom.

Read more

A court in Abuja has ordered that investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu be held in prison pending a hearing on his bail application.

The charges

Ojukwu, who works for the Foundation for Investigative Journalism, was arraigned on a 12-count charge of allegedly breaching Nigeria's Cybercrimes Act, and the court ordered his remand on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The charges relate to reports he published about Adejoke Orelope-Adefulire, who was senior special assistant on the Sustainable Development Goals under former President Muhammadu Buhari and now holds the same role under President Bola Tinubu. The charges have not been tested at trial.

Press freedom concerns

The decision drew widespread criticism from media freedom advocates, who have long argued that the cybercrimes law is used to silence journalists. According to the Committee to Protect Journalists, at least 29 journalists have been prosecuted under the law since it was enacted in 2015. Nigeria is ranked 112th out of 180 countries in the 2026 World Press Freedom Index compiled by Reporters Without Borders.

Background

Ojukwu was previously detained by police for several days in 2024 over a report on the same official, a case that also drew national and international criticism.

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Indonesian Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has reportedly handed his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto, after the Supreme Court ruled that a high-school equivalence certificate he used to qualify for office was invalid.

Read more

Indonesia's Vice-President, Gibran Rakabuming Raka, has resigned, according to an aide who said the letter was handed directly to President Prabowo Subianto on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. It was not immediately clear whether the President had accepted it.

The court ruling

The resignation follows a ruling by Indonesia's Supreme Court that declared invalid a high-school equivalence certificate Gibran used during the 2024 election campaign. Indonesian law requires anyone running for vice-president to have completed senior high school. Gibran, who studied in Singapore and Australia, had relied on a local equivalence document and argued that his university education was enough proof. The court reportedly found no evidence of a foreign qualification that could legally replace a senior high school certificate, and pointed to an earlier Constitutional Court ruling that reached the same conclusion. On Tuesday he had asked for a chance to challenge the ruling.

Political pressure

The ruling prompted swift political action, with more than 100 lawmakers reportedly backing moves against him.

Background

Gibran is the eldest son of former President Joko Widodo.

He said only three officials are now authorised to speak on behalf of the council: Senator Abdulaziz Yari, the Director General; Minister of Solid Minerals Dele Alake; and Minister of Information Mohammed Idris.

What happened on live TV

It all started on Tuesday, when APC official Alwan Hassan appeared as a guest on a live television politics programme.

While discussing the achievements of the APC-led government, Hassan pointed to the 112 emergency number as an example of a working public service. He explained that Nigerians could dial it whenever they needed urgent help, whether in a medical emergency, a security threat or a road accident.

To prove his point, he challenged the host to dial 112 right there in the studio.

That was where things took an unexpected turn. The host tried the number, but the call failed to connect. He tried again, with the same result.

Hassan insisted the line was working and suggested network problems might have stopped the calls going through. His explanation did little to stop the criticism that followed.

The backlash

Clips of the interview spread quickly online, with Nigerians questioning the reliability of a service people are meant to depend on in life-threatening situations. Opposition figures also seized on the incident to challenge the government's claims about improved public services.

The real question

Is removing spokespersons really the answer, or should the government be more concerned about finding out why the emergency calls failed?

Someone calling 112 during an armed robbery, a serious accident or a medical emergency may not have the luxury of trying again and again until the network connects.

One failed demonstration does not prove the whole emergency system is broken. But it raises questions that deserve proper answers, and for many Nigerians, those answers matter far more than who is allowed to speak for a political campaign.

Background

The update

In its national health security update released on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said 34 other contacts had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. As of 9 November, Nigeria had confirmed only one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, with no further confirmed cases.

The response

Idris said the response involves the Federal Ministry of Health, the FCT Administration, state authorities and development partners, with contact tracing, investigation of reported cases, laboratory testing, infection prevention and control, safe patient care and stronger screening at entry points.

Background

Ebola is a severe and often deadly viral disease spread through contact with the body fluids of infected people. The 21-day monitoring period reflects the maximum incubation time of the virus. The case in Nigeria is linked to an outbreak in Central Africa; the United States has just barred travellers who have been in the Democratic Republic of Congo in the past 21 days. Advice

Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness, vomiting or unexplained bleeding, especially after travel or contact with a suspected case, to report to a health facility immediately.
