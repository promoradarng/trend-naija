Dangote cuts petrol gantry price again, now ₦1,210 per litre

2 Nov 2026, 09:44 GMT

The Dangote Refinery has reportedly reduced its petrol gantry price by ₦55 to ₦1,210 per litre, citing a fall in European petrol benchmark prices. Depot prices in Lagos have also dropped, averaging about ₦1,259 per litre.

Read more

Petrol prices are falling again in Nigeria after the Dangote Refinery announced another cut.

The new price

According to reports on Monday, 2 November 2026, the refinery reduced its petrol gantry price by ₦55, from ₦1,265 to ₦1,210 per litre, a drop of about 4.35 per cent. The cut was announced through marketers linked to the refinery. Industry sources said it followed a fall in the European petrol benchmark that the refinery uses to set its prices, as well as better supply.

Depot prices

Other Lagos depots had already reduced their prices at the end of last week. Depot owners including African Terminal, Rainoil, Ardova and NIPCO were selling petrol at about ₦1,260 per litre, with Aiteo slightly lower at ₦1,255. Prices also fell in Port Harcourt and Calabar. Diesel prices have moved in the opposite direction after the refinery raised its diesel gantry price last week.

What it means for motorists

Gantry and depot prices are what marketers pay before selling at filling stations, so the cuts could lead to lower pump prices in the coming days. However, actual pump prices also depend on transport costs and how quickly retailers pass on savings.

Background

This is the refinery's second petrol price cut in less than a week. Since fuel subsidies were removed in 2023, prices have been set by the market, and the Dangote Refinery has become a major influence on what Nigerians pay at the pump.

The claim

An X user posted a short video of Obi with a caption attributing words to him, suggesting he had said he knew he could not beat Tinubu at the polls and was only staying in the race because young supporters asked him not to quit. The post was viewed more than 170,000 times.

What he actually said

According to a fact check published on Sunday, 1 November 2026, the clip came from a diaspora town hall Obi held in Manchester, United Kingdom, on 18 October 2026. In the full recording, he asked supporters to back a candidate with a record of public service and told them he needed their support. A review of both the short clip and the full recording found that he did not mention Tinubu, did not say he could not win, and did not talk about quitting the race.

Verdict

The claim is false. There is no evidence that Obi made the statement attributed to him in the caption.

Why it matters

Misleading captions on genuine videos are one of the most common forms of false information during election campaigns. INEC and civil society groups have warned about deepfakes and fake news ahead of the 2027 elections, and voters are advised to check claims against full recordings or trusted sources before sharing them.

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) has unveiled another product under his growing RATEL brand, and this time it is something many Nigerian households may find useful, especially with the country's unpredictable electricity supply.

The new product is the RATEL Sensor Light, a motion-activated light that comes on automatically when it detects movement.

How much it costs

According to VDM, the sensor light comes in two sizes: the small size costs ₦4,200, while the big size costs ₦9,000.

Why it could be useful

Anyone living in Nigeria knows how frustrating the electricity situation can be. Imagine NEPA suddenly takes light at midnight and everywhere goes dark. You need to leave your bedroom, walk downstairs, check on your children or find your way outside to switch on the generator. Before you even find your torchlight, you have already knocked your leg against a chair.

This is where a sensor light could come in handy. It can be placed along staircases, corridors, bedroom entrances, wardrobes, storerooms and other dark corners of the house. Once it detects movement within its range, it switches on automatically, with no need to search for a wall switch in the dark.

A possible security benefit

The light could also help around entrances, gates and backyards. If somebody quietly approaches that area at night, the space around the sensor suddenly becomes bright.

Of course, a sensor light cannot replace proper home security, but it may help discourage unwanted visitors and make movement around a compound more noticeable.

VDM also explained that the product comes with different lighting colour options, including multicolour settings.

What buyers should check

At ₦4,200 for the smaller version, some households may find it affordable enough to install several units: one on the staircase, another in the corridor and perhaps two around the entrance. However, buyers should check the battery life, charging method, weather resistance and detection range before deciding where to install it.

A growing RATEL range

What stands out about this latest RATEL product is the idea behind it. VDM appears to be paying attention to the everyday inconveniences Nigerians face, especially those caused by poor electricity supply. From power stations to smaller household gadgets, he is gradually expanding his product range.

There is something worth appreciating about businesses that identify ordinary problems and offer affordable solutions. You don't always need to spend hundreds of thousands of naira to make your home more comfortable, and sometimes even a small gadget can save you the stress of walking around in darkness.

Imagine having four of these lights around your house. NEPA takes light, but as you move around, the lights come on automatically. Small enjoyment!
