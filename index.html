The federal government has reportedly told ministries, departments and agencies to keep their workforce at current levels in 2027. Agencies that ignore the new reporting rules risk losing budget provisions.

Read more

The federal government has reportedly directed all ministries, departments and agencies (MDAs) to keep their workforce at current levels for the 2027 fiscal year.

The circular

The directive is contained in a circular dated 9 November 2026 from the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, titled the 2027 Workforce Budget Call Circular. It was addressed to ministers, permanent secretaries, service chiefs and heads of federal institutions.

The new rules

Under the circular, MDAs may not create new departments, units or other structures without clearance from the Head of Service. Every workforce budget proposal must include the institution's approved organisational structure, and no vacancies may be created in structures that have not been officially approved.

The government is said to have singled out posts on Grade Levels 15 to 17, the senior ranks of the federal civil service, warning agencies against creating unauthorised vacancies at those levels. Agencies that can show a genuine staffing need must back their requests with evidence.

Penalties

Institutions that fail to comply with the new reporting requirements reportedly risk having their budget provisions cut off.

Highest and lowest states

Imo recorded the highest annual headline inflation in October at 28.66%, followed by Enugu at 22.81% and Kaduna at 21.55%. At the other end, Taraba had the lowest rate at 6.12%, followed by Sokoto at 6.68% and Ekiti at 7.64%.

What the money is for

The programme is meant to expand access to electricity through renewable sources such as solar mini-grids and stand-alone solar systems, particularly for homes, businesses, schools and health centres in areas the national grid does not reach.

In 2023, the World Bank approved $750 million for Nigeria's Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up (DARES) project, which aims to bring clean electricity to millions of Nigerians. The new $500 million would raise the World Bank's total financing for the project to $1.25 billion.

"Where is the renewable energy?"

The news has been met with frustration by many Nigerians, who say they are yet to see the impact of the money already borrowed.

Millions of households and small businesses still depend on generators, while frequent grid collapses and rising electricity tariffs continue to bite. For many people, the question is simple: where is the renewable energy?

The debt question

The request also comes amid growing concern about Nigeria's rising debt. Critics argue that new loans should only be taken when there is clear evidence that earlier funds have been well spent and are delivering results.

Supporters of the programme argue that renewable energy is essential for closing Nigeria's huge electricity gap, and that such projects take time to deliver.

A man presumed dead after a motorcycle accident near Evwreni in Delta State reportedly woke up in a mortuary, sparking celebrations by residents in a video that has gone viral.

A man who was presumed dead after an accident in Delta State has reportedly woken up in a mortuary.

What happened

According to reports on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the man was involved in a motorcycle accident near Evwreni and was taken to a mortuary after being presumed dead. He is said to have later woken up there. A video shared online shows residents celebrating his survival. Voices in the video say that when he woke up, the first thing he asked for was a cigarette and a lighter.

Celebrations

Residents rubbed white powder on their faces and bodies, a traditional sign of celebration often used at a child's birth, and also applied it to the survivor. Others shared snacks with passers-by to mark the occasion, while some urged him to go for a proper medical check-up.

Background

It is not clear who declared the man dead or whether he was examined by a doctor before being taken to the mortuary. The authorities had not commented on the incident at the time of writing.
