Nollywood legend Pete Edochie dies at 79

25 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Veteran actor Pete Edochie, famous for playing Okonkwo in Things Fall Apart, has died at the age of 79. His family says he died in Enugu early on Wednesday after a long battle with prostate cancer.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actor Pete Edochie has died at the age of 79, his family has announced.

The announcement

In a statement shared on Instagram by his son, Leo Pete Edochie, on behalf of the family, the family said the actor died at 12:30am on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, in Enugu, after a prolonged battle with prostate cancer. The statement, titled 'An Iroko Has Fallen', described him as a beloved husband, father, brother and kinsman.

He is survived by his wife, Josephine Edochie, his children, including actors Yul and Linc Edochie, his siblings and other relatives. The family said funeral arrangements would be announced in due course.

His life and career

Pete Edochie hailed from Akamnato, Ifite Nteje, in Anambra State. He began his career in broadcasting in 1967 before becoming one of Nigeria's best-known actors. His major breakthrough came in 1987, when he played Okonkwo in the Nigerian Television Authority's adaptation of Chinua Achebe's Things Fall Apart, a role that brought him recognition far beyond Nigeria.

Known for his commanding voice and powerful screen presence, he became famous for portraying traditional patriarchs and kings in countless Nollywood films. He was honoured with the national award of Member of the Order of the Niger (MON).

His final weeks

In October, during a visit to Ghana, he spoke openly about his declining health, saying walking had become difficult, and prayed that he would not become a burden to those around him. Just this week, actress Toyin Abraham and her crew knelt to welcome him on her film set, and he had spoken publicly about his son Yul days earlier.

Tributes

News of his death has drawn an outpouring of grief from fans, many of whom described it as the end of an era for Nigerian film.

Governor and colleagues pay tribute

Enugu State Governor Peter Mbah described Edochie as an institution and said his death was a personal loss for the state, which the actor considered his birthplace and first port of call. Actress Funke Akindele, who worked with him in the film Egg of Life, recalled growing up watching him play Okonkwo and said his words of encouragement had stayed with her.

Tinubu: 'larger than life'

President Bola Tinubu, in a condolence statement from the State House, described Edochie as one of Nigeria's greatest actors and a founding figure of Nollywood, graceful and larger than life both on screen and in person. Joke Silva, whose husband Olu Jacobs died earlier this year, Toyin Abraham, Omoni Oboli and his daughter-in-law May Edochie were among those who also paid tribute, while his son Uche shared the last photo he took with his father.

More tributes

Content creator Peller also mourned the actor, sharing a moment from their last meeting and expressing gratitude for the time they spent together.

CAS restores Senegal as AFCON 2025 champions, overturning CAF ruling

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Court of Arbitration for Sport has confirmed Senegal as winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. It overturned in full the CAF Appeal Board decision that had handed the title to Morocco.

Read more

Senegal are once again Africa's champions after the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) overturned the decision that had stripped them of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations title.

The verdict

CAS delivered its ruling on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, reversing in full the decision of CAF's Appeal Board, which had awarded the title to hosts Morocco. With the verdict, Senegal are officially recognised as the 2025 AFCON champions.

How it started

Senegal beat Morocco 1-0 after extra time in the final at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat on 18 January 2026, with Pape Gueye scoring the only goal. During the match, Senegal's players walked off the pitch for about 15 minutes in protest at a late penalty awarded to Morocco, which was later saved.

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation then took legal action, and on 17 March CAF's Appeal Board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match, awarding Morocco a 3-0 win and the trophy. The Senegalese Football Federation refused to accept the decision and appealed to CAS.

What it means

The ruling ends months of legal uncertainty over the continent's top football title. Before the verdict, CAF President Patrice Motsepe had said the body would accept whatever CAS decided.

Morocco to fight on

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation said it rejected the ruling and would challenge it through every legal channel available, citing what it described as procedural failures. Senegal star Sadio Mane welcomed the decision.

CAF responds

In a short statement, CAF acknowledged the ruling and pledged to act on it. The trophy, medals and prize money were never taken back from Senegal during the dispute and will remain with the team.

A Federal High Court in Warri, Delta State, has nullified the nomination of former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege as the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate for Delta Central Senatorial District in the 2027 elections.

The judgment

Delivering judgment on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, Justice Hyeladzira Ajiya Nganjiwa ruled that the NDC failed to comply with mandatory provisions of the Electoral Act in nominating Omo-Agege. The court directed INEC to remove his name from the list of senatorial candidates and barred him from presenting himself as the party's candidate.

Why the court ruled against him

The suit was filed by Professor Alexander Osondu Akpodiete, who challenged Omo-Agege's nomination, his resignation from the APC and his eligibility to take part in the NDC primary. The court held that the NDC did not submit its membership register to INEC as required, and that Omo-Agege was still an APC member when he took part in the party's senatorial primary on 18 May 2026. The judge first dismissed five preliminary objections raised by Omo-Agege, the NDC and INEC.

Background

The ruling came a day after gunmen attacked Omo-Agege's campaign event in Uduere, Ughelli North, killing several people. The senator escaped unhurt.

Omo-Agege to appeal

Omo-Agege rejected the judgment and said he had directed his lawyers to file an appeal immediately. He disputed the finding that his name was missing from the NDC membership register submitted to INEC before the primaries.
