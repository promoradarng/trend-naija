Blord vs VDM: who is fooling who? Blord claims his power station can run a fridge, fan, TV and treadmill for 12 hours

29 Oct 2026, 16:54 GMT

A day after VeryDarkMan's Ratel power station ran a TV, fan and fridge for 5 hours 17 minutes in his test, Blord has claimed his 1,000W unit can run a big fridge, an industrial fan, a TV and a treadmill for 12 hours. Nigerians are asking him to back it up with a full, uninterrupted test and to show its battery capacity.

Read more

Barely 24 hours after VeryDarkMan demonstrated the performance of his Ratel power station, Blord has come out with his own claims, and Nigerians are already raising serious questions.

According to Blord, his 1,000W-rated power station can power a big refrigerator, a 128W industrial fan, a television and even a treadmill for 12 solid hours!

Wait a minute! How is that possible?

VDM tested his 1,200W-rated power station, and it lasted 5 hours and 17 minutes under the conditions of his demonstration.

Now Blord is claiming his 1,000W unit can run all those appliances for 12 hours, without showing a complete, uninterrupted test to back up the claim.

Blord, Nigerians need evidence, not just words!

However, there is something important here. A power station's watt rating tells us how much power it can deliver at once, while its battery capacity, measured in watt-hours (Wh), determines how long it can run appliances. So battery life cannot be judged from the 1,000W and 1,200W ratings alone.

Blord, show us the battery capacity, connect all those appliances and let the test run for 12 hours. No editing, no shortcuts!

VDM has shown his demonstration. Nigerians are waiting to see yours.

Do you believe Blord's 12-hour claim, or should he prove it with a proper live test?

State police moves closer as 30 state assemblies reportedly back constitution change

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Thirty state Houses of Assembly have now approved the constitutional amendment that would allow states to set up their own police, according to Chief of Staff Femi Gbajabiamila. That is well above the 24 needed, and the bill now goes back to the National Assembly before it can be sent to President Tinubu for assent.

Read more

The plan to allow Nigerian states to run their own police forces has crossed a major hurdle. Femi Gbajabiamila, Chief of Staff to the President and chairman of the Presidential Working Group on the reform, said 30 state Houses of Assembly have now endorsed the constitutional amendment, comfortably above the 24 required.

What happened

Gbajabiamila gave the update at the Nigerian Economic Summit in Abuja. He said 24 assemblies had approved the proposal by 23 October, and six more have since joined. Lagos, Niger, Kogi and Ebonyi were among the latest to vote in favour. A full list of the 30 states has not been published.

What happens next

The National Assembly must now collect the state resolutions and send the amendment bill to President Bola Tinubu for his signature. Separately, a draft National Policing Bill and 29 related amendment bills were sent to the Ministry of Justice on 19 October for review. After that review they will go to the President and then to the National Assembly.

The money question

Gbajabiamila stressed that states wanting their own police must show they can pay for it over time, including salaries, training and maintenance, and not just vehicles and equipment. States that are not ready will continue to rely on federal policing. He asked business groups to suggest ways of supporting funding, but warned that private donations must never buy influence over an investigation. Responding to fears that governors could misuse state police against opponents, he said a citizen's right to protection must not depend on who they support or where they come from.

Background

Calls for state police have grown for years amid kidnapping, banditry and communal attacks in many parts of the country. Senate President Godswill Akpabio also defended the reform this week. Earlier in October, only a handful of assemblies had voted, which made the recent rush of approvals a surprise to many observers.

Background

The crash is Spain's second deadly high-speed rail disaster of 2026. What happened

The corridors are planned to run from Sokoto through Abuja into parts of the South. The government will start with the Lagos–Ogun, Benin–Port Harcourt and Abuja–Kaduna–Kano corridors. Cooking gas now sells for up to ₦1,600 per kg as demand rises

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Cooking gas prices range from about ₦1,200 to ₦1,600 per kg across Nigeria, with Bauchi the most expensive, according to regulator data. Daily consumption rose to 5.2 kilotonnes in September, ahead of supply, and stocks cover only about 16 days.

Read more

Nigerian households are paying between about ₦1,200 and ₦1,600 for a kilogramme of cooking gas, according to the latest data from the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (NMDPRA) for September 2026.

The prices

The regulator's survey found the highest price in Bauchi, at ₦1,600 per kg, and the lowest in Abuja, at about ₦1,200. In Lagos, prices ranged from ₦1,250 to ₦1,400. That means refilling a 12.5kg cylinder now costs roughly ₦15,000 to ₦20,000 depending on location.

Demand and supply

Daily consumption of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) rose by about 8 per cent to 5.2 kilotonnes in September, well above the official benchmark of 3.9 kilotonnes. Supply also rose, to about 4.7 kilotonnes a day, but still fell short of consumption by roughly 500 tonnes daily. Local production now provides almost all the gas, with NLNG and Seplat supplying more than half, while imports fell sharply. The Dangote Refinery supplied about 15 per cent. Stocks were enough for about 16 days, against a required 30, although the regulator said the figures did not point to a nationwide shortage.

Background

Gas prices rose by up to ₦75 per kg in two days earlier this month as international crude prices climbed, with some suppliers raising their rates from around ₦1,175 to ₦1,250 per kg. More families have switched to gas from kerosene and charcoal in recent years, pushing up demand.

What it means

For many families already under pressure from food and transport costs, the price of a refill has become another monthly worry. The regulator says the rise in local supply has helped shield buyers from sharper swings in world prices.

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

FIFA reportedly earned about $5 billion from tickets and hospitality at the 2026 World Cup in North America, roughly five times the figure from Qatar 2022. Fan groups have called the prices extortionate, and FIFA says it will publish official figures after its audit.

Read more

FIFA reportedly earned about $5 billion from ticket sales and hospitality at the 2026 World Cup in the United States, Canada and Mexico, far more than its original estimate and around five times what it made in Qatar four years ago.

The figures

According to a report in a British newspaper, the $5 billion figure compares with an earlier FIFA estimate of $3.3 billion for the same revenue stream. Total revenue from the tournament is put at about $15 billion. Qatar 2022 reportedly brought in $929 million from tickets and hospitality. The 2026 tournament had 40 more matches because of the expanded 48-team format, but much of the increase is said to come from higher prices and dynamic pricing, where ticket costs rise with demand. FIFA's resale platform also reportedly takes a 30 per cent cut of resold tickets.

Criticism

Fan groups have described the prices as extortionate and scandalous. Football Supporters Europe called them a monumental betrayal of fans, many of whom also faced high travel and hotel costs in North America.

FIFA's response

FIFA has not officially confirmed the $5 billion figure. A spokesperson said more than 100,000 of the 6.81 million tickets were sold at $60, including more than 1,000 for the final. The spokesperson said the money would be reinvested in football, including support for its 211 member associations, and that official revenue figures would appear in its annual report after audits. FIFA also plans to raise funding for member associations to $6.35 billion for 2027 to 2030.

Why it matters

The figures are likely to add to debate over the cost of attending major tournaments, with fan groups already pressing for price caps at future World Cups.

The Zamfara State Police Command has arrested Umar Ibrahim Gandi, the man seen in a viral video calling for AK-47 rifles to carry out attacks against Christians in Enugu State and Jos, Plateau State.

Police identified the suspect as a 22-year-old native of Maradun Local Government Area of Zamfara State. They say the video was recorded on Friday, October 23, 2026, and that he faces allegations of incitement to violence, disturbance of public peace and criminal intimidation. The Commissioner of Police said an investigation is under way and that the suspect will be charged before a court.

In the video, the suspect, wearing the uniform of the JIBWIS (Izala) first-aid group, is heard chanting for rifles to kill people he calls "infidels" during Sunday worship. According to the police, JIBWIS has disassociated itself from his alleged conduct.

His statements raised serious concerns about religious extremism, hate speech and threats to innocent lives, and the video attracted widespread condemnation, with many Nigerians calling on the DSS and the police to identify and arrest him.

Particularly troubling was the presence of young children during the recording, raising questions about the exposure of minors to violent extremist messages.

Nigerians will now expect a thorough investigation and appropriate legal action based on the evidence. Like any suspect, he is presumed innocent until proven guilty in court.
