A year on, he has not named a new government, and a long-promised cabinet reshuffle has yet to come. The bigger worry is succession. In April, parliament restored the post of vice president, whose holder would automatically take over if the president died, resigned or could no longer serve. Only Biya can fill it, and he has not. In June, a sealed envelope containing a supposed presidential decree naming a vice president was delivered to state broadcaster CRTV, a document the government later disputed. Without a vice president, Senate President Aboubakary Abdoulaye would lead until fresh elections. Many analysts see Ferdinand Ngoh Ngoh, Secretary-General of the Presidency, as the country's de facto ruler and a possible vice president-in-waiting. Government spokesman René Emmanuel Sadi insists the timing of any appointments is the president's call alone. Critics say Cameroon is being run on autopilot while it battles a separatist conflict in its English-speaking regions and attacks by Islamic State-linked fighters in the north.
