Chief Ezibe, widely regarded as one of the founding fathers of the famous Balogun Market in Lagos, was honoured by traders, associates, friends and well-wishers who gathered to celebrate his life and his contribution to commerce.

"My leader! My leader!"

Something remarkable happened when Obi arrived. The atmosphere suddenly changed. Traders stepped away from their businesses, and a large crowd gathered around him, cheering and calling out, "My leader! My leader!"

Many raised their phones to capture the moment, while members of his team, wearing T-shirts printed with "Rest in Peace Omeluora", waved to the crowd from his car.

For those watching, the reception was another reminder of the strong support Obi continues to enjoy among sections of the trading community, particularly in Lagos. He had come for a solemn occasion, yet his presence drew the kind of attention usually seen at major political gatherings.

Who was Chief Okey Ezibe (Omeluora)?

To many traders, the late Chief Sir Okey Ezibe was more than a successful businessman. He was a respected figure whose contributions helped shape the business community around Balogun Market, one of Nigeria's busiest commercial centres.

For years, Balogun Market has provided opportunities for thousands of traders, importers, wholesalers and small business owners. People like Omeluora are remembered for building business networks, encouraging younger entrepreneurs and strengthening the trading community. His passing marks the loss of a respected figure in that community.

Obi's reception gets people talking

Despite the disagreements and controversies that surround Nigerian politics, Obi still draws enthusiastic crowds in parts of the country, and the scenes at Trade Fair appeared to reflect that connection.

Some will see the reception as a sign of his political popularity ahead of the 2027 general election. Others may simply see it as a warm welcome for a familiar public figure attending the funeral of a respected businessman.

Whatever the interpretation, the excitement around his arrival became one of the memorable moments of the day.

Remembering Omeluora

Beyond the cheers and the political talk, the gathering was ultimately about remembering Chief Okey Ezibe and the legacy he left behind.

A man can build wealth, businesses and influence in his lifetime, but what people say about him after his death often tells another story. For Omeluora, the respect shown by the trading community speaks to the place he held in their lives.

Rest in peace, Omeluora. A great iroko has fallen. His name will continue to be remembered among those who helped commerce grow in Lagos.

Nigeria's external debt rises to $145 billion, World Bank report says

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Nigeria's external debt rose 6.6% to about $145 billion in 2025, according to the World Bank's latest International Debt Report. Some debt ratios improved, but repayments took a bigger share of export earnings.

Read more

Nigeria's external debt reached about $145.0 billion in 2025, according to the World Bank's International Debt Report 2026.

The figures

According to reports on Friday, 6 November 2026, the total was 6.6 per cent higher than the year before and about 74 per cent higher than the $83.2 billion recorded in 2015. Public and publicly guaranteed debt made up about $62.4 billion of the total, private debt without government guarantees about $54.5 billion, and short-term debt about $28.1 billion. Multilateral lenders were the biggest creditors of the government, at about $31.4 billion, including about $18.5 billion owed to the World Bank's International Development Association. China remained Nigeria's largest single-country lender, owed about $5.7 billion.

Burden of repayments

Some measures of the debt burden improved: external debt fell to 212 per cent of export earnings and to 51 per cent of gross national income. But debt service took a bigger share of income, rising to 19 per cent of exports and 5 per cent of gross national income.

The global picture

The World Bank said debt service payments by low- and middle-income countries reached $1.4 trillion in 2025, the fifth yearly rise in a row, driven largely by payments to private lenders such as bondholders. It noted that loans from official lenders carried an average interest rate of 1.9 per cent, compared with 9.6 per cent from private creditors.

Why it matters

The figures come as President Bola Tinubu seeks Senate approval for new external borrowing of about $7.7 billion, a request opposition figures, including Atiku Abubakar, have urged lawmakers to reject. The government says borrowing is needed to fund infrastructure and that its reforms are improving the economy.

FCCPC says some vegetable oils and salt in Nigerian markets fail nutrient standards

6 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

The consumer protection agency FCCPC says 12% of vegetable oil samples and 2% of salt samples tested in markets and homes did not contain the nutrients required under Nigeria's food fortification rules. It has not named the brands.

Read more

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) has raised concerns about the quality of some vegetable oils and salt sold in Nigeria, after tests found that some products did not meet national fortification standards.

What the tests found

In a statement on Thursday, 5 November 2026, the commission's Director of Corporate Affairs, Ondaje Ijagwu, said the study was carried out over two months in randomly selected markets and households in northern and southern Nigeria, together with the Global Alliance for Improved Nutrition (GAIN). It examined 200 samples, made up of 100 vegetable oils and 100 salt products. According to the findings, 12 per cent of the vegetable oil samples and 2 per cent of the salt samples did not contain enough of the required nutrients. Of the oil samples, 49 were unbranded and 51 carried brand names, while 19 of the salt samples were unbranded and 81 were branded.

Brands not named

The commission did not name the affected brands or say whether branded or unbranded products accounted for most of the failures. It said it had issued compliance directives to the manufacturers involved. The FCCPC also complained that few companies were using a digital tracking platform for fortified foods: only six vegetable oil makers had registered, and results for just eight of the 100 oil samples had been uploaded.

What fortification means

Nigeria's fortification rules require some everyday foods to contain added vitamins and minerals, such as vitamin A in vegetable oil and iodine in salt, to help prevent nutritional deficiencies, especially among children and pregnant women. Products that fall short are not necessarily unsafe to eat, but they do not deliver the nutrients consumers are entitled to expect.

What happens next

The commission said it would continue to monitor compliance. Consumers are advised to buy from trusted sources and check packaging and expiry dates.

Nigeria's Flamingos have reached the final of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup for the first time after beating Brazil 3-1 in the semi-final. Awawu Bashiru scored twice and Queen Joseph once. They face Japan in the final.

Nigeria's under-17 women's team, the Flamingos, have made history by reaching the final of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup for the first time.

The match

The Flamingos beat Brazil 3-1 in the semi-final on Wednesday night, 4 November 2026, according to reports. Queen Joseph opened the scoring in the 56th minute, and Awawu Bashiru doubled the lead in the 71st minute before adding her second goal in the 80th minute to put the game beyond Brazil's reach.

A record run

The win beats the team's previous best performance, a bronze medal at the 2022 edition of the tournament. On the way to the semi-final, the Flamingos beat Venezuela in the quarter-finals, a result that earned praise from the Confederation of African Football.

The final

The Flamingos will now face Japan in the final as they chase Nigeria's first FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup title. Japan, one of the strongest sides in women's youth football, won the tournament in 2014.

Background

The result comes at a difficult time for Nigerian women's football at senior level. The Super Falcons failed to qualify for the Women's World Cup for the first time, and no Nigerian player was shortlisted for this year's CAF Women's Player of the Year award. The Flamingos' success has been welcomed as a sign that a new generation of players is coming through.

FIFA's message

FIFA congratulated the Flamingos on qualifying for the final, a first for Nigeria at this level of the women's game.

How people reacted

Nigerians celebrated the win across social media, praising the young players and their coaches and urging the government and the football federation to support them ahead of the final.

Content creator Roskey has called out social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) over his decision to resurface a video involving Nigerian music superstar Davido, popularly known as OBO.

According to Roskey, some things should be left alone, especially when a married man and his family could be dragged into unnecessary controversy.

"You went too far"

Roskey questioned why VDM would bring back a video of Davido enjoying Zlatan's song, knowing fully well that the singer is now married to Chioma.

What appears to have upset him even more is that VDM attended Davido's wedding. In Roskey's view, that alone should have been reason enough for VDM to show some restraint.

"VeryDarkMan, you went too far by posting that video of Davido. You know OBO is married now, and his wife, Chioma, won't be happy seeing that video circulating again," Roskey said.

He argued that marriage comes with responsibilities, and that people who have access to celebrities should know when to draw the line.

"He barely danced"

Roskey also questioned what Davido actually did in the video to deserve so much attention. According to him, Davido barely danced in the footage. He simply raised his hand while Zlatan's song was playing.

So why make such a big issue out of something that looked so harmless?

The Carter Efe question

Roskey went further and questioned VDM's priorities, bringing up his clash with fellow entertainer Carter Efe, who recently made insulting remarks about VDM's mother in a video.

"Why haven't you gone after Carter Efe, who insulted your mother? Why are you targeting Davido? Or is there something you're not telling us?" he asked.

That last question has added another layer to the controversy. Roskey appears to believe there may be more behind VDM's decision to revisit the Davido video, although he presented no evidence of any hidden motive.

What about Davido's marriage?

Roskey's bigger concern is the possible effect of these online fights on Davido's marriage. Celebrities are constantly under public scrutiny, but he believes there should be some consideration for their spouses and families, particularly when old videos are brought back into circulation.

Whether the video poses any real threat to Davido's marriage remains a matter of speculation, and there is no sign that Chioma has publicly expressed displeasure over the footage.

For now, Roskey's message to VDM is clear: whatever point he is trying to make, Davido's marriage should not become collateral damage.
