Usher and Chris Brown reportedly argued backstage before delayed Atlanta show

7 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Usher and Chris Brown reportedly argued backstage before their joint concert in Atlanta on Thursday, contributing to a delay of nearly three hours. The dispute was said to be over whether to start while fans were still arriving.

Read more

R&B stars Usher and Chris Brown reportedly had an argument backstage before their concert in Atlanta on Thursday night, 5 November 2026, which contributed to a delay of almost three hours.

What happened

According to reports citing people close to the tour, the disagreement was about whether to start the show while many fans were still stuck in heavy traffic around the venue. Usher reportedly wanted to go ahead, while Brown preferred to wait until more concertgoers had reached their seats. The two are said to have exchanged words, with one source describing them as highly competitive throughout their joint tour and blaming the clash on egos.

Tour to continue

The sources reportedly said the two had not settled their differences, but did not believe the row threatened the rest of the tour. The pair were scheduled to perform in Atlanta again on Friday night. Some fans complained online about the long wait before the show began.

Background

Usher and Chris Brown are two of the biggest names in R&B and have collaborated in the past. The two reportedly had a confrontation at Brown's birthday party in 2023, when Usher was said to have stepped in after Brown began shouting at singer Teyana Taylor. Neither singer had publicly commented on the latest reports at the time of writing.

Why it matters

Both artists have large fan bases in Nigeria, and Chris Brown has strong links with Nigerian stars, having worked with Davido and other Afrobeats acts.

Davido's aide Israel DMW has denied that the singer had any hand in an alleged plot to have VeryDarkMan arrested, or in Naira Marley's troubles during the Mohbad case. He says those who told VDM otherwise lied.

Davido's loyal aide, Israel DMW, has come out to defend his boss following speculation linking the Afrobeats superstar to an alleged plot to get social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) arrested.

"Oga had no hands in it"

In a post addressed to "Dearest Nigerians", Israel said Davido had no hand in Naira Marley's ordeal during the Mohbad case, nor in any plot to see VDM arrested.

He said those who were in the WhatsApp group linked to the matter got there without Davido's instructions or approval, and that those who told VDM otherwise "fully lied".

Sam Klef, Mohbad's catalogue and Zino

Israel also asked why Davido would have any issue with Sam Klef, and denied that the singer ever asked for Mohbad's music catalogue or tried to sign Zino.

"Naira and oga are cool"

According to Israel, there was serious pressure on Davido at the time to unfollow Naira Marley on Instagram, but he categorically refused, saying he wanted the police to do their job and did not want to act on hearsay.

"Naira and oga are cool," he wrote, adding that the two even made a hit song together.

On an incident at Naira Marley's house, Israel said it involved Tolu 30BG, who had a disagreement with one of Naira's boys, and insisted that nobody was beaten or attacked.

Why the denial matters

VDM is known for speaking openly about controversial issues and challenging influential people, which has earned him both strong supporters and fierce critics. Whenever the names Davido and VeryDarkMan appear in the same controversy, Nigerians are bound to have plenty to say.

Israel's message is straightforward: whatever controversy surrounds VDM, his boss should not be accused of involvement without proof.

It is important to note that the allegations remain unverified. Israel's denial is his own position, and there is no established evidence that Davido took part in any plot to have VDM arrested.

Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 to win Nigeria's first U-17 Women's World Cup

7 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Nigeria's Flamingos have won the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup, beating Japan 2-1 in the final in Rabat, Morocco. Awawu Bashiru and Oluebube Umejiaku scored, making Nigeria the first African team to win the tournament.

Read more

Nigeria's under-17 women's team, the Flamingos, are world champions after beating Japan 2-1 in the final of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup in Rabat, Morocco, on Saturday, 7 November 2026.

The match

Awawu Bashiru gave Nigeria the lead in the 39th minute at the Mohammed VI Football Complex, and Oluebube Umejiaku doubled the advantage two minutes after half-time, in the 47th minute. Japan pulled a goal back through Fuki Ito in the 80th minute, but the Flamingos held on to claim the trophy.

History made

The victory makes Nigeria the first African country to win the competition, and gives the country its first FIFA world title in women's football at any level. Before this tournament, the Flamingos' best finish was third place in 2022. They reached the final by beating Brazil 3-1 in the semi-final, a match in which Bashiru also scored twice, while Japan beat North Korea on penalties in their semi-final.

Background

Japan came into the final as one of the strongest teams in youth women's football, having won the tournament in 2014 and finished as runners-up in 2010 and 2016. CAF president Patrice Motsepe had travelled to Rabat to watch the final after praising the Flamingos for making Africa proud. Before the match, captain Harmony Chidi said the team was determined to bring the trophy home.

What happens next

The win comes as the senior Super Eagles prepare for their AFCON qualifiers against Tanzania next week.
