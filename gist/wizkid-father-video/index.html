Mohbad's family asks for DNA test delay, wants details of US lab

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

The family of late singer Mohbad has asked for the DNA test on his son to be postponed until it is told which US laboratory will analyse the samples. The family says it is not opposing the test but wants transparency. The test was scheduled for Saturday in Lagos.

Read more

The family of the late singer Ilerioluwa 'Mohbad' Aloba has asked for the planned DNA test on his son, Liam, to be put on hold until it receives more information about how the samples will be handled.

What the family wants

In a statement signed by family head Samuel Aloba on Thursday, 29 October 2026, the family asked for the identity, address and accreditation of the laboratory in the United States that will analyse the samples. It also asked whether the Lagos State DNA and Forensic Centre would carry out the test itself or only collect samples to be sent abroad, and requested clear procedures to protect the samples on their journey. The family asked that all parties be given proper notice and that the court give further directions if needed.

Not opposing the test

The family said it was not against the paternity test and was not claiming that anything improper had happened. It said it only wanted transparency and safeguards to prevent any possible mishandling. The test had been scheduled to take place on Saturday, 31 October, at a hospital in Yaba, Lagos. It is not yet clear whether it will go ahead as planned.

Background

Mohbad died on 12 September 2023 at the age of 27, and his death sparked nationwide protests and calls for justice. The paternity of his son has since been the subject of a bitter dispute between his father, Joseph Aloba, and his widow, Omowunmi. A Lagos court ordered in May 2026 that the test be carried out by the Lagos State DNA and Forensic Centre.

What happens next

The widow's camp had not responded to the latest demand at the time of the report. Further court directions may be needed if the test is delayed.

D'Banj shares emotional tribute to late son Daniel

30 Oct 2026, 06:44 GMT

Singer D'Banj has shared a heartfelt message to his late son Daniel, saying he misses him and that the family has found a safe place in their healing. The boy drowned in 2018 when he was 13 months old.

Read more

Nigerian music star D'Banj has opened up about the loss of his son in an emotional post that touched many of his fans.

What he wrote

In an Instagram post shared on Thursday, 29 October 2026, the singer told his late son, Daniel, that he missed him and said he could not imagine what the boy's mother had been through. He wrote that the family had reached a safe space in their healing, alongside his wife, Lineo, and their other children. He thanked his son for teaching him what life really means, said he believed the boy was proud of the family, and asked him to greet his late uncle Femi. The post included several photos of father and son together.

Background

D'Banj's son, Daniel Oyebanjo III, drowned in June 2018 at the family home in Lagos when he was 13 months old. The tragedy shocked the entertainment industry, and the singer and his wife received messages of support from fans and colleagues at the time. D'Banj, one of the pioneers of modern Nigerian pop music, has spoken in the past about how the loss changed his life and his faith.

How people reacted

Fans and fellow celebrities flooded the comments with prayers and messages of comfort, praising the singer for speaking openly about grief and healing.

Tiwa Savage hits back at critic who mocked her age over 'African Baddie' single

29 Oct 2026, 20:44 GMT

Tiwa Savage has responded to a social media user who said she was too old to call herself a 'baddie', correcting him that she is 46 and listing her achievements. Her new single 'African Baddie' is due out on Friday.

Read more

Afrobeats star Tiwa Savage has fired back at a man who mocked her age over the title of her new single, 'African Baddie', in an exchange that quickly went viral.

What happened

On Thursday, 29 October 2026, a social media user reportedly questioned why a woman of 40 would call herself a 'baddie' and suggested she was not serious. Replying in a single post, the singer corrected him, saying she is actually 46, listed her achievements and described herself as unbothered. She asked what exactly his problem was and used the same post to promote her new release.

The new single

'African Baddie' is scheduled for release on Friday, 30 October. The singer has long presented herself as Africa's 'bad girl', and the title builds on that image.

Background

Tiwa Savage is one of Africa's most successful female artists. She has won major awards, signed with Universal Music Group and performed at the coronation concert of King Charles III in 2023. Her career spans more than a decade of hit songs, and she has frequently spoken about ageism and the pressure on women in the music industry.

How people reacted

Fans rallied behind the singer, praising her confidence and saying age should not limit how women express themselves. Others joked that the critic had given her free promotion a day before the song's release.

"Igbo businessmen are ashamed of Blord": content creator Peter Mbah praises VeryDarkMan's marketing strategy

30 Oct 2026, 11:15 GMT

Content creator Peter Mbah has criticised Blord's approach to marketing his power stations, saying VeryDarkMan has shown a better grasp of product promotion by documenting his Ratel units from production in China to live testing in Nigeria. He accused Blord of turning a business competition into a personal battle.

Read more

The business rivalry between entrepreneur Blord and social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has taken another turn, with content creator Peter Mbah publicly criticising the way Blord is marketing his power stations.

What Peter Mbah said

In a strongly worded statement, Peter Mbah said he was disappointed with what he called Blord's poor marketing strategy, and argued that VeryDarkMan has shown a better understanding of how to promote a product and engage customers.

He said Igbo businessmen are widely respected for their business intelligence, creativity and marketing skills, and questioned why Blord, despite his experience, seems more focused on social media confrontations than on convincing buyers of what his product can do.

Why he praised VDM

According to Peter Mbah, VeryDarkMan has documented the whole journey of his Ratel power stations, from the production process in China to the arrival of the container in Nigeria and practical tests of the units on camera.

He said this approach has drawn public attention and helped build confidence among potential buyers.

Blord was first, but VDM is louder

Peter Mbah also pointed out that Blord's power stations reportedly arrived in Nigeria before VDM's, yet VDM appears to be generating far more public discussion through his demonstrations.

He accused Blord of turning what should have been healthy business competition into a personal fight.

The bigger lesson for Nigerian entrepreneurs

Beyond the rivalry, the row raises a wider question about how products are sold today. Customers increasingly want practical proof that a product can deliver what it promises. Showing how it performs, explaining its features and answering buyers' concerns can be more persuasive than trading insults with a competitor.

In the end, though, both brands will be judged on product quality, battery capacity, durability, price and customer satisfaction.

For Peter Mbah, VeryDarkMan has already taken the lead in winning public interest.
