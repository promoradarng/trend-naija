Atiku criticises Tinubu over retired police officers, promises pension reforms

2 Nov 2026, 18:01 GMT

Atiku Abubakar, presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has criticised President Bola Tinubu over the treatment of retired police officers and unveiled what he called a Police Dignity Guarantee Plan.

What he said

In comments reported on Monday, 2 November 2026, Atiku condemned what he described as the government's failure to protect retired officers who have been protesting at the National Pension Commission in Abuja over their pensions. He reportedly faulted the administration for not acting on a police exit bill which he said has been awaiting the President's signature since March 2026.

His promises

Atiku said that if elected, he would restore gratuity for retired officers and implement a Police Pension Board Bill within 30 days of taking office. He said police officers who served the country deserve to retire with dignity.

Background

Retired police officers have held repeated protests in Abuja over what they say are poor pensions under the contributory pension scheme, and have called for police to be removed from the scheme. The federal government has said it is working on reforms to improve welfare for serving and retired officers. Atiku has recently launched several attacks on the government's record, including accusing Tinubu of failing to keep his promises on electricity. The APC has dismissed such criticism, saying the government's reforms are beginning to show results.

What happens next

The Presidency had not responded to Atiku's latest comments at the time of reporting.

  • Atiku says a police exit bill has been unsigned since March.
  • He promises to restore gratuity for retired officers.
  • He would act on a pension board bill within 30 days.

Peter Obi corrects claims that his Washington visit was an endorsement from the US Congress

18 Nov 2026, 16:43 GMT

Peter Obi says his recent trip to Washington involved talks on Nigeria's development, citizens' welfare and democracy, and that he neither sought nor announced any endorsement, after social media posts claimed the US Congress had backed him.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has corrected claims that his recent visit to Washington amounted to an endorsement from the United States Congress.

What Obi said

According to Obi, the trip involved discussions about Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and the state of democracy. He said he neither sought nor announced any endorsement.

He said he met serving and former US lawmakers and other stakeholders during the visit.

How the confusion started

The confusion followed a viral video in which retired US Army Colonel Albert Short praised Obi.

Some social media posts presented those personal remarks as if they were official backing from the US Congress, and the claim spread quickly online.

What the facts show

There is no public evidence that the US Congress as an institution has endorsed Obi's 2027 presidential bid. Congress does not endorse candidates in other countries' elections.

A meeting with politicians, or kind words from an individual, is not the same as an endorsement from their institution.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections drawing closer, claims about foreign support for candidates are likely to keep spreading on social media. Obi's correction is a reminder for supporters on all sides to check claims before sharing them.

DSS says it found no evidence it planned to disrupt ADC campaign in Sokoto, asks Atiku to retract or provide proof

18 Nov 2026, 07:57 GMT

The Department of State Services says its internal investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off in Sokoto. It has asked Atiku Abubakar to retract or clarify his allegation, or provide evidence.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) says its investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential campaign in Sokoto.

How it started

Earlier this month, former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar accused the leadership of the DSS of recruiting protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto.

The allegation drew wide attention, and the DSS responded by ordering an investigation into the claim.

The DSS findings

The agency now says its investigation found nothing to support the allegation that it planned to recruit protesters or disrupt the ADC campaign.

It has asked Atiku to either retract or clarify his claim, or to provide evidence that could support further investigation.

Not the final word

It is important to note that this is the DSS's own preliminary finding. It was not an independent investigation, which means the agency effectively investigated an allegation made against itself.

Atiku has not issued a fresh public response to the DSS findings.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections approaching, allegations that security agencies are being used against opposition parties are likely to keep coming up. Public trust depends on such claims being properly examined, whether they turn out to be true or not.

Dangote Refinery considers bigger IPO after ₦6 trillion in subscriptions

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering enlarging its share sale by up to 30% after investors put in nearly ₦6 trillion. That could take the offer to about $2.1 billion, one of Africa's largest.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering expanding its initial public offering (IPO) by as much as 30%, after strong investor demand pushed subscriptions to nearly ₦6 trillion, or about $4.5 billion.

The numbers

The refinery, owned by billionaire Aliko Dangote, initially planned to sell 4.1 billion shares. Demand is said to have exceeded the shares on offer by nearly three times, prompting talks about increasing the allocation.

If the expansion goes ahead, the value of the share sale could rise to about $2.1 billion, potentially making it one of the biggest stock market offerings on the continent.

Why it matters

The refinery is the largest in Africa, and its pricing decisions have recently driven changes in pump prices across Nigeria.

Air Peace offers ₦80,000 domestic fares to serving and retired military personnel

Air Peace chairman Allen Onyema has announced a special ₦80,000 one-way domestic fare for serving and retired members of the armed forces. He said it recognises their sacrifices for the country.

Read more

Air Peace has announced a special one-way domestic fare of ₦80,000 for serving and retired military personnel.

The announcement

The airline's chairman, Allen Onyema, disclosed the initiative on Monday, 16 November 2026, while delivering a lecture at the Chief of Defence Intelligence Annual Conference in Abuja.

He said the discounted fare would apply across all of Air Peace's domestic routes, and would cover serving officers and veterans who have served Nigeria since independence in 1960.

Why the airline is doing it

Onyema said the initiative was meant to recognise the sacrifices made by members of the armed forces in protecting the country.
