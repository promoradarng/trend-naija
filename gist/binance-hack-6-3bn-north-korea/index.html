Binance reportedly hit by $6.3bn hack, the biggest crypto theft on record

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has reportedly suffered a hack worth about $6.3 billion, which would make it the largest crypto theft ever recorded.

What happened

Binance announced the breach on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and suspended deposits and withdrawals while it worked to contain the damage. Its preliminary investigation reportedly found that the attackers got in through a compromised laptop belonging to one of its engineers, and used that access to carry out unauthorised transactions. Founder Changpeng Zhao confirmed that the exchange appeared to have been the victim of a cyberattack.

What Binance says

The exchange said about $3 billion of the stolen funds had already been frozen, although other reports put the frozen amount lower. Binance said it would cover all remaining losses, assured customers that their holdings were safe and said it was working with law enforcement. It promised a full technical report once its investigation is complete.

Who is suspected

Blockchain analysis firm TRM said it believed with high confidence that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group. The firm warned that the stolen money could be used to fund North Korea's weapons programmes. North Korea has not commented.

Compensation plan

In an update on 30 October, Binance said it had set aside more than $6.34 billion of its own funds to compensate affected users, separate from the roughly $3 billion that has been frozen. More than $5 billion of that reserve is already held in the same cryptocurrencies that were stolen, and the exchange is buying the rest on the market, which it warned could take longer for some tokens. It said customers would be able to check their holdings through its proof-of-reserves system, but gave no date for when compensation will be completed.

FBI names the hackers

In an advisory dated 31 October, the US Federal Bureau of Investigation said North Korean state-backed hackers linked to the TraderTraitor group were responsible for the theft. The FBI said the stolen funds were being converted mainly into Ether and could be moved through decentralised finance platforms and instant swap services to hide their trail. It published a list of wallet addresses linked to the theft and urged crypto services to block transactions involving them. The FBI worked with the US Secret Service and the Internal Revenue Service on the case, and Binance founder Changpeng Zhao thanked the agencies for helping to track and freeze funds.

Market impact

The price of Bitcoin reportedly fell by about 5 per cent to below $77,000 after news of the breach. The previous record crypto theft was about $1.5 billion stolen from the Bybit exchange in 2025, also blamed on North Korean hackers.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Binance has millions of users in Africa, and Nigeria is one of the world's biggest markets for crypto trading. Users are being advised to watch for official updates from the exchange and to be wary of scammers posing as Binance support staff.

  • Investigators link the attack to a North Korean hacking group.

Kenyan cult leader Paul Mackenzie jailed for 200 years over Shakahola deaths

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

A Kenyan court has sentenced pastor Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison for the murder of 190 children and one adult who died after he ordered his followers to fast. His assistant was jailed for 160 years.

Read more

A court in Kenya has sentenced cult leader Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison over the deaths of 191 people linked to his church.

The sentence

The High Court in the coastal city of Mombasa handed down the sentence on Friday, 6 November 2026, after finding Mackenzie, leader of the Good News International Church, guilty of murdering 190 children and one adult. Justice Diana Kavedza said he bore the main criminal responsibility for moving his followers to a remote area and ordering them to fast to death, telling them it was the way to meet Jesus. She described the killings as cold and calculated and said the accused had shown no remorse. His assistant pastor, Smart Mwakalama, was sentenced to 160 years.

The trial

Mackenzie was tried alongside 28 of his followers, including guards who enforced the fasting and men who helped hide the graves. All 29 were found guilty on 19 October. The prosecution had asked for the death penalty for Mackenzie. He also faces terrorism and other charges in separate cases.

Background

Mackenzie was arrested in April 2023 after a rights group alerted the authorities and some followers were rescued. Police later exhumed more than 400 bodies from shallow graves on a large ranch in the Shakahola forest in Kilifi County. Kenya's national human rights commission put the death toll at 429 in 2024, and many victims have still not been identified. The case shocked Kenya and led to calls for tighter regulation of churches.

How people reacted

Some relatives of victims said they had hoped for a death sentence and criticised the authorities for not acting sooner.

Bavarian deputy leader Hubert Aiwanger found dead in Berlin hotel

6 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Hubert Aiwanger, Bavaria's deputy premier and leader of Germany's Free Voters party, has been found dead in a Berlin hotel room at the age of 55. Prosecutors say an autopsy found no sign that anyone else was involved.

Read more

Hubert Aiwanger, the deputy premier of the German state of Bavaria and leader of the Free Voters party, has died at the age of 55.

What happened

Aiwanger was found dead in his hotel room in central Berlin on the evening of Wednesday, 4 November 2026, police and prosecutors confirmed. On Thursday, prosecutors said an autopsy had found no evidence that anyone else was involved in his death. He had reportedly been due to speak at the opening of his party's new Berlin office on Wednesday afternoon, but pulled out, citing health concerns.

Who he was

Aiwanger became leader of the Free Voters in Bavaria in 2006 and national party chairman in 2010. Under him, the party entered the Bavarian state parliament in 2008, and since 2018 it has governed Bavaria as the junior coalition partner of the Christian Social Union (CSU), with Aiwanger serving as deputy premier and economy minister. Known for his blunt, folksy style, he presented his party as an alternative to the CSU, but it never won seats in the national parliament.

Tributes

Bavarian premier Markus Söder described his sudden death as an absolute tragedy. Aiwanger is survived by his partner, Free Voters politician Tanja Schweiger, and their two children.

What happens next

The Free Voters will need to choose a new leader, and the Bavarian government will appoint a new deputy premier and economy minister.

Bomb threats hit at least 25 passenger flights across Europe

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

At least 25 flights across Europe were targeted by bomb threats on Thursday, forcing diversions, emergency landings and fighter jet escorts. No explosives were found on the aircraft searched. It was the second wave of threats this week.

Read more

At least 25 passenger flights across Europe were hit by bomb threats on Thursday, 5 November 2026, causing diversions, emergency landings and security checks at several airports.

What happened

According to reports, an Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome carrying 151 passengers and six crew was diverted to a French military air base at Évreux, where bomb disposal experts inspected it. Three Ryanair flights to Bulgaria from Copenhagen, Madrid and London were also targeted. Two landed in Sofia and the third was diverted to Plovdiv. Italian fighter jets escorted a Finnair flight from Helsinki to Rome after a bomb alert. Flights operated by Wizz Air, LOT Polish Airlines and Aegean Airlines were also affected, with incidents reported in countries including France, Spain, Poland, Denmark, Italy, the Czech Republic, Austria and Romania.

No explosives found

No explosives were found on any of the aircraft searched so far, and all the affected planes landed safely. The threats followed another wave on Tuesday, when at least seven flights across Europe were targeted.

Background

Hoax bomb threats against airlines cause major disruption and cost, as pilots and authorities must treat every threat as real. Investigators have not said publicly who is behind the latest threats or whether the incidents are linked. European security agencies have warned in recent years of attempts to disrupt transport and infrastructure.

What it means for travellers

Passengers flying in Europe, including Nigerians travelling for the holidays, may face extra security checks and delays while investigations continue.

Tinubu approves Ibom Deep Seaport request during Akwa Ibom campaign, promises jobs and economic development

6 Nov 2026, 20:21 GMT

At his campaign rally in Uyo, President Bola Tinubu announced on the spot that he had approved a request from Akwa Ibom's leadership for international export status for the Ibom Deep Seaport. He also promised jobs, skills development and investment in the state's palm economy.

Read more

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has approved a request linked to the long-awaited Ibom Deep Seaport, announcing the decision while addressing supporters at his presidential campaign rally in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State.

"I look, and I approve it"

Speaking from the podium, flanked by party leaders and security officials, Tinubu told the crowd that the state's leadership had brought a request to him.

"Now your leader makes a request, for international export status. I look, and I approve it," he said, to cheers from supporters.

The Ibom Deep Seaport has been one of Akwa Ibom's biggest ambitions for years, with successive state governments pushing for it as a way to open the state to international trade and create jobs.

Promises of jobs and skills

Tinubu also used the occasion to make several promises about the state's economic future.

"I promise you skill development. I promise you jobs," he said. "We are going to plant a lot of economic trees."

He pointed to the palm tree, a major part of the region's economy, as an opportunity. "There is no reason why we cannot use the value of palm trees and the palm here," he said, adding that there was no reason the country could not create skills development that is "everlasting".

"Development must be even"

The President said growth must reach every part of the country. "Development must be even, inclusive and reasonable. It must bring progress and create economic value," he said.

What it means

If the approval leads to real progress on the seaport, it could be a major boost for Akwa Ibom's economy, bringing shipping, logistics and related businesses to the state.

But campaign promises are easy to make at rallies. Nigerians will be watching to see whether the approval is followed by funding, construction and the jobs that have been promised.
