Binance reportedly hit by $6.3bn hack, the biggest crypto theft on record

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has reportedly suffered a hack worth about $6.3 billion, which would make it the largest crypto theft ever recorded.

What happened

Binance announced the breach on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and suspended deposits and withdrawals while it worked to contain the damage. Its preliminary investigation reportedly found that the attackers got in through a compromised laptop belonging to one of its engineers, and used that access to carry out unauthorised transactions. Founder Changpeng Zhao confirmed that the exchange appeared to have been the victim of a cyberattack.

What Binance says

The exchange said about $3 billion of the stolen funds had already been frozen, although other reports put the frozen amount lower. Binance said it would cover all remaining losses, assured customers that their holdings were safe and said it was working with law enforcement. It promised a full technical report once its investigation is complete.

Who is suspected

Blockchain analysis firm TRM said it believed with high confidence that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group. The firm warned that the stolen money could be used to fund North Korea's weapons programmes. North Korea has not commented.

Compensation plan

In an update on 30 October, Binance said it had set aside more than $6.34 billion of its own funds to compensate affected users, separate from the roughly $3 billion that has been frozen. More than $5 billion of that reserve is already held in the same cryptocurrencies that were stolen, and the exchange is buying the rest on the market, which it warned could take longer for some tokens. It said customers would be able to check their holdings through its proof-of-reserves system, but gave no date for when compensation will be completed.

FBI names the hackers

In an advisory dated 31 October, the US Federal Bureau of Investigation said North Korean state-backed hackers linked to the TraderTraitor group were responsible for the theft. The FBI said the stolen funds were being converted mainly into Ether and could be moved through decentralised finance platforms and instant swap services to hide their trail. It published a list of wallet addresses linked to the theft and urged crypto services to block transactions involving them. The FBI worked with the US Secret Service and the Internal Revenue Service on the case, and Binance founder Changpeng Zhao thanked the agencies for helping to track and freeze funds.

Market impact

The price of Bitcoin reportedly fell by about 5 per cent to below $77,000 after news of the breach. The previous record crypto theft was about $1.5 billion stolen from the Bybit exchange in 2025, also blamed on North Korean hackers.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Binance has millions of users in Africa, and Nigeria is one of the world's biggest markets for crypto trading. Users are being advised to watch for official updates from the exchange and to be wary of scammers posing as Binance support staff.

  • Binance says the hack began with an engineer's compromised laptop.
  • About $3bn has reportedly been frozen; Binance says it will cover losses.
  • Investigators link the attack to a North Korean hacking group.

Suspended Reform UK councillor convicted over post saying Nigerians should be 'melted down'

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Glenn Gibbins, a suspended Reform UK councillor, has been found guilty of sending a malicious communication after a Facebook post saying Nigerians should be melted down and used to fill potholes.

Read more

A suspended Reform UK councillor in England has been convicted over a social media post about Nigerians.

The case

Glenn Gibbins, 66, was found guilty at South Tyneside Magistrates' Court of sending a malicious communication, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. In a Facebook post, he had complained about the number of Nigerians in the town and said they should be melted down and used to fill potholes. He told the court on Monday that the post was 'naughty' but that he did not mean it literally. The prosecutor said the comment conjured up despicable images and implied that Nigerians were less than human.

How it came to light

The post was found by the anti-racism group Hope Not Hate when it reviewed Gibbins's social media after he was announced as a Reform UK candidate for Sunderland City Council. He was later suspended by the party.

Background

Reform UK is led by Nigel Farage. Nigerians make up one of the largest African communities in the UK.

Paul McCartney becomes a billionaire, third-richest musician in the world

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Former Beatle Paul McCartney is now worth about $1.4 billion, according to Forbes, making him the world's third-richest musician after Jay-Z and Taylor Swift.

Read more

Paul McCartney has officially become a billionaire.

The figures

Forbes now values the former Beatles member's fortune at about $1.4 billion, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, making him the third-richest musician in the world. Only rapper and businessman Jay-Z, at about $2.8 billion, and Taylor Swift, at about $2 billion, are ranked higher.

How he made it

Forbes attributes his wealth to six decades of songwriting, shrewd acquisitions of music rights and personal assets. Despite their global fame in the 1960s, The Beatles earned relatively little from their early record contracts, something McCartney has spoken about in the past.

Background

McCartney co-wrote many of The Beatles' most famous songs with John Lennon and went on to a hugely successful solo career and with the band Wings.

James Gunn and Peter Safran to step down as DC Studios bosses

10 Nov 2026, 17:47 GMT

James Gunn and Peter Safran will step down as co-CEOs of DC Studios at the end of November, four years after taking charge, following Skydance's purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery. Former Marvel executive Jeremy Latcham will take over.

Read more

Filmmaker James Gunn and producer Peter Safran are stepping down as co-chief executives of DC Studios.

The announcement

The pair announced on Monday, 9 November 2026, that they will leave their roles at the end of November, four years after taking charge, following Skydance's purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery. They will be succeeded by former Marvel Studios executive Jeremy Latcham, who worked as a producer on films including 'Iron Man', 'The Avengers' and 'Guardians of the Galaxy'.

Gunn's message

In an Instagram post, Gunn, who directed the 2025 film 'Superman', described leading DC Studios as a dream come true but said the job had been challenging, with corporate demands taking a toll on his personal life and leaving him little time with loved ones. He thanked fans and credited Safran with handling much of the corporate side of their work. Gunn will continue to work on several DC projects despite leaving the leadership role.

Background

Gunn and Safran took over DC Studios in late 2022 with plans to build a new connected universe of DC superhero films and series. The change comes as the new owners of Warner Bros. reshape the company.

US Congress to hold hearing on Nigeria's protection of Christians and the Middle Belt

10 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

A US House subcommittee chaired by Congressman Chris Smith will hold a hearing on Thursday, 12 November, to assess Nigeria's efforts to protect Christians and curb violence in the Middle Belt, with State Department officials due to testify.

Read more

A United States congressional committee will hold a hearing this week to review Nigeria's record on protecting Christians and tackling violence in the Middle Belt.

The hearing

The House Foreign Affairs Africa Subcommittee will hold the session on Thursday, 12 November 2026, according to a notice from the office of its chairman, Republican Congressman Chris Smith of New Jersey, issued on Monday. The hearing is titled 'One Year Later: Assessing Nigeria's Efforts to Protect Christians and Secure the Middle Belt'.

What will be discussed

The hearing will examine religious violence by extremist groups, including Boko Haram, and how effectively the Nigerian government has responded to attacks on vulnerable communities. It will also consider what measures the United States could press for to make sure perpetrators are held accountable and to prevent future attacks on Christians and non-radical Muslims. The first panel will hear from two senior State Department officials, Frank Garcia, assistant secretary of state for African affairs, and Riley Barnes, assistant secretary of state for democracy, human rights and labour, followed by outside experts.

Background

The title refers to developments around a year ago, when the US designated Nigeria a 'Country of Particular Concern' over religious freedom. The Nigerian government has rejected claims of a targeted genocide against Christians, saying insecurity affects people of all faiths, and has pointed to military operations against armed groups. Attacks have continued in states such as Plateau and Benue, including a deadly attack in Barkin Ladi at the weekend.
