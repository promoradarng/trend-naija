Binance reportedly hit by $6.3bn hack, the biggest crypto theft on record

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has reportedly suffered a hack worth about $6.3 billion, which would make it the largest crypto theft ever recorded.

What happened

Binance announced the breach on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and suspended deposits and withdrawals while it worked to contain the damage. Its preliminary investigation reportedly found that the attackers got in through a compromised laptop belonging to one of its engineers, and used that access to carry out unauthorised transactions. Founder Changpeng Zhao confirmed that the exchange appeared to have been the victim of a cyberattack.

What Binance says

The exchange said about $3 billion of the stolen funds had already been frozen, although other reports put the frozen amount lower. Binance said it would cover all remaining losses, assured customers that their holdings were safe and said it was working with law enforcement. It promised a full technical report once its investigation is complete.

Who is suspected

Blockchain analysis firm TRM said it believed with high confidence that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group. The firm warned that the stolen money could be used to fund North Korea's weapons programmes. North Korea has not commented.

Compensation plan

In an update on 30 October, Binance said it had set aside more than $6.34 billion of its own funds to compensate affected users, separate from the roughly $3 billion that has been frozen. More than $5 billion of that reserve is already held in the same cryptocurrencies that were stolen, and the exchange is buying the rest on the market, which it warned could take longer for some tokens. It said customers would be able to check their holdings through its proof-of-reserves system, but gave no date for when compensation will be completed.

FBI names the hackers

In an advisory dated 31 October, the US Federal Bureau of Investigation said North Korean state-backed hackers linked to the TraderTraitor group were responsible for the theft. The FBI said the stolen funds were being converted mainly into Ether and could be moved through decentralised finance platforms and instant swap services to hide their trail. It published a list of wallet addresses linked to the theft and urged crypto services to block transactions involving them. The FBI worked with the US Secret Service and the Internal Revenue Service on the case, and Binance founder Changpeng Zhao thanked the agencies for helping to track and freeze funds.

Market impact

The price of Bitcoin reportedly fell by about 5 per cent to below $77,000 after news of the breach. The previous record crypto theft was about $1.5 billion stolen from the Bybit exchange in 2025, also blamed on North Korean hackers.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Binance has millions of users in Africa, and Nigeria is one of the world's biggest markets for crypto trading. Users are being advised to watch for official updates from the exchange and to be wary of scammers posing as Binance support staff.

  • About $3bn has reportedly been frozen; Binance says it will cover losses.
  • Investigators link the attack to a North Korean hacking group.

Guinea deports 1,557 West Africans convicted of illegal gold mining

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Guinea has deported 1,557 West Africans, mostly from Burkina Faso and Mali, who had been jailed for illegal gold mining, as it steps up a crackdown on unauthorised mining.

Read more

Guinea has expelled 1,557 West African nationals who were arrested and convicted for illegal gold mining.

The deportation

According to judicial and security sources quoted in reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, the deportees, mostly citizens of Burkina Faso and Mali, were arrested at mining sites during security operations. After serving prison sentences of 18 months to two years, they were taken in a convoy of 44 vehicles from the capital, Conakry, to the border town of Kouremale on the Mali border, arriving on Saturday.

The crackdown

Guinean authorities stepped up operations against illegal mining in early November, particularly in Siguiri, a major gold-producing region in the north-east. The government says the aim is to stop illegal exploitation of mineral resources and limit environmental damage from unregulated mining.

Background

The crackdown has also raised tensions between miners and the authorities.

A court in Jordan has sentenced three men to death by hanging for a deadly attack on a church in the south of the country.

The verdict

The State Security Court on Sunday, 15 November 2026, found the men, two brothers and a relative by marriage, guilty of terrorist attacks resulting in death, weapons offences, conspiracy to commit terrorist acts and promoting the ideas of a terrorist organisation, according to a statement from the Jordanian military.

The attack

Gunmen killed nine people and wounded 25 when they stormed a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud village, in the Karak governorate, on 23 October 2026. The three suspects fled after the attack and were arrested three days later.

The attack shocked Jordan, a Muslim-majority country where Christians make up around three per cent of the population.

Who is affected

According to reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Zambia and the Republic of the Congo have pulled out, while representatives from Zimbabwe and South Sudan were still awaiting decisions ahead of the final on 24 November. FG seeks another $500 million World Bank loan to expand renewable energy programme

16 Nov 2026, 16:20 GMT

The Federal Government is seeking an additional $500 million loan from the World Bank for its renewable energy programme, which would raise the World Bank's total financing for the project to $1.25 billion. Many Nigerians are asking where the results of earlier spending can be seen.

Read more

The Federal Government is seeking an additional $500 million loan from the World Bank to expand its renewable energy programme.

What the money is for

The programme is meant to expand access to electricity through renewable sources such as solar mini-grids and stand-alone solar systems, particularly for homes, businesses, schools and health centres in areas the national grid does not reach.

In 2023, the World Bank approved $750 million for Nigeria's Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up (DARES) project, which aims to bring clean electricity to millions of Nigerians. The new $500 million would raise the World Bank's total financing for the project to $1.25 billion.

"Where is the renewable energy?"

The news has been met with frustration by many Nigerians, who say they are yet to see the impact of the money already borrowed.

Millions of households and small businesses still depend on generators, while frequent grid collapses and rising electricity tariffs continue to bite. For many people, the question is simple: where is the renewable energy?

The debt question

The request also comes amid growing concern about Nigeria's rising debt. Critics argue that new loans should only be taken when there is clear evidence that earlier funds have been well spent and are delivering results.

Supporters of the programme argue that renewable energy is essential for closing Nigeria's huge electricity gap, and that such projects take time to deliver.
