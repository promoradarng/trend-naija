Binance reportedly hit by $6.3bn hack, the biggest crypto theft on record

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has reportedly suffered a hack worth about $6.3 billion, which would make it the largest crypto theft ever recorded.

What happened

Binance announced the breach on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and suspended deposits and withdrawals while it worked to contain the damage. Its preliminary investigation reportedly found that the attackers got in through a compromised laptop belonging to one of its engineers, and used that access to carry out unauthorised transactions. Founder Changpeng Zhao confirmed that the exchange appeared to have been the victim of a cyberattack.

What Binance says

The exchange said about $3 billion of the stolen funds had already been frozen, although other reports put the frozen amount lower. Binance said it would cover all remaining losses, assured customers that their holdings were safe and said it was working with law enforcement. It promised a full technical report once its investigation is complete.

Who is suspected

Blockchain analysis firm TRM said it believed with high confidence that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group. The firm warned that the stolen money could be used to fund North Korea's weapons programmes. North Korea has not commented.

Compensation plan

In an update on 30 October, Binance said it had set aside more than $6.34 billion of its own funds to compensate affected users, separate from the roughly $3 billion that has been frozen. More than $5 billion of that reserve is already held in the same cryptocurrencies that were stolen, and the exchange is buying the rest on the market, which it warned could take longer for some tokens. It said customers would be able to check their holdings through its proof-of-reserves system, but gave no date for when compensation will be completed.

FBI names the hackers

In an advisory dated 31 October, the US Federal Bureau of Investigation said North Korean state-backed hackers linked to the TraderTraitor group were responsible for the theft. The FBI said the stolen funds were being converted mainly into Ether and could be moved through decentralised finance platforms and instant swap services to hide their trail. It published a list of wallet addresses linked to the theft and urged crypto services to block transactions involving them. The FBI worked with the US Secret Service and the Internal Revenue Service on the case, and Binance founder Changpeng Zhao thanked the agencies for helping to track and freeze funds.

Market impact

The price of Bitcoin reportedly fell by about 5 per cent to below $77,000 after news of the breach. The previous record crypto theft was about $1.5 billion stolen from the Bybit exchange in 2025, also blamed on North Korean hackers.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Binance has millions of users in Africa, and Nigeria is one of the world's biggest markets for crypto trading. Users are being advised to watch for official updates from the exchange and to be wary of scammers posing as Binance support staff.

US flights resume after five-hour nationwide ground stop caused by telecom outage

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The US Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop on all departing flights on Friday, citing safety. More than 2,000 flights were cancelled and over 10,000 delayed, and officials blamed a telecommunications outage.

Read more

The United States briefly halted all departing flights across the country on Friday, 13 November 2026, causing widespread disruption.

What happened

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a sudden nationwide ground stop at 3.54pm Eastern time, citing extraordinary circumstances and safety, and said all departing traffic should remain on the ground until further notice. It said inbound international flights would be handled case by case. At the time, more than 4,000 flights bound for US destinations were in the air, according to flight-tracking data.

The impact

Within hours, more than 2,000 flights had been cancelled and more than 10,000 delayed, according to flight-tracking figures, and lawmakers demanded an explanation.

The cause

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication so far of anything malicious, and that the problem appeared to be a telecommunications outage. He said President Donald Trump had been briefed.

Ground stop lifted

The FAA lifted the ground stop after about five hours, with Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy saying airspace reopened at 8.15pm Eastern time, although operations would resume gradually. He said the outage was caused by overloaded switches and routers that led to gaps in communications and radar, and officials admitted that new technology added to the air traffic control network had overwhelmed older parts of the system. Officials confirmed it was not a cyberattack. More than 1.5 million passengers were affected, in the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years.

Iran fires drones at US base in Bahrain for first time in two months

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Iran launched drones at a US military installation in Bahrain on Friday in what it called retaliation for an American strike, its first attack on a US base in the Gulf in over two months. US forces said both drones were intercepted with no damage.

Iran has targeted a United States military facility in Bahrain, its first attempt to strike a US base in a Gulf state in more than two months.

What happened

US Central Command said American air defences intercepted and destroyed two Iranian attack drones launched towards Bahrain early on Friday, 13 November 2026, and reported no casualties or damage. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed through state-linked media that they had destroyed a US early-warning radar on the island, home to the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, in response to an alleged attack on a telecommunications tower in the southern Iranian town of Sirik. The US rejected that claim.

Escalation

The strike marks a sharp escalation after a pause since early September, during which Iran had focused on disrupting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Hopes of a deal between Washington and Tehran appear to be fading, with President Donald Trump reportedly telling Vice-President JD Vance that the US does not need a deal.

Why it matters

The US also recently withdrew from the FIFA U-17 World Cup in Qatar, citing a travel advisory linked to the risk of conflict in the region.

US revokes more than 6,000 visas in crackdown on 'birth tourism'

13 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The US State Department says its birth tourism task force has revoked more than 6,000 visas held by foreigners who travelled to give birth in the United States or helped others to do so. It did not say which nationalities were affected.

Read more

The United States has revoked more than 6,000 visas in a crackdown on so-called birth tourism.

What was announced

In a statement on X on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the State Department said its Birth Tourism Prevention Task Force had cancelled the visas of foreign nationals found to have taken part in or facilitated the practice. It said the crackdown targets not only people who travelled to give birth but also those who helped arrange such trips, and described the practice as illicit. The department did not release a breakdown of the nationalities affected.

What is birth tourism

Birth tourism refers to travelling to the United States specifically to give birth, so that the child acquires US citizenship under the birthright citizenship provision of the 14th Amendment to the US Constitution.

Why it matters to Nigerians

Giving birth in the US has been popular among some Nigerians who can afford it, and the US has previously warned that visitor visas may be refused to applicants suspected of travelling to give birth.
