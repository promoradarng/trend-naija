Binance reportedly hit by $6.3bn hack, the biggest crypto theft on record

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has reportedly suffered a hack worth about $6.3 billion, which would make it the largest crypto theft ever recorded.

What happened

Binance announced the breach on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and suspended deposits and withdrawals while it worked to contain the damage. Its preliminary investigation reportedly found that the attackers got in through a compromised laptop belonging to one of its engineers, and used that access to carry out unauthorised transactions. Founder Changpeng Zhao confirmed that the exchange appeared to have been the victim of a cyberattack.

What Binance says

The exchange said about $3 billion of the stolen funds had already been frozen, although other reports put the frozen amount lower. Binance said it would cover all remaining losses, assured customers that their holdings were safe and said it was working with law enforcement. It promised a full technical report once its investigation is complete.

Who is suspected

Blockchain analysis firm TRM said it believed with high confidence that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group. The firm warned that the stolen money could be used to fund North Korea's weapons programmes. North Korea has not commented.

Compensation plan

In an update on 30 October, Binance said it had set aside more than $6.34 billion of its own funds to compensate affected users, separate from the roughly $3 billion that has been frozen. More than $5 billion of that reserve is already held in the same cryptocurrencies that were stolen, and the exchange is buying the rest on the market, which it warned could take longer for some tokens. It said customers would be able to check their holdings through its proof-of-reserves system, but gave no date for when compensation will be completed.

FBI names the hackers

In an advisory dated 31 October, the US Federal Bureau of Investigation said North Korean state-backed hackers linked to the TraderTraitor group were responsible for the theft. The FBI said the stolen funds were being converted mainly into Ether and could be moved through decentralised finance platforms and instant swap services to hide their trail. It published a list of wallet addresses linked to the theft and urged crypto services to block transactions involving them. The FBI worked with the US Secret Service and the Internal Revenue Service on the case, and Binance founder Changpeng Zhao thanked the agencies for helping to track and freeze funds.

Market impact

The price of Bitcoin reportedly fell by about 5 per cent to below $77,000 after news of the breach. The previous record crypto theft was about $1.5 billion stolen from the Bybit exchange in 2025, also blamed on North Korean hackers.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Binance has millions of users in Africa, and Nigeria is one of the world's biggest markets for crypto trading. Users are being advised to watch for official updates from the exchange and to be wary of scammers posing as Binance support staff.

  • Investigators link the attack to a North Korean hacking group.

US appeals court gives Trump new route to challenge hush money conviction

26 Nov 2026, 14:41 GMT

A US federal appeals court has revived Donald Trump's bid to move his New York hush money case into federal court, where he hopes to have it dismissed on presidential immunity grounds.

Read more

A US federal appeals court has given President Donald Trump a fresh opening to try to overturn his New York hush money conviction.

The ruling

In a short order issued on Monday, 23 November 2026, a three-judge panel of the 2nd US Circuit Court of Appeals sent the dispute back to a federal district court in Manhattan, instructing a judge to rule quickly on Trump's request to transfer the case to federal court.

The panel found that the previous judge had been wrong to conclude that moving a case to federal court after conviction was never possible.

What Trump wants

If the case is transferred, Trump intends to seek its dismissal on presidential immunity grounds. Any decision could later return to the 2nd Circuit.

Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie settle long legal battle over French winery

Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie have settled their years-long dispute over Château Miraval out of court. The terms were not disclosed.

Read more

Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie have settled their long-running legal battle over the sale of their French winery, Château Miraval.

The settlement

In a joint statement on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, the former couple said they had resolved the case out of court in the best interests of their family and would not comment further. They added that any comments attributed to anonymous sources did not come from either of them or anyone close to them. The terms of the settlement were not disclosed.

The dispute

Pitt, 62, and Jolie, 51, bought the estate in the south of France in 2008 and had been in dispute over it since they split in 2016. In 2022, Pitt sued Jolie, claiming they had agreed that neither would sell their share without the other's consent, and accusing her of selling her stake to a Russian businessman without his permission. Jolie disputed his account in her response.

At least 30 killed as fuel tanker overturns and bursts into flames in Cairo

26 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

At least 30 people died and 15 were injured when a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday. The blaze engulfed 27 vehicles.

Read more

At least 30 people have died and 15 others were injured after a fuel tanker overturned and burst into flames on a busy stretch of the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday, 25 November 2026.

What happened

According to Egypt's Ministry of Health and Population, the fire broke out above Tunnel 10 in the densely populated Al-Warraq area of Imbaba, a suburb of Greater Cairo. Preliminary findings suggest the privately operated tanker swerved, hit a concrete road divider and toppled over, causing an explosion that spread to 27 nearby vehicles.

The response

Authorities sent 39 ambulances to the scene, taking survivors to three hospitals in the area. Firefighters brought the blaze under control, and heavy machinery was brought in to clear the burnt-out vehicles.

Condolences

Egyptian President Abdel-Fattah el-Sisi and Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly sent condolences to the families of the victims.
