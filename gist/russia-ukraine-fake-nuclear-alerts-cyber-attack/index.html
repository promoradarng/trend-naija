Hackers send fake nuclear attack alerts to millions in Russia and Ukraine

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

Emergency alert services in both Russia and Ukraine were reportedly hit by cyber attacks that sent false warnings of nuclear, chemical and radiation threats to civilians, according to reports on Monday, 2 November 2026.

In Russia

Fake nuclear attack warnings appeared on channels linked to Russia's Emergency Situations Ministry in Moscow, St Petersburg and several other regions at about 2.45am, reportedly telling residents to gather three weeks' supply of food and take shelter in cellars. Similar messages were reported on the ministry's mobile app, by text message and on the state-backed messaging platform MAX. The ministry described it as a massive cyber attack, but said its sirens, loudspeakers, television and radio warning systems had not been affected. It has not been established who carried out the attack.

In Ukraine

Hours earlier, Ukraine's Air Alert service sent a series of false notifications claiming radiation and chemical threats in Kyiv, a nuclear alert across the country and street fighting in the capital, and telling residents to stay indoors. Ukrainian authorities confirmed the service had been hacked and said it was working normally again after midnight. The app normally warns people of incoming Russian missile and drone attacks, often giving them only minutes to find shelter. Hours after the false alerts, real warnings sounded during a Russian attack on Odesa that reportedly injured four people.

Background

The incidents came amid continued strikes by both sides and talk of possible negotiations. A Kremlin adviser said he believed President Vladimir Putin would take a phone call from President Volodymyr Zelensky, although Putin has refused his calls in the past.

Why it matters

Experts warn that attacks on alert systems are dangerous because they can cause panic and make people less likely to trust genuine warnings.

  • False alerts warned of nuclear, chemical and radiation threats.
  • Russia called it a massive cyber attack.
  • Both countries said their systems were restored.

Four Nigerian-Americans win seats in US midterm elections

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Four Nigerian-Americans reportedly won state and local races in the US midterms, including Will Jawando as Montgomery County executive in Maryland and Onitsha-born Junior Ezeonu in the Texas House. A fifth, Georgia's Segun Adeyina, was leading in an uncalled race.

Read more

Nigerian-Americans have recorded a string of wins in the United States midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, according to reports.

Who won

Four Nigerian-Americans reportedly won state and local offices in Maryland, Texas, Washington DC and Pennsylvania. In Maryland, Democrat Will Jawando, whose father is Nigerian, defeated Republican Esther Wells to become Montgomery County executive, winning about 78 per cent of the vote with most ballots counted. He previously served on the Montgomery County Council. In Texas, 27-year-old Democrat Junior Ezeonu won the District 101 seat in the state House of Representatives. Born in Onitsha, Anambra State, he moved to Texas at the age of two, was elected to the Grand Prairie City Council in 2021 and became the city's youngest mayor pro tempore. He had defeated the incumbent in the Democratic primary and faced no Republican opponent. In Washington DC, Democrat Oye Owolewa won an at-large seat on the District of Columbia Council.

Still counting

A fifth Nigerian-American, Georgia state Representative Segun Adeyina, was reportedly leading in his race, which had not yet been called.

Background

The wins came on a night when Democrats were projected to take control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate. Nigerian-Americans are one of the most highly educated immigrant groups in the United States, and a growing number have entered politics at local, state and national level in recent years.

How people reacted

Many Nigerians at home and abroad celebrated the results online, describing them as a proud moment for the diaspora.

First Muslim senator and first Black woman governor among US midterm history-makers

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Abdul El-Sayed has reportedly become the first Muslim US senator after winning in Michigan, Keisha Lance Bottoms is the first Black woman elected governor of a US state, and Gina Hinojosa is set to be Texas's first Latina governor after defeating Greg Abbott.

Read more

The 2026 US midterm elections produced several historic firsts, according to results and projections reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

First Muslim senator

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed reportedly defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race, becoming the first Muslim member of the US Senate. A former Detroit health director, he ran on a progressive platform including Medicare for All and higher taxes on billionaires, and won the seat left by retiring Democrat Gary Peters. His campaign survived a bitter contest in which a Democratic rival he beat in the primary endorsed his Republican opponent.

First Black woman governor

In Georgia, former Atlanta mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms, 56, defeated Republican businessman Rick Jackson, becoming the first Black woman elected governor of any US state and Georgia's first female governor. Her victory reportedly ends 24 years of Republican control of the office. Atlanta-born Nigerian singer Davido shared the news on his Instagram Story.

Texas turns blue

In Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa reportedly defeated Republican governor Greg Abbott, who was seeking a fourth term, by about 53.5 per cent to 46.5 per cent. When she takes office in January 2027, the 52-year-old will be the state's first Latina governor and its first Democratic governor since Ann Richards left office in 1995.

More firsts

In New Hampshire, Democratic congressman Chris Pappas, 46, defeated former Republican senator John E. Sununu to become the first openly gay man elected to the US Senate, keeping the seat of retiring Democrat Jeanne Shaheen. In New Mexico, Democrat Deb Haaland, a member of Laguna Pueblo and a former US interior secretary, won the governorship, becoming the first Native American woman elected governor of a US state.

Kansas breaks a long streak

In Kansas, Democrat Adam Hamilton, a pastor who leads the largest United Methodist church in the country, reportedly defeated Republican senator Roger Marshall, becoming the first Democrat elected to the US Senate from the state since 1932. He ran as a moderate and won endorsements from some prominent Kansas Republicans.

Background

The results came as Democrats were projected to win control of both the House and the Senate, in what many analysts described as a rebuke of President Donald Trump's second-term agenda amid concern over the economy, tariffs and the conflict with Iran.

Democrats projected to win both House and Senate in blow to Trump

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

US networks are projecting that the Democrats have won control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate in the 2026 midterm elections. The party reportedly won Senate seats in states such as Texas, Iowa and Kansas, which Trump carried easily in 2024.

Read more

The Democratic Party is projected to have won control of both chambers of the United States Congress in the 2026 midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November, according to projections by US television networks reported early on Wednesday.

The results

According to the projections, the Democrats have taken the House of Representatives and the Senate. The Senate results were described as particularly striking, with Democrats reportedly winning seats in states that President Donald Trump carried by wide margins in 2024, including Texas, South Carolina, Ohio, Kansas and Iowa. Texas had reportedly not elected a Democrat to statewide office since 1994. Final results in some races may take days to confirm.

Why voters turned

Analysts have pointed to Trump's low approval ratings, voter anger over tariffs and inflation, and the long-running conflict with Iran as reasons for the swing. On the eve of the vote, US forces carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, while Trump told supporters that Iran might eventually have to be given 'the hammer'.

Reactions

Trump sought to distance himself from the results, writing on social media that he was not on the ballot and that Republicans would have won in a landslide if he had been. He had held rallies in 20 states in the final weeks of the campaign. In a later interview, he said he was willing to accept the results and would look to work with the Democrats. Former President Barack Obama said voters had sent a clear message that they want politics that brings people together, and praised candidates who won in areas once seen as long shots. Among the night's firsts, Abdul El-Sayed became the first Muslim US senator, Keisha Lance Bottoms became the first Black woman elected governor of a US state, and Gina Hinojosa defeated Texas governor Greg Abbott.

What it means

With control of both chambers, Democrats will be able to block much of the President's legislative agenda, launch investigations with the power to compel testimony, and hold up his nominees, including judges, during the last two years of his term. Trump remains president until January 2029.

Why it matters for Nigeria

US policy on trade, aid and immigration affects Nigeria directly. A Democratic Congress could push back on measures such as visa restrictions and changes to trade programmes, including this week's decision to remove South Africa and Rwanda from the AGOA duty-free scheme. The White House had not formally responded to the projections at the time of reporting.

Rochas Okorocha and Governor Hope Uzodinma seen laughing and singing together despite political rivalry. Who is fooling who?

4 Nov 2026, 23:06 GMT

Former Imo State Governor Rochas Okorocha and current Governor Hope Uzodinma have been seen smiling, joking and singing together, even though their camps have been locked in a legal dispute over a senatorial candidacy. The moment is a reminder to supporters not to turn politics into personal wars.

Read more

Nigerian politics will teach you lessons no classroom ever can.

Just look at former Imo State Governor Rochas Okorocha and the current governor, Hope Uzodinma, smiling, joking and singing together like two old friends who have never had a political disagreement.

Rivals on paper

These are the same political figures whose camps have been involved in a legal dispute over a senatorial candidacy.

Yet here they are, laughing and enjoying themselves, while some of their supporters are outside exchanging insults, fighting imaginary battles and treating political disagreements like personal family wars.

The real problem

Some supporters have destroyed friendships, fought their neighbours and made enemies of people they have known for years, all because of politicians who can sit together tomorrow, shake hands and laugh over a bottle of expensive wine.

The painful part is that some supporters are even willing to risk their lives defending these politicians. Meanwhile, the politicians themselves understand that politics is a game of interests, and today's enemy can become tomorrow's ally.

Look at Rochas and Hope again. Do they look like people who cannot stand each other?

A lesson for supporters

This should be a serious lesson to Nigerians, especially those who insult, threaten and attack others over political differences.

Support whoever you want. Campaign for your preferred candidate. But never allow political loyalty to turn you into an enemy of your fellow citizens.

At the end of the day, the politicians you are fighting for may be laughing together while you are still carrying their quarrel on your head.

Over to you

Nigerians, when will we learn that politicians are not worth dying for? Look at the picture and tell us: who is really fooling who?
