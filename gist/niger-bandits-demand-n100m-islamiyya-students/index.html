Bandits demand over ₦100 million for abducted Niger Islamiyya students, teacher says

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The kidnappers of a group of Islamiyya students abducted in Niger State have demanded more than ₦100 million for their release, according to their teacher.

What the teacher said

Malam Aminu Bako, whose students were taken while helping him harvest maize on his farm in Rafi Local Government Area, said on Thursday, 5 November 2026, that the bandits had contacted village leaders with the ransom demand. He said 15 children were abducted, and called for urgent military action to rescue them. According to him, only the acting chairman of the local government and the divisional police officer had visited since the abduction, and families were relying on local vigilantes to track the kidnappers.

Where the children may be

Bako said information gathered by vigilantes suggests the children are still in Niger State, held in an isolated building guarded by heavily armed men, possibly without enough food. He said he was under intense pressure from worried parents.

Background

The students were abducted on 31 October from a farm in Tungan-Bako village near Pandogari. Police at the time said 14 students were taken and that a joint security operation had been launched. Rafi and surrounding areas have suffered repeated attacks by armed groups, and kidnapping for ransom remains widespread in north-west and north-central Nigeria. Paying ransom is illegal in Nigeria, although families often feel they have no choice.

What happens next

Families and community leaders are calling on the federal and state governments to step up efforts to rescue the children.

  • The kidnappers reportedly want over ₦100 million.
  • The teacher says 15 children were taken.
  • He is calling for military action.

The Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics (ASUP), Zone C, has begun a strike in state-owned polytechnics and colleges of technology in four South-West states over unpaid academic allowances.

Where and when

The strike affects institutions in Ogun, Oyo, Osun and Ekiti States. The union said it took effect from Monday, 16 November 2026, after a 15-day ultimatum to the state governments expired.

The announcement was made at a press conference at The Polytechnic, Ibadan, on Thursday, 19 November.

The demands

The union wants immediate implementation of the Consolidated Academic Tools Allowance and the Earned Academic Allowance for academic staff, as well as payment of arrears dating back to 1 January 2026. It said the federal government had concluded the renegotiation of the union's agreement, but the state governments had yet to implement it.

The business community in Enugu State has been thrown into mourning following reports that billionaire businessman Chief Festus Oshaba-Onuh has died in a road accident.

What happened

Oshaba-Onuh, the chief executive of Ferotex Group and popularly known as Ferotex, reportedly died on the morning of Thursday, 19 November 2026, in a crash on the Enugu-Port Harcourt Expressway. He was said to be travelling back to Enugu at the time.

How the news spread

News of his death emerged after an Enugu-based social media influencer shared a video of the crash scene, showing a damaged SUV said to have been involved. The influencer described the death as a major loss to Enugu State and beyond.

Governor confirms

Enugu State Governor Peter Mbah confirmed the death in a social media post on Thursday, describing it as a grievous loss to the entire state and a painful personal blow. He described Oshaba-Onuh as a great son of the state. The businessman was known for his work in the construction sector.
