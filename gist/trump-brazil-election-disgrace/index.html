Trump calls Brazil's presidential election 'a disgrace' as Flavio Bolsonaro claims victory from the US

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

US President Donald Trump has reportedly called Brazil's presidential election a disgrace, raising tensions with Latin America's largest country.

What he said

Asked by reporters on Thursday, 29 October 2026, whether he accepted the victory of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, Trump reportedly described the election as a disgrace and an embarrassment to Brazilians, but said he did not want to say more at that time. He did not say whether he recognised Lula as Brazil's president.

Background

Lula, a veteran left-wing leader, faced Flavio Bolsonaro, the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, in the election. Flavio Bolsonaro, an ally of Trump, has claimed he was the true winner. He reportedly travelled to the United States the day before Trump's remarks, after going into exile from Brazil, and declared himself the rightful leader. Earlier this week, a Brazilian court had banned him from leaving the country, according to reports at the time.

Why it matters

Trump's comments reportedly go further than his earlier remarks on the vote and are likely to strain relations between Washington and Brasília. The United States and Brazil are major trading partners, and relations were already tense after Trump imposed tariffs on Brazilian goods and criticised the prosecution of Jair Bolsonaro.

What happens next

Brazil's government has not yet responded publicly to the latest comments, and it is not clear whether the United States will formally question the result.

  • Trump would not say whether he recognises Lula's win.
  • Flavio Bolsonaro declared himself the rightful leader after arriving in the US.
  • The comments are expected to strain US-Brazil relations.

Pope Leo XIV casts mail-in ballot in US midterm elections

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

The Vatican says Pope Leo XIV has voted by mail in the US midterm elections in his home state of Illinois, believed to be the first time a pope has voted in an American election. The Vatican said he believes citizens have a right and duty to take part in democracy.

Read more

Pope Leo XIV, the first American pope, has voted in the United States midterm elections, the Vatican has confirmed.

What happened

The Holy See press office said on Friday, 30 October 2026, that the Pope had cast a mail-in ballot in Illinois, where he was born. It said he had exercised his right to vote as a US citizen whenever he was eligible, and that he believes citizens have both a right and a duty to take part in democratic processes. His ballot is expected to include races for the US Senate, the governorship of Illinois and the House of Representatives. He is believed to be the first pope ever to vote in an American election.

Background

The Pope was born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago and holds both American and Peruvian citizenship, having spent many years as a missionary and bishop in Peru. He was elected pope in May 2025 after the death of Pope Francis. Public records show he voted regularly in Illinois before becoming pope, including in the 2024 presidential election. The Vatican did not say who he voted for.

Why it matters

The US midterm elections in November will decide control of Congress, and the Pope's decision to vote has drawn wide attention because of his role as a global religious leader. Supporters praised him for setting an example of civic duty, while others questioned whether a pope should take part in a national election.

What happens next

The Pope is expected to continue his public schedule, including a planned visit to South America.

Kemi Badenoch hits back after being called a 'Nigerian scammer' online

30 Oct 2026, 11:49 GMT

UK Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has condemned online attacks referring to her Nigerian background, after a former adviser linked to Nigel Farage repeatedly called her a scammer. She said Britain is not a racist country and the attacks would not work, while Farage said the comment was over the top but not racist.

Read more

Kemi Badenoch, the Nigerian-raised leader of Britain's Conservative Party, has condemned online attacks that referred to her heritage, saying they show what kind of people her critics are.

What happened

According to reports on Friday, 30 October 2026, Raheem Kassam, a former adviser linked to Nigel Farage's camp, repeatedly called Badenoch a scammer in social media posts over the weekend, mocking her Nigerian background and warning people not to give her their bank details. He also accused her of using her heritage to advance her career when it suited her.

What Badenoch said

Speaking on a British breakfast television programme, Badenoch said some people in Reform UK appeared to think that attacking her race would help them. She said Britain is not a racist country and that such attacks would not work, and urged former Conservative voters who had switched to Reform to come back.

Reform's response

Robert Jenrick, a former Conservative MP who has defected to Reform UK, reportedly declined to call the comments racist, describing them as perhaps rude or unpleasant. Badenoch said Jenrick was now Nigel Farage's problem. Farage said that mentioning that someone grew up in Nigeria was not racist, but later described Kassam's comment as over the top. A Labour spokesperson criticised Jenrick for not condemning the remarks.

Background

Badenoch was born in London, grew up partly in Lagos and moved back to Britain as a teenager. She became Conservative leader in 2024. Reform UK has been rising in opinion polls, and several former Conservatives have joined the party, increasing tensions between the two.

Binance reportedly hit by $6.3bn hack, the biggest crypto theft on record

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Crypto exchange Binance has reportedly lost about $6.3 billion in digital assets in a hack it says began with an engineer's compromised laptop. The exchange has frozen about $3 billion, promised to cover customers' losses and paused deposits and withdrawals. Investigators have linked the attack to a North Korean hacking group.

Read more

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has reportedly suffered a hack worth about $6.3 billion, which would make it the largest crypto theft ever recorded.

What happened

Binance announced the breach on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and suspended deposits and withdrawals while it worked to contain the damage. Its preliminary investigation reportedly found that the attackers got in through a compromised laptop belonging to one of its engineers, and used that access to carry out unauthorised transactions. Founder Changpeng Zhao confirmed that the exchange appeared to have been the victim of a cyberattack.

What Binance says

The exchange said about $3 billion of the stolen funds had already been frozen, although other reports put the frozen amount lower. Binance said it would cover all remaining losses, assured customers that their holdings were safe and said it was working with law enforcement. It promised a full technical report once its investigation is complete.

Who is suspected

Blockchain analysis firm TRM said it believed with high confidence that the attack was carried out by TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking unit linked to the Lazarus Group. The firm warned that the stolen money could be used to fund North Korea's weapons programmes. North Korea has not commented.

Market impact

The price of Bitcoin reportedly fell by about 5 per cent to below $77,000 after news of the breach. The previous record crypto theft was about $1.5 billion stolen from the Bybit exchange in 2025, also blamed on North Korean hackers.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Binance has millions of users in Africa, and Nigeria is one of the world's biggest markets for crypto trading. Users are being advised to watch for official updates from the exchange and to be wary of scammers posing as Binance support staff.

VeryDarkMan unveils RATEL Sensor Light, an affordable motion-activated light for Nigerian homes

31 Oct 2026, 14:32 GMT

VeryDarkMan has added a motion-activated sensor light to his growing RATEL brand, priced at ₦4,200 for the small size and ₦9,000 for the big size. The light switches on automatically when it detects movement, which could help households during power cuts and add a little security around the compound.

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) has unveiled another product under his growing RATEL brand, and this time it is something many Nigerian households may find useful, especially with the country's unpredictable electricity supply.

The new product is the RATEL Sensor Light, a motion-activated light that comes on automatically when it detects movement.

How much it costs

According to VDM, the sensor light comes in two sizes:

- Small size: ₦4,200

- Big size: ₦9,000

Why it could be useful

Anyone living in Nigeria knows how frustrating the electricity situation can be. Imagine NEPA suddenly takes light at midnight and everywhere goes dark. You need to leave your bedroom, walk downstairs, check on your children or find your way outside to switch on the generator. Before you even find your torchlight, you have already knocked your leg against a chair.

This is where a sensor light could come in handy. It can be placed along staircases, corridors, bedroom entrances, wardrobes, storerooms and other dark corners of the house. Once it detects movement within its range, it switches on automatically, with no need to search for a wall switch in the dark.

A possible security benefit

The light could also help around entrances, gates and backyards. If somebody quietly approaches that area at night, the space around the sensor suddenly becomes bright.

Of course, a sensor light cannot replace proper home security, but it may help discourage unwanted visitors and make movement around a compound more noticeable.

VDM also explained that the product comes with different lighting colour options, including multicolour settings.

What buyers should check

At ₦4,200 for the smaller version, some households may find it affordable enough to install several units: one on the staircase, another in the corridor and perhaps two around the entrance. However, buyers should check the battery life, charging method, weather resistance and detection range before deciding where to install it.

A growing RATEL range

What stands out about this latest RATEL product is the idea behind it. VDM appears to be paying attention to the everyday inconveniences Nigerians face, especially those caused by poor electricity supply. From power stations to smaller household gadgets, he is gradually expanding his product range.

There is something worth appreciating about businesses that identify ordinary problems and offer affordable solutions. You don't always need to spend hundreds of thousands of naira to make your home more comfortable, and sometimes even a small gadget can save you the stress of walking around in darkness.

Imagine having four of these lights around your house. NEPA takes light, but as you move around, the lights come on automatically. Small enjoyment!
