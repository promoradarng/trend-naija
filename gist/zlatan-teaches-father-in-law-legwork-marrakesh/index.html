Zlatan teaches father-in-law his famous legwork ahead of Marrakesh white wedding

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Street-pop star Zlatan Ibile has delighted fans with a video of him teaching his father-in-law his famous dance moves.

What happened

The clip, which spread online on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, was reportedly filmed at a welcome party for family and friends in Marrakesh, Morocco, where Zlatan and his wife, Davita, are holding their white wedding this weekend. In the video, Zlatan takes to the dance floor with his wife's father and breaks into his trademark 'Gbeseh' legwork. The older man gamely copies the moves as guests cheer.

Background

Zlatan and Davita recently held a high-profile wedding ceremony in Nigeria. The singer's new song 'Inferno', featuring Peruzzi and Shoday, recently topped the Apple Music Nigeria chart and has been seen as a response to activist VeryDarkMan, who criticised the wedding. Davido publicly congratulated Zlatan on the song's success.

Davido on the dance floor

Davido was among the celebrities at the welcome party, and a video of him dancing between two female guests also went viral, drawing playful reactions from fans.

The wedding

The white wedding took place in Morocco on Wednesday, 4 November. Davido attended with his wife, Chioma, who wore a pleated cape gown, while the singer wore a black tuxedo.

Tears at the altar

A video from the white wedding in Marrakesh showed Zlatan in tears at the altar as the officiant opened the ceremony with a prayer, even before his bride walked in. The couple had already held a traditional wedding in Lagos on 24 October and a private civil ceremony. Guests in Morocco included Davido and Chioma, Carter Efe and Pretty Mike.

How fans reacted

Fans praised the father-in-law for his enthusiasm and described the moment as heartwarming. Many said it showed a good relationship between Zlatan and his in-laws, and looked forward to seeing pictures from the white wedding.

  • The video was filmed at a welcome party in Marrakesh.
  • Zlatan's white wedding takes place this weekend.
  • Fans praised his father-in-law's enthusiasm.

Former Kaduna State Governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly told a Federal High Court in Abuja that he never intercepted the telephone conversations of National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu, and never asked anyone to do so for him.

In court

El-Rufai opened his defence on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, before Justice Joyce Abdulmalik, after his no-case submission was dismissed in October. Led in evidence by his lawyer, he told the court that he had never owned equipment capable of intercepting calls and had no technical knowledge of how such interception works.

He also denied having any access to the NSA's communication networks or personal devices.

How the case began

The case arises from remarks El-Rufai made in a television interview on 13 February 2026. He denies the charges.

What happens next

The trial continues as El-Rufai presents his defence.

Seyi Law admits his finances were better before Tinubu, but backs reforms

25 Nov 2026, 11:41 GMT

Comedian Seyi Law has reportedly admitted that living standards have dropped under President Tinubu and that his own finances were better before 2023. He still defended the government's reforms, saying good policies take years to stabilise.

Read more

Comedian Seyi Law, a known supporter of President Bola Tinubu, has reportedly admitted that living standards have fallen sharply under the current administration.

What he said

Speaking on a podcast hosted by Nedu, Seyi Law is said to have acknowledged that his personal finances were much better before Tinubu took office in 2023, and that it would be hypocritical to say Nigerians are not suffering.

Still backing the reforms

He nevertheless defended the government's economic reforms, arguing that Nigerians would benefit in the long run. Comparing Nigeria with China, he said major national transformations often come with heavy early sacrifices. When the host asked whether that meant Nigerians must suffer greatly first, he said people must feel the pain of a policy before reaping its benefits, adding that good policies usually stabilise after 10 to 12 years.

How people reacted

His remarks have sparked debate online.
