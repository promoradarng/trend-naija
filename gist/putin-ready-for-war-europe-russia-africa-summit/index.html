Putin tells Europe Russia is 'ready for war' but warns there would be no winners

30 Oct 2026, 06:44 GMT

Russian President Vladimir Putin has issued a fresh warning to European countries, reportedly saying Russia is ready for war with Europe even though it does not want one.

What he said

Speaking at a Russia–Africa summit in Moscow, reported on Thursday, 29 October 2026, Putin is said to have told any European country seeking a conflict to prepare for it, and warned that if things escalated there would be no winners at all. He argued that Western efforts to defeat Russia strategically had failed. He also said Moscow did not want the Baltic Sea to face disruption like that seen in the Strait of Hormuz during the recent Iran war, which pushed up fuel prices around the world.

The summit

The meeting was attended by 48 countries, including 22 heads of state, according to reports. African countries have deepened trade and security ties with Russia in recent years, and the summit was seen as part of Moscow's effort to keep its influence on the continent.

Background

Tensions between Russia and NATO countries have risen in recent months. Several European governments have accused Russia of sabotage, surveillance of undersea cables and drone incursions into their airspace, including over Latvia and Estonia. Germany raised its assessment of the risk of war with Russia earlier this month. Meanwhile, the war in Ukraine continues, and a Russian strike on a railway station was reported to have killed 14 people this week, an attack Ukraine's president condemned.

What happens next

European leaders are expected to respond to the remarks, and NATO is likely to keep up its patrols in the Baltic region.

  • Putin says Russia does not want war but is ready for it.
  • He warned there would be 'no winners' if tensions escalate.
  • He spoke at a Russia–Africa summit attended by 48 countries.

Canada resumes Express Entry draws, invites 1,999 for permanent residence

24 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Canada has resumed Express Entry draws, inviting 1,999 provincial nominees to apply for permanent residence on 23 November. It was the first draw under new ranking rules that reward Canadian work experience and high-wage job offers.

Read more

Canada has resumed its Express Entry draws after a short pause, issuing 1,999 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) for permanent residence to provincial nominees on 23 November 2026.

The draw

According to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada, candidates needed a minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score of 673 to be invited, and must have created their profile before 31 July 2026.

New rules

It was the first draw since major changes to the CRS took effect on 3 November 2026. Candidates can now earn extra points through a new Labour Market Integration Factor, which rewards Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation.

Why it matters

Express Entry is one of Canada's main routes to permanent residence for skilled workers.

Police probe reported break-in at William and Kate's Norfolk home

Police are investigating a reported break-in at Anmer Hall, the Norfolk home of the Prince and Princess of Wales. The family were in Windsor at the time and are safe.

Read more

Police are investigating a reported break-in at Anmer Hall, the Norfolk country home of the Prince and Princess of Wales, on Monday night, 23 November 2026.

What happened

Intruders are said to have entered the property while Prince William, Catherine and their children, Prince George, Princess Charlotte and Prince Louis, were at their home in Windsor. The family were safe.

About Anmer Hall

The ten-bedroom Georgian house sits on the King's Sandringham Estate and was a wedding gift from the late Queen Elizabeth II. The couple regularly use it for weekends and school holidays.

Business as usual

The reported intrusion came ahead of William's visit to Downing Street on Tuesday, where he met the Prime Minister to discuss shared priorities, including homelessness.

Two men charged

Police said they were called to Anmer Hall shortly before 6pm on Monday after reports of suspicious activity and arrested two men nearby. The two Albanian nationals have been charged with entering a dwelling with intent to steal and trespassing on a protected site, and are due to appear before magistrates in Norwich.

Iran reportedly gives singer Parastoo Ahmadi 74 lashes for performing without hijab

24 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Iranian authorities have reportedly flogged singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her team over an online concert she performed without a headscarf. Rights groups say they also received two-year bans on artistic work.

Read more

Iranian authorities have reportedly administered 74 lashes to the singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her production team over a concert she performed without wearing the mandatory Islamic headscarf.

What happened

According to a human rights lawyer and monitoring groups, the punishment was carried out on Monday, 23 November 2026, at the prosecutor's office in the city of Qom.

The concert

The case stems from a virtual concert Ahmadi recorded in December 2024 at a historic caravanserai in Qom and streamed on YouTube. In the video, which was widely viewed, she performed traditional Persian music with male musicians while wearing a sleeveless dress with her hair uncovered, defying rules that ban women from singing solo in public and appearing without a hijab.

The sentence

A court found Ahmadi and four male musicians and crew members guilty of offending public decency by publishing what it called immoral content online. As well as the lashes, each of the five was reportedly given a two-year ban on artistic activity.
