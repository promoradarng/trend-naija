Carter Efe and Peller miss out as India's Ashswag wins international streamer award

13 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Nigerian content creators Carter Efe and Peller have missed out on an international award at the 2026 Streamer Awards.

The result

The two were nominated for Best International Streamer after the organisers released the shortlist in October. At the ceremony, reported on Friday, 13 November 2026, the prize went to Indian streamer Ashswag, who beat the Nigerian pair and other nominees from around the world.

Why it mattered

The nominations put the Nigerian duo among creators from around the world competing for the category.

Peller and Phyna react

In a post on X on Thursday night, Peller thanked his fans, saying their loyalty meant more to him than any trophy and that the nomination itself was a blessing. He complained, however, that the result of his category was announced on the red carpet before the main ceremony began, which he called unfair. Reality star Phyna went further in a livestream, accusing the organisers of using the Nigerian nominees to attract Nigerian viewers and engagement with no real intention of giving them the award.

Background

Peller rose to prominence as one of Nigeria's biggest TikTok live streamers, while Carter Efe, a skit maker and musician, has also built a large streaming following.

  • They were nominated for Best International Streamer.
  • India's Ashswag won the award.
  • Neither has reacted publicly.

Veekee James sparks debate after moving ₦4m scholarship over winner's reaction

Fashion entrepreneur Veekee James has reportedly withdrawn a ₦4 million scholarship from a recipient at a graduation event and given it to someone else, after the winner's flat reaction. The move has divided opinion online.

Fashion entrepreneur Veekee James is at the centre of an online debate after she reportedly withdrew a ₦4 million scholarship from one recipient and gave it to another during a live event.

What happened

The incident took place on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, at the graduation ceremony of fashion designer Prudent Gabriel's fashion school. James attended with her husband, Femi Atere. When a woman's name was announced as the scholarship winner, she is said to have responded with a reaction many in the room saw as flat and unenthusiastic.

James then intervened and redirected the scholarship to another attendee.

The debate

Videos of the moment have divided opinion online. Some people backed James, saying the award should go to someone who truly values it, while others felt the original winner was treated unfairly and that people show excitement in different ways.

VeryDarkMan warns Tinubu over deadly attack on NDC campaign in Delta

24 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Activist VeryDarkMan has issued a strong warning to President Tinubu after gunmen killed four people, including NDC youth leader Efemena Umukoro, at Omo-Agege's campaign event in Delta. He said political violence could spiral out of control.

Read more

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has issued a fierce public warning to President Bola Tinubu following the deadly attack on an NDC campaign event in Uduere, Ughelli North, Delta State.

What he said

In a video posted on Instagram, VDM told the president that he would burn Nigeria down, arguing that targeting opposition figures with gunfire is a dangerous political strategy that could spiral far beyond anyone's control. He warned that even people close to the president would not be safe if the cycle of violence continued, and drew attention to the fact that the attack happened in Delta State.

The attack

Gunmen opened fire on former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege's campaign team on Monday, 23 November 2026, while he was addressing supporters. Four people were killed, according to his campaign, including Efemena Umukoro, an Urhobo youth leader popularly known as General Akima. Omo-Agege was unhurt.

Other reactions

Other social media commentators also condemned the killings, and Delta State Governor Sheriff Oborevwori called for everyone involved to be arrested regardless of their party. The Inspector-General of Police has ordered an investigation.

The other side

No response from the presidency or the APC to VDM's remarks was included in the reports.

Former Kaduna Governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly told a Federal High Court in Abuja that he never intercepted the phone calls of National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu. He opened his defence on Wednesday.

Former Kaduna State Governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly told a Federal High Court in Abuja that he never intercepted the telephone conversations of National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu, and never asked anyone to do so for him.

In court

El-Rufai opened his defence on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, before Justice Joyce Abdulmalik, after his no-case submission was dismissed in October. Led in evidence by his lawyer, he told the court that he had never owned equipment capable of intercepting calls and had no technical knowledge of how such interception works.

He also denied having any access to the NSA's communication networks or personal devices.

How the case began

The case arises from remarks El-Rufai made in a television interview on 13 February 2026. He denies the charges.

What happens next

The trial continues as El-Rufai presents his defence.

Seyi Law admits his finances were better before Tinubu, but backs reforms

25 Nov 2026, 11:41 GMT

Comedian Seyi Law has reportedly admitted that living standards have dropped under President Tinubu and that his own finances were better before 2023. He still defended the government's reforms, saying good policies take years to stabilise.

Read more

Comedian Seyi Law, a known supporter of President Bola Tinubu, has reportedly admitted that living standards have fallen sharply under the current administration.

What he said

Speaking on a podcast hosted by Nedu, Seyi Law is said to have acknowledged that his personal finances were much better before Tinubu took office in 2023, and that it would be hypocritical to say Nigerians are not suffering.

Still backing the reforms

He nevertheless defended the government's economic reforms, arguing that Nigerians would benefit in the long run. Comparing Nigeria with China, he said major national transformations often come with heavy early sacrifices. When the host asked whether that meant Nigerians must suffer greatly first, he said people must feel the pain of a policy before reaping its benefits, adding that good policies usually stabilise after 10 to 12 years.

How people reacted

His remarks have sparked debate online.
