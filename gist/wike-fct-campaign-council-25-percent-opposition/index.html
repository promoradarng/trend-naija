Wike tells FCT campaign council not to let ADC or NDC reach 25% in Abuja

16 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Federal Capital Territory Minister Nyesom Wike has reportedly directed President Bola Tinubu's campaign team in Abuja to make sure no opposition party wins up to 25 per cent of the votes in the territory at the 2027 elections.

What happened

Wike gave the directive on Monday, 16 November 2026, while inaugurating a 466-member APC Presidential Campaign Council and Rainbow Coalition in Abuja. The coalition is said to also include members of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) who back the president.

He named the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) as the main opposition parties the council should work against, saying they should not be allowed to reach 25 per cent. He reportedly said simply winning was not enough and that members would be held personally responsible for the outcome in the FCT.

Why 25 per cent matters

Under the Constitution, a presidential candidate must win at least 25 per cent of votes in two-thirds of the states and the FCT to be declared winner on the first ballot, which makes the territory an important battleground.

Jabs at opponents

Wike is also said to have dismissed claims that the ADC has strong support in the North or a real chance in the FCT, and challenged former Rivers governor Rotimi Amaechi to test his party's strength in Rivers State.

The other side

No response from the ADC or NDC was included in the reports. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

  • He named the ADC and NDC as the parties to beat.
  • The FCT 25% rule is key in presidential elections.

Court freezes $100m linked to Governors' Forum in Paris Club fees dispute

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

An Abuja High Court has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum, pending a dispute over legal and consultancy fees tied to Paris Club refunds. The order does not decide who owns the money.

Read more

A High Court of the Federal Capital Territory in Abuja has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum (NGF), in a dispute over unpaid legal and consultancy fees connected to the Paris Club refunds.

The order

Justice O.C. Agbaza granted the interim freezing order on 19 November 2026, following an application by a lawyer against the NGF's incorporated trustees and 29 commercial banks. The order covers a single total sum of $100 million, about ₦135.6 billion, across the affected accounts.

What it means

The order preserves the disputed money until the court hears an application for an injunction or makes a further order. It does not decide who owns the funds.

The claim

The lawyer is seeking payment under an indemnity he says the NGF issued in December 2018, after the federal government paid $100 million to the forum to hold pending a decision on liability for professional fees. No response from the NGF was included in the reports.

Seyi Law admits his finances were better before Tinubu, but backs reforms

25 Nov 2026, 11:41 GMT

Comedian Seyi Law has reportedly admitted that living standards have dropped under President Tinubu and that his own finances were better before 2023. He still defended the government's reforms, saying good policies take years to stabilise.

Read more

Comedian Seyi Law, a known supporter of President Bola Tinubu, has reportedly admitted that living standards have fallen sharply under the current administration.

What he said

Speaking on a podcast hosted by Nedu, Seyi Law is said to have acknowledged that his personal finances were much better before Tinubu took office in 2023, and that it would be hypocritical to say Nigerians are not suffering.

Still backing the reforms

He nevertheless defended the government's economic reforms, arguing that Nigerians would benefit in the long run. Comparing Nigeria with China, he said major national transformations often come with heavy early sacrifices. When the host asked whether that meant Nigerians must suffer greatly first, he said people must feel the pain of a policy before reaping its benefits, adding that good policies usually stabilise after 10 to 12 years.

How people reacted

His remarks have sparked debate online.

Veekee James sparks debate after moving ₦4m scholarship over winner's reaction

Fashion entrepreneur Veekee James has reportedly withdrawn a ₦4 million scholarship from a recipient at a graduation event and given it to someone else, after the winner's flat reaction. The move has divided opinion online.

Read more

Fashion entrepreneur Veekee James is at the centre of an online debate after she reportedly withdrew a ₦4 million scholarship from one recipient and gave it to another during a live event.

What happened

The incident took place on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, at the graduation ceremony of fashion designer Prudent Gabriel's fashion school. James attended with her husband, Femi Atere. When a woman's name was announced as the scholarship winner, she is said to have responded with a reaction many in the room saw as flat and unenthusiastic.

James then intervened and redirected the scholarship to another attendee.

The debate

Videos of the moment have divided opinion online. Some people backed James, saying the award should go to someone who truly values it, while others felt the original winner was treated unfairly and that people show excitement in different ways.
