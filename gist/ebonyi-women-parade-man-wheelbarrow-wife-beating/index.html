Ebonyi women parade man in wheelbarrow over alleged repeated beating of his wife

7 Nov 2026, 18:58 GMT

Women in Ishiagu community, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly taken an unusual step to show their anger over domestic violence, publicly parading a man accused of repeatedly beating his wife.

Pushed around in a wheelbarrow

According to reports circulating online, the women placed the man in a wheelbarrow and pushed him around the community, apparently determined to make an example of him over his alleged treatment of his wife.

Photos show a large group of women surrounding the man, who sits in the wheelbarrow in a yellow T-shirt as several women push and guide it along a dirt path, with one carrying a calabash.

"Enough is enough"

The incident has drawn attention because of the unusual punishment the women chose. They appeared to have reached a point where they could no longer tolerate the alleged abuse.

In many communities, domestic violence is still treated as a private family matter, and victims are sometimes encouraged to endure abuse for the sake of their marriage. The women of Ishiagu reportedly decided that enough was enough.

Justice or humiliation?

Their action raises an important question about how communities should respond when a woman is repeatedly subjected to violence in her own home.

Many people may applaud the women for standing up against domestic abuse. Others may question whether publicly humiliating an accused person, who has not been tried, is the right approach.

The full circumstances of the incident, including the man's side of the story and whether the authorities have stepped in, remain unclear.

Images of recharge cards bearing the logo of the All Progressives Congress (APC), reportedly handed out at President Bola Tinubu's campaign rally in Rivers State, have sparked debate on social media.

What happened

A man who came across pictures of the ₦200 airtime cards questioned online what anyone could achieve with such a small amount, and his comment, shared on Thursday, 12 November 2026, quickly drew reactions. The cards were said to have circulated among people who attended the rally.

How people reacted

Some users mocked the giveaway and said the money would be better spent on fixing the country, while others joked about how the airtime might be used. Some warned that small gifts like this could still influence some voters.

The APC had not publicly responded to the recharge card images at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

The son of the second wife at the centre of the viral Ukehe controversy has spoken out, begging Nigerians to stop attacking his mother.

"Is it not enough already?"

"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum. Is it not enough already? That woman does not deserve all this hate," he said.

"Do you want her to die? If anything happens to my mum, you people won't like it. Please, this is enough."

How it started

His mother has faced a wave of criticism online since a video from his father's Ozo title-taking ceremony went viral. In the video, the second wife's children reportedly refused to spray money on the first wife, Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

Claims then spread online that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over being childless and had moved back to her father's home. Those claims have not been independently verified.

Since then, well-wishers have visited Mrs Anulika with gifts and money, and the Enugu governor's wife sent a representative with cash and promises of a house and a business.

The other side of the story

The son's plea is a reminder that there are always two sides to a family story, and that online anger can quickly turn into harassment.

Sympathy for Mrs Anulika is understandable. But insults and threats against another woman, whose side of the story has not been heard, help no one. Families caught up in viral moments are still real people.

EU raises alarm over election violence and hate speech ahead of 2027 polls

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The European Union's ambassador to Nigeria, Gautier Mignot, has warned that rising election-related violence, hate speech and intimidation threaten democratic progress and could discourage Nigerians from voting in 2027.

Read more

The European Union has expressed concern about rising election-related violence, hate speech and political intimidation in Nigeria ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What the EU said

Speaking at the opening of the 2026 Civil Society and Human Rights Defenders Forum in Abuja, reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the EU Ambassador to Nigeria and ECOWAS, Gautier Mignot, warned that such incidents erode democratic gains and discourage citizens from taking full part in elections. He said free civic and political space allows civil society groups and citizens to monitor how elections are run, track campaign promises and hold leaders to account, and helps prevent authorities from manipulating the rules.

Support for institutions

Mignot said open public debate, independent media, peaceful assembly and an active civil society are vital to credible elections. He said the EU would continue to support Nigerian institutions, including the National Human Rights Commission, to help create a free and fair electoral environment, while stressing that it would not interfere in domestic politics.

Background

The warning follows a string of violent incidents linked to campaigns in several states. Peter Obi's convoy was attacked in Calabar, NDC supporters' vehicles were destroyed at a rally in Delta, and APC supporters were attacked ahead of a rally in Katsina. The Inspector-General of Police has also warned officers against being used by politicians. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
