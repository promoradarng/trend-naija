Ebonyi women parade man in wheelbarrow over alleged repeated beating of his wife

7 Nov 2026, 18:58 GMT

Women in Ishiagu community, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly taken an unusual step to show their anger over domestic violence, publicly parading a man accused of repeatedly beating his wife.

Pushed around in a wheelbarrow

According to reports circulating online, the women placed the man in a wheelbarrow and pushed him around the community, apparently determined to make an example of him over his alleged treatment of his wife.

Photos show a large group of women surrounding the man, who sits in the wheelbarrow in a yellow T-shirt as several women push and guide it along a dirt path, with one carrying a calabash.

"Enough is enough"

The incident has drawn attention because of the unusual punishment the women chose. They appeared to have reached a point where they could no longer tolerate the alleged abuse.

In many communities, domestic violence is still treated as a private family matter, and victims are sometimes encouraged to endure abuse for the sake of their marriage. The women of Ishiagu reportedly decided that enough was enough.

Justice or humiliation?

Their action raises an important question about how communities should respond when a woman is repeatedly subjected to violence in her own home.

Many people may applaud the women for standing up against domestic abuse. Others may question whether publicly humiliating an accused person, who has not been tried, is the right approach.

The full circumstances of the incident, including the man's side of the story and whether the authorities have stepped in, remain unclear.

Peter Obi says insecurity stops him walking freely in Nigeria, unlike in the UK

22 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi says security warnings prevent him from taking simple walks in Nigeria, recalling how he walked and took trains freely while living in London.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has spoken about worsening insecurity in Nigeria, saying safety warnings now stop him from taking simple walks or jogging in his own neighbourhood.

What he said

Addressing the Global Peter Obi Supporters Summit in Abuja on Saturday, 21 November 2026, the former Anambra governor recalled living in the UK, on the same street as the Mayor of London. He said they would walk the streets and take the train without security escorts.

He contrasted that with Nigeria today, recalling that he used to walk freely to church in Lagos in the 1980s and 1990s, but said security concerns now prevent him from doing so.

The campaign

Insecurity is one of the major issues in the 2027 campaign, ahead of the 16 January election.

Tom Brady buys minority stake in Chelsea

22 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

NFL legend Tom Brady has reportedly bought a minority stake in Chelsea Football Club. The size of the stake and price were not disclosed.

Read more

American football great Tom Brady has reportedly bought a minority stake in Premier League club Chelsea.

The deal

The size of the seven-time Super Bowl winner's stake and the amount he paid have not been disclosed. Chelsea is valued at about $4.2 billion by one leading business publication.

Leaving Birmingham

The move comes weeks after Brady sold his 3.3% stake in Birmingham City, who play in England's second tier. The reports cited English Football League rules on holding stakes in more than one club as the reason.

Growing sports portfolio

Since retiring from the NFL, Brady has built up a range of sports investments. He holds a 5% stake in the Las Vegas Raiders and part-owns the Las Vegas Aces basketball team, an E1 boat-racing team and a pickleball team.

Chelsea's season

The news came on the same day Chelsea suffered a heavy 4-1 home defeat to Leeds United.

Catherine, Princess of Wales, has opened up about the physical and emotional effects of her cancer diagnosis in an upcoming BBC documentary titled Heal.

The documentary

The 30-minute film follows her recovery and return to public life. In a preview released ahead of the broadcast, the princess speaks with other people facing serious health challenges and reflects on life after treatment.

What she said

She said cancer changes a person physically, but also shifts something much deeper, describing recovery as a process of reconnecting with things she once knew and finding hope and joy alongside uncertainty.

Background

The film is set against the backdrop of her National Three Peaks Challenge in June 2026, when she climbed the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within 24 hours.
