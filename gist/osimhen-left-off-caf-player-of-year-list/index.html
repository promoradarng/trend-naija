Osimhen left off CAF Men's Player of the Year shortlist

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Super Eagles striker Victor Osimhen has been left out of the shortlist for the 2026 CAF Men's Player of the Year award, according to nominations released by the Confederation of African Football on Wednesday, 4 November.

The snub

The 27-year-old had what many considered a strong year. He helped Nigeria finish third at the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations in Morocco and was a key figure as Galatasaray won the Turkish Super Lig title in 2026. Osimhen won the award in 2023, becoming the first Nigerian to do so since Nwankwo Kanu in 1999.

Who made the list

The reigning African Player of the Year, Morocco and Paris Saint-Germain defender Achraf Hakimi, heads the 10-man shortlist, which reportedly also includes Senegal's Sadio Mané, Ghana's Antoine Semenyo and Ivory Coast's Amad Diallo.

Background

The omission follows news this week that no Nigerian player made the shortlist for the women's award for the first time since it began in 2001. Nigeria still has nominations in other categories, including the Super Falcons for Women's National Team of the Year. The CAF Awards ceremony will be held on 7 December at the Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Creative Arts in Lagos.

How people reacted

Nigerian fans reacted angrily, with many saying Osimhen's achievements deserved recognition and questioning how the shortlist was chosen. Others said the competition was strong this year.

  • Osimhen won AFCON bronze and the Turkish league title.
  • Achraf Hakimi heads the 10-man shortlist.
  • The awards hold in Lagos on 7 December.

Nigeria wins both 100m golds at Youth Olympics as Rosemary Nwankwo and Gift Bright triumph

10 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Seventeen-year-old Rosemary Nwankwo won the girls' 100m at the Youth Olympic Games in Dakar in a wind-assisted 11.16 seconds, and Gift Bright won the boys' race, making Nigeria the first country to win both 100m titles at the same Games.

Read more

Nigeria has made history at the 2026 Youth Olympic Games in Dakar, Senegal, winning gold in both the girls' and boys' 100 metres.

The races

Rosemary Chigozie Nwankwo, a 17-year-old secondary school student, won the girls' 100m final in a wind-assisted 11.16 seconds, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. Arwa Ali of the United Arab Emirates took silver in 11.41 seconds and Dana Salem of Qatar won bronze in 11.48. Nwankwo had reached the final by winning her semi-final in 11.53 seconds. Her compatriot Gift Bright then won the boys' 100m in a personal best of 10.28 seconds.

A first

The double makes Nigeria the first country to win both 100m titles at the same edition of the Youth Olympic Games. Nwankwo's win was also Nigeria's first gold medal of the Dakar Games, and she becomes the third Nigerian woman to win the event, after Josephine Omaka in Singapore in 2010 and Rosemary Chukwuma in Buenos Aires in 2018.

Background

The Dakar Games are the first Olympic event ever held in Africa. The sprint golds come days after Nigeria's Flamingos won the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup in Morocco, adding to a remarkable run for Nigerian youth sport.

Former Arsenal manager Arsène Wenger is reportedly considering a bid to challenge Gianni Infantino in the upcoming FIFA presidential election.

What is reported

According to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, key football figures, including leaders within European governing body UEFA, have been lobbying the 77-year-old Frenchman to stand before the nomination deadline of 18 November, and a decision is expected this week. Manchester United are increasingly expected to sack head coach Michael Carrick after a poor start to the 2026/27 season.

What is reported

According to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox are expected to recommend to minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazer family that the 45-year-old be dismissed. No final decision has been made.

Poor results

Court strikes out ₦6bn money-laundering charges against Mompha, socialite says

10 Nov 2026, 17:47 GMT

Socialite Ismaila Mustapha, known as Mompha, says a court has struck out the ₦6 billion money-laundering charges filed against him by the EFCC, calling it vindication after seven years. The EFCC had not commented.

Read more

Socialite Ismaila Mustapha, popularly known as Mompha, says a court has struck out the money-laundering charges brought against him by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC).

What he said

In an Instagram post on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Mompha said the court struck out the ₦6 billion charges on the grounds that they amounted to an abuse of court process and forum shopping. He described the outcome as personal vindication after what he called years of harassment, called the prosecution a witch-hunt, praised the judge and thanked his supporters. He also mocked the anti-graft agency in the post.

Background

Mompha's legal battle with the EFCC began in 2019, when he was arrested after returning to Nigeria. He has always denied the allegations, saying he came home to invest. The case dragged on for about seven years. The EFCC had not publicly commented on the ruling at the time of writing, and it was not immediately clear whether the commission would appeal or file fresh charges.

What it means

Mompha says his legal ordeal is now over.
