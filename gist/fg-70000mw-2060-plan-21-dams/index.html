FG targets 70,000MW of electricity by 2060, eyes 21 dams for hydropower

30 Oct 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Federal Government has set a long-term target of generating 70,000 megawatts of electricity by 2060, the Minister of Power, Joseph Tegbe, has reportedly said.

What he said

Speaking at the Nigeria Energy Leadership Summit in Lagos, in comments reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, Tegbe acknowledged the gap between today's supply and the target. He said Nigeria has more than 13,000MW of installed capacity, but actual generation is below 5,000MW. He linked the need for much more power to growing demand from industry, technology and data centres.

The 21 dams

Tegbe said 21 dams along the route of the Sokoto–Badagry Superhighway have been identified for possible hydropower development, and that President Bola Tinubu has directed that they be rehabilitated and studied for electricity generation. He also mentioned the long-delayed Mambilla hydropower project and the proposed East and West super grids approved by the Federal Executive Council this week. No cost figures were given.

Background

Earlier this week, the Federal Executive Council approved procurement for new super grids meant to raise transmission capacity from about 5,000MW to 19,000MW within two years. The government has also ordered the clearing of hundreds of containers of power equipment stuck at the ports. Frequent grid collapses and poor supply remain one of the biggest complaints of Nigerians and businesses.

What it means

The 2060 target is a long-range goal, and experts say steady progress on transmission and gas supply will be needed long before new dams can make a difference.

  • The target is 70,000MW of generation by 2060.
  • 21 dams along the Sokoto–Badagry corridor have been identified for hydropower.
  • Current generation is below 5,000MW.

The Lagos State Government has relaunched its Ounje Eko food discount programme for the festive season, offering residents a 20% reduction on selected food items in 66 markets across the state.

When and where

The Commissioner for Agriculture and Food Systems, Abisola Olusanya, announced on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, that the scheme will run on three Sundays: 29 November, 6 December and 20 December. The markets will open from 9am to 4pm and cover all 57 local government and local council development areas, across the five IBILE divisions of Ikeja, Badagry, Ikorodu, Lagos Island and Epe.

Why it matters

The commissioner said the initiative was designed to ease pressure on household budgets as residents prepare for Christmas and the New Year. It comes amid concerns that many families have yet to feel real relief from high food prices, even though food inflation has slowed.

CBN keeps interest rate at 23% despite slowing inflation

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has held its benchmark interest rate at 23% at the last policy meeting of the year. Analysts had warned that election-year spending could put fresh pressure on prices.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 23%, maintaining a tight policy stance even though inflation has continued to slow.

The decision

Announcing the outcome in Abuja on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, after the Monetary Policy Committee's final two-day meeting of the year, CBN Governor Olayemi Cardoso said members voted unanimously to hold the Monetary Policy Rate at 23%.

The committee also left the corridor around the rate unchanged, and kept the cash reserve requirement for commercial banks at 45% and for merchant banks at 16%.

Why it held

The decision reflects caution amid renewed pressure on the naira and uncertainty at home and abroad. Analysts had said the recent drop in inflation might not be enough to justify a cut, warning that spending ahead of the 2027 elections could increase money in circulation and push prices up again.

What it means

Borrowing costs for businesses and households are expected to remain high for now.

FG disowns ₦100,000 family grant portal, warns Nigerians not to share personal details

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Federal Ministry of Youth Development has said the widely shared NFSP ₦100,000 family grant website is fraudulent. It warned people not to submit bank details, passwords, PINs or OTPs.

Read more

The federal government has disowned a widely publicised ₦100,000 family support grant, warning Nigerians not to register on the website promoting it.

The warning

In a disclaimer on its official X account, the Federal Ministry of Youth Development said the website for the so-called Nigeria Family Strengthening and Development Programme (NFSP) was fraudulent and was neither authorised nor run by the ministry.

The ministry urged people to stop using the platform immediately and not to upload personal documents. It specifically warned against sharing bank details, passwords, PINs and one-time passwords (OTPs).

Why it matters

The purported grant had drawn wide attention online in recent weeks, with many Nigerians rushing to apply. There are now concerns that some applicants may have already submitted sensitive personal information.

Christmas airfares double as return tickets hit ₦822,000

24 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Domestic airfares have reportedly risen by as much as 100% in three weeks, with some return tickets now above ₦800,000 ahead of Christmas. Travellers heading to the South-East and South-South are hit hardest.

Nigerians planning to fly home for Christmas are facing steep price rises, with domestic airfares reportedly up by as much as 100% in just three weeks.

The prices

Return tickets on some routes now cost more than ₦800,000, with fares as high as ₦822,000 recorded on some airline booking platforms. Airlines including Air Peace and United Nigeria are among those whose prices have gone up.

Airlines including Air Peace and United Nigeria are among those that have raised fares.

The increases are raising the cost of travel for millions of Nigerians, especially those heading to the South-East and South-South for the festive season.

Why prices are rising

Airlines are said to be struggling to meet the seasonal surge in demand with limited aircraft, while also dealing with higher operating costs. Security worries on major highways are also pushing more travellers towards flying.

Several carriers are reportedly adding extra flights on domestic and international routes between late November and mid-December to cope with the expected rush, but fares have continued to climb.
