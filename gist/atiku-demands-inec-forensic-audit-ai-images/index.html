Atiku demands forensic audit of voter register after INEC admits AI-generated photos

30 Oct 2026, 17:42 GMT

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has demanded an independent forensic audit of Nigeria's voter register following INEC's admission that AI-generated photos were entered into it.

What he demanded

In a statement issued on Friday, 30 October 2026, by Phrank Shaibu, communications director of the ADC presidential campaign, Atiku called for the audit to be carried out by accredited experts with the participation of political parties, civil society groups and international observers. He asked that no disputed records be certified until the audit is complete, and that INEC name, suspend and prosecute every officer involved. He also asked the commission to preserve voter records, image files and access logs, and to publish the names of companies contracted to produce sensitive election materials. The statement warned that the campaign would go to court to force disclosure if INEC refused.

The 20 million claim

The statement claimed that more than 20 million AI-generated images may be involved. The campaign did not publish evidence for the figure, and INEC has not given a number of affected records.

INEC's position

INEC's ICT director, Lawrence Bayode, said this week that some registration officers with system access entered AI-generated and poor-quality photos, and that the commission knows how it happened but not why. Atiku's campaign described that explanation as an evasion. INEC says the online register is still being cleaned up and that the final version will be certified on 15 December.

What happens next

Other opposition parties are expected to join calls for an audit, and the issue is likely to dominate election debates in the coming weeks.

  • Atiku wants an independent forensic audit before certification.
  • His campaign claims up to 20 million images may be affected, without published evidence.
  • He has threatened to go to court if INEC does not disclose details.

INEC chairman says AI is not an enemy of democracy, but warns of deepfakes

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

INEC chairman Joash Amupitan says artificial intelligence can help run elections, but warned that cheap AI tools can be used to clone voices and fake announcements. His comments follow INEC's admission that AI-generated photos got into the voter register.

Read more

The Chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Amupitan, says artificial intelligence (AI) is not an enemy of democracy, even though its misuse could create serious problems for elections.

What he said

Speaking at the Beyond the Ballot Summit in Abuja on Monday, 2 November 2026, Amupitan reportedly said election bodies around the world use machine-assisted processes to find duplicate records in large voter registers, plan logistics, analyse data and work more efficiently. However, he warned that generative AI has put powerful tools in the hands of people who want to disrupt elections or mislead voters, and that abilities which once needed a lot of money and technical skill can now be obtained very cheaply. He gave examples such as cloning the voices of public figures and creating realistic fake recordings of election officials making false announcements.

Background

His comments come days after INEC's ICT director admitted that some registration officers had entered AI-generated and poor-quality photos into the preliminary voter register, which was published online on 26 October. INEC says it detected the problem with its biometric identification system and is cleaning up the register before it is certified on 15 December. Opposition parties and civil society groups have called for a full audit, and ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has demanded a forensic review. Amupitan had earlier announced that INEC would use AI in parts of the electoral system ahead of the 2027 elections.

Why it matters

Fake videos and audio have already been shared during the campaign, and several politicians have accused rivals of using deepfakes. Observers say voters will need to be careful about what they share as the January 2027 elections approach.

The NDC is linked in online discussions to Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso, while Governor Soludo has reportedly been mobilising support for President Bola Tinubu.

Nigerian politics has taken another interesting turn, and this time the attention is on the family of Anambra State Governor, Professor Chukwuma Charles Soludo.

The video

A Facebook video reportedly showing Ozonna Soludo, the governor's son, dancing to and promoting a song associated with the NDC has sparked reactions online. In online discussions, the NDC has been linked to Peter Obi and Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso.

Father and son in different camps?

What makes the situation interesting is the apparent political contrast between father and son.

While Ozonna appears to be enjoying a song associated with a different political camp, his father, Governor Soludo, has reportedly been mobilising political stakeholders in Anambra State in support of President Bola Ahmed Tinubu.

A dance is not a declaration

Dancing to a political song does not automatically mean someone has joined or endorsed a party. Ozonna has not made any confirmed political declaration through the video.

Even so, the moment has attracted attention because of his father's position. It is also a reminder that family ties do not necessarily decide political loyalty. A father may support one candidate while his son prefers another.

The big question

If Governor Soludo is backing Tinubu while his son appears to be promoting a song associated with Obi and Kwankwaso, what does that say about the changing political atmosphere ahead of 2027?

Former Labour Party chairman Abure rules out backing Obi, open to Tinubu or Atiku

2 Nov 2026, 09:44 GMT

Julius Abure, the former national chairman of the Labour Party, has reportedly said he will not support Peter Obi in 2027 but could back either President Tinubu or Atiku Abubakar. Obi's camp says it has moved on.

Read more

Julius Abure, who led the Labour Party when Peter Obi was its presidential candidate in 2023, has said he will not support Obi in the 2027 election.

What he said

In a newspaper interview reported on Monday, 2 November 2026, Abure reportedly said he was open to working with any of the major candidates but was very sure he would not back Obi, who is now the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate. He said he could support either President Bola Tinubu or Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, depending on the outcome of a pending Supreme Court case on the Labour Party's leadership, whether a candidate's agenda matches his views, and how acceptable the candidate is to voters. He said that if the court has not ruled by the end of November, he will consult his supporters.

His criticism of Obi

Abure argued that Obi had squandered the goodwill and structures that helped him win more than six million votes in 2023, and predicted he would do worse in 2027. He also questioned Obi's leadership, accusing him and Abia Governor Alex Otti of undermining the party.

The other side

Obi's allies have previously accused Abure of breaking an agreement to hold an inclusive party convention. The NDC and Obi's camp dismissed his latest comments, saying they have moved on and are focused on the election.

Background

The Labour Party has been split by a long leadership dispute since the 2023 election, which eventually led Obi to leave and join the NDC.

Seun Okinbaloye says his 2027 rigging warning named no party or candidate

2 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Channels TV anchor Seun Okinbaloye has clarified his widely shared warning about rigging in the 2027 elections, saying it did not accuse any party, candidate or sponsor. Reactions remain divided, with some praising his courage and others accusing him of backtracking.

Read more

Popular Channels Television anchor Seun Okinbaloye has responded to the storm over his recent comments on election rigging ahead of 2027.

What he said

Speaking on the channel on Sunday, 1 November 2026, Okinbaloye said his earlier remarks had been widely misread. He said his warning had named no political party, accused no candidate and identified no sponsor. Critics had interpreted his original comments as a claim that rigging of the 2027 elections had already begun.

Background

Okinbaloye's original warning went viral last week, coming amid wider concerns about the integrity of the voter register after INEC admitted that some of its registration officers had entered AI-generated photos. Opposition candidates, including Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi, have called for audits and promised to prosecute anyone involved in rigging. INEC says the register is still being cleaned up and will be certified in December.

How people reacted

Opinion was divided. Some viewers praised the anchor for speaking out and for clarifying his position, saying he was simply doing his job as a journalist. Others accused him of damage control and asked why he had not named those he believed were planning to rig. Several users said INEC itself should identify and prosecute staff involved in irregularities in the register.

Why it matters

Okinbaloye presents one of Nigeria's most-watched political programmes, and his comments carry weight with voters and politicians alike. The debate shows how sensitive questions about election credibility have become as campaigning intensifies.
