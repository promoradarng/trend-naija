INEC drops some governorship candidates from list, cites Supreme Court ruling

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has removed a number of governorship and state assembly candidates from its list for the 2027 elections, according to reports on Friday, 30 October 2026.

Why candidates were removed

INEC reportedly cited a Supreme Court judgment of 24 September 2026 that upheld provisions of the Electoral Act 2026 requiring parties to submit their membership registers before fielding candidates. The commission said it was bound by the Constitution to give effect to decisions of superior courts. It did not say how many candidates were affected in total.

Who is affected

Among those missing from the list were Isa Ali Pantami, the PDP governorship candidate in Gombe State, and Abdulsalam Gwarzo, the NDC candidate in Kano State. Both were reportedly later cleared to proceed, subject to the outcome of cases in court. However, sources said INEC did not extend the same clearance to Ladi Adebutu, the PDP governorship candidate in Ogun State. INEC is expected to issue a formal statement.

Background

INEC published its list of cleared governorship and state assembly candidates on 10 October, and parties that believed their candidates had been wrongly excluded had until 24 October to petition the commission. The 2027 primaries produced many disputes, and courts have already ordered changes to several candidacies, including the Imo West senatorial ticket.

Update

In INEC's updated list released on Saturday, 31 October, Pantami was added as the PDP governorship candidate for Gombe after the commission upheld a petition. Gwarzo and his running mate were still missing, marked 'Court Order'. INEC said it had no problem with Gwarzo, but that an order from a Federal High Court in Kano bars it from publishing the name of any NDC candidate until the court gives a final ruling. INEC said it upheld 66 petitions and dismissed 64 nationwide.

What happens next

Affected parties are expected to go to court or press INEC for a review, and the final list could change again before the January elections.

  • INEC cited a Supreme Court ruling on party membership registers.
  • Pantami and Gwarzo were reportedly cleared, subject to court cases.
  • Adebutu was reportedly not cleared.

Court sacks Omo-Agege as NDC senatorial candidate for Delta Central

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Warri has voided Ovie Omo-Agege's nomination as the NDC candidate for Delta Central and ordered INEC to remove his name. The judgment came a day after gunmen attacked his campaign.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Warri, Delta State, has nullified the nomination of former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege as the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate for Delta Central Senatorial District in the 2027 elections.

The judgment

Delivering judgment on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, Justice Hyeladzira Ajiya Nganjiwa ruled that the NDC failed to comply with mandatory provisions of the Electoral Act in nominating Omo-Agege. The court directed INEC to remove his name from the list of senatorial candidates and barred him from presenting himself as the party's candidate.

Why the court ruled against him

The suit was filed by Professor Alexander Osondu Akpodiete, who challenged Omo-Agege's nomination, his resignation from the APC and his eligibility to take part in the NDC primary. The court held that the NDC did not submit its membership register to INEC as required, and that Omo-Agege was still an APC member when he took part in the party's senatorial primary on 18 May 2026. The judge first dismissed five preliminary objections raised by Omo-Agege, the NDC and INEC.

Background

The ruling came a day after gunmen attacked Omo-Agege's campaign event in Uduere, Ughelli North, killing several people. The senator escaped unhurt.

INEC warns 'rig and die' slogan could endanger polling staff and voters

24 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

INEC has cautioned politicians and supporters against the popular 'rig and die' slogan ahead of 2027. A national commissioner said linking an electoral offence to death could incite mob attacks at polling units.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has warned political actors and their supporters against using the slogan 'rig and die' ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What INEC said

Speaking on Monday, 23 November 2026, at a media engagement in Abuja on ethical election reporting, National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, represented by an INEC official, said that whatever its originators intended, a slogan that links an electoral offence to death is dangerous. He warned that it could encourage mob attacks at voting centres.

Who is at risk

The commissioner reportedly stressed that ordinary voters and ad-hoc election staff at polling units, rather than senior politicians, are the most physically exposed if such slogans lead to violence.

Background

The phrase has become widely repeated on social media and at rallies, with some saying it is aimed only at those who would rig the vote. INEC's warning comes amid growing concern about election-related violence, including Monday's deadly attack on an NDC campaign event in Delta State.

Tinubu formally declares for second term, claims Otti and four South-East states back him

24 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

At a town hall with Igbo leaders in Lagos, President Tinubu formally declared he is seeking a second term. He also claimed Imo, Enugu, Ebonyi and Anambra, as well as Abia Governor Alex Otti, are with him.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has formally declared his intention to seek a second term, while claiming the support of Abia State Governor Alex Otti and four South-East states.

The declaration

Tinubu spoke on Monday, 23 November 2026, at a town hall meeting with Igbo leaders, traders and youths, organised by the Ndigbo National Dialogue and Development Initiatives at the Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Creative Arts in Iganmu, Lagos. He said the constitution allowed him to ask for a second term and that he wanted one, adding that the large turnout made him happy.

He framed his appeal around national unity, saying no Nigerian chose their ethnicity and all citizens share a common destiny.

The South-East claim

The president listed Imo, Enugu, Ebonyi and Anambra as states he said were firmly behind him, and singled out Governor Otti, who was elected on the Labour Party platform, as a supporter. He said the absence of some of those he named from the event should not be read as displeasure.

The other side

No response from Governor Otti was included in the reports. The South-East is widely regarded as a stronghold of the NDC's Peter Obi, who is from Anambra State.

The video follows recent remarks by Wike at events in Rivers State, where he repeatedly downplayed Obi's chances in 2027 and claimed he had neutralised the 'Obi Kererenke' wave. Wike, a former PDP governor, is now one of President Bola Tinubu's leading backers.
