Paraguay's President Santiago Peña resigns over bank collapse scandal

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned after the lower house voted to begin impeachment proceedings over the collapse of a bank he was once linked to as a shareholder. He apologised to Paraguayans.

Read more

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned amid a political crisis caused by the collapse of a bank he was previously linked to.

What happened

In a video message posted on X, Peña said he was stepping down to protect the country's stability and democratic institutions, a day after opposition lawmakers requested his impeachment and the lower house of Congress voted to begin proceedings, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. He apologised to the people if he had failed them at any point, saying it was never his intention to betray their trust.

The bank collapse

The crisis began when Ueno Bank collapsed and froze its operations, reportedly leaving large numbers of customers unable to access their accounts. Lawmakers accused Peña of conflicts of interest, pointing to his past shareholding in the company that owned the bank.

Background

Peña, an economist and former finance minister, took office in August 2023 as a member of the long-dominant Colorado Party. Under Paraguay's constitution, the vice-president takes over when a president leaves office before the end of the term.

What happens next

Attention now turns to who will lead the country for the rest of the term, and to investigations into the bank's collapse and whether depositors will recover their money.

Spain's transport minister resigns after deadly train crashes

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Spain's Transport Minister Oscar Puente has announced his resignation after two major train crashes in less than a year, including an October collision south of Madrid that killed 69 people. He said staying on would be inconsistent with political dignity.

Read more

Spain's Transport Minister, Oscar Puente, has announced that he will resign following two serious train crashes in less than a year.

What he said

Puente told lawmakers on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, that a transport minister in a country like Spain could not stay in office after two accidents of such severity, regardless of whether he was directly responsible. He described the decision as a matter of political dignity, consistency and empathy, and said he had decided to step down within minutes of the latest crash but stayed on to manage the aftermath and introduce safety measures. He did not say exactly when he would leave office, and confirmed he would not stand in Spain's early general election on 29 November.

The latest crash

The resignation came six days after a crash on 28 October near Urda, south of Madrid, in which a high-speed train run by the state operator Renfe hit the back of a stationary train run by a private operator on the Madrid–Andalusia line. The crash reportedly killed 69 people and injured more than 200. Authorities have blamed a combination of human and computer failures, and Puente said a red signal should have stopped the train.

Background

Spain has one of Europe's largest high-speed rail networks. The two crashes have caused public anger over rail safety.

What happens next

Investigations into both crashes are continuing.

UK court rejects Ibori's appeal to cut £101.5m confiscation order

5 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Court of Appeal in London has dismissed former Delta governor James Ibori's bid to reduce a £101.5 million confiscation order made after his 2012 conviction for money laundering. Ibori says he is consulting his lawyers.

Read more

Britain's Court of Appeal has dismissed a challenge by former Delta State governor James Ibori to reduce a £101.5 million confiscation order against him, according to reports.

The ruling

The court rejected the appeal on Tuesday, 3 November 2026. Ibori had sought to reduce the amount by about £4.3 million. The order was issued by Southwark Crown Court in July 2023, directing him to pay or face an eight-year prison term in default. The appeal had delayed enforcement, which can now proceed unless he takes the case to the UK Supreme Court.

Ibori's response

Ibori reportedly said he was consulting his lawyers on his next steps and expressed frustration at how long the proceedings had taken.

Background

Ibori governed Delta State from 1999 to 2007. He was arrested in Dubai and extradited to Britain in 2011. In 2012 he pleaded guilty in a London court to laundering tens of millions of dollars of public funds through British banks and properties, and was sentenced to 13 years in prison. He was released in late 2016 after serving about half of the sentence and returned to Nigeria in 2017. The confiscation proceedings followed investigations by Nigerian and British anti-corruption authorities, and Nigeria has pushed for the recovered funds to be returned.

Why it matters

The case is one of the most significant examples of international cooperation to recover stolen public funds from Nigeria.

Bandits demand over ₦100 million for abducted Niger Islamiyya students, teacher says

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The teacher of the Islamiyya students abducted from his farm in Rafi, Niger State, says the kidnappers have demanded more than ₦100 million for their release. He said families have had little official help and called for military action.

Read more

The kidnappers of a group of Islamiyya students abducted in Niger State have demanded more than ₦100 million for their release, according to their teacher.

What the teacher said

Malam Aminu Bako, whose students were taken while helping him harvest maize on his farm in Rafi Local Government Area, said on Thursday, 5 November 2026, that the bandits had contacted village leaders with the ransom demand. He said 15 children were abducted, and called for urgent military action to rescue them. According to him, only the acting chairman of the local government and the divisional police officer had visited since the abduction, and families were relying on local vigilantes to track the kidnappers.

Where the children may be

Bako said information gathered by vigilantes suggests the children are still in Niger State, held in an isolated building guarded by heavily armed men, possibly without enough food. He said he was under intense pressure from worried parents.

Background

The students were abducted on 31 October from a farm in Tungan-Bako village near Pandogari. Police at the time said 14 students were taken and that a joint security operation had been launched. Rafi and surrounding areas have suffered repeated attacks by armed groups, and kidnapping for ransom remains widespread in north-west and north-central Nigeria. Paying ransom is illegal in Nigeria, although families often feel they have no choice.

What happens next

Families and community leaders are calling on the federal and state governments to step up efforts to rescue the children.
