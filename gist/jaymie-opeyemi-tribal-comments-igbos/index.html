Democrats projected to win both House and Senate in blow to Trump

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

US networks are projecting that the Democrats have won control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate in the 2026 midterm elections. The party reportedly won Senate seats in states such as Texas, Iowa and Kansas, which Trump carried easily in 2024.

Read more

The Democratic Party is projected to have won control of both chambers of the United States Congress in the 2026 midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November, according to projections by US television networks reported early on Wednesday.

The results

According to the projections, the Democrats have taken the House of Representatives and the Senate. The Senate results were described as particularly striking, with Democrats reportedly winning seats in states that President Donald Trump carried by wide margins in 2024, including Texas, South Carolina, Ohio, Kansas and Iowa. Texas had reportedly not elected a Democrat to statewide office since 1994. Final results in some races may take days to confirm.

Why voters turned

Analysts have pointed to Trump's low approval ratings, voter anger over tariffs and inflation, and the long-running conflict with Iran as reasons for the swing. On the eve of the vote, US forces carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, while Trump told supporters that Iran might eventually have to be given 'the hammer'.

What it means

With control of both chambers, Democrats will be able to block much of the President's legislative agenda, launch investigations with the power to compel testimony, and hold up his nominees, including judges, during the last two years of his term. Trump remains president until January 2029.

Why it matters for Nigeria

US policy on trade, aid and immigration affects Nigeria directly. A Democratic Congress could push back on measures such as visa restrictions and changes to trade programmes, including this week's decision to remove South Africa and Rwanda from the AGOA duty-free scheme. The White House had not formally responded to the projections at the time of reporting.

Petrol depots cut prices below Dangote's ₦1,300 gantry rate

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Several petrol depots in Lagos and Port Harcourt have reportedly cut prices to as low as ₦1,283 per litre, below Dangote refinery's new fixed gantry price of ₦1,300. Pump prices may take time to fall because of transport costs and existing stock.

Read more

Petrol marketers have started selling below the Dangote refinery's new ₦1,300 per litre gantry price, raising hopes that fuel could become a little cheaper for Nigerians.

The new prices

Price checks in Lagos and Port Harcourt on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, reportedly showed several depots selling petrol up to ₦17 per litre below the refinery's official rate. A depot in Port Harcourt listed the lowest price at ₦1,283 per litre. The Dangote refinery recently raised its gantry price to a fixed ₦1,300 per litre, saying it wanted to bring more stability to petrol pricing.

What it means for motorists

Depot prices are what marketers pay before transporting fuel to filling stations, so lower depot prices do not immediately mean cheaper fuel at the pump. Transport costs and stock bought at higher prices may delay any reduction for consumers, but analysts say competition between depots could gradually push pump prices down.

Background

Since the removal of the petrol subsidy in 2023, prices have been set by the market, and the Dangote refinery has become the biggest player in local supply. Its pricing decisions now strongly influence what other marketers charge. Days ago it had cut its gantry price to ₦1,210, before moving to the new fixed rate of ₦1,300, and depot owners have responded with their own changes. Petrol prices remain far higher than before the subsidy was removed, adding to the cost of transport and food. Meanwhile, cooking gas prices rose by up to 16 per cent at Lagos depots in recent days.

What happens next

Marketers say prices will depend on supply, exchange rates and global oil prices, which rose slightly this week after US strikes on Iran.

Senate President Godswill Akpabio has sparked reactions after saying that politicians are struggling to find private jets to hire as campaigning for the 2027 elections gathers pace.

What he said

Speaking during a Senate debate on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Akpabio reportedly said there were not enough private jets available for hire, and linked the problem to the failure of domestic airlines to keep up with rising demand for air travel. He pointed to the December festive season as a time when the country's aviation capacity is badly stretched, and urged the federal government to take urgent steps to address the problems facing local airlines.

The debate

The comments came during debate on a motion sponsored by Senator Tahir Monguno of Borno North, which urged the government to renegotiate the Bilateral Air Services Agreement between Nigeria and China so that Nigerian airlines can fly directly to China.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the remark, saying it showed how far some politicians are from the everyday struggles of Nigerians facing high costs of food and transport. Others said Akpabio was making a broader point about the poor state of domestic aviation, which often leaves ordinary passengers stranded by delays and cancellations.

Background

Nigeria's domestic airlines have long faced problems including ageing fleets, high fuel costs, foreign exchange shortages and frequent flight disruptions. The government has promised reforms to strengthen the sector. Akpabio has been Senate President since 2023 and is a leading member of the APC.

NPA says Nigeria's exports rose 25% in two years as port revenue quadrupled

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The head of the Nigerian Ports Authority has reportedly said exports grew by more than 25% in two years, driven by cocoa, cashew nuts and manufactured goods, and that port revenue has quadrupled. A $1 billion upgrade of Apapa port is planned.

Read more

Nigeria's exports have grown by more than 25 per cent over the past two years, according to the Nigerian Ports Authority (NPA), which credits government reforms and new export facilities.

What was said

NPA Managing Director Abubakar Dantsoho made the disclosure at the annual World Ports Conference in London, organised by the International Association of Ports and Harbours, according to reports on Tuesday, 3 November 2026. He said the growth was driven by higher exports of agricultural products such as cocoa and cashew nuts, as well as manufactured goods. He also said revenue generated by Nigerian ports had increased four times, reflecting higher cargo volumes and improved efficiency.

New facilities

Dantsoho said four Export Processing Terminals would reduce delays for exporters, and that the government plans a $1 billion upgrade of Apapa port in Lagos, along with new digital systems to make Nigerian ports more competitive.

Background

Nigeria has long depended on crude oil for most of its export earnings, and successive governments have tried to grow non-oil exports. The weaker naira since 2023 has made Nigerian goods cheaper abroad, which has helped some exporters, though it has also raised the cost of imported inputs. Exporters have complained in the past about congestion, delays and high costs at the ports, especially in Lagos.

Why it matters

Higher exports bring in more foreign currency, which can support the naira. The currency gained slightly on Tuesday after losing ground in October.
