The son of the second wife at the centre of the viral Ukehe controversy has spoken out, begging Nigerians to stop attacking his mother.

"Is it not enough already?"

"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum. Is it not enough already? That woman does not deserve all this hate," he said.

"Do you want her to die? If anything happens to my mum, you people won't like it. Please, this is enough."

How it started

His mother has faced a wave of criticism online since a video from his father's Ozo title-taking ceremony went viral. In the video, the second wife's children reportedly refused to spray money on the first wife, Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

Claims then spread online that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over being childless and had moved back to her father's home. Those claims have not been independently verified.

Since then, well-wishers have visited Mrs Anulika with gifts and money, and the Enugu governor's wife sent a representative with cash and promises of a house and a business.

The other side of the story

The son's plea is a reminder that there are always two sides to a family story, and that online anger can quickly turn into harassment.

Sympathy for Mrs Anulika is understandable. But insults and threats against another woman, whose side of the story has not been heard, help no one. Families caught up in viral moments are still real people.

Socialite Ismaila Mustapha, popularly known as Mompha, says a court has struck out the money-laundering charges brought against him by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC).

What he said

In an Instagram post on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Mompha said the court struck out the ₦6 billion charges on the grounds that they amounted to an abuse of court process and forum shopping. He described the outcome as personal vindication after what he called years of harassment, called the prosecution a witch-hunt, praised the judge and thanked his supporters. He also mocked the anti-graft agency in the post.

Background

Mompha's legal battle with the EFCC began in 2019, when he was arrested after returning to Nigeria. He has always denied the allegations, saying he came home to invest. The case dragged on for about seven years. The EFCC had not publicly commented on the ruling at the time of writing, and it was not immediately clear whether the commission would appeal or file fresh charges.

What it means

Mompha says his legal ordeal is now over.

James Gunn and Peter Safran to step down as DC Studios bosses

10 Nov 2026, 17:47 GMT

James Gunn and Peter Safran will step down as co-CEOs of DC Studios at the end of November, four years after taking charge, following Skydance's purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery. Former Marvel executive Jeremy Latcham will take over.

Read more

Filmmaker James Gunn and producer Peter Safran are stepping down as co-chief executives of DC Studios.

The announcement

The pair announced on Monday, 9 November 2026, that they will leave their roles at the end of November, four years after taking charge, following Skydance's purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery. They will be succeeded by former Marvel Studios executive Jeremy Latcham, who worked as a producer on films including 'Iron Man', 'The Avengers' and 'Guardians of the Galaxy'.

Gunn's message

In an Instagram post, Gunn, who directed the 2025 film 'Superman', described leading DC Studios as a dream come true but said the job had been challenging, with corporate demands taking a toll on his personal life and leaving him little time with loved ones. He thanked fans and credited Safran with handling much of the corporate side of their work. Gunn will continue to work on several DC projects despite leaving the leadership role.

Background

Gunn and Safran took over DC Studios in late 2022 with plans to build a new connected universe of DC superhero films and series. The change comes as the new owners of Warner Bros. reshape the company.
