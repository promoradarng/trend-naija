ADC names venue as Atiku kicks off presidential campaign in Sokoto

4 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has named the venue for the official launch of Atiku Abubakar's 2027 presidential campaign in Sokoto.

The venue

According to reports, the campaign will be flagged off at the party's secretariat on Ilorin Road, Sokoto, on Wednesday, 4 November 2026. Senator Ibrahim Lamido, who coordinates the ADC presidential campaign in the state, said Atiku was expected to arrive at Sultan Abubakar III International Airport at about 9am, and invited supporters and members of the public to welcome him before moving to the venue. He urged party executives in all 23 local government areas of the state to mobilise supporters.

Why the venue was kept quiet

The party had earlier withheld details of the venue, citing alleged intimidation, an attack on the convoy of its governorship candidate and the sealing of one of its campaign offices. On Tuesday, Atiku accused the Department of State Services of recruiting protesters to disrupt the rally, a claim the DSS had not publicly answered. The Sokoto State Government said it sealed the office over questions about who owns the building and to prevent further disturbances.

Background

Atiku, a former vice-president, is the ADC's candidate after the party became the platform of an opposition coalition. Sokoto is in the North-West, a region with a large share of Nigeria's voters, and is governed by the APC. Other candidates, including President Bola Tinubu of the APC and Peter Obi of the NDC, have also been holding rallies and town halls across the country.

What happens next

Security agencies are expected to be deployed in Sokoto for the event, and the ADC plans further rallies in other states.

  • The launch holds at the ADC secretariat on Ilorin Road, Sokoto.
  • Atiku is expected to arrive at 9am.
  • The party cited intimidation for keeping the venue quiet.

Atiku says nearly 40% of bread makers have shut down under Tinubu

14 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Campaigning in Bauchi, ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar said the Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria has lost close to 40 per cent of its members in two years, blaming soaring energy and financing costs under President Tinubu.

Read more

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has accused the Tinubu administration of pushing Nigeria's baking industry to the brink.

What he said

In a statement released in Bauchi on Saturday, 14 November 2026, by Phrank Shaibu, director of strategic communication for his campaign, Atiku said the Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria had lost close to 40 per cent of its members in two years. He was in the state as part of what his campaign calls its 'affordable life' tour.

Rising costs

According to the statement, energy accounts for roughly half of most bakeries' production costs. It said one operator's monthly diesel bill had risen from ₦9 million to ₦19 million, and that switching to alternative power could cost a small bakery ₦40 million to ₦50 million, money most cannot raise. The statement blamed high energy prices and expensive financing for the closures.

Bakers confirm

The Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria itself said at an exhibition in Lagos that about 40 per cent of its members had shut down in the past two years because of rising energy costs, expensive loans and economic conditions. Its president, Emmanuel Onuorah, called on the federal government to provide grants and single-digit interest loans to businesses in the real sector, warning that more closures could threaten jobs.

Background

Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has made the cost of living a central theme of his campaign. The government says its reforms, including the removal of fuel subsidy, were necessary to stabilise the economy and free up money for infrastructure. The Presidency had not responded to the statement at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

"If Peter Obi doesn't win in 2027, I'll relocate to Burkina Faso": GehGeh says Obi's supporters back him out of genuine love

14 Nov 2026, 13:25 GMT

Social media personality GehGeh says Peter Obi is the only presidential candidate Nigerians support out of genuine love and belief in his ability to lead, and has vowed to leave Nigeria for Burkina Faso if Obi loses in 2027.

Read more

Popular social media personality GehGeh has declared strong support for Peter Obi ahead of the 2027 presidential election, saying Obi stands apart because of the genuine love and confidence his supporters have in him.

"Genuine love and belief"

According to GehGeh, many Nigerians backing Obi, the NDC presidential candidate, do so because they believe he is qualified, capable and able to lead the country in a better direction.

He argued that things are different with some other candidates, whose supporters, in his view, are more interested in what they personally stand to gain if their candidate wins.

The Burkina Faso vow

GehGeh went further, saying that if Obi fails to win in 2027, he will give up on Nigeria completely and relocate to Burkina Faso.

His statement reflects his frustration with the country's political situation and his belief that Nigeria needs a different kind of leadership.

Different, or just more emotional?

His declaration also raises a fair question. Is support for Obi genuinely different from that for other politicians, or are Nigerians simply becoming more emotionally attached to their preferred candidates? Supporters of every major candidate would argue that their backing is sincere.

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has denied claims that its chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, is planning to resign.

Reports dismissed

The commission dismissed the reports, which had been circulating ahead of the 2027 general elections, and maintained that the chairman is not stepping down.

Why it matters

Any change at the top of the electoral body this close to a general election would raise serious concerns about preparations for the polls. INEC has faced intense scrutiny in recent weeks, including controversy over AI-generated images in the voter register and growing tension around the "Rig and Die" slogan.
