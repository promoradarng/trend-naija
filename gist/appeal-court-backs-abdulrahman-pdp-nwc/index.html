Appeal Court upholds Abdulrahman-led PDP leadership, losing faction heads to Supreme Court

4 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Court of Appeal in Abuja has ruled in favour of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) leadership led by Abdulrahman Mohammed, the latest turn in the party's long-running leadership crisis.

The judgment

On Wednesday, 4 November 2026, a three-member special panel led by Justice Ugochukwu Anthony Ogakwu dismissed an appeal filed by former Senate President Adolphus Wabara and seven others, according to reports. The appellants had asked the court to compel the Independent National Electoral Commission to recognise an interim National Working Committee led by Turaki instead of the Abdulrahman-led NWC. The panel upheld an earlier Federal High Court judgment that backed the Abdulrahman-led committee.

What happens next

The losing faction announced plans to take the matter to the Supreme Court, vowing to continue the legal battle.

Background

The PDP, Nigeria's main opposition party for much of the past decade, has been split by leadership disputes since its defeat in the 2023 elections. Several of its leading figures have left to join other parties or opposition coalitions, while others, including FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, have backed President Bola Tinubu. The party has continued to field candidates for the 2027 elections in many states, but its internal crisis has raised questions about its strength.

Why it matters

Who controls the party's national leadership affects the nomination of candidates and the party's dealings with INEC ahead of the January 2027 elections.

  • The court affirmed the Abdulrahman-led NWC.
  • The Wabara-led appellants lost.
  • The losing faction plans to go to the Supreme Court.

INEC asks court to cancel subpoena for Tinubu's 1999 and 2023 election records

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

INEC has asked the Federal High Court in Abuja to set aside a subpoena ordering its chairman to appear and produce President Tinubu's 1999 and 2023 nomination documents in the trial of activist Omoyele Sowore.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has asked the Federal High Court in Abuja to set aside a subpoena requiring its chairman to produce President Bola Tinubu's election documents.

The application

INEC's lawyer, Alex Izinyon, a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, filed the motion on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, the day before INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan was due to appear as a defence witness in the criminal trial of activist Omoyele Sowore. The subpoena, issued by Justice Mohammed Umar, had directed the INEC chairman to produce Tinubu's nomination forms from the 1999 Lagos governorship election, including an affidavit relating to lost certificates, and from the 2023 presidential election.

The case

Sowore is being prosecuted by the State Security Service over social media posts in which he described the President as a criminal. His defence team asked the court to call the INEC chairman and Joe Igbokwe, a former APC publicity secretary in Lagos, as witnesses.

Background

Questions about the President's academic records have been raised by critics for years and were the subject of legal challenges after the 2023 election, which the Supreme Court dismissed. The President and his supporters have consistently rejected claims of wrongdoing.

What he said

In an interview in Abuja on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Agbese, a Labour Party member representing Ado/Ogbadibo/Okpokwu Federal Constituency in Benue State, said the Speaker had created a working environment that put national interests above party loyalty. Jigawa declares holiday as Tinubu visits for rally and project launches

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Jigawa State declared Tuesday a public holiday for President Tinubu's first official visit since 2023. He is due to hold an APC rally and commission projects including an agricultural mechanisation programme with 400 tractors.

Read more

Jigawa State has declared Tuesday, 10 November 2026, a public holiday for a visit by President Bola Tinubu.

The visit

Governor Umar Namadi told journalists in Dutse on Monday that the President would hold an All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally and inaugurate four projects. He said it would be Tinubu's first official visit to the state since he took office in May 2023. The projects include a technical services centre, a skills acquisition centre named after the President, an agricultural mechanisation programme and a dualised road in Dutse.

The farm equipment

The agricultural package includes 400 tractors, 60 combine harvesters, 150 boom sprayers and 150 seeders, along with other equipment intended to boost farm output in the state.

Power pledge at the rally

At the APC rally in Jigawa, Tinubu promised to lower the cost of living and provide reliable electricity, saying the old privatisation model was not working. During his 2023 campaign he said Nigerians should not re-elect him if he failed to provide stable power in his first term.

Background

The Jigawa visit continues the President's campaign tour, which in the past week has taken him to Akwa Ibom, Rivers and Ebonyi states. Namadi, elected on the APC platform in 2023, is a strong supporter of the President's re-election. The North-West, which includes Jigawa, has one of the largest voting populations in Nigeria.

Tinubu launches ₦52,500 minimum monthly pension for 173,209 retirees

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

President Tinubu has activated a guaranteed minimum pension of ₦52,500 a month for 173,209 retirees under the Contributory Pension Scheme, a provision that has been in law since 2004 but was never enforced. He also launched free basic healthcare for low-income pensioners.

Read more

Retirees under Nigeria's Contributory Pension Scheme will now receive at least ₦52,500 a month, after President Bola Tinubu activated a minimum pension guarantee in Abuja.

What was announced

At the Presidential Pension Security Initiatives ceremony at the State House on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the President, represented by Senate President Godswill Akpabio, declared the guarantee active for 173,209 qualifying retirees. He described it as a floor beneath Nigerian retirees and urged future governments to raise it but never remove it. The Director-General of the National Pension Commission (PenCom), Omolola Oloworaran, confirmed the figure and said more than 65 per cent of private-sector retirees currently earn less than the new minimum.

Who benefits

According to PenCom, 54 per cent of the qualifying retirees come from the private sector, 34 per cent from federal service and 12 per cent from state employment. More than ₦400bn in the Pension Protection Fund will finance the guarantee.

Healthcare and arrears

The President also launched PenCare, a healthcare plan that will give low-income pensioners free access to essential health services, with access cards to be distributed in the coming months. He said the government had issued a ₦758bn bond to clear pension arrears and approved a ₦32,000 monthly adjustment for affected federal pensioners.

Background

The minimum pension was first written into the Pension Reform Act of 2004 and kept in the 2014 law, but no government had put it into effect until now.
