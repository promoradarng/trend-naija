"I have lost everything I worked for, I am starting afresh" – Blessing CEO breaks down in tears as she recounts losing her son and her time in custody

27 Oct 2026, 18:43 GMT

Controversial Nigerian relationship therapist and social media personality Blessing CEO has sparked emotional reactions after breaking down in tears while recounting some of the most painful experiences of her life, including the loss of her son and her time in custody.

In an emotional account, Blessing opened up about the personal struggles she has endured, revealing that her experiences have forced her to reflect deeply on her life, decisions and the direction she wants to take going forward.

According to her, she has lost virtually everything she spent years working to build and is now faced with the difficult task of starting all over again.

One of the most touching moments came when she spoke about losing her son, an experience that appeared to leave her overwhelmed with grief.

She also reflected on her time in detention and how it affected her emotionally and personally.

However, what caught the attention of many was her willingness to examine her past actions and acknowledge that there may be areas where she got things wrong.

Blessing expressed her readiness to identify her mistakes, make necessary corrections and take a different path in life.

For someone who has built a public image around controversial opinions, outspoken statements and heated social media exchanges, her emotional reflections have introduced another side of her personality to the public.

She now appears determined to rebuild her life, even if it means starting from nothing.

Her story raises important questions about personal growth, forgiveness and second chances. Can someone who has made mistakes in the past genuinely change and rebuild their life?

Whatever opinions people may have about Blessing CEO, watching someone speak about losing a child, being locked up and watching years of hard work disappear is a reminder that public figures also face painful personal struggles.

Carter Efe and Peller miss out as India's Ashswag wins international streamer award

13 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Nigerian streamers Carter Efe and Peller lost out on the Best International Streamer prize at the 2026 Streamer Awards, which went to Indian streamer Ashswag.

Read more

Nigerian content creators Carter Efe and Peller have missed out on an international award at the 2026 Streamer Awards.

The result

The two were nominated for Best International Streamer after the organisers released the shortlist in October. At the ceremony, reported on Friday, 13 November 2026, the prize went to Indian streamer Ashswag, who beat the Nigerian pair and other nominees from around the world.

Why it mattered

The nominations put the Nigerian duo among creators from around the world competing for the category.

Peller and Phyna react

In a post on X on Thursday night, Peller thanked his fans, saying their loyalty meant more to him than any trophy and that the nomination itself was a blessing. He complained, however, that the result of his category was announced on the red carpet before the main ceremony began, which he called unfair. Reality star Phyna went further in a livestream, accusing the organisers of using the Nigerian nominees to attract Nigerian viewers and engagement with no real intention of giving them the award.

Background

Peller rose to prominence as one of Nigeria's biggest TikTok live streamers, while Carter Efe, a skit maker and musician, has also built a large streaming following.

Blessing CEO admits she did not have breast cancer in interview with VeryDarkMan

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Relationship coach Blessing CEO has admitted in an interview with VeryDarkMan that she did not have breast cancer, months after announcing that she was battling the illness and receiving donations from the public.

Read more

Social media personality and relationship coach Blessing CEO has admitted that she did not have breast cancer, months after telling the public she was battling the disease.

What she said

In an interview with activist VeryDarkMan (VDM), reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, she said a medical check-up had found a lump in her breast, but that it was not cancerous. She admitted that she used the discovery to tell people it was cancer because she wanted financial support. She said she first gave the impression that she was thinking of selling her car and house, before fans encouraged her to share her account details so they could donate.

How the donations ended

She said money started coming in from members of the public until VeryDarkMan questioned the fundraising and demanded proof of her diagnosis, after which the donations stopped.

VeryDarkMan criticised

VeryDarkMan later asked Nigerians to forgive her, drawing criticism from some social media users. Commentator Japhman, in a video on 12 November, accused him of a double standard, saying the activist's 'Ratel' movement is built on demanding accountability from others.

"If Peter Obi doesn't win in 2027, I'll relocate to Burkina Faso": GehGeh says Obi's supporters back him out of genuine love

Social media personality GehGeh says Peter Obi is the only presidential candidate Nigerians support out of genuine love and belief in his ability to lead, and has vowed to leave Nigeria for Burkina Faso if Obi loses in 2027.

Read more

Popular social media personality GehGeh has declared strong support for Peter Obi ahead of the 2027 presidential election, saying Obi stands apart because of the genuine love and confidence his supporters have in him.

"Genuine love and belief"

According to GehGeh, many Nigerians backing Obi, the NDC presidential candidate, do so because they believe he is qualified, capable and able to lead the country in a better direction.

He argued that things are different with some other candidates, whose supporters, in his view, are more interested in what they personally stand to gain if their candidate wins.

The Burkina Faso vow

GehGeh went further, saying that if Obi fails to win in 2027, he will give up on Nigeria completely and relocate to Burkina Faso.

His statement reflects his frustration with the country's political situation and his belief that Nigeria needs a different kind of leadership.

Different, or just more emotional?

His declaration also raises a fair question. Is support for Obi genuinely different from that for other politicians, or are Nigerians simply becoming more emotionally attached to their preferred candidates? Supporters of every major candidate would argue that their backing is sincere.

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has denied claims that its chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, is planning to resign.

The commission dismissed the reports, which had been circulating ahead of the 2027 general elections, and maintained that the chairman is not stepping down.

Any change at the top of the electoral body this close to a general election would raise serious concerns about preparations for the polls. INEC has faced intense scrutiny in recent weeks, including controversy over AI-generated images in the voter register and growing tension around the "Rig and Die" slogan.
