Don Jazzy says he blew his first big music money on 50 bottles of champagne

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

Music producer and Mavin Records founder Michael Collins Ajereh, better known as Don Jazzy, has admitted that he wasted his first major earnings from music, and says the lesson changed his life.

What he said

Speaking on an episode of the 'On The Grind' podcast, in comments that spread on Monday, 2 November 2026, Don Jazzy said that when he got his first big money from music, he bought 50 bottles of champagne and went clubbing. He said he quickly pulled himself together and began investing in signing new artistes, adding that, as a result, he no longer has to work.

His advice

The producer urged young people to put the money they earn in their youth into profitable businesses rather than spending it on luxuries. He said they did not necessarily need to start a record label, but should find something lucrative to invest in.

Background

Don Jazzy co-founded Mo' Hits Records with D'banj in 2004 and produced many of the label's biggest hits. After the label split in 2012, he set up Mavin Records, which has gone on to sign and develop stars such as Tiwa Savage, Rema, Ayra Starr, Johnny Drille and Crayon. The label attracted a major investment from Universal Music Group in 2024. He is also known for his generosity online and recently announced a technology scholarship for young Nigerians.

How people reacted

Many social media users praised his honesty and said the advice was useful for young people who start earning early. Others joked that 50 bottles of champagne would be a dream night out, while some pointed out that his success depended on spotting talent, which not every investor can do.

Suspected thugs attack Kwankwaso's convoy in Kano, smash about seven vehicles

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Suspected thugs reportedly attacked the campaign convoy of NDC vice-presidential candidate Rabiu Kwankwaso and Kano governorship candidate Aminu Abdussalam Gwarzo in Bichi on Sunday evening, injuring team members and damaging about seven vehicles.

Read more

The campaign convoy of Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) vice-presidential candidate and former Kano governor, was reportedly attacked by suspected thugs in Kano State on Sunday evening, 15 November 2026.

What happened

According to Ibrahim Garba Shu'aibu, spokesperson for the NDC's Kano governorship candidate, Aminu Abdussalam Gwarzo, the convoy had passed peacefully through Tofa, Rimin Gado, Kabo, Gwarzo, Shanono and Bagwai local government areas before reaching Bichi. He said the team was warned that its route into Bichi had been blocked by people opposed to the rally, and that stones were thrown when the delegation arrived. The meeting went ahead, but as the convoy left, a group of armed youths near the local government secretariat reportedly attacked it, injuring some members of the team and smashing about seven vehicles. A media aide to Kwankwaso also confirmed the incident on social media.

Response

The police had not publicly commented on the incident at the time of writing, and it is not clear who was behind the attack.

Background

The attack is the latest in a series of incidents of political violence ahead of the 2027 elections. Suspected thugs stormed an NDC town hall in Lagos last week, and supporters of other parties, including the APC, have also been attacked at rallies in several states. President Bola Tinubu has ordered security agencies to deal firmly with thuggery, and the House of Representatives is investigating attacks on political events. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Soludo tells politicians to keep campaigns out of churches

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Anambra governor Charles Soludo has urged politicians not to turn places of worship into campaign grounds, saying churches should be neutral spaces where people of all political views can worship in peace.

Read more

Anambra State governor Charles Soludo has called on politicians to stop turning churches into campaign platforms.

What he said

Speaking at the 30th ministry anniversary of Prophet Chukwuemeka Ohanaemere, popularly known as Odumeje, in Onitsha, reported on Monday, 16 November 2026, Soludo said places of worship should be holy places where people of all political persuasions can worship God in peace. He said politicians have their own venues for politics and urged them to keep the two clearly separate. The governor said he had personally followed a policy of not using religious venues for campaigning.

Background

Earlier this month, a Catholic priest apologised for singing Peter Obi's campaign song in church, and Yoruba Muslim clerics said they would not endorse any candidate. Soludo, whose party APGA has endorsed President Bola Tinubu for 2027, has pledged to deliver half of Anambra's votes to the President.

Zamfara police rescue 19 women and children after 22 days with bandits

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Zamfara police say they have rescued 19 women and children who had been held by bandits for 22 days, after a joint operation with the DSS and local hunters near Gusau. All were found unharmed.

Read more

Police in Zamfara State say they have rescued 19 women and children who had spent 22 days in the hands of bandits.

The rescue

In a statement, police spokesperson DSP Yazid Abubakar said the victims had been abducted from Tungar Haki village in Gusau Local Government Area and taken to a bandits' hideout near Kungurmi village. They were rescued on Saturday, 14 November 2026, in a coordinated operation involving the police anti-kidnapping unit, officers attached to Government House, the Department of State Services and local hunters, following intelligence about their location.

Reunited

The police said all 19 victims were found unharmed and have been reunited with their families.

Background

Zamfara is one of the states worst affected by banditry and kidnapping for ransom. On Sunday, bandits killed a senior aide to the state governor during an attack in Kaura Namoda and reportedly abducted several worshippers.

Guinea deports 1,557 West Africans convicted of illegal gold mining

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Guinea has deported 1,557 West Africans, mostly from Burkina Faso and Mali, who had been jailed for illegal gold mining, as it steps up a crackdown on unauthorised mining.

Read more

Guinea has expelled 1,557 West African nationals who were arrested and convicted for illegal gold mining.

The deportation

According to judicial and security sources quoted in reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, the deportees, mostly citizens of Burkina Faso and Mali, were arrested at mining sites during security operations. After serving prison sentences of 18 months to two years, they were taken in a convoy of 44 vehicles from the capital, Conakry, to the border town of Kouremale on the Mali border, arriving on Saturday.

The crackdown

Guinean authorities stepped up operations against illegal mining in early November, particularly in Siguiri, a major gold-producing region in the north-east. The government says the aim is to stop illegal exploitation of mineral resources and limit environmental damage from unregulated mining.

Background

The crackdown has also raised tensions between miners and the authorities.
