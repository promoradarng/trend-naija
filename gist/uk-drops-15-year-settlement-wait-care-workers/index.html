UK drops plan to make care workers wait 15 years for settlement

2 Nov 2026, 18:01 GMT

The UK government has reportedly dropped a plan that would have required some migrant care workers already in Britain to wait 15 years before they could settle permanently, a change that affects many Nigerians working in the sector.

What changed

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood told the House of Commons on Monday, 2 November 2026, that care workers who joined the social care visa route before May 2025 and earn at least £25,000 a year in the sector will keep a five-year route to indefinite leave to remain. She also scrapped the proposed 15-year wait for other lower-paid workers, replacing it with a 10-year route.

The new rules

Mahmood said settlement will now be more closely linked to a person's contribution, adding that the right to live in Britain must be earned. A five-year route will remain for people earning more than £50,000 a year and for qualifying workers in health and education. People earning more than £125,000 could settle in three years. Most other migrants, including those earning under £50,000, would face a 10-year route, while being able to renew their temporary visas.

Why the plan was changed

The reversal followed opposition from Labour MPs, trade unions and migrant groups, who argued it was unfair to change the rules for people who came to Britain under different conditions.

What it means for Nigerians

Nigerians are among the largest groups of overseas care workers recruited to the UK in recent years. Indefinite leave to remain allows a person to live, work and study in Britain without time limits and claim benefits where eligible, and is usually a step towards citizenship.

  • Care workers who joined before May 2025 keep a five-year route.
  • Most other lower earners face a 10-year route.
  • High earners above £125,000 can settle in three years.

At least 20 people have reportedly been killed in an operation involving Salvadoran troops targeting suspected gang members in Haiti.

A country held hostage

The reported incident comes as Haiti continues to struggle with widespread gang violence, kidnappings and insecurity, particularly in areas where armed groups have taken control of communities and forced thousands of residents to flee.

For years, ordinary Haitians have lived under the constant threat of armed gangs, with many families unable to go about their daily lives without fear.

Foreign forces on the ground

Soldiers from El Salvador have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an internationally backed mission to help the Haitian police fight the gangs, alongside forces from other countries.

The reported operation has raised fresh questions about the growing role of foreign security forces in efforts to restore order in the troubled Caribbean nation.

What we don't know

The circumstances of the reported deaths, including the identities of those killed and the exact role played by the Salvadoran troops, have not been independently confirmed.

US bars travellers who have been in DR Congo in the last 21 days over Ebola

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The US CDC has temporarily barred anyone who has been in the Democratic Republic of Congo within the past 21 days, including US citizens and transit passengers, from boarding flights to the United States because of Ebola.

Read more

The United States has imposed a temporary travel restriction on people who have recently been in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) because of Ebola.

The rule

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) said anyone who has been in the DRC in the past 21 days may not board a flight to the United States, according to reports on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. The rule applies to American citizens as well as foreigners, and covers passengers whose aircraft stopped at a DRC airport even if they did not leave the plane. Affected travellers must wait until 21 days have passed since they left the DRC. The US Embassy in Kinshasa announced the measure on social media.

Why 21 days

The period matches the maximum incubation time of the Ebola virus.

Background

The restriction comes amid an Ebola outbreak in Central Africa. Nigeria is currently monitoring contacts of one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, and says no further cases have been confirmed.

Suspended Reform UK councillor convicted over post saying Nigerians should be 'melted down'

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Glenn Gibbins, a suspended Reform UK councillor, has been found guilty of sending a malicious communication after a Facebook post saying Nigerians should be melted down and used to fill potholes.

Read more

A suspended Reform UK councillor in England has been convicted over a social media post about Nigerians.

The case

Glenn Gibbins, 66, was found guilty at South Tyneside Magistrates' Court of sending a malicious communication, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. In a Facebook post, he had complained about the number of Nigerians in the town and said they should be melted down and used to fill potholes. He told the court on Monday that the post was 'naughty' but that he did not mean it literally. The prosecutor said the comment conjured up despicable images and implied that Nigerians were less than human.

How it came to light

The post was found by the anti-racism group Hope Not Hate when it reviewed Gibbins's social media after he was announced as a Reform UK candidate for Sunderland City Council. He was later suspended by the party.

Background

Reform UK is led by Nigel Farage. Nigerians make up one of the largest African communities in the UK.

Paul McCartney becomes a billionaire, third-richest musician in the world

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Former Beatle Paul McCartney is now worth about $1.4 billion, according to Forbes, making him the world's third-richest musician after Jay-Z and Taylor Swift.

Read more

Paul McCartney has officially become a billionaire.

The figures

Forbes now values the former Beatles member's fortune at about $1.4 billion, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, making him the third-richest musician in the world. Only rapper and businessman Jay-Z, at about $2.8 billion, and Taylor Swift, at about $2 billion, are ranked higher.

How he made it

Forbes attributes his wealth to six decades of songwriting, shrewd acquisitions of music rights and personal assets. Despite their global fame in the 1960s, The Beatles earned relatively little from their early record contracts, something McCartney has spoken about in the past.

Background

McCartney co-wrote many of The Beatles' most famous songs with John Lennon and went on to a hugely successful solo career and with the band Wings.
