UK drops plan to make care workers wait 15 years for settlement

2 Nov 2026, 18:01 GMT

The UK government has reportedly dropped a plan that would have required some migrant care workers already in Britain to wait 15 years before they could settle permanently, a change that affects many Nigerians working in the sector.

What changed

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood told the House of Commons on Monday, 2 November 2026, that care workers who joined the social care visa route before May 2025 and earn at least £25,000 a year in the sector will keep a five-year route to indefinite leave to remain. She also scrapped the proposed 15-year wait for other lower-paid workers, replacing it with a 10-year route.

The new rules

Mahmood said settlement will now be more closely linked to a person's contribution, adding that the right to live in Britain must be earned. A five-year route will remain for people earning more than £50,000 a year and for qualifying workers in health and education. People earning more than £125,000 could settle in three years. Most other migrants, including those earning under £50,000, would face a 10-year route, while being able to renew their temporary visas.

Why the plan was changed

The reversal followed opposition from Labour MPs, trade unions and migrant groups, who argued it was unfair to change the rules for people who came to Britain under different conditions.

What it means for Nigerians

Nigerians are among the largest groups of overseas care workers recruited to the UK in recent years. Indefinite leave to remain allows a person to live, work and study in Britain without time limits and claim benefits where eligible, and is usually a step towards citizenship.

  • Most other lower earners face a 10-year route.
  • High earners above £125,000 can settle in three years.

At least 30 killed as fuel tanker overturns and bursts into flames in Cairo

26 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

At least 30 people died and 15 were injured when a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday. The blaze engulfed 27 vehicles.

Read more

At least 30 people have died and 15 others were injured after a fuel tanker overturned and burst into flames on a busy stretch of the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday, 25 November 2026.

What happened

According to Egypt's Ministry of Health and Population, the fire broke out above Tunnel 10 in the densely populated Al-Warraq area of Imbaba, a suburb of Greater Cairo. Preliminary findings suggest the privately operated tanker swerved, hit a concrete road divider and toppled over, causing an explosion that spread to 27 nearby vehicles.

The response

Authorities sent 39 ambulances to the scene, taking survivors to three hospitals in the area. Firefighters brought the blaze under control, and heavy machinery was brought in to clear the burnt-out vehicles.

Condolences

Egyptian President Abdel-Fattah el-Sisi and Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly sent condolences to the families of the victims.

Puerto Rico's Jennifer Barreto has made history as the first mother to win the Miss Universe crown.

The final

The 27-year-old was crowned Miss Universe 2026 on Tuesday, 24 November, at the grand finale of the pageant's 75th edition, held at the José Miguel Agrelot Coliseum in San Juan, Puerto Rico. She beat contestants from 115 other countries.

A historic win

Barreto is married and has a seven-year-old daughter, making her the first mother and the first married woman to win the title. Her victory also gives Puerto Rico its sixth Miss Universe crown, ending a 20-year wait.

The winning answer

In the final question round, she was asked what human value should never be lost as artificial intelligence grows. She stressed using AI responsibly and keeping human values alive through consistent actions.

Report records 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in four months

26 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

A civic group says it has documented 124 incidents of election-related violence and 22 deaths across Nigeria since July. It warned the trend could worsen as the January 2027 elections approach.

Read more

A non-partisan civic organisation has warned of rising political violence in Nigeria, saying it recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in the past four months.

The figures

In a statement in Abuja on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, the Kimpact Development Initiative (KDI) said the incidents were documented between 1 July and 23 November 2026 through its election violence monitoring project. Incidents rose from three in July to 48 in October, with another 40 recorded in the first half of November alone.

The warning

The group warned that the trend could worsen as the 16 January 2027 elections draw closer unless political parties, security agencies and other stakeholders take urgent action. It pointed to growing political intolerance and inflammatory rhetoric among politicians and their supporters as key triggers.

KDI stressed that the problem cuts across parties and should not be blamed on any single group, and urged candidates to restrain their supporters.

Background

The report comes days after deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states, and after INEC warned that the popular 'rig and die' slogan could endanger polling staff and voters. The House of Representatives has also warned that a state of emergency may be needed in Delta if violence continues.
