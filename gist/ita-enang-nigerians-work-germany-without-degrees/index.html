Nigerians with trade skills can work in Germany without degrees, says ambassador Ita Enang

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Nigeria's ambassador to Germany, Senator Ita Enang, says Nigerians with vocational and technical skills can find work in Germany even without a university degree.

What he said

Speaking in a television interview on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Enang reportedly urged Nigerians to look at opportunities in jobs such as driving, hospitality, woodwork, welding and construction. He said many people overlook these options because they assume employers in Europe only want graduates. He said one concern since he took office was the small number of Nigerians he found working in Germany, although some hold senior positions.

The challenges

Enang said getting a visa is one of the main hurdles, and advised people to learn German and obtain the qualifications needed for their chosen trade. He said he was learning German himself to better understand the country's institutions, and praised Germans for the importance they attach to their language.

Background

Germany faces shortages of skilled workers in many sectors and has opened new routes for foreign workers in recent years, including for people with vocational training. Many young Nigerians are looking for opportunities abroad because of unemployment and high living costs at home. The government has also opened applications for its free vocational training programme, which offers courses in trades such as solar installation and plumbing.

Why it matters

Experts say skilled trades are increasingly in demand in Europe, and that proper certification and language skills can open doors for Nigerians who do not have degrees.

  • He advised Nigerians to learn German.
  • Visas remain a major hurdle.

Tinubu asks Senate to decriminalise attempted suicide

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

President Tinubu has sent a bill to the Senate that would remove criminal penalties for attempted suicide and replace them with medical and psychological support. The National Mental Health Amendment Bill was read during plenary.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has reportedly asked the Senate to decriminalise attempted suicide and put in place medical and psychological support for survivors instead of punishment.

The bill

The proposal is contained in the National Mental Health Amendment Bill, 2026, which Senate President Godswill Akpabio read during plenary on Tuesday, 17 November 2026. It seeks to amend the National Mental Health Act of 2021 by removing criminal liability for attempted suicide, which currently attracts prison terms under Nigeria's Criminal and Penal Codes, and replacing it with a public health approach.

In his letter to the Senate, the President urged lawmakers to give the bill quick consideration, saying it was designed to decriminalise attempted suicide and support anyone who tries to take their own life.

Why it matters

If passed, the law would treat attempted suicide as a health issue requiring care and treatment rather than a crime.

Getting help

Anyone who is struggling or thinking about suicide is encouraged to speak to someone they trust or contact a health professional or local support service.

Tinubu announces home loans of up to ₦100m at 9.75% interest over 20 years

17 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Tinubu says eligible Nigerians can get home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed 9.75% interest rate, repayable over 20 years. Buyers must pay at least 10% of the property's value upfront.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has announced that eligible Nigerians can now access home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed interest rate of 9.75%, repayable over 20 years.

How it works

In a post on his X account on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the President reportedly said the loans are available through the ₦1 trillion MOFI Real Estate Investment Fund (MREIF). Applicants must pay a deposit of at least 10% of the property's value to qualify.

Using a ₦10 million home as an example, he said the buyer would pay ₦1 million upfront and then about ₦85,400 a month over the 20 years.

Extra options

He added that workers in the Contributory Pension Scheme can use up to 25% of their retirement savings towards the deposit, while married couples can apply jointly to qualify for bigger loans.

What happens next

Full eligibility rules and the application process were not detailed in the reports.

Nigeria's Minister of Defence, Christopher Musa, has reportedly said the United States is providing the Nigerian military with satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help locate militant leaders ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What the minister said

Speaking to journalists on Monday, 16 November 2026, Musa said troops had killed a number of bandit and terrorist commanders in overnight operations across the north-west and north-east. He described the operations as happening almost daily and said they were backed by much better intelligence.

Enugu Assembly passes bill limiting rent increases

17 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Enugu State House of Assembly has passed a Landlord and Tenant Amendment Bill that reportedly allows rent increases only after four years, with the increase capped. A lawmaker said it would cut what residents pay to secure accommodation by over 60%.

Read more

The Enugu State House of Assembly has passed the Landlord and Tenant Amendment Bill, which is meant to protect tenants from sudden and steep rent increases.

What happened

The bill passed its third reading on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, after lawmakers considered a report by a joint committee drawn from the House committees on housing, public petitions, ethics and the judiciary.

The committee chairman, Okey Mbah, who represents Nkanu East, said the report followed wide consultation with stakeholders and that the law would end the exploitation of tenants. He said it would cut more than 60% of what residents typically pay when securing accommodation in the state.

What the bill says

Under the bill, rent can reportedly only be raised after four years, and any increase is capped at 2%.

What happens next

The bill is expected to go to the governor for assent before it becomes law.
