Naira Marley announces Marlians Fest comeback for 12 December

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

Singer Naira Marley has announced that his Marlians Fest concert is returning this December.

What he announced

In a long post on his social media pages on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the Marlian Records founder said the show will hold on 12 December at the Eagles Club Field in Surulere, Lagos, as part of the 'Detty December' season. He spoke about a difficult period in his life without going into detail, saying rumours and negative stories had circulated about him and that he had deliberately stepped back to find peace and heal. He described the concert as an event about growth, healing and moving forward, and dedicated it partly to everyone 'we have lost along the way'. He said friends would join him on stage and thanked supporters who stood by him.

Background

Naira Marley became one of Nigeria's most popular street-pop stars with songs such as 'Soapy' and 'Issa Goal'. He faced intense public criticism after the death of his former signee, Mohbad, in September 2023, when questions were raised about Mohbad's earlier complaints against the label. Naira Marley was arrested and questioned by police over the death and was later released; he has denied any wrongdoing, and no charges against him have been reported in connection with Mohbad's death. A coroner's inquest was held into the circumstances. This week, VeryDarkMan has also revived discussion of the Mohbad case during his feud with Davido, while Davido's aide denied any link between the singer and Naira Marley's troubles.

How people reacted

Davido was among those who liked the announcement on Instagram. Fans were divided, with some welcoming his return and others saying the wounds from Mohbad's death were still fresh.

Nigerian singer Temilade Openiyi, known as Tems, has opened up about living with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and how it affects her relationships with family and friends.

What she said

In a radio interview in Lagos, the singer said keeping in touch with people is something she finds difficult, even though she has many supportive people around her.

She explained that ADHD sometimes makes her switch topics suddenly during conversations, and that she often forgets to reach out to people. She admitted she has not always been consistent in communicating with people who matter to her, but said she is trying.

US corrections officer accused of inviting another man home, then calling him an intruder and firing gun when boyfriend returned

20 Nov 2026, 09:20 GMT

Lasacia Gore, a 34-year-old corrections officer in Michigan, has been charged with six felonies. Prosecutors say she invited another man to her home, then reported him as an intruder and fired a gun when her boyfriend came back unexpectedly. She has pleaded not guilty.

Read more

A 34-year-old woman in Michigan, United States, has been charged with six felonies after police say she invited another man to her home while her boyfriend was away, then reported him as an intruder when her partner returned unexpectedly.

The 7am phone call

According to prosecutors, Lasacia Gore, a corrections officer with Wayne County, called police at around 7am on Monday, claiming an unknown man had broken into her home in Eastpointe and was fighting with her boyfriend.

But investigators say they found that she had invited the man over herself, and had even left the door unlocked for him.

What police say happened

According to police, things turned violent when her boyfriend came home unexpectedly, found the visitor in the house and confronted him, leading to a fight.

Eastpointe Police Chief Corey Haines said Gore fired a gun while both men were wrestling on the kitchen floor. The bullet missed them and struck the wall above them.

"You told me to leave the door open"

Macomb County Prosecutor Pete Lucido said the visitor's account contradicted hers. "She's making a call saying she doesn't know him and this and that, and he says, 'What are you talking about? You're the one who told me to leave the door open and I'm coming in!'" he said.

"It's crazy. This could have been very tragic and somebody could've died," Lucido added.

Chief Haines also expressed concern that a corrections officer was allegedly involved in making a false police report and firing a weapon.

The charges

Gore faces six felony charges, including assault with a dangerous weapon, discharging a firearm at a building, filing a false felony report, lying to investigators and two firearm-related counts.

She pleaded not guilty at her arraignment on Wednesday. Her bond was set at $30,000, with a 10 per cent option. If released, she must stay at home under GPS monitoring, surrender her weapons and avoid contact with the complainant.

She is due back in court on 2 December for a probable cause conference. The charges are allegations and have not been proven in court.
