Ebonyi women parade man in wheelbarrow over alleged repeated beating of his wife

7 Nov 2026, 18:58 GMT

Women in Ishiagu, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly paraded a man around the community in a wheelbarrow over claims that he repeatedly beat his wife. The unusual punishment has sparked debate about domestic violence and community justice.

Women in Ishiagu community, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly taken an unusual step to show their anger over domestic violence, publicly parading a man accused of repeatedly beating his wife.

Pushed around in a wheelbarrow

According to reports circulating online, the women placed the man in a wheelbarrow and pushed him around the community, apparently determined to make an example of him over his alleged treatment of his wife.

Photos show a large group of women surrounding the man, who sits in the wheelbarrow in a yellow T-shirt as several women push and guide it along a dirt path, with one carrying a calabash.

"Enough is enough"

The incident has drawn attention because of the unusual punishment the women chose. They appeared to have reached a point where they could no longer tolerate the alleged abuse.

In many communities, domestic violence is still treated as a private family matter, and victims are sometimes encouraged to endure abuse for the sake of their marriage. The women of Ishiagu reportedly decided that enough was enough.

Justice or humiliation?

Their action raises an important question about how communities should respond when a woman is repeatedly subjected to violence in her own home.

Many people may applaud the women for standing up against domestic abuse. Others may question whether publicly humiliating an accused person, who has not been tried, is the right approach.

The full circumstances of the incident, including the man's side of the story and whether the authorities have stepped in, remain unclear.

The body of Joseph Owusu, a 24-year-old Ghanaian church organist from the Bronx, was found in a large black bag left near a trash can on a Brooklyn sidewalk. CCTV footage shows two people taking the bag from a white car. Ghana's government has demanded justice.

The Ghanaian community in the United States has been thrown into mourning again following the disturbing discovery of the body of 24-year-old Joseph Owusu, a talented church musician who lived in the Bronx, New York.

Found in a bag on the sidewalk

Owusu's body was discovered inside a large black bag left near a trash can on a sidewalk in Brooklyn on 30 October 2026.

CCTV footage shows two unidentified people taking the bag out of a white car, leaving it by the roadside and driving away.

Who was Joseph Owusu?

The young man was the organist at the Ghana United Methodist Church in the Bronx. He was also studying computer science and worked as a caregiver for people living with disabilities.

A family demands answers

His devastated family is demanding justice as investigators work to establish how he died and who abandoned his body. Ghana's embassy in the United States has confirmed his death, and the Ghanaian government has called on US authorities to ensure those responsible are found and brought to justice.

Two young lives in days

The tragedy came just a day after another young Ghanaian, 15-year-old Javits Frimpong, was shot dead while walking to school in the Bronx.

Two young Ghanaians, two heartbreaking deaths in New York within days. Both families are now waiting for answers.

The viral video

The suspects were made to lie face down on the ground in a single line by the side of a road, with each person's face pressed against the backside of the man in front of him. Their hands appear to be tied behind them.

The incident has sparked mixed reactions online. Many people found the punishment hilarious, joking that South Africans seem to have invented a brand-new style of discipline.

Amnesty International condemns Zamfara killings, says burning of 12 family members alive exposes failure of Nigerian authorities

8 Nov 2026, 21:28 GMT

Amnesty International has condemned the reported killing of 12 members of one family in Zamfara State, who were said to have been burned alive, calling it another sign of the authorities' failure to protect communities from armed attacks.

Read more

Amnesty International has strongly condemned the reported killing of 12 members of a family in Zamfara State, describing the horrifying incident as another painful reminder of the Nigerian authorities' failure to protect innocent citizens from violent attacks.

An entire family wiped out

Imagine an entire family wiped out in such a cruel way. Twelve human beings, reportedly burned alive. People who had their own dreams, responsibilities and hopes for tomorrow, only to have their lives cut short in circumstances no family should ever experience.

The tragedy has once again raised serious questions about the worsening security situation in parts of northern Nigeria, where many families live under the constant threat of armed attacks, kidnappings and killings.

A failure to protect

For Amnesty International, the incident reflects a much bigger problem: the continued inability of the authorities to guarantee the safety of communities that have suffered attack after attack.

For years, residents of Zamfara and neighbouring states have endured violence from armed groups. Villages have been raided, homes destroyed, residents abducted and families forced to abandon communities where they have lived for generations.

Despite repeated assurances from government officials and security agencies, reports of fresh atrocities keep emerging.

Every citizen deserves protection

The Nigerian government has a constitutional duty to protect lives and property. That duty extends to every citizen, whether they live in Abuja, Lagos, Zamfara or a remote village far from the attention of the national media.

The victims deserve justice, and their surviving relatives deserve more than another round of official condolences. There must be a thorough investigation, those responsible must be brought to justice, and the authorities must take meaningful steps to prevent similar tragedies.

A country cannot claim to be making progress when families are being burned alive in their homes and communities are left wondering who will be attacked next.
