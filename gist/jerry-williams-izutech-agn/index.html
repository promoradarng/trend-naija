Concern grows over Nollywood actor Jerry Williams as producer Izutech calls out Actors Guild of Nigeria over alleged neglect

27 Oct 2026, 15:44 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the welfare of popular Nollywood actor Jerry Williams after filmmaker and producer Izutech publicly questioned the Actors Guild of Nigeria (AGN) over its alleged failure to provide sustained support for the troubled actor.

According to Izutech, the Actors Guild of Nigeria had previously promised to take responsibility for Jerry Williams' situation, but he is now questioning what became of that commitment.

The producer alleged that the once-popular Nollywood star is facing serious personal challenges, including suspected substance-related problems and concerns about his mental health.

Izutech further claimed that Jerry Williams has allegedly been sending private messages to people on Instagram, appealing for financial assistance to buy food and meet his basic needs.

The claims have raised concerns about the actor's welfare, particularly among those who remember his performances in several Nollywood productions.

Expressing his disappointment, Izutech questioned why an actor who contributed to Nigeria's entertainment industry should allegedly be left struggling without adequate support.

He also disclosed his willingness to intervene again by arranging a rehabilitation programme lasting up to two years, which he believes could help the actor recover and rebuild his life.

However, the producer admitted that such an intervention would require considerable financial resources and appealed for support from colleagues, industry stakeholders and well-meaning Nigerians.

The development has once again brought attention to the welfare of entertainers in Nollywood, especially actors facing health challenges, financial difficulties or personal crises after years in the spotlight.

It also raises questions about the responsibility of professional associations to support members experiencing serious difficulties.

While Izutech's allegations have attracted concern, the actor's current medical condition and the extent of any assistance provided by the Actors Guild of Nigeria have not been independently verified.

The bigger question remains: what support systems are available to Nollywood actors when they can no longer cope with life's challenges?

For many fans, the hope is that Jerry Williams receives appropriate professional care, genuine support and an opportunity to regain his health and stability.

Should the Actors Guild of Nigeria establish a dedicated welfare and rehabilitation fund to support actors facing serious personal and health challenges?

Nollywood actress and evangelist Tonto Dikeh has urged Nigerians to drop negative assumptions about wealthy people.

What she said

Speaking during a recent ministration, in a video shared on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Dikeh challenged popular stereotypes that link wealth to wrongdoing. She reportedly said that not every rich man is a ritualist and not every rich woman sleeps with men to get money, adding that some people work hard for many years before their efforts pay off. She acknowledged that some people do acquire wealth through questionable means, but said it is unfair to dismiss the genuine success of others.

Her message

Dikeh encouraged people to change their mindset and appreciate the sacrifices, struggles and commitment that many successful people make to build their businesses and careers. She said patience, perseverance and honest work remain real paths to financial stability.

Background

Tonto Dikeh rose to fame as an actress and has also been a singer and politician, running as a deputy governorship candidate in Rivers State in 2023. In recent years she has become more active in Christian ministry and often shares sermons and motivational messages online. Suspicion that sudden wealth comes from rituals or fraud is common in Nigerian public conversation, and is often fuelled by stories of so-called money rituals in the media.

How people reacted

Many fans agreed with her and said hard-working people are often wrongly judged, while others said the stereotypes exist because of real cases of people getting rich through crime.

Sola Sobowale opens care home for older people in Ibadan, Makinde commissions it

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Veteran actress Sola Sobowale has opened Joseph and Esther Homes, a residential care facility for older adults in Akobo, Ibadan. Governor Seyi Makinde commissioned the home, with Nollywood stars in attendance.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actress Sola Sobowale has opened a residential care home for older people in Ibadan, Oyo State.

The opening

Oyo governor Seyi Makinde formally commissioned the facility, named Joseph and Esther Homes, on Tuesday, 3 November 2026. Located in the Akobo area of Ibadan, it is designed to give older adults a safe and supportive place where they can live independently while receiving professional care. The governor toured the indoor and outdoor areas and cut the ribbon.

The idea behind it

The home operates under the motto 'Live Well, Age Well, Thrive Together'. Its organisers say it is meant to complement family care rather than replace it, helping families look after their elderly relatives better. Formal care homes are still uncommon in Nigeria, where older people have traditionally been cared for at home by relatives.

Who attended

Sobowale welcomed guests alongside veteran actor Adebayo Salami. Other Nollywood figures at the event included Kunle Afolayan, Femi Adebayo and Ibrahim Chatta. The launch was streamed live on the actress's Instagram page.

Background

Sola Sobowale is one of Nollywood's most respected actresses, known for her role in the 'King of Boys' films and decades of work in Yoruba and English-language cinema.

How people reacted

Fans praised the project, saying it addresses a growing need as more young Nigerians move to cities or abroad, leaving ageing parents behind.
