Concern grows over Nollywood actor Jerry Williams as producer Izutech calls out Actors Guild of Nigeria over alleged neglect

27 Oct 2026, 15:44 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the welfare of popular Nollywood actor Jerry Williams after filmmaker and producer Izutech publicly questioned the Actors Guild of Nigeria (AGN) over its alleged failure to provide sustained support for the troubled actor.

According to Izutech, the Actors Guild of Nigeria had previously promised to take responsibility for Jerry Williams' situation, but he is now questioning what became of that commitment.

The producer alleged that the once-popular Nollywood star is facing serious personal challenges, including suspected substance-related problems and concerns about his mental health.

Izutech further claimed that Jerry Williams has allegedly been sending private messages to people on Instagram, appealing for financial assistance to buy food and meet his basic needs.

The claims have raised concerns about the actor's welfare, particularly among those who remember his performances in several Nollywood productions.

Expressing his disappointment, Izutech questioned why an actor who contributed to Nigeria's entertainment industry should allegedly be left struggling without adequate support.

He also disclosed his willingness to intervene again by arranging a rehabilitation programme lasting up to two years, which he believes could help the actor recover and rebuild his life.

However, the producer admitted that such an intervention would require considerable financial resources and appealed for support from colleagues, industry stakeholders and well-meaning Nigerians.

The development has once again brought attention to the welfare of entertainers in Nollywood, especially actors facing health challenges, financial difficulties or personal crises after years in the spotlight.

It also raises questions about the responsibility of professional associations to support members experiencing serious difficulties.

While Izutech's allegations have attracted concern, the actor's current medical condition and the extent of any assistance provided by the Actors Guild of Nigeria have not been independently verified.

The bigger question remains: what support systems are available to Nollywood actors when they can no longer cope with life's challenges?

For many fans, the hope is that Jerry Williams receives appropriate professional care, genuine support and an opportunity to regain his health and stability.

Should the Actors Guild of Nigeria establish a dedicated welfare and rehabilitation fund to support actors facing serious personal and health challenges?

Media personality Chude Jideonwo has paid tribute to the late Nollywood legend Pete Edochie with a memorable story about a visit to the actor's home in Enugu.

The cow

In an Instagram post, Jideonwo recalled arriving late at Edochie's residence to conduct a special interview. He said the veteran actor quickly pointed out that he had come to the home of a titled man empty-handed and asked for a cow. Jideonwo said the cow was indeed delivered.

His tribute

Jideonwo described Edochie as a proud man without arrogance, who commanded any room he entered and never felt threatened by other people. He said the actor's death had made him reflect on an older Nigerian identity rooted in culture and dignity.

Background

Pete Edochie, one of Nigeria's most celebrated actors and broadcasters, died on 25 November 2026 at the age of 79 in Enugu. Tributes have continued to pour in from across the entertainment industry, including from Patience Ozokwo and his family.

Omotola Jalade opens up on difficult bond with late mother and Nollywood blacklist

28 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Veteran actress Omotola Jalade-Ekeinde has spoken about her strained relationship with her late mother and her regrets since her death. She also said she was once blacklisted in Nollywood for demanding better standards.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actress Omotola Jalade-Ekeinde has spoken openly about her complicated relationship with her late mother and about being blacklisted in the film industry early in her career.

A difficult bond

In an interview released on Friday, 27 November 2026, Omotola said she and her mother did not always get along. Her mother worried that the film industry would expose her daughter to immorality, and the tension grew when Omotola began dating her husband, Captain Matthew Ekeinde. When he visited to formally ask for her hand, her mother reportedly questioned whether her daughter was ready for marriage.

Omotola said the relationship later improved and the two became much closer. She admitted that she still has regrets about the care and support she never got to show her mother, and said she now finds herself giving that care to other people's mothers. She added that the experience shaped her decision to make her directorial debut.

'Troublesome Eight'

Turning to her career, the actress said she was blacklisted after pushing for better standards and professionalism in Nollywood. She said she was one of a group of actors labelled the 'Troublesome Eight' because they demanded improvements. She also said she had a manager as far back as the 1990s, when professional management was not widely understood in the industry.

Motherhood

The mother of four also spoke about her approach to parenting and the lessons motherhood has taught her.
