Concern grows over Nollywood actor Jerry Williams as producer Izutech calls out Actors Guild of Nigeria over alleged neglect

27 Oct 2026, 15:44 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the welfare of popular Nollywood actor Jerry Williams after filmmaker and producer Izutech publicly questioned the Actors Guild of Nigeria (AGN) over its alleged failure to provide sustained support for the troubled actor.

According to Izutech, the Actors Guild of Nigeria had previously promised to take responsibility for Jerry Williams' situation, but he is now questioning what became of that commitment.

The producer alleged that the once-popular Nollywood star is facing serious personal challenges, including suspected substance-related problems and concerns about his mental health.

Izutech further claimed that Jerry Williams has allegedly been sending private messages to people on Instagram, appealing for financial assistance to buy food and meet his basic needs.

The claims have raised concerns about the actor's welfare, particularly among those who remember his performances in several Nollywood productions.

Expressing his disappointment, Izutech questioned why an actor who contributed to Nigeria's entertainment industry should allegedly be left struggling without adequate support.

He also disclosed his willingness to intervene again by arranging a rehabilitation programme lasting up to two years, which he believes could help the actor recover and rebuild his life.

However, the producer admitted that such an intervention would require considerable financial resources and appealed for support from colleagues, industry stakeholders and well-meaning Nigerians.

The development has once again brought attention to the welfare of entertainers in Nollywood, especially actors facing health challenges, financial difficulties or personal crises after years in the spotlight.

It also raises questions about the responsibility of professional associations to support members experiencing serious difficulties.

While Izutech's allegations have attracted concern, the actor's current medical condition and the extent of any assistance provided by the Actors Guild of Nigeria have not been independently verified.

The bigger question remains: what support systems are available to Nollywood actors when they can no longer cope with life's challenges?

For many fans, the hope is that Jerry Williams receives appropriate professional care, genuine support and an opportunity to regain his health and stability.

Should the Actors Guild of Nigeria establish a dedicated welfare and rehabilitation fund to support actors facing serious personal and health challenges?

NDC boycotts IGP's security meeting, says its members are not safe

24 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Nigeria Democratic Congress has reportedly refused to attend a security meeting called by the Inspector-General of Police, accusing security agencies of failing to protect its members from attacks. It blamed thugs it said were loyal to the APC.

Read more

The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has announced that it will boycott a security meeting convened by the Inspector-General of Police, citing attacks on its members ahead of the 2027 elections.

The party's reasons

In a statement on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, signed by its National Publicity Secretary, the party said it no longer felt safe under the Nigeria Police Force and would not lend legitimacy to a security process that had failed to protect its members.

The NDC accused security agencies of failing to prevent repeated attacks it alleged were carried out by thugs loyal to the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC). It pointed to incidents in Benue, Edo, Cross River, Enugu and Delta states, including what it described as an attempt to stop its presidential candidate, Peter Obi, from entering Benue State.

The Delta attack

The boycott follows Monday's deadly attack on the campaign team of the party's Delta Central senatorial candidate, Ovie Omo-Agege, in Ughelli North. The party alleged that security personnel were complicit. Police have since invited Omo-Agege and others for questioning as part of their investigation.

The other side

No response from the APC or the police to the NDC's allegations was included in the reports.

Court voids APC's Bayelsa West senatorial candidacy

24 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly voided Fidelis Agbiki's candidacy as the APC's Bayelsa West senatorial candidate. The court upheld claims that his name was missing from the party's membership register.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly nullified the candidacy of High Chief Fidelis Agbiki, the All Progressives Congress (APC) senatorial candidate for Bayelsa West in the 2027 elections.

The case

The petitioners raised two main objections to his candidacy. First, they argued that his name did not appear in the APC membership register submitted to INEC, meaning he was not a recognised member of the party. Second, they claimed he did not resign from a political appointment before taking part in the senatorial primary.

The ruling

Justice Mohammed Garba Umar upheld the petition, finding that the primary breached the Electoral Act.

Background

The judgment came on the same day a Federal High Court in Warri voided the NDC candidacy of former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege for Delta Central on similar grounds relating to membership registers. Pre-election court cases are common in Nigeria, and both rulings can be appealed.
