Dangote cuts petrol depot price to ₦1,290 as other marketers also reduce rates

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Petrol depot prices are falling across Nigeria, with the Dangote Refinery now selling at the lowest rate among major depots in Lagos.

The new prices

According to depot price data for Thursday, 29 October 2026, Dangote reduced its petrol price from ₦1,308 to ₦1,290 per litre, a cut of ₦18. Other Lagos depots followed with smaller reductions: Pinnacle and Rainoil now sell at about ₦1,300, African Terminal at about ₦1,307, and MRS and NIPCO at about ₦1,310. In Port Harcourt, several depots cut their prices to about ₦1,325, while depots in Calabar sell at between ₦1,330 and ₦1,335.

Diesel prices

The petrol cut came shortly after Dangote raised its diesel gantry price to ₦1,840 per litre. Some Lagos depots have since undercut that price, selling diesel at about ₦1,825.

What it means for motorists

Depot prices are what marketers pay before transporting fuel to filling stations, so the cuts could lead to slightly lower pump prices in the coming days if retailers pass on the savings. However, pump prices also depend on transport costs and each marketer's margins, so the effect may vary from place to place.

Background

Since petrol subsidies were removed in 2023, prices have been set by the market and change often in response to crude oil prices and the exchange rate. The Dangote Refinery, which began selling petrol in 2024, has become a major influence on local prices. Cooking gas and diesel prices have also been rising, putting pressure on households and businesses.

Several killed as commercial bus catches fire on Lagos-Badagry Expressway

26 Nov 2026, 14:41 GMT

Several passengers died and more than a dozen were injured when a commercial bus burst into flames at Magbon on the Lagos-Badagry Expressway on Thursday morning. Officials gave death tolls of six and eight.

Read more

Several people were killed and others injured when a commercial bus caught fire on the Lagos-Badagry Expressway on Thursday morning, 26 November 2026.

What happened

The incident happened near the Checkpoint area, inward Magbon, between 8am and 9am. The bus, travelling from Mile 2 towards Badagry, reportedly suffered brake failure, and the driver lost control. The vehicle somersaulted and burst into flames, trapping passengers inside.

The toll

Officials gave different figures. The Lagos State Fire and Rescue Service put the death toll at six adults, two women and four men, with 12 others injured. The Lagos State Traffic Management Authority (LASTMA) said eight passengers died and 13 suffered severe burns. The injured were taken to Badagry General Hospital.

Driver fled

The LASTMA general manager said the driver fled the scene after the crash, leaving passengers trapped, and offered condolences to the families of the victims. Traffic and road safety officers were deployed to the scene.

Dangote Refinery cuts diesel price by ₦125 per litre

26 Nov 2026, 14:41 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reduced its diesel gantry price from ₦1,720 to ₦1,595 per litre, effective Thursday. It comes days after it suspended a planned ₦150 cut, and ahead of its stock market listing.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery has reduced the gantry price of diesel by ₦125 per litre nationwide.

The new price

The cut, effective from Thursday, 26 November 2026, lowers the price at the refinery from ₦1,720 to ₦1,595 per litre, a reduction of about 7%. The refinery said the change would reduce energy and haulage costs for manufacturers, transporters and marketers, and that customers' accounts would be credited accordingly.

Background

The refinery had announced a ₦150 cut earlier this month but suspended it on 21 November pending an internal review. It has also cut its petrol price several times in recent weeks, amid growing competition with importers.

Stock market debut

The price cut comes ahead of the refinery's planned listing on the Nigerian Exchange on 1 December, after its oversubscribed public offering.

NERC warns DisCos not to charge customers for free meters

26 Nov 2026, 14:41 GMT

The electricity regulator has warned distribution companies not to charge customers for meters supplied under its ₦30 billion Meter Acquisition Fund. Both the meters and installation must be free.

Read more

The Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission (NERC) has warned electricity distribution companies (DisCos) not to charge customers for meters supplied under its ₦30 billion Meter Acquisition Fund.

The rules

In an order on the third tranche of the scheme, NERC said both the meters and their installation must be free for eligible customers. The tranche covers all 11 DisCos, prioritising unmetered Band A customers and extending to Band B customers.

Every meter supplied under the scheme must display the name of the relevant DisCo and the tranche of the fund it came from.

Sanctions

The regulator warned that DisCos could face penalties if their actions or failure to meet requirements delay installations.
