Dangote cuts petrol depot price to ₦1,290 as other marketers also reduce rates

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Petrol depot prices are falling across Nigeria, with the Dangote Refinery now selling at the lowest rate among major depots in Lagos.

The new prices

According to depot price data for Thursday, 29 October 2026, Dangote reduced its petrol price from ₦1,308 to ₦1,290 per litre, a cut of ₦18. Other Lagos depots followed with smaller reductions: Pinnacle and Rainoil now sell at about ₦1,300, African Terminal at about ₦1,307, and MRS and NIPCO at about ₦1,310. In Port Harcourt, several depots cut their prices to about ₦1,325, while depots in Calabar sell at between ₦1,330 and ₦1,335.

Diesel prices

The petrol cut came shortly after Dangote raised its diesel gantry price to ₦1,840 per litre. Some Lagos depots have since undercut that price, selling diesel at about ₦1,825.

What it means for motorists

Depot prices are what marketers pay before transporting fuel to filling stations, so the cuts could lead to slightly lower pump prices in the coming days if retailers pass on the savings. However, pump prices also depend on transport costs and each marketer's margins, so the effect may vary from place to place.

Background

Since petrol subsidies were removed in 2023, prices have been set by the market and change often in response to crude oil prices and the exchange rate. The Dangote Refinery, which began selling petrol in 2024, has become a major influence on local prices. Cooking gas and diesel prices have also been rising, putting pressure on households and businesses.

  • Other depots in Lagos, Port Harcourt and Calabar also cut prices.
  • Pump prices may ease if retailers pass on the savings.

Nigerian stock market loses ₦2.27 trillion in one day

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Investors lost about ₦2.27 trillion on the Nigerian Exchange on Tuesday as the All-Share Index fell 1.38%. Trading volume and deals also dropped.

Read more

The Nigerian stock market closed sharply lower on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, as sell-offs in major stocks wiped about ₦2.27 trillion off market value in a single trading session.

The numbers

Data from the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) showed the All-Share Index fell by 3,491.13 points, or 1.38%, to close at 249,318.65, down from 252,809.78 in the previous session. Total market capitalisation dropped by the same percentage, from ₦164.15 trillion to ₦161.88 trillion.

Trading activity

Activity also slowed. Investors traded 564.04 million shares, down 16.26% from 673.6 million in the previous session, while the number of deals fell by 18.91% to 30,992.

Bright spots

The losses were partly cushioned by buying interest in consumer goods and insurance stocks, with Cadbury Nigeria among the top gainers.

NCC gives MTN, Airtel and others until 31 December to use idle spectrum or lose it

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigerian Communications Commission has told telecom operators to prove they are using their allocated spectrum by 31 December 2026 or risk losing it. It wants better network quality for subscribers.

Read more

The Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) has given telecommunications operators, including MTN and Airtel, until 31 December 2026 to show they are effectively using their allocated spectrum or risk having it taken back.

What spectrum is

Spectrum refers to the radio frequencies operators use to carry calls, internet data and other wireless services. Because it is a limited national resource, the regulator wants to make sure allocations are supporting real network deployment.

Why the NCC is acting

The directive is aimed at ensuring valuable frequencies are not left idle while many Nigerians complain of poor network quality, congestion and limited broadband coverage.

What operators must do

Under the directive, operators must show that their assigned frequencies are being actively used through investment in network infrastructure and improved services. Unused spectrum could be reclaimed and reassigned.

The federal government has unveiled plans to build 167 compressed natural gas (CNG) refuelling stations across Nigeria, as part of efforts to make cheaper transport fuel more widely available and reduce dependence on petrol.

The plan

A presentation on the initiative reportedly highlighted a CNG price of ₦430, alongside plans to expand refuelling infrastructure and tackle challenges facing the sector.
