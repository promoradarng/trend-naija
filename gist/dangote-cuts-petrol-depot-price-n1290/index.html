Dangote cuts petrol depot price to ₦1,290 as other marketers also reduce rates

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Petrol depot prices are falling across Nigeria, with the Dangote Refinery now selling at the lowest rate among major depots in Lagos.

The new prices

According to depot price data for Thursday, 29 October 2026, Dangote reduced its petrol price from ₦1,308 to ₦1,290 per litre, a cut of ₦18. Other Lagos depots followed with smaller reductions: Pinnacle and Rainoil now sell at about ₦1,300, African Terminal at about ₦1,307, and MRS and NIPCO at about ₦1,310. In Port Harcourt, several depots cut their prices to about ₦1,325, while depots in Calabar sell at between ₦1,330 and ₦1,335.

Diesel prices

The petrol cut came shortly after Dangote raised its diesel gantry price to ₦1,840 per litre. Some Lagos depots have since undercut that price, selling diesel at about ₦1,825.

What it means for motorists

Depot prices are what marketers pay before transporting fuel to filling stations, so the cuts could lead to slightly lower pump prices in the coming days if retailers pass on the savings. However, pump prices also depend on transport costs and each marketer's margins, so the effect may vary from place to place.

Background

Since petrol subsidies were removed in 2023, prices have been set by the market and change often in response to crude oil prices and the exchange rate. The Dangote Refinery, which began selling petrol in 2024, has become a major influence on local prices. Cooking gas and diesel prices have also been rising, putting pressure on households and businesses.

FRSC says 19,236 Nigerians died in road crashes since 2023

10 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

The FRSC says 19,236 people died and 118,720 were injured in 37,789 road crashes across Nigeria between January 2023 and September 2026, with speeding and reckless driving the biggest killers.

Read more

The Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) says 19,236 Nigerians were killed in road crashes between January 2023 and September 2026.

The figures

The FRSC's Abia State Sector Commander, Ngozi Ezeoma, told journalists on Monday, 9 November 2026, that there were 37,789 road crashes in the period, in which 118,720 people were injured. She said the figures came from the FRSC's Road Traffic Database Management System, and that speeding and human recklessness remain the biggest killers on Nigerian roads.

Remembrance day

She spoke ahead of the World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, which the United Nations marks on the third Sunday of November. She said the command would visit crash victims in selected hospitals as part of the commemoration.

Background

Christmas return air tickets climb as high as ₦822,000 on some routes

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Return tickets on some domestic routes for the Christmas and New Year period have reached ₦822,000, with Air Peace listing about ₦676,000 for Lagos–Anambra return between 24 December and 2 January.

Read more

Nigerians planning to fly home for Christmas and the New Year are facing steep fares, with return tickets on some domestic routes reaching ₦822,000.

The fares

According to checks reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, major carriers including Air Peace, United Nigeria Airlines and ValueJet have listed much higher fares for December 2026 and early January 2027, especially on routes linking Lagos and Abuja to the South-East and South-South. Air Peace listed a Lagos–Anambra return ticket between 24 December and 2 January at about ₦676,000, with the outbound flight at ₦341,500 and the return at ₦335,500. An Abuja–Anambra return costs about ₦677,000, and Lagos–Owerri about ₦671,000. The highest return fares found reached ₦822,000.

Why fares rise

Demand for flights to the South-East and South-South surges every December as many Nigerians travel home for the holidays, weddings and other ceremonies. Airlines say the high cost of aviation fuel, foreign exchange for maintenance and leasing, and a shortage of aircraft push up prices.

Background

Last week, one-way economy fares for flights around 20 and 21 December were reported at up to ₦337,500, increases of as much as 208 per cent. The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission previously accused some airlines of price-fixing during the 2025 festive season, which the airlines denied. Many travellers turn to road transport instead, despite security concerns on some highways.

What it means

Travellers are being advised to book early to avoid the highest prices.

Woman confronts herder after cattle destroy her farm crops in Nasarawa State

10 Nov 2026, 00:44 GMT

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle strayed onto her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months growing. The incident has reopened the question of who pays when a farmer's livelihood is wiped out.

Read more

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle entered her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months cultivating.

Months of work gone

For someone who depends on farming to survive, watching cattle destroy months of hard work can be heartbreaking.

Imagine spending your money on seeds, preparing the land, planting and waiting patiently for harvest, only to wake up one day and find your crops destroyed. Worse still, many farmers borrow money to cultivate their land, hoping to repay the loan after harvest.

An old source of tension

Incidents like this remain one of the main causes of tension between farmers and herders in parts of Nigeria, and some have escalated into violence in the past.

The question is: who compensates the woman for her losses? Who takes responsibility for the destroyed crops?

Protection for everyone

Farmers deserve protection, just as herders have a right to carry out their lawful activities. But nobody should be allowed to destroy another person's livelihood without consequences.

Clear rules on grazing, quick ways to settle disputes and fair compensation could help prevent confrontations like this from turning into something worse.
