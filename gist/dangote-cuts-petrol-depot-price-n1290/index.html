Dangote cuts petrol depot price to ₦1,290 as other marketers also reduce rates

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Petrol depot prices are falling across Nigeria, with the Dangote Refinery now selling at the lowest rate among major depots in Lagos.

The new prices

According to depot price data for Thursday, 29 October 2026, Dangote reduced its petrol price from ₦1,308 to ₦1,290 per litre, a cut of ₦18. Other Lagos depots followed with smaller reductions: Pinnacle and Rainoil now sell at about ₦1,300, African Terminal at about ₦1,307, and MRS and NIPCO at about ₦1,310. In Port Harcourt, several depots cut their prices to about ₦1,325, while depots in Calabar sell at between ₦1,330 and ₦1,335.

Diesel prices

The petrol cut came shortly after Dangote raised its diesel gantry price to ₦1,840 per litre. Some Lagos depots have since undercut that price, selling diesel at about ₦1,825.

What it means for motorists

Depot prices are what marketers pay before transporting fuel to filling stations, so the cuts could lead to slightly lower pump prices in the coming days if retailers pass on the savings. However, pump prices also depend on transport costs and each marketer's margins, so the effect may vary from place to place.

Background

Since petrol subsidies were removed in 2023, prices have been set by the market and change often in response to crude oil prices and the exchange rate. The Dangote Refinery, which began selling petrol in 2024, has become a major influence on local prices. Cooking gas and diesel prices have also been rising, putting pressure on households and businesses.

Cooking gas prices rise; refilling 12.5kg now costs up to ₦22,500

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Cooking gas has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kg to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800 depending on location. Some households are cutting back or switching to other fuels ahead of Christmas.

Read more

Nigerian households are facing fresh pressure as cooking gas prices rise across major cities ahead of the Christmas season.

The new prices

The price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kilogram to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800, depending on location. That means refilling a 12.5kg cylinder can now cost as much as ₦22,500, up from about ₦17,500.

How households are coping

Market findings suggest some Nigerians are now buying smaller quantities, cutting down on how much gas they use or switching to other cooking fuels.

FG to release stored rice and maize to bring down food prices

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Agriculture Minister Abubakar Kyari says the government will release rice, maize and other grains from its stocks into the market to cut food prices. It also plans to distribute three million bags of fertiliser within a month.

Read more

The federal government has announced plans to release stored rice, maize and other grains into the market in a bid to bring down food prices.

What was announced

Minister of Agriculture and Food Security Abubakar Kyari disclosed the plan on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, at the presentation of the 2026 Wet Season Agricultural Performance Survey Report in Abuja.

He said grains bought under the Bank of Agriculture's Guaranteed Minimum Price programme would soon be released to the market.

Fertiliser for farmers

The government also plans to distribute three million bags of fertiliser to farmers within the next month to boost production and improve food supply across the country.

Background

The Guaranteed Minimum Price scheme gives farmers an assured buyer for their produce.

Naira weakens to ₦1,363 per dollar in official market

19 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The naira lost ₦7.43 against the dollar on Wednesday to close at ₦1,363.23 in the official market, and fell to ₦1,430 on the parallel market. Analysts point to reduced dollar inflows and rising demand.

Read more

The naira weakened against the US dollar in both the official and parallel foreign exchange markets on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, amid reduced foreign exchange inflows and rising demand for dollars.

Official market

Data from the Nigerian Autonomous Foreign Exchange Market (NAFEM) showed the naira fell by ₦7.43, or 0.54%, to close at ₦1,363.23 to the dollar, from ₦1,355.80 the previous day. It also lost ground against the euro, closing at ₦1,495.60, but gained slightly against the British pound to ₦1,795.24.

Parallel market

On the parallel market, the naira slipped by ₦10 to trade at ₦1,430 to the dollar, while at one major bank's foreign exchange desk it closed at ₦1,368.

Why it matters

The fall comes a day after the naira lost about ₦25 against the dollar, and as the Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC move to boost foreign exchange inflows.

FG selects eight startups for over ₦1 billion iDICE funding

19 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The federal government has named eight Nigerian startups to receive about ₦134 million each in equity investment under the iDICE programme. They will also get mentorship and business support.

Read more

The federal government has unveiled eight Nigerian startups selected to receive about ₦134 million each under the Investment in Digital and Creative Enterprises (iDICE) programme.

The funding

The companies will share a combined investment of more than ₦1 billion through Growth Lab, the second phase of the iDICE Startup Bridge initiative. Each startup will receive $100,000 in equity investment, along with business development support, mentorship and access to industry experts.

The aim

The programme is designed to help Nigerian entrepreneurs grow their businesses, create jobs and compete more effectively in the digital and creative economy, and to help the startups attract further investment.
