Oshiomhole says ₦70,000 transport allowance shows Tinubu cares about workers

6 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Senator Adams Oshiomhole has defended the federal government's ₦70,000 monthly transport allowance for workers, saying it doubles the pay of minimum wage earners. He also compared Tinubu's record with that of Atiku Abubakar.

Read more

Senator Adams Oshiomhole, who represents Edo North, has defended the federal government's decision to pay workers a ₦70,000 monthly transport allowance.

What he said

Speaking on a television politics programme on Thursday, 5 November 2026, the former Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC) president said the government was doing well. He argued that a worker earning ₦70,000 who receives an extra ₦70,000 would effectively see a 100 per cent increase, and that anyone who understands what small amounts mean to ordinary people would appreciate that President Bola Tinubu cares.

The allowance

The government approved the ₦70,000 monthly transport intervention for workers at all levels for six months from November 2026, after a meeting with the NLC on 27 October. As part of the same agreement, outstanding allowances are to be paid in instalments. Some labour voices have said workers need a permanent wage increase rather than short-term relief.

Comments on Atiku

Oshiomhole also looked ahead to the 2027 presidential election, which he described as a choice between Tinubu, whom he credited with unifying the exchange rate, and Atiku Abubakar of the ADC. He criticised the Obasanjo administration, in which Atiku was vice-president, saying it struggled to pay workers regularly and allowed multiple exchange rates.

How people reacted

Supporters of the government welcomed his comments, while critics said the allowance does little to offset high food and transport costs. The national minimum wage is currently ₦70,000 a month.

"I would have been worried if Amaechi stood a chance in Rivers State": Wike fires back at political rivals

6 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has dismissed Rotimi Amaechi's chances in Rivers State, insisting he remains a major force there even though he is no longer governor. He also claimed the APC barely existed in Rivers before he backed it, and that he has "killed the Obi wave" in the FCT.

Read more

The Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, has once again spoken boldly about his political influence in Rivers State, dismissing the chances of former Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi and insisting he remains a major force in the state.

"You cannot wish me away"

According to Wike, he would only have reason to worry if Amaechi stood a realistic chance of a significant political comeback in Rivers State.

The former Rivers governor made it clear that leaving Government House in Port Harcourt has not reduced his influence or weakened the structures he built over the years.

"I am not a governor, I am a minister, but you cannot wish me away in Rivers State, because the people know what I stand for and because of the people I have raised," Wike said.

His statement appears to be a direct message to those who believe his appointment as FCT Minister has taken him away from the centre of Rivers politics.

Claims about the APC

Wike also claimed that the All Progressives Congress (APC) had little political strength in Rivers State before he decided to support the party.

"Before I decided to support the APC, the party did not exist in Rivers State," he said.

That is a bold claim, given the long rivalry between Wike and Amaechi, who both served as governors of Rivers State at different times.

"I have killed the Obi wave"

Wike did not limit his remarks to Rivers. He also turned his attention to the movement around NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, claiming he had already weakened its influence in the FCT.

"As for the NDC, I have killed the Obi wave in the FCT," he said.

The remarks reflect Wike's confidence in his political strength, although his claims about the APC and about Obi's support remain his own assessment.

The road to 2027

With the 2027 general elections approaching, the political battle in Rivers State is expected to draw even more attention. Wike, Amaechi and other influential figures may once again find themselves on opposing sides as parties prepare for another contest.

For now, Wike is making one thing clear to his opponents: he may no longer be governor, but he has no intention of surrendering his influence in Rivers State.

Kenyan pastor sentenced to 200 years in prison over Shakahola starvation tragedy that claimed hundreds of lives

7 Nov 2026, 05:40 GMT

Kenyan preacher Paul Nthenge Mackenzie has reportedly been sentenced to 200 years in prison over the deaths of 191 members of his Good News International Church, including 190 children. He and 28 others were found guilty in October.

Read more

Kenyan preacher Paul Nthenge Mackenzie has reportedly been sentenced to 200 years in prison over the deaths of 191 members of his Good News International Church, including 190 children, in one of the most disturbing religious tragedies in Kenya's history.

The sentence

The Mombasa High Court handed down the sentence on Friday, 6 November 2026, following its ruling on 19 October in which Mackenzie and 28 other defendants were found guilty. The prosecution had earlier asked the court to impose the death penalty.

What happened in Shakahola

According to the prosecution, between 2021 and 2023 Mackenzie gathered his followers on a 325-hectare property in the Shakahola Forest, near the coastal town of Malindi in south-eastern Kenya.

There, he convinced them that starving themselves to death would allow them to meet Jesus.

Imagine the level of trust these families must have placed in their religious leader. Parents, children and entire households were drawn into a belief system that eventually turned deadly.

During the trial, the defence argued that the children had taken part in the fasting voluntarily. The judge rejected that argument.

How the tragedy came to light

The case first drew international attention in April 2023, when authorities discovered the first bodies buried in the forest.

What first looked like an isolated discovery soon turned into something far more horrifying. As investigators kept searching, more graves were uncovered. Eventually, more than 400 bodies were exhumed, revealing the enormous scale of the disaster.

More than 200 witnesses testified during the proceedings. Mackenzie had been in custody since 14 April 2023, when he surrendered to authorities after the first victims were found.

The children who died

Kenya, a country where religion plays an important part in everyday life, was deeply shaken by the discoveries.

For many, the most painful part of the story is the number of children who died. These were young lives that depended on adults for protection, yet they became victims of circumstances they could neither understand nor control.

A warning about blind obedience

The case is a troubling reminder of what can happen when people give up their ability to question religious authority, and it raises serious questions about religious manipulation and the protection of vulnerable people.

Faith is deeply personal, and millions find comfort, hope and direction in religion. But when a religious leader begins demanding actions that threaten human life, the consequences can be devastating.

Enugu governor's wife sends representative to Ukehe woman with financial support, promises new house and business

6 Nov 2026, 23:49 GMT

Mrs Nkechinyere Mbah, wife of the Enugu State governor, has sent a representative to a woman in Ukehe with an envelope of cash, and promised to build her a house and help her set up a business so she can support herself.

Read more

A woman from Ukehe in Enugu State has received financial assistance from the wife of the state governor, Mrs Nkechinyere Mbah, who sent a representative to visit her with promises that could significantly improve her living conditions.

Cash, a house and a business

During the visit, the representative handed the woman an envelope containing cash on behalf of the governor's wife. But the help did not end there.

Mrs Mbah also promised to build a house for the woman and to help her set up a business that would give her a steady income, so she can take care of herself without depending on others to survive.

Photos from the visit show women celebrating with raised hands outside a building in Ukehe, and an envelope being handed over as a crowd gathers around.

Support that lasts

For someone facing difficult living conditions, a decent roof and a business that can put food on the table could make a lasting difference.

What makes the gesture stand out is the promise of long-term support. Money solves an immediate problem, but helping someone build a business can give them a livelihood for years to come.

Remembering the vulnerable

The visit has also drawn attention to the importance of identifying vulnerable people in our communities and offering help that goes beyond temporary relief.

Many Nigerians are struggling quietly, especially elderly women, widows and people without a reliable income. Sometimes what they need most is a chance to become financially independent.

For the Ukehe woman, the hope now is that the promises are fulfilled and that she soon has a comfortable home and a business of her own.
