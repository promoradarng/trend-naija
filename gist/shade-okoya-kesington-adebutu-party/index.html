Woman sues MTN after father is arrested twice over recycled phone number

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A woman has reportedly taken MTN to court after her father was arrested twice because the phone number he was given had previously been used by someone linked to crimes. The case has raised concerns about recycled numbers.

Read more

A Nigerian woman has reportedly sued telecommunications company MTN after her father was arrested twice over a phone number he received.

What happened

According to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026, MTN reassigned a recycled phone number to her father. Unknown to him, the number had allegedly been used by a previous owner for criminal activity. As a result, he was reportedly arrested on two separate occasions over matters he had no knowledge of. His daughter has now taken legal action, holding MTN responsible for the distress the family suffered. MTN has not publicly commented on the case.

Why it matters

Telecom operators in Nigeria, as in many countries, recycle numbers that have been inactive for a long period and reassign them to new customers. The case has raised questions about whether operators should check that numbers are not linked to criminal investigations before giving them out, and about how security agencies verify that the current holder of a number is the person they are looking for.

Background

Nigeria requires all SIM cards to be linked to the National Identification Number, a policy meant to make it easier to trace criminals. However, problems with outdated records and recycled numbers have led to complaints from people who receive calls, messages or even police visits meant for previous owners.

How people reacted

Many social media users shared similar experiences with recycled numbers and said they hoped the case would force telecom companies to change their practices.

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Peter Obi condemns court ruling nullifying ADC chairman, says ruling party is engineering instability

5 Nov 2026, 20:38 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has condemned a Federal High Court ruling in Abuja that nullified the emergence of the ADC national chairman and all his actions. Obi warned the ruling could create serious uncertainty for opposition parties ahead of 2027, and accused the ruling party of engineering it.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has condemned the Federal High Court ruling that nullified the emergence of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) national chairman and the actions taken under his leadership.

"Do not undermine democracy"

In a statement on X titled "Do not Undermine Democracy", Obi said he received the news of the order by the Federal High Court in Abuja "with shock", describing it as "unsettling and surprising".

He was careful to say he was not challenging the court itself. "I do not challenge the ruling of the court. The court of law is constituted to determine disputes according to the law," he wrote.

Uncertainty before 2027

Obi's concern, he explained, is the timing. "My concern is that three months before a general election, it is in the public interest to have certainty. It is not good to have too many uncertainties about candidates and parties a few weeks before a presidential election."

Accusation against the ruling party

He went further, alleging that the instability was "obviously engineered by the ruling party, just as they have done against me in the Labour Party", and that the same approach was being used "to close down civic space for a robust campaign".

Obi also linked it to what he described as violence against opposition campaigns, saying it came "from the same rulebook that manufactures violence against us in our campaigns."

These are Obi's allegations, and the ruling party has not been quoted responding to them in his statement.

A call to the judiciary

Obi called on the judiciary to rise up and protect democracy by making sure opposition parties and their candidates are allowed to take part freely in the electoral process.

The National Democratic Coalition (NDC) has taken its political campaign to Lagos, and judging by the crowd that turned out, the party appears determined to make its presence felt ahead of the 2027 general elections.

A charged atmosphere

The atmosphere was charged with excitement as supporters gathered to welcome the campaign team. Photos from the event show party leaders addressing the crowd, and streets packed with supporters, many in red caps, cheering and holding up their phones.

For a party seeking to establish itself as a serious political force, such a turnout could be an encouraging sign.

Why Lagos matters

This is Lagos, a state widely regarded as one of Nigeria's most important political battlegrounds and a stronghold of President Bola Tinubu's political influence.

Taking a campaign into Lagos and drawing a large crowd is bound to generate political discussion, especially as opposition parties begin positioning themselves for 2027.

Crowds versus votes

The real question goes beyond the number of people who attended. Can the NDC turn the enthusiasm at its campaign into actual votes when Nigerians return to the polling units?

Nigeria has seen massive political rallies before, only for the election results to tell a different story.

Still, one thing is becoming clear. The political atmosphere ahead of 2027 is gradually heating up, and Lagos will remain a major battleground.
