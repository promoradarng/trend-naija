Utomi says an Obi government would aim for ₦500 to the dollar

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

Political economist Pat Utomi, a leading figure in the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has reportedly said that a Peter Obi government would work towards an exchange rate of ₦500 to the dollar.

What he said

In a post on X reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, Utomi said the target would be part of Obi's economic agenda. He argued that the naira could be strengthened from its current rate of about ₦1,350 to ₦500 per dollar through investment in security, electricity and roads, together with market reforms. He also projected large falls in the prices of everyday goods, claiming for example that a 50kg bag of imported rice could drop from about ₦55,000 to ₦27,500 and a loaf of sliced bread from ₦1,750 to ₦800.

The questions

The NDC has not said how long such an improvement would take, or what monetary and fiscal steps would be used to achieve and sustain it. Reaching ₦500 would require the naira to gain about 63 per cent against the dollar. The naira traded at about ₦1,347 on the official market and around ₦1,385 on the parallel market at the end of last week. No independent economists or government officials had responded to the target at the time of the report.

Background

The exchange rate has been one of the biggest economic issues since the naira was floated in 2023, when it weakened sharply. The Tinubu government says its reforms have stabilised the currency, while the opposition argues that the weaker naira has driven up living costs. Utomi, who once ran for president himself, is a long-time ally of Obi.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters welcomed the promise, while critics described it as unrealistic and questioned how it could be achieved without large reserves.

  • Utomi says the target is ₦500 to the dollar, from about ₦1,350 now.
  • He projected big falls in food and fuel prices.
  • The NDC has not given a timeline.

Makinde launches Reset Agenda, proposes sharing oil windfall with ordinary Nigerians

5 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde has unveiled his Reset Agenda manifesto in Abuja, proposing that oil earnings above the budget benchmark go directly to citizens' welfare. His running mate, former DSS chief Lawal Musa Daura, warned that the opposition is being suppressed.

Read more

Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde, presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), has launched his manifesto for the 2027 election.

The proposals

At an event at the Transcorp Hilton in Abuja on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Makinde reportedly called for a system in which crude oil earnings above the federal budget benchmark are channelled directly into the welfare of ordinary Nigerians, arguing that citizens of such a richly endowed country have no reason to be poor. The manifesto, called the Reset Agenda, also sets out proposals on electricity, governance and electoral reform. He was joined by his running mate, Alhaji Lawal Musa Daura, and APM National Chairman Yusuf Mamman Dantalle.

Running mate's warning

Daura, a former Director-General of the Department of State Services, reportedly warned that opposition parties were being suppressed ahead of the elections.

Background

Makinde is completing his second term as Oyo governor, having been elected on the PDP platform. He is now running against President Bola Tinubu of the APC, Atiku Abubakar of the ADC and Peter Obi of the NDC, among others, in the presidential election scheduled for 16 January 2027. A well-known Obidient mobiliser, Serah Ibrahim, introduced him at the launch and said she helped draft the Reset Agenda. Several of his state officials have resigned in recent weeks.

How people reacted

Makinde's supporters praised the focus on welfare and electricity, while critics questioned how the oil windfall plan would be funded and whether he could build a national base in time.

Wike denies role in Okorocha judgment, asks why Uzodimma fears Rainbow Coalition

5 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has denied any link to the court ruling that made Rochas Okorocha the APC's Imo West Senate candidate instead of Governor Hope Uzodimma. He said Okorocha is his friend and questioned why Uzodimma is afraid of the Rainbow Coalition.

Read more

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has rejected suggestions that he was behind a court judgment that removed Imo State governor Hope Uzodimma as the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate for the Imo West senatorial district.

What Wike said

Speaking at his monthly media chat in Abuja on Thursday, 5 November 2026, Wike reportedly described claims linking him to the ruling as mischievous, saying he neither filed nor took part in the case. He pointed out that he is not a member of the APC. He said Uzodimma had earlier declared that there was no Rainbow Coalition in Imo State, which Wike called uncivilised, and that it was wrong to credit him with the judgment. He also asked why the governor was afraid of the coalition, saying both men could tell their supporters to vote for President Bola Tinubu. Wike said he owed no apology for his friendship with Okorocha, and that his main focus was the President's re-election.

The court ruling

A Federal High Court in Abuja recently ordered INEC to publish Okorocha's name as the APC candidate for Imo West, after he argued that he won the party's primary. Uzodimma has not said publicly whether he will appeal.

Background

The Rainbow Coalition is a group of PDP and other politicians led by Wike that backs Tinubu's re-election. It has caused friction with some APC governors, including Uzodimma, who see it as a rival structure. Okorocha governed Imo from 2011 to 2019 and has long been a rival of Uzodimma. Former APC chairman Abdullahi Ganduje visited Okorocha to congratulate him after the ruling.

Ambode pledges to mobilise support for Tinubu and Hamzat, plans Epe rally

5 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Former Lagos governor Akinwunmi Ambode has reportedly pledged to mobilise support for President Tinubu's re-election and APC Lagos governorship candidate Obafemi Hamzat. His group plans to host Hamzat and other APC candidates in Epe next week.

Read more

Former Lagos State governor Akinwunmi Ambode has declared that he will mobilise support for President Bola Tinubu's re-election and for Dr Obafemi Hamzat, the All Progressives Congress (APC) governorship candidate in Lagos.

What was announced

A statement from the Ambode Support Associates, signed by former Lagos State Emergency Management Agency general manager Adesina Tiamiyu, said the decision followed a closed-door meeting between Ambode and his political allies on Wednesday, 4 November 2026. The group said it would host Hamzat and other APC candidates in Epe, Ambode's home town, next week to show party unity. It said its mobilisation drive would target grassroots communities, market women, artisans and people with disabilities across the state.

Background

Ambode governed Lagos from 2015 to 2019 but was denied a second-term ticket by the APC after falling out with party leaders, and was succeeded by Babajide Sanwo-Olu. Hamzat, the current deputy governor, is the party's candidate to succeed Sanwo-Olu in 2027. Ambode's return to active campaigning for the party is seen as a boost to APC unity in Lagos, where opposition parties are also seeking to make gains.

What happens next

The Epe rally is expected to take place next week, with details to be announced.

Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly removed the David Mark-led caretaker leadership of the ADC and nullified its decisions, including the nomination of Atiku Abubakar as presidential candidate. The ruling came a day after Atiku's Sokoto rally.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

Atiku responds

In a statement issued from Birnin Kebbi on Thursday through his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku condemned the ruling and described it as a deliberate attempt to derail his campaign after the large turnout at his Sokoto launch. His campaign pointed to an earlier Federal High Court ruling that had dismissed a similar challenge to the David Mark-led leadership, and said the judgment would be challenged at the Court of Appeal. It said the campaign would continue, moving on to Kebbi State.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign.
