No NIN, no flight: FG sets 1 January deadline for domestic air passengers

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The federal government has announced that passengers without a valid National Identification Number (NIN) will not be allowed to board domestic flights from 1 January 2027.

The new system

Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development Festus Keyamo announced the policy while unveiling the Verified Passenger Authentication System (VPASS), a digital platform that links passengers' identities directly to their NIN records.

Keyamo said the system will help authorities confirm genuine passengers, prevent unauthorised access to restricted airport areas and make sure airline passenger lists are accurate.

Why it is being introduced

The minister pointed to irregularities in ticket sales, including bulk buying and reselling of tickets under different identities, as problems the government wants to stop. He said tying bookings to verified identity records would make it harder for anyone to travel using false details.

What airlines must do

Keyamo directed the Nigerian Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) to ensure that domestic airlines connect to the platform before 31 December 2026, ahead of the January deadline.

What it means for travellers

Anyone planning to fly within Nigeria in the new year will need to make sure their NIN is valid and matches the details they use to book tickets.

  • The rule starts on 1 January 2027.
  • Airlines must link up to the VPASS system by 31 December.
  • It aims to stop ticket fraud and fake identities.

At least 30 feared dead, about 300 missing as boat carrying mourners capsizes in Bayelsa

20 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

At least 30 people are feared dead and about 300 missing after an overloaded boat carrying mourners to a burial capsized near Oweikorogha in Southern Ijaw, Bayelsa State. Police marine units are leading rescue efforts.

Read more

A boat said to be carrying more than 300 passengers has capsized in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of Bayelsa State, with many people feared dead or missing.

What happened

The boat was travelling from Ayama community to Ukubie for a burial ceremony when it capsized at about noon on Friday, 20 November 2026, shortly after passing Oweikorogha community.

The exact number of passengers on board and the number of casualties had not been established at the time of the reports, but residents fear several people may have lost their lives.

Rescue efforts

The Bayelsa State Police Command spokesperson, DSP Musa Muhammad, confirmed the incident and said the Commissioner of Police had deployed the Marine Police Unit and the local divisional police officer for rescue operations. He said further details on casualties and missing persons would be released.

A former state chairman of the Maritime Workers Union also confirmed the incident.

Death toll rises

Later reports put the number feared dead at at least 30, with around 300 others unaccounted for. Witnesses described the boat as heavily overloaded, and a community source said some people had raised concerns as passengers boarded.

What happens next

Rescue and recovery efforts are continuing, and official casualty figures are expected once they are confirmed.
