Woman sues MTN after father is arrested twice over recycled phone number

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Nigerian woman has reportedly sued telecommunications company MTN after her father was arrested twice over a phone number he received.

What happened

According to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026, MTN reassigned a recycled phone number to her father. Unknown to him, the number had allegedly been used by a previous owner for criminal activity. As a result, he was reportedly arrested on two separate occasions over matters he had no knowledge of. His daughter has now taken legal action, holding MTN responsible for the distress the family suffered. MTN has not publicly commented on the case.

Why it matters

Telecom operators in Nigeria, as in many countries, recycle numbers that have been inactive for a long period and reassign them to new customers. The case has raised questions about whether operators should check that numbers are not linked to criminal investigations before giving them out, and about how security agencies verify that the current holder of a number is the person they are looking for.

Background

Nigeria requires all SIM cards to be linked to the National Identification Number, a policy meant to make it easier to trace criminals. However, problems with outdated records and recycled numbers have led to complaints from people who receive calls, messages or even police visits meant for previous owners.

How people reacted

Many social media users shared similar experiences with recycled numbers and said they hoped the case would force telecom companies to change their practices.

Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

A man identified as Mr Mensar reportedly spent five years in prison for failing to pay child support, only for a DNA test after his release to show the child was not his. When questioned by the judge, the child's mother accepted responsibility.

Read more

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

"I would have been worried if Amaechi stood a chance in Rivers State": Wike fires back at political rivals

6 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has dismissed Rotimi Amaechi's chances in Rivers State, insisting he remains a major force there even though he is no longer governor. He also claimed the APC barely existed in Rivers before he backed it, and that he has "killed the Obi wave" in the FCT.

Read more

The Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, has once again spoken boldly about his political influence in Rivers State, dismissing the chances of former Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi and insisting he remains a major force in the state.

"You cannot wish me away"

According to Wike, he would only have reason to worry if Amaechi stood a realistic chance of a significant political comeback in Rivers State.

The former Rivers governor made it clear that leaving Government House in Port Harcourt has not reduced his influence or weakened the structures he built over the years.

"I am not a governor, I am a minister, but you cannot wish me away in Rivers State, because the people know what I stand for and because of the people I have raised," Wike said.

His statement appears to be a direct message to those who believe his appointment as FCT Minister has taken him away from the centre of Rivers politics.

Claims about the APC

Wike also claimed that the All Progressives Congress (APC) had little political strength in Rivers State before he decided to support the party.

"Before I decided to support the APC, the party did not exist in Rivers State," he said.

That is a bold claim, given the long rivalry between Wike and Amaechi, who both served as governors of Rivers State at different times.

"I have killed the Obi wave"

Wike did not limit his remarks to Rivers. He also turned his attention to the movement around NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, claiming he had already weakened its influence in the FCT.

"As for the NDC, I have killed the Obi wave in the FCT," he said.

The remarks reflect Wike's confidence in his political strength, although his claims about the APC and about Obi's support remain his own assessment.

The road to 2027

With the 2027 general elections approaching, the political battle in Rivers State is expected to draw even more attention. Wike, Amaechi and other influential figures may once again find themselves on opposing sides as parties prepare for another contest.

For now, Wike is making one thing clear to his opponents: he may no longer be governor, but he has no intention of surrendering his influence in Rivers State.

"Oga had no hands in it"

In a post addressed to "Dearest Nigerians", Israel said Davido had no hand in Naira Marley's ordeal during the Mohbad case, nor in any plot to see VDM arrested.

He said those who were in the WhatsApp group linked to the matter got there without Davido's instructions or approval, and that those who told VDM otherwise "fully lied".

Sam Klef, Mohbad's catalogue and Zino

Israel also asked why Davido would have any issue with Sam Klef, and denied that the singer ever asked for Mohbad's music catalogue or tried to sign Zino.

"Naira and oga are cool"

According to Israel, there was serious pressure on Davido at the time to unfollow Naira Marley on Instagram, but he categorically refused, saying he wanted the police to do their job and did not want to act on hearsay.

"Naira and oga are cool," he wrote, adding that the two even made a hit song together.

On an incident at Naira Marley's house, Israel said it involved Tolu 30BG, who had a disagreement with one of Naira's boys, and insisted that nobody was beaten or attacked.

Why the denial matters

VDM is known for speaking openly about controversial issues and challenging influential people, which has earned him both strong supporters and fierce critics. Whenever the names Davido and VeryDarkMan appear in the same controversy, Nigerians are bound to have plenty to say.

Israel's message is straightforward: whatever controversy surrounds VDM, his boss should not be accused of involvement without proof.

It is important to note that the allegations remain unverified. Israel's denial is his own position, and there is no established evidence that Davido took part in any plot to have VDM arrested.
