FG seeks another $500 million World Bank loan to expand renewable energy programme

16 Nov 2026, 16:20 GMT

The Federal Government is seeking an additional $500 million loan from the World Bank to expand its renewable energy programme.

What the money is for

The programme is meant to expand access to electricity through renewable sources such as solar mini-grids and stand-alone solar systems, particularly for homes, businesses, schools and health centres in areas the national grid does not reach.

In 2023, the World Bank approved $750 million for Nigeria's Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up (DARES) project, which aims to bring clean electricity to millions of Nigerians. The new $500 million would raise the World Bank's total financing for the project to $1.25 billion.

"Where is the renewable energy?"

The news has been met with frustration by many Nigerians, who say they are yet to see the impact of the money already borrowed.

Millions of households and small businesses still depend on generators, while frequent grid collapses and rising electricity tariffs continue to bite. For many people, the question is simple: where is the renewable energy?

The debt question

The request also comes amid growing concern about Nigeria's rising debt. Critics argue that new loans should only be taken when there is clear evidence that earlier funds have been well spent and are delivering results.

Supporters of the programme argue that renewable energy is essential for closing Nigeria's huge electricity gap, and that such projects take time to deliver.

Cooking gas prices rise; refilling 12.5kg now costs up to ₦22,500

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Cooking gas has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kg to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800 depending on location. Some households are cutting back or switching to other fuels ahead of Christmas.

Read more

Nigerian households are facing fresh pressure as cooking gas prices rise across major cities ahead of the Christmas season.

The new prices

The price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kilogram to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800, depending on location. That means refilling a 12.5kg cylinder can now cost as much as ₦22,500, up from about ₦17,500.

How households are coping

Market findings suggest some Nigerians are now buying smaller quantities, cutting down on how much gas they use or switching to other cooking fuels.

The federal government has announced plans to release stored rice, maize and other grains into the market in a bid to bring down food prices.

What was announced

Minister of Agriculture and Food Security Abubakar Kyari disclosed the plan on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, at the presentation of the 2026 Wet Season Agricultural Performance Survey Report in Abuja.

He said grains bought under the Bank of Agriculture's Guaranteed Minimum Price programme would soon be released to the market.

Fertiliser for farmers

The government also plans to distribute three million bags of fertiliser to farmers within the next month to boost production and improve food supply across the country.

Background

The Guaranteed Minimum Price scheme gives farmers an assured buyer for their produce.
