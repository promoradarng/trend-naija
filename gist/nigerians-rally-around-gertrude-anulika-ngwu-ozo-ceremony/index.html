Nigerians rally around Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu after humiliating incident at husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony

6 Nov 2026, 08:20 GMT

What was supposed to be a day of celebration has turned into a story of humiliation, public sympathy and unexpected kindness for Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, whose experience at her husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony has drawn wide attention on social media.

Kind-hearted Nigerians have continued to visit her, offering financial help, gifts and words of encouragement after a viral video showed her being treated in a way many viewers described as humiliating.

The viral video

The video captured an uncomfortable moment during the traditional ceremony when the children of her husband's second wife reportedly refused to spray money on her, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

According to accounts shared alongside the footage, the children had earlier sprayed money on their biological mother. When the MC encouraged them to do the same for Mrs Anulika, they declined.

For many Nigerians who watched, it was a painful scene: a woman attending an important ceremony in her husband's life, yet appearing to be left out of a celebration in which she should have felt included and respected.

Years of mockery

The story became even more emotional following claims that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over her inability to have children.

Reports online suggest she eventually moved back to her father's home after allegedly facing repeated ridicule from her co-wife and the woman's children. These allegations have not been independently verified.

Strangers show her love

Nigerians who were touched by her story have decided to show her some love. In recent days, well-wishers have visited her at her father's residence with gifts, financial support and encouragement.

Some supporters have donated hundreds of thousands of naira, while others bought her a new mobile phone. More visits are planned: one support group has announced a grand visit to her village in Ukeh, Enugu State, on Friday, 6 November 2026, with supporters from Anambra, Imo and Abia invited to meet at Ekochin Bus Stop, 9th Mile.

Perhaps that is the most touching part of this story. People who have never met Mrs Anulika are going out of their way to make her smile. Strangers who watched a few minutes of her experience online have become concerned about her welfare.

A conversation about childlessness

Her story has also reopened an important conversation about how women who struggle with childlessness are treated in some Nigerian families.

Why should a woman's value in marriage be measured by the number of children she has? Why should a woman who has spent years in a marriage be made to feel less important simply because she has not given birth?

Marriage comes with its own difficulties, but no woman deserves to be publicly ridiculed or denied basic respect because of her circumstances.

Compassion still lives

Whatever may have happened behind the scenes in this family, the incident has clearly touched a nerve. The growing support for Mrs Anulika shows that compassion still has a place in society. Even at a time when many people are struggling financially, some are willing to share what little they have to comfort someone they believe has been treated unfairly.

One can only hope the support continues beyond social media and that she finds lasting peace, dignity and happiness. To every Nigerian who has visited, donated or offered her a kind word, your gesture deserves recognition. Sometimes, all a person needs is to know that somebody cares.

Over to you

Should a woman who has no biological children be treated differently in her husband's family, especially when he marries another wife who has children? What happened to compassion, fairness and respect in our traditional family system?

A live television debate took an awkward turn on Tuesday night, 10 November 2026, when Nigeria's national emergency number failed to connect after a ruling party official insisted it worked.

What happened

Speaking on a Channels Television programme to highlight the government's achievements, Alwan Hassan, a member of the APC presidential campaign council, said the 112 emergency response system had been upgraded and worked reliably nationwide. To prove his point, he dared the anchor, Seun Okinbaloye, to call the number live on air. The anchor accepted, dialled 112 on his phone, but the call did not go through.

Background

The 112 toll-free number is meant to connect people to police, fire, ambulance and other emergency services across Nigeria. Emergency response became a talking point last week when actor Kunle Afod complained about the response to a crash on the Lagos–Ibadan Expressway.

Ebola: 28 contacts still under watch in Nigeria, 34 cleared, says NCDC

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The NCDC says 28 people linked to Nigeria's single imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a type of Ebola, remain under surveillance, while 34 others have been cleared after 21 days of monitoring. No further cases have been confirmed.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says 28 people linked to the country's imported Ebola case are still being monitored.

The update

In its national health security update released on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said 34 other contacts had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. As of 9 November, Nigeria had confirmed only one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, with no further confirmed cases.

The response

Idris said the response involves the Federal Ministry of Health, the FCT Administration, state authorities and development partners, with contact tracing, investigation of reported cases, laboratory testing, infection prevention and control, safe patient care and stronger screening at entry points.

Background

Ebola is a severe and often deadly viral disease spread through contact with the body fluids of infected people. The 21-day monitoring period reflects the maximum incubation time of the virus. The case in Nigeria is linked to an outbreak in Central Africa; the United States has just barred travellers who have been in the Democratic Republic of Congo in the past 21 days. Nigeria contained an Ebola outbreak in 2014.

Advice

Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness, vomiting or unexplained bleeding, especially after travel or contact with a suspected case, to report to a health facility immediately.

US bars travellers who have been in DR Congo in the last 21 days over Ebola

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The US CDC has temporarily barred anyone who has been in the Democratic Republic of Congo within the past 21 days, including US citizens and transit passengers, from boarding flights to the United States because of Ebola.

Read more

The United States has imposed a temporary travel restriction on people who have recently been in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) because of Ebola.

The rule

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) said anyone who has been in the DRC in the past 21 days may not board a flight to the United States, according to reports on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. The rule applies to American citizens as well as foreigners, and covers passengers whose aircraft stopped at a DRC airport even if they did not leave the plane. Affected travellers must wait until 21 days have passed since they left the DRC. The US Embassy in Kinshasa announced the measure on social media.

Why 21 days

The period matches the maximum incubation time of the Ebola virus.

Background

The restriction comes amid an Ebola outbreak in Central Africa. Nigeria is currently monitoring contacts of one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, and says no further cases have been confirmed.
