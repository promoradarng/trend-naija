Nigerians rally around Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu after humiliating incident at husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony

6 Nov 2026, 08:20 GMT

What was supposed to be a day of celebration has turned into a story of humiliation, public sympathy and unexpected kindness for Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, whose experience at her husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony has drawn wide attention on social media.

Kind-hearted Nigerians have continued to visit her, offering financial help, gifts and words of encouragement after a viral video showed her being treated in a way many viewers described as humiliating.

The viral video

The video captured an uncomfortable moment during the traditional ceremony when the children of her husband's second wife reportedly refused to spray money on her, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

According to accounts shared alongside the footage, the children had earlier sprayed money on their biological mother. When the MC encouraged them to do the same for Mrs Anulika, they declined.

For many Nigerians who watched, it was a painful scene: a woman attending an important ceremony in her husband's life, yet appearing to be left out of a celebration in which she should have felt included and respected.

Years of mockery

The story became even more emotional following claims that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over her inability to have children.

Reports online suggest she eventually moved back to her father's home after allegedly facing repeated ridicule from her co-wife and the woman's children. These allegations have not been independently verified.

Strangers show her love

Nigerians who were touched by her story have decided to show her some love. In recent days, well-wishers have visited her at her father's residence with gifts, financial support and encouragement.

Some supporters have donated hundreds of thousands of naira, while others bought her a new mobile phone. More visits are planned: one support group has announced a grand visit to her village in Ukeh, Enugu State, on Friday, 6 November 2026, with supporters from Anambra, Imo and Abia invited to meet at Ekochin Bus Stop, 9th Mile.

Perhaps that is the most touching part of this story. People who have never met Mrs Anulika are going out of their way to make her smile. Strangers who watched a few minutes of her experience online have become concerned about her welfare.

A conversation about childlessness

Her story has also reopened an important conversation about how women who struggle with childlessness are treated in some Nigerian families.

Why should a woman's value in marriage be measured by the number of children she has? Why should a woman who has spent years in a marriage be made to feel less important simply because she has not given birth?

Marriage comes with its own difficulties, but no woman deserves to be publicly ridiculed or denied basic respect because of her circumstances.

Compassion still lives

Whatever may have happened behind the scenes in this family, the incident has clearly touched a nerve. The growing support for Mrs Anulika shows that compassion still has a place in society. Even at a time when many people are struggling financially, some are willing to share what little they have to comfort someone they believe has been treated unfairly.

One can only hope the support continues beyond social media and that she finds lasting peace, dignity and happiness. To every Nigerian who has visited, donated or offered her a kind word, your gesture deserves recognition. Sometimes, all a person needs is to know that somebody cares.

US corrections officer accused of inviting another man home, then calling him an intruder and firing gun when boyfriend returned

20 Nov 2026, 09:20 GMT

Lasacia Gore, a 34-year-old corrections officer in Michigan, has been charged with six felonies. Prosecutors say she invited another man to her home, then reported him as an intruder and fired a gun when her boyfriend came back unexpectedly. She has pleaded not guilty.

Read more

A 34-year-old woman in Michigan, United States, has been charged with six felonies after police say she invited another man to her home while her boyfriend was away, then reported him as an intruder when her partner returned unexpectedly.

The 7am phone call

According to prosecutors, Lasacia Gore, a corrections officer with Wayne County, called police at around 7am on Monday, claiming an unknown man had broken into her home in Eastpointe and was fighting with her boyfriend.

But investigators say they found that she had invited the man over herself, and had even left the door unlocked for him.

What police say happened

According to police, things turned violent when her boyfriend came home unexpectedly, found the visitor in the house and confronted him, leading to a fight.

Eastpointe Police Chief Corey Haines said Gore fired a gun while both men were wrestling on the kitchen floor. The bullet missed them and struck the wall above them.

"You told me to leave the door open"

Macomb County Prosecutor Pete Lucido said the visitor's account contradicted hers. "She's making a call saying she doesn't know him and this and that, and he says, 'What are you talking about? You're the one who told me to leave the door open and I'm coming in!'" he said.

"It's crazy. This could have been very tragic and somebody could've died," Lucido added.

Chief Haines also expressed concern that a corrections officer was allegedly involved in making a false police report and firing a weapon.

The charges

Gore faces six felony charges, including assault with a dangerous weapon, discharging a firearm at a building, filing a false felony report, lying to investigators and two firearm-related counts.

She pleaded not guilty at her arraignment on Wednesday. Her bond was set at $30,000, with a 10 per cent option. If released, she must stay at home under GPS monitoring, surrender her weapons and avoid contact with the complainant.

She is due back in court on 2 December for a probable cause conference. The charges are allegations and have not been proven in court.

IGP tells doctors: no police report needed to treat gunshot victims

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Inspector-General of Police Olatunji Disu has reminded doctors and hospitals that the law does not require a police report before treating gunshot victims. He urged health workers to put saving lives first.

The Inspector-General of Police, Olatunji Disu, has urged doctors and other health workers to treat gunshot victims immediately, without first demanding a police report.

What the IGP said

In a message posted on the Nigeria Police Force's official X account on Thursday, 19 November 2026, Disu stressed that the law does not require hospitals to obtain a police report before attending to people with gunshot wounds.

He appealed to doctors and health workers to prioritise saving lives instead of waiting for police documents before starting emergency treatment.

Help for the public

The IGP also encouraged members of the public not to hesitate to help injured people get to hospital.

Background

Nigeria has a law requiring hospitals to treat gunshot victims without first demanding a police report.

UN top court finds Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority during the 2017 military crackdown. It is the first time the court has found a state responsible for genocide.

Read more

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the United Nations' highest court, has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority during a military crackdown in 2017.

The ruling

In a landmark judgment on Thursday, 19 November 2026, the court found Myanmar responsible for genocide in northern Rakhine State. The decision was reached by a 9-6 majority and marks the first time in the court's 80-year history that it has found a state responsible for committing genocide.

The court said genocidal intent was the only reasonable conclusion that could be drawn from the evidence before it.

The case

The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019, accusing Myanmar of violating the 1948 Genocide Convention. The 2017 crackdown killed thousands of Rohingya and forced more than 700,000 to flee the country.

Myanmar's position

Myanmar had denied the accusations, saying its forces were responding to attacks by armed groups.

Polytechnic lecturers begin strike in Ogun, Oyo, Osun and Ekiti

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

ASUP Zone C has begun a strike in state-owned polytechnics and colleges of technology in four South-West states over unpaid academic allowances and arrears.

Read more

The Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics (ASUP), Zone C, has begun a strike in state-owned polytechnics and colleges of technology in four South-West states over unpaid academic allowances.

Where and when

The strike affects institutions in Ogun, Oyo, Osun and Ekiti States. The union said it took effect from Monday, 16 November 2026, after a 15-day ultimatum to the state governments expired.

The announcement was made at a press conference at The Polytechnic, Ibadan, on Thursday, 19 November.

The demands

The union wants immediate implementation of the Consolidated Academic Tools Allowance and the Earned Academic Allowance for academic staff, as well as payment of arrears dating back to 1 January 2026. It said the federal government had concluded the renegotiation of the union's agreement, but the state governments had yet to implement it.
