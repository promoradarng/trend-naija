Nigerians rally around Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu after humiliating incident at husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony

6 Nov 2026, 08:20 GMT

What was supposed to be a day of celebration has turned into a story of humiliation, public sympathy and unexpected kindness for Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, whose experience at her husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony has drawn wide attention on social media.

Kind-hearted Nigerians have continued to visit her, offering financial help, gifts and words of encouragement after a viral video showed her being treated in a way many viewers described as humiliating.

The viral video

The video captured an uncomfortable moment during the traditional ceremony when the children of her husband's second wife reportedly refused to spray money on her, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

According to accounts shared alongside the footage, the children had earlier sprayed money on their biological mother. When the MC encouraged them to do the same for Mrs Anulika, they declined.

For many Nigerians who watched, it was a painful scene: a woman attending an important ceremony in her husband's life, yet appearing to be left out of a celebration in which she should have felt included and respected.

Years of mockery

The story became even more emotional following claims that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over her inability to have children.

Reports online suggest she eventually moved back to her father's home after allegedly facing repeated ridicule from her co-wife and the woman's children. These allegations have not been independently verified.

Strangers show her love

Nigerians who were touched by her story have decided to show her some love. In recent days, well-wishers have visited her at her father's residence with gifts, financial support and encouragement.

Some supporters have donated hundreds of thousands of naira, while others bought her a new mobile phone. More visits are planned: one support group has announced a grand visit to her village in Ukeh, Enugu State, on Friday, 6 November 2026, with supporters from Anambra, Imo and Abia invited to meet at Ekochin Bus Stop, 9th Mile.

Perhaps that is the most touching part of this story. People who have never met Mrs Anulika are going out of their way to make her smile. Strangers who watched a few minutes of her experience online have become concerned about her welfare.

A conversation about childlessness

Her story has also reopened an important conversation about how women who struggle with childlessness are treated in some Nigerian families.

Why should a woman's value in marriage be measured by the number of children she has? Why should a woman who has spent years in a marriage be made to feel less important simply because she has not given birth?

Marriage comes with its own difficulties, but no woman deserves to be publicly ridiculed or denied basic respect because of her circumstances.

Compassion still lives

Whatever may have happened behind the scenes in this family, the incident has clearly touched a nerve. The growing support for Mrs Anulika shows that compassion still has a place in society. Even at a time when many people are struggling financially, some are willing to share what little they have to comfort someone they believe has been treated unfairly.

One can only hope the support continues beyond social media and that she finds lasting peace, dignity and happiness. To every Nigerian who has visited, donated or offered her a kind word, your gesture deserves recognition. Sometimes, all a person needs is to know that somebody cares.

Over to you

Should a woman who has no biological children be treated differently in her husband's family, especially when he marries another wife who has children? What happened to compassion, fairness and respect in our traditional family system?

Woman confronts herder after cattle allegedly destroy her farm crops in Nasarawa State

10 Nov 2026, 00:44 GMT

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle allegedly strayed onto her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months growing. The incident has reopened the question of who pays when a farmer's livelihood is wiped out.

Read more

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle allegedly entered her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months cultivating.

Months of work gone

For someone who depends on farming to survive, watching cattle destroy months of hard work can be heartbreaking.

Imagine spending your money on seeds, preparing the land, planting and waiting patiently for harvest, only to wake up one day and find your crops destroyed. Worse still, many farmers borrow money to cultivate their land, hoping to repay the loan after harvest.

An old source of tension

Incidents like this remain one of the main causes of tension between farmers and herders in parts of Nigeria, and some have escalated into violence in the past.

The question is: who compensates the woman for her losses? Who takes responsibility for the destroyed crops?

Protection for everyone

Farmers deserve protection, just as herders have a right to carry out their lawful activities. But nobody should be allowed to destroy another person's livelihood without consequences.

Clear rules on grazing, quick ways to settle disputes and fair compensation could help prevent confrontations like this from turning into something worse.

Over to you

If cattle destroy a farmer's crops, should the owner be made to pay full compensation?

INEC removes over 1.6 million invalid registrants from voter register

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

INEC says it has removed 1,608,660 invalid registrants and 876 dead voters from the register after a public review of the preliminary list. The cleaned-up register will be published on 16 December.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has removed 1,608,660 invalid registrants from Nigeria's voter register.

What INEC announced

The commission said on Monday, 9 November 2026, that the removals followed complaints, objections and claims submitted by the public after it displayed the preliminary voter register in October. It also deleted the records of 876 voters confirmed to have died, after verifying claims submitted during the review. INEC said the display of the preliminary register was meant to allow citizens and civil society groups to flag errors, duplicates and other irregularities.

What happens next

INEC said it will publish the final, cleaned-up register on 16 December 2026, as required by the Electoral Act. The commission expects more than 103 million registered voters for the 2027 elections.

Background

The figure is in line with the roughly 1.5 million questionable records INEC said earlier it had identified. A former INEC director, Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, questioned last week how so many invalid entries could have entered the register despite biometric safeguards, and called on the commission to explain how they were detected. Opposition parties have also raised concerns about alleged AI-generated photographs in the register.

Why it matters

A credible voter register is key to public confidence in the 16 January 2027 presidential election.

Chief Justice swears in 375 judges for 2027 election tribunals

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Chief Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun has sworn in 375 judges to serve on election petition tribunals for 2027, warning them to resist political pressure and cautioning politicians and lawyers against abusing ex parte applications.

Read more

The Chief Justice of Nigeria, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, has sworn in 375 judges who will serve on the election petition tribunals for the 2027 general elections.

The ceremony

The swearing-in took place on Monday, 9 November 2026, at the Body of Benchers auditorium in Abuja, alongside the launch of the second edition of the Judicial Electoral Manual and a training workshop for judicial officers.

The CJN's warning

Justice Kekere-Ekun charged the judges to uphold judicial independence, resist political pressure and deliver justice without fear or favour, in line with the Constitution, judicial ethics and the rule of law. She also warned political parties, lawyers, the media and the public against behaviour aimed at undermining the judicial process, and specifically cautioned against the abuse of ex parte applications, which are orders granted without hearing the other side.

Background

Election petition tribunals hear challenges to the results of governorship, National Assembly and state assembly elections. Courts have played a major role in recent Nigerian elections, and pre-election cases are already shaping the 2027 contest, including a Federal High Court ruling that voided Atiku Abubakar's ADC nomination, which the party has appealed, and a ruling on the Imo West Senate ticket. Concerns about conflicting court orders, especially ex parte orders, have been raised repeatedly in recent years.

Victor Umeh: Any Igbo person against Peter Obi is an enemy of the Igbo

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Senator Victor Umeh of the NDC has said any Igbo person who campaigns against Peter Obi's presidential bid is an enemy of the Igbo people, and urged the South-East to unite behind him. He also cautioned the Anambra government.

Read more

Senator Victor Umeh, who represents Anambra Central, has warned that any Igbo person working against Peter Obi's presidential ambition is an enemy of the Igbo people.

What he said

In a video that circulated on Monday, 9 November 2026, Umeh, a member of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), described Obi as the conscience of the Igbo people in the 2027 election and urged South-East residents to unite behind his bid. He cautioned political leaders in the region, particularly the Anambra State Government, against actions that could undermine Obi's campaign.

Background

Obi, a former Anambra governor, is the NDC's presidential candidate, with Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate. While he remains popular in the South-East, several of the region's governors have declared support for President Bola Tinubu. Anambra Governor Charles Soludo's APGA last week adopted Tinubu as its candidate and promised him half of the state's votes, while Ebonyi Governor Francis Nwifuru on Monday promised Tinubu 80 per cent of Ebonyi's votes. The NDC in Anambra has set a target of 98 per cent of the state's votes for Obi.

Reactions

The Anambra government had not publicly responded at the time of writing.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
