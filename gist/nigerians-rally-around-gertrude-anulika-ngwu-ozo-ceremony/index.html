Nigerians rally around Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu after humiliating incident at husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony

6 Nov 2026, 08:20 GMT

What was supposed to be a day of celebration has turned into a story of humiliation, public sympathy and unexpected kindness for Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, whose experience at her husband's Ozo title-taking ceremony has drawn wide attention on social media.

Kind-hearted Nigerians have continued to visit her, offering financial help, gifts and words of encouragement after a viral video showed her being treated in a way many viewers described as humiliating.

The viral video

The video captured an uncomfortable moment during the traditional ceremony when the children of her husband's second wife reportedly refused to spray money on her, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

According to accounts shared alongside the footage, the children had earlier sprayed money on their biological mother. When the MC encouraged them to do the same for Mrs Anulika, they declined.

For many Nigerians who watched, it was a painful scene: a woman attending an important ceremony in her husband's life, yet appearing to be left out of a celebration in which she should have felt included and respected.

Years of mockery

The story became even more emotional following claims that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over her inability to have children.

Reports online suggest she eventually moved back to her father's home after allegedly facing repeated ridicule from her co-wife and the woman's children. These allegations have not been independently verified.

Strangers show her love

Nigerians who were touched by her story have decided to show her some love. In recent days, well-wishers have visited her at her father's residence with gifts, financial support and encouragement.

Some supporters have donated hundreds of thousands of naira, while others bought her a new mobile phone. More visits are planned: one support group has announced a grand visit to her village in Ukeh, Enugu State, on Friday, 6 November 2026, with supporters from Anambra, Imo and Abia invited to meet at Ekochin Bus Stop, 9th Mile.

Perhaps that is the most touching part of this story. People who have never met Mrs Anulika are going out of their way to make her smile. Strangers who watched a few minutes of her experience online have become concerned about her welfare.

A conversation about childlessness

Her story has also reopened an important conversation about how women who struggle with childlessness are treated in some Nigerian families.

Why should a woman's value in marriage be measured by the number of children she has? Why should a woman who has spent years in a marriage be made to feel less important simply because she has not given birth?

Marriage comes with its own difficulties, but no woman deserves to be publicly ridiculed or denied basic respect because of her circumstances.

Compassion still lives

Whatever may have happened behind the scenes in this family, the incident has clearly touched a nerve. The growing support for Mrs Anulika shows that compassion still has a place in society. Even at a time when many people are struggling financially, some are willing to share what little they have to comfort someone they believe has been treated unfairly.

One can only hope the support continues beyond social media and that she finds lasting peace, dignity and happiness. To every Nigerian who has visited, donated or offered her a kind word, your gesture deserves recognition. Sometimes, all a person needs is to know that somebody cares.

Over to you

Should a woman who has no biological children be treated differently in her husband's family, especially when he marries another wife who has children? What happened to compassion, fairness and respect in our traditional family system?

All is fair in war, but has VeryDarkMan gone too far with Davido?

9 Nov 2026, 17:57 GMT

Amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VeryDarkMan has reportedly brought up the deaths of the singer's late friends, a painful chapter long surrounded by unproven rumours. Criticism is fair, but should personal tragedy ever be used as ammunition?

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) does not seem to believe in fighting fairly. When he has a disagreement with someone, he appears willing to go to any length to make his point, even if it means bringing up some of the most painful moments in that person's life.

A painful chapter

Over the years, Davido has lost several close friends and associates in tragic circumstances. Their deaths set off all kinds of rumours and conspiracy theories online.

Some people even claimed the deaths were connected to rituals or sacrifices for fame and success. None of those claims was ever proven, and there is no credible evidence linking Davido to any of their deaths.

Imagine losing people who were very close to you, only to have strangers turn their deaths into accusations against you. Davido has lived with those memories for years. He has carried on with his music career, built his family and moved forward with his life, but moving forward does not mean the pain has disappeared.

Reopening old wounds

Now, amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VDM has reportedly brought those painful memories back into public discussion. This is where things appear to be getting out of hand.

You can criticise Davido's music, lifestyle, public statements or behaviour. You can disagree with him and challenge his opinions. That is perfectly normal. But bringing up the deaths of his close friends because of a disagreement raises serious questions.

What exactly is the intention? Is it to expose wrongdoing, to make a legitimate point, or simply to hurt someone where he is most vulnerable?

Where are the limits?

VDM has built a reputation for confronting people without holding back, and his supporters may argue he is only giving his opponents the same treatment they give others. But surely there should be limits.

Davido is not above criticism, and neither is VeryDarkMan. Both are public figures who should be held accountable for their actions. Using someone's personal tragedies as ammunition in a public quarrel, however, is something else entirely.

There are some experiences people never fully recover from. They may laugh and smile in public, but that does not mean they have forgotten. If winning an argument requires reopening another person's deepest wounds, perhaps the argument has already gone too far.

Over to you

People say all is fair in love and war, but does that mean we should abandon all compassion when we disagree with someone? Has VDM crossed the line with Davido, or is this just two public figures fighting without limits?

INEC warns against 'Rig and Die' slogan ahead of 2027 elections, raises concerns over electoral violence

9 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

INEC has raised serious concern over the growing use of the "Rig and Die" slogan by some political actors ahead of the 2027 elections, warning that such language could encourage violence. But the slogan also points to a deeper problem: Nigerians' fear that their votes will not count.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has expressed serious concern over the growing use of the controversial "Rig and Die" slogan by some political actors ahead of Nigeria's 2027 general elections.

A dangerous slogan

According to the electoral body, such language is dangerous and could encourage political violence, putting innocent Nigerians at risk as the country prepares for another major election.

Why it has come to this

The controversy also raises a question that cannot be ignored: why has Nigeria's electoral system reached a point where political actors feel the need to use such threatening language?

For years, Nigerians have complained about electoral malpractice, vote buying, intimidation, ballot box snatching and alleged manipulation of results. Many voters have grown frustrated, especially when they believe their votes do not count.

With the 2027 elections approaching, political tensions are already building.

Confidence in the process

INEC is right to warn against inflammatory statements that could endanger lives. But the commission must also recognise that public confidence in the electoral process remains a major concern.

Nigerians want elections where votes are counted honestly, results are transmitted transparently, and those caught manipulating the process face real consequences.

Political leaders, too, have a responsibility to avoid statements that could turn ordinary citizens into victims of electoral violence.

No election should cost an innocent Nigerian their life. At the same time, electoral offenders must never be allowed to operate without consequences.

Over to you

Should INEC be more concerned about the slogan "Rig and Die", or about fixing the electoral weaknesses that make Nigerians fear rigging in the first place?

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has reportedly promised a ₦10 million reward to each councillor in Nigeria whose ward votes for President Bola Tinubu in the 2027 presidential election.

What he said

Wike made the pledge during a political engagement in Kwali Area Council of the Federal Capital Territory, according to reports on Monday, 9 November 2026. He also praised his Rainbow Coalition, saying it was bringing together politicians from across the country, and urged residents of Kwali and the wider FCT to back the President's re-election, arguing that a second term would bring more development to Abuja.

Background

Wike, a former Rivers governor and PDP chieftain, leads the Rainbow Coalition of politicians from the PDP and other parties backing Tinubu. He has been one of the President's most vocal campaigners, frequently criticising opposition candidates Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar.

Why it matters

INEC this week also warned political actors against inflammatory rhetoric ahead of the elections.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
