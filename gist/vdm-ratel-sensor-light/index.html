VeryDarkMan unveils RATEL Sensor Light, an affordable motion-activated light for Nigerian homes

31 Oct 2026, 14:32 GMT

VeryDarkMan (VDM) has unveiled another product under his growing RATEL brand, and this time it is something many Nigerian households may find useful, especially with the country's unpredictable electricity supply.

The new product is the RATEL Sensor Light, a motion-activated light that comes on automatically when it detects movement.

How much it costs

According to VDM, the sensor light comes in two sizes: the small size costs ₦4,200, while the big size costs ₦9,000.

Why it could be useful

Anyone living in Nigeria knows how frustrating the electricity situation can be. Imagine NEPA suddenly takes light at midnight and everywhere goes dark. You need to leave your bedroom, walk downstairs, check on your children or find your way outside to switch on the generator. Before you even find your torchlight, you have already knocked your leg against a chair.

This is where a sensor light could come in handy. It can be placed along staircases, corridors, bedroom entrances, wardrobes, storerooms and other dark corners of the house. Once it detects movement within its range, it switches on automatically, with no need to search for a wall switch in the dark.

A possible security benefit

The light could also help around entrances, gates and backyards. If somebody quietly approaches that area at night, the space around the sensor suddenly becomes bright.

Of course, a sensor light cannot replace proper home security, but it may help discourage unwanted visitors and make movement around a compound more noticeable.

VDM also explained that the product comes with different lighting colour options, including multicolour settings.

What buyers should check

At ₦4,200 for the smaller version, some households may find it affordable enough to install several units: one on the staircase, another in the corridor and perhaps two around the entrance. However, buyers should check the battery life, charging method, weather resistance and detection range before deciding where to install it.

A growing RATEL range

What stands out about this latest RATEL product is the idea behind it. VDM appears to be paying attention to the everyday inconveniences Nigerians face, especially those caused by poor electricity supply. From power stations to smaller household gadgets, he is gradually expanding his product range.

There is something worth appreciating about businesses that identify ordinary problems and offer affordable solutions. You don't always need to spend hundreds of thousands of naira to make your home more comfortable, and sometimes even a small gadget can save you the stress of walking around in darkness.

Imagine having four of these lights around your house. NEPA takes light, but as you move around, the lights come on automatically. Small enjoyment!

VeryDarkMan says he will register his own Ratel party and fund it himself

28 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Activist VeryDarkMan has announced plans to register the Ratel Political Party before 2032, saying he will pay the registration costs from his business earnings. He said the movement will start with grassroots offices.

Social media activist Martins Vincent Otse, popularly known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has announced plans to register his own political party, the Ratel Political Party, before 2032.

Paying his own way

In a video, VDM said he would fund the project with his own earnings from his businesses, rather than relying on supporters. He said he had heard the registration fee was over ₦50 million and that he would raise it. INEC's party registration guidelines set a non-refundable administrative fee of ₦50 million, and payment does not guarantee approval.

Grassroots first

VDM said the Ratel movement would begin by focusing on grassroots positions, such as local government chairmen and councillors, rather than seats in the National Assembly.

Background

'Ratel' is the name VDM uses for his followers. This week he also urged opposition leaders Peter Obi, Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde to unite under one party against President Tinubu.

Omotola Jalade opens up on difficult bond with late mother and Nollywood blacklist

28 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Veteran actress Omotola Jalade-Ekeinde has spoken about her strained relationship with her late mother and her regrets since her death. She also said she was once blacklisted in Nollywood for demanding better standards.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actress Omotola Jalade-Ekeinde has spoken openly about her complicated relationship with her late mother and about being blacklisted in the film industry early in her career.

A difficult bond

In an interview released on Friday, 27 November 2026, Omotola said she and her mother did not always get along. Her mother worried that the film industry would expose her daughter to immorality, and the tension grew when Omotola began dating her husband, Captain Matthew Ekeinde. When he visited to formally ask for her hand, her mother reportedly questioned whether her daughter was ready for marriage.

Omotola said the relationship later improved and the two became much closer. She admitted that she still has regrets about the care and support she never got to show her mother, and said she now finds herself giving that care to other people's mothers. She added that the experience shaped her decision to make her directorial debut.

'Troublesome Eight'

Turning to her career, the actress said she was blacklisted after pushing for better standards and professionalism in Nollywood. She said she was one of a group of actors labelled the 'Troublesome Eight' because they demanded improvements. She also said she had a manager as far back as the 1990s, when professional management was not widely understood in the industry.

Motherhood

The mother of four also spoke about her approach to parenting and the lessons motherhood has taught her.
