Democrats projected to win both House and Senate in blow to Trump

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The Democratic Party is projected to have won control of both chambers of the United States Congress in the 2026 midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November, according to projections by US television networks reported early on Wednesday.

The results

According to the projections, the Democrats have taken the House of Representatives and the Senate. The Senate results were described as particularly striking, with Democrats reportedly winning seats in states that President Donald Trump carried by wide margins in 2024, including Texas, South Carolina, Ohio, Kansas and Iowa. Texas had reportedly not elected a Democrat to statewide office since 1994. Final results in some races may take days to confirm.

Why voters turned

Analysts have pointed to Trump's low approval ratings, voter anger over tariffs and inflation, and the long-running conflict with Iran as reasons for the swing. On the eve of the vote, US forces carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, while Trump told supporters that Iran might eventually have to be given 'the hammer'.

Reactions

Trump sought to distance himself from the results, writing on social media that he was not on the ballot and that Republicans would have won in a landslide if he had been. He had held rallies in 20 states in the final weeks of the campaign. In a later interview, he said he was willing to accept the results and would look to work with the Democrats. Former President Barack Obama said voters had sent a clear message that they want politics that brings people together, and praised candidates who won in areas once seen as long shots. Among the night's firsts, Abdul El-Sayed became the first Muslim US senator, Keisha Lance Bottoms became the first Black woman elected governor of a US state, and Gina Hinojosa defeated Texas governor Greg Abbott.

Trump on the Iran war

Speaking to reporters at the White House on Wednesday, Trump said it was largely true that the war with Iran had contributed to the Republican losses, as Vice President JD Vance had suggested, pointing to the rise in petrol prices it caused. He said he was not considering removing Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, and that he was open to working with Democrats as long as they were not destructive, adding that he still had the power to veto their bills.

What it means

With control of both chambers, Democrats will be able to block much of the President's legislative agenda, launch investigations with the power to compel testimony, and hold up his nominees, including judges, during the last two years of his term. Trump remains president until January 2029.

Why it matters for Nigeria

US policy on trade, aid and immigration affects Nigeria directly. A Democratic Congress could push back on measures such as visa restrictions and changes to trade programmes, including this week's decision to remove South Africa and Rwanda from the AGOA duty-free scheme. The White House had not formally responded to the projections at the time of reporting.

  • Democrats are projected to control both the House and Senate.
  • They reportedly won Senate seats in Texas, Iowa and Kansas.
  • Trump remains president until January 2029.

Spain's transport minister resigns after deadly train crashes

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Spain's Transport Minister Oscar Puente has announced his resignation after two major train crashes in less than a year, including an October collision south of Madrid that killed 69 people. He said staying on would be inconsistent with political dignity.

Read more

Spain's Transport Minister, Oscar Puente, has announced that he will resign following two serious train crashes in less than a year.

What he said

Puente told lawmakers on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, that a transport minister in a country like Spain could not stay in office after two accidents of such severity, regardless of whether he was directly responsible. He described the decision as a matter of political dignity, consistency and empathy, and said he had decided to step down within minutes of the latest crash but stayed on to manage the aftermath and introduce safety measures. He did not say exactly when he would leave office, and confirmed he would not stand in Spain's early general election on 29 November.

The latest crash

The resignation came six days after a crash on 28 October near Urda, south of Madrid, in which a high-speed train run by the state operator Renfe hit the back of a stationary train run by a private operator on the Madrid–Andalusia line. The crash reportedly killed 69 people and injured more than 200. Authorities have blamed a combination of human and computer failures, and Puente said a red signal should have stopped the train.

Background

Spain has one of Europe's largest high-speed rail networks. The two crashes have caused public anger over rail safety.

What happens next

Investigations into both crashes are continuing.

UK court rejects Ibori's appeal to cut £101.5m confiscation order

5 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Court of Appeal in London has dismissed former Delta governor James Ibori's bid to reduce a £101.5 million confiscation order made after his 2012 conviction for money laundering. Ibori says he is consulting his lawyers.

Read more

Britain's Court of Appeal has dismissed a challenge by former Delta State governor James Ibori to reduce a £101.5 million confiscation order against him, according to reports.

The ruling

The court rejected the appeal on Tuesday, 3 November 2026. Ibori had sought to reduce the amount by about £4.3 million. The order was issued by Southwark Crown Court in July 2023, directing him to pay or face an eight-year prison term in default. The appeal had delayed enforcement, which can now proceed unless he takes the case to the UK Supreme Court.

Ibori's response

Ibori reportedly said he was consulting his lawyers on his next steps and expressed frustration at how long the proceedings had taken.

Background

Ibori governed Delta State from 1999 to 2007. He was arrested in Dubai and extradited to Britain in 2011. In 2012 he pleaded guilty in a London court to laundering tens of millions of dollars of public funds through British banks and properties, and was sentenced to 13 years in prison. He was released in late 2016 after serving about half of the sentence and returned to Nigeria in 2017. The confiscation proceedings followed investigations by Nigerian and British anti-corruption authorities, and Nigeria has pushed for the recovered funds to be returned.

Why it matters

The case is one of the most significant examples of international cooperation to recover stolen public funds from Nigeria.

Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly removed the David Mark-led caretaker leadership of the ADC and nullified its decisions, including the nomination of Atiku Abubakar as presidential candidate. The ruling came a day after Atiku's Sokoto rally.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign. Neither Atiku nor the party had responded publicly at the time of reporting.

Cristiano Ronaldo retires from international football at 41

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Cristiano Ronaldo has announced his retirement from international football, ending a 23-year career with Portugal in which he became the all-time leading scorer in men's international football. He announced it in a letter read in an Instagram video.

Read more

Cristiano Ronaldo has announced his retirement from international football, bringing to an end one of the most remarkable careers in the history of the game.

The announcement

The 41-year-old shared the news on Thursday, 5 November 2026, in an Instagram video in which he read a letter to the Portuguese people. He said the day had come to hand back the national team shirt and captain's armband, which he had never felt belonged only to him, and that the decision was made with the heart as well as the head. He described a journey that began in August 2003 and said that when he looks back he sees not only goals and trophies but the people who supported him, including Portuguese emigrants around the world.

A record career

Ronaldo made his Portugal debut in 2003 and went on to become the most-capped player and all-time top scorer in men's international football. He led Portugal to victory at Euro 2016, their first major title, and also won the UEFA Nations League twice. He played at six World Cups.

Club career continues

Ronaldo will continue playing for Al Nassr in Saudi Arabia. Last week he scored a hat-trick for the club, taking his career total to 985 goals, and he has said he wants to reach 1,000.

How people reacted

Fans, players and clubs around the world paid tribute, with many calling him one of the greatest players of all time.
