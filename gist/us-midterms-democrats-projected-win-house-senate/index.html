Democrats projected to win both House and Senate in blow to Trump

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The Democratic Party is projected to have won control of both chambers of the United States Congress in the 2026 midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November, according to projections by US television networks reported early on Wednesday.

The results

According to the projections, the Democrats have taken the House of Representatives and the Senate. The Senate results were described as particularly striking, with Democrats reportedly winning seats in states that President Donald Trump carried by wide margins in 2024, including Texas, South Carolina, Ohio, Kansas and Iowa. Texas had reportedly not elected a Democrat to statewide office since 1994. Final results in some races may take days to confirm.

Why voters turned

Analysts have pointed to Trump's low approval ratings, voter anger over tariffs and inflation, and the long-running conflict with Iran as reasons for the swing. On the eve of the vote, US forces carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, while Trump told supporters that Iran might eventually have to be given 'the hammer'.

Reactions

Trump sought to distance himself from the results, writing on social media that he was not on the ballot and that Republicans would have won in a landslide if he had been. He had held rallies in 20 states in the final weeks of the campaign. In a later interview, he said he was willing to accept the results and would look to work with the Democrats. Former President Barack Obama said voters had sent a clear message that they want politics that brings people together, and praised candidates who won in areas once seen as long shots. Among the night's firsts, Abdul El-Sayed became the first Muslim US senator, Keisha Lance Bottoms became the first Black woman elected governor of a US state, and Gina Hinojosa defeated Texas governor Greg Abbott.

Trump on the Iran war

Speaking to reporters at the White House on Wednesday, Trump said it was largely true that the war with Iran had contributed to the Republican losses, as Vice President JD Vance had suggested, pointing to the rise in petrol prices it caused. He said he was not considering removing Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, and that he was open to working with Democrats as long as they were not destructive, adding that he still had the power to veto their bills.

What it means

With control of both chambers, Democrats will be able to block much of the President's legislative agenda, launch investigations with the power to compel testimony, and hold up his nominees, including judges, during the last two years of his term. Trump remains president until January 2029.

Why it matters for Nigeria

US policy on trade, aid and immigration affects Nigeria directly. A Democratic Congress could push back on measures such as visa restrictions and changes to trade programmes, including this week's decision to remove South Africa and Rwanda from the AGOA duty-free scheme. The White House had not formally responded to the projections at the time of reporting.

  • Democrats are projected to control both the House and Senate.
  • They reportedly won Senate seats in Texas, Iowa and Kansas.
  • Trump remains president until January 2029.

Microsoft outage disrupts Outlook, Teams and Copilot in 74 countries

13 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

A global Microsoft 365 outage that began on Thursday has disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot for users in 74 countries and territories, with nearly 140,000 problem reports logged at its peak. Microsoft says it is working to restore services.

Read more

Microsoft is working to restore its services after a global outage disrupted Outlook email, Teams and its Copilot AI assistant for users around the world.

What happened

The outage began on Thursday, 12 November 2026, and affected Microsoft 365 services in 74 countries and territories, according to reports on Friday. Many users were unable to load their inboxes or send and receive emails, while others had trouble with Teams calls and Copilot. Reports of Outlook problems on an outage-tracking website peaked at nearly 140,000 worldwide.

Microsoft's explanation

Microsoft initially blamed the problem on unusually high traffic across parts of its infrastructure, which caused connection errors. The company said it had paused some deployments while it worked on a fix.

Why it matters

Outlook and Teams are widely used by businesses and organisations around the world, including in Nigeria.

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu becomes New Jersey's lieutenant governor

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu has been appointed lieutenant governor of the US state of New Jersey, becoming the first immigrant to hold the post. She will also serve as Secretary of State.

Read more

Shirley U. Emehelu, a lawyer born in Nigeria, has been appointed lieutenant governor of New Jersey in the United States.

The appointment

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill appointed Emehelu on Monday, 9 November 2026, making her the state's fifth lieutenant governor and the first immigrant to hold the office. She was sworn in at the Statehouse in Trenton by the state's Chief Justice, Stuart Rabner. The appointment also means that, for the first time in New Jersey's history, women hold the state's two highest executive offices at the same time. Emehelu will also serve as Secretary of State, a role that covers government administration and oversight of elections.

Why she was chosen

Sherrill described Emehelu as an experienced public servant and pointed to her record prosecuting financial crimes, protecting consumers, defending civil rights and tackling government waste. The governor also praised her leadership as acting Secretary of State, including overseeing the state's recent elections.

Her Nigerian roots

Emehelu, who moved from Nigeria to the United States as a child, said she was proud of what the appointment means for Black women, immigrants and people who share her Nigerian heritage, and expressed hope that it would inspire young people to serve. She said she would work with the governor on the cost of living and economic opportunity.

Sweden returns 39 Benin Bronzes to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Sweden has handed back 39 Benin artefacts taken during the British invasion of Benin in 1897, at a ceremony in Lagos. Six of the objects will stay in Sweden on a 10-year loan.

Read more

Sweden has returned 39 Benin Bronzes and other artefacts to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted from the Benin Kingdom.

The handover

Representatives of Sweden's National Museums of World Culture formally handed over the objects at a ceremony in Lagos on Thursday, 12 November 2026. They will be received by Nigeria's National Commission for Museums and Monuments. The collection includes cultural objects known as Emwin Arre. Six of the 39 artefacts will remain in Sweden under a 10-year loan arrangement.

Why it matters

The objects were among thousands taken by British forces who attacked and burned Benin City in 1897 before bringing the kingdom under British rule. Michael Barrett, curator for Africa at Sweden's National Museums of World Culture, described the return as an important step in addressing colonial-era looting of cultural property.

Background

Nigeria has been campaigning for decades for the return of the Benin Bronzes, which are held in museums and private collections around the world. The Netherlands returned 119 objects in 2025, and Germany handed over 20 Benin Bronzes in 2022. Many more remain abroad.

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to lead its election observation mission to Nigeria.

The appointment

The appointment was announced in a statement from Akufo-Addo's office on Thursday, 12 November 2026. He will oversee the deployment of ECOWAS observers for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections, scheduled for 16 January 2027. The mission will monitor the electoral process and report on how the polls are conducted.

Who is Akufo-Addo

Akufo-Addo served two terms as Ghana's president, from 2017 to January 2025.

Background

International attention on Nigeria's election is growing. The European Union has raised concerns about violence and hate speech, and a senior US State Department official told Congress this week that the vote could spur violence. INEC says preparations are on track, with non-sensitive materials already being moved to the states.
