Edo police summon council chairman over alleged attack on NDC office in Afuze

20 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Edo State Police Command has summoned the chairman of Owan East Local Government Area, Ernest Oyaole Aitalegbe, over his alleged link to an attack on the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) secretariat in Afuze.

What police said

Police spokesperson ASP Eno Ikoedem said in a statement on Friday, 20 November 2026, that investigations had begun into the incident. The attack reportedly involved armed men suspected to be operatives of the Edo State Security Network.

The council chairman's response to the allegation was not included in the reports. He has not been charged with any offence.

Court freezes $100m linked to Governors' Forum in Paris Club fees dispute

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

An Abuja High Court has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum, pending a dispute over legal and consultancy fees tied to Paris Club refunds. The order does not decide who owns the money.

Read more

A High Court of the Federal Capital Territory in Abuja has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum (NGF), in a dispute over unpaid legal and consultancy fees connected to the Paris Club refunds.

The order

Justice O.C. Agbaza granted the interim freezing order on 19 November 2026, following an application by a lawyer against the NGF's incorporated trustees and 29 commercial banks. The order covers a single total sum of $100 million, about ₦135.6 billion, across the affected accounts.

What it means

The order preserves the disputed money until the court hears an application for an injunction or makes a further order. It does not decide who owns the funds.

The claim

The lawyer is seeking payment under an indemnity he says the NGF issued in December 2018, after the federal government paid $100 million to the forum to hold pending a decision on liability for professional fees. No response from the NGF was included in the reports.

Seyi Law admits his finances were better before Tinubu, but backs reforms

25 Nov 2026, 11:41 GMT

Comedian Seyi Law has reportedly admitted that living standards have dropped under President Tinubu and that his own finances were better before 2023. He still defended the government's reforms, saying good policies take years to stabilise.

Read more

Comedian Seyi Law, a known supporter of President Bola Tinubu, has reportedly admitted that living standards have fallen sharply under the current administration.

What he said

Speaking on a podcast hosted by Nedu, Seyi Law is said to have acknowledged that his personal finances were much better before Tinubu took office in 2023, and that it would be hypocritical to say Nigerians are not suffering.

Still backing the reforms

He nevertheless defended the government's economic reforms, arguing that Nigerians would benefit in the long run. Comparing Nigeria with China, he said major national transformations often come with heavy early sacrifices. When the host asked whether that meant Nigerians must suffer greatly first, he said people must feel the pain of a policy before reaping its benefits, adding that good policies usually stabilise after 10 to 12 years.

How people reacted

His remarks have sparked debate online.
