Petrol prices at Lagos depots rise by up to ₦60 a litre as diesel falls

8 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Petrol prices at major fuel depots in Lagos rose by as much as ₦60 per litre this week, while diesel prices fell, according to market data reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026.

The figures

Between Monday and Friday, the average petrol price across seven comparable depots in Lagos rose by about ₦41 per litre, while diesel fell by about ₦33. Some of the biggest increases were recorded at African Terminal, Integrated and A.A. Rano, which each raised their prices from ₦1,220 to ₦1,280 per litre. Aiteo raised its price by ₦56 to ₦1,277, Rain Oil by ₦55 to ₦1,280 and Nipco by ₦29 to ₦1,279, while MRS Tincan cut its price by ₦30 to ₦1,280. Similar movements were seen at depots in Warri and Port Harcourt.

Crude prices fell

The increases came even though international crude oil prices fell during the week, with Brent crude reportedly dropping by almost 5 per cent. Domestic supply conditions and marketers' costs also affect local prices.

Background

Depot prices are what marketers pay before selling to motorists, so changes usually feed through to filling-station prices within days. Petrol prices are now set by the market after the removal of the fuel subsidy in 2023, and have fluctuated with competition between the Dangote refinery, NNPC and importers. President Bola Tinubu said in Uyo this week that the government will never return to the subsidy. Cooking gas prices have also reportedly risen to about ₦1,900 per kilogram.

Cooking gas also up

Cooking gas prices have also risen. Retail prices reached about ₦1,800 per kilogram in Lagos and Ogun states after depot prices rose from ₦1,100 to as much as ₦1,445 per kilogram, an increase of about 31 per cent, according to reports on Sunday. Most depots were selling at ₦1,445, while Dangote's depot was selling at ₦1,150. At ₦1,800 per kilogram, refilling a 12.5kg cylinder costs about ₦22,500.

Pump prices dip

By Monday, some pump prices had eased slightly. NNPC cut its Abuja price to ₦1,325 per litre after a price move by the Dangote refinery, and other Abuja outlets cut by about ₦10 to ₦1,410. In Lagos, MRS reduced its price from ₦1,350 to ₦1,331 and NNPC from ₦1,360 to ₦1,340, though prices in both cities remain above ₦1,300 per litre.

What it means

Motorists may see higher pump prices in the coming days, while businesses that rely on diesel could get some relief.

  • Some depots raised petrol from ₦1,220 to ₦1,280 a litre.
  • Diesel prices fell during the week.
  • Global crude prices dropped.

UK now buys more refined fuel than crude oil from Nigeria, data shows

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

UK trade figures show Britain bought £1.02 billion of refined petroleum products from Nigeria in the year to June 2026, more than double the year before, overtaking crude oil imports, as Dangote Refinery expands exports.

Read more

Nigeria's petroleum trade with the United Kingdom has shifted sharply towards refined fuel, according to UK government figures.

The figures

The UK's trade and investment factsheet, released on 6 November 2026 and reported this weekend, shows Britain bought £1.02 billion worth of refined petroleum products from Nigeria in the 12 months to June 2026. That was more than double the figure a year earlier and made up 53.5 per cent of all UK goods imports from Nigeria. Over the same period, UK purchases of Nigerian crude oil fell by 56.2 per cent to £550.3 million, meaning refined fuel exports exceeded crude shipments by about £467 million. Nigeria recorded a petroleum trade surplus of about £245 million with Britain.

Why it matters

The change reflects the growth of the Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Lagos, which can process 650,000 barrels a day and has been expanding its exports.

Background

The government has also announced plans for a new NNPC refinery in Edo State.

FG plans 24-hour electricity pilot for Lagos, Ogun and Abuja

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

Power Minister Joseph Tegbe says the Lagos–Ogun axis and the Abuja distribution area have been chosen for a pilot scheme to provide 24-hour electricity, but no start date has been set.

Read more

The federal government says it plans to pilot round-the-clock electricity supply in parts of Lagos, Ogun and Abuja.

What the minister said

Speaking at a luncheon with senior media editors in Lagos on Friday, 13 November 2026, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said the Lagos–Ogun axis and the Abuja electricity distribution franchise area had been selected for the proposed 24-hour supply pilot. He said the areas account for more than half of Nigeria's electricity demand, and that the scheme would focus on economic centres where reliable power could boost industry and reduce dependence on costly generators.

Conditions

The minister said implementation would depend on technical readiness and commercial viability, and no start date was announced.

Background

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi this week promised 10,000 megawatts within four years, while a cleric called on the minister to resign over the power situation. Another state has separately announced a target of 24-hour power by 2029.

NNPC cuts petrol prices in Lagos, Abuja, Kaduna and Sokoto

15 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

NNPC has reportedly cut pump prices at its outlets in Lagos, Abuja, Kaduna and Sokoto by between ₦52 and ₦66 per litre, with Lagos now at ₦1,289, the lowest of the four.

Read more

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC) has reduced petrol pump prices at its retail stations in four cities, according to its latest price sheet reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026.

The new prices

In Lagos, the price fell by ₦61, from ₦1,350 to ₦1,289 per litre, the lowest of the four locations. In Abuja it dropped by ₦55, from ₦1,380 to ₦1,325. Kaduna motorists will now pay ₦1,329, down ₦52 from ₦1,381, while Sokoto saw the biggest cut, ₦66, with the price falling from ₦1,378 to ₦1,312.

Why prices are falling

The cuts follow a series of reductions in wholesale depot prices and Dangote Refinery's switch to pricing linked to international petrol prices, which reportedly brought its loading price down to ₦1,250 per litre. MRS stations and depot owners have also lowered their prices in recent days.

Background

A recent comparison listed Nigeria among the countries with the cheapest petrol in West Africa.
