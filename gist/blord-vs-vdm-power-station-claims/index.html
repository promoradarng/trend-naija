Blord vs VDM: who is fooling who? Blord claims his power station can run a fridge, fan, TV and treadmill for 12 hours

29 Oct 2026, 16:54 GMT

Barely 24 hours after VeryDarkMan demonstrated the performance of his Ratel power station, Blord has come out with his own claims, and Nigerians are already raising serious questions.

According to Blord, his 1,000W-rated power station can power a big refrigerator, a 128W industrial fan, a television and even a treadmill for 12 solid hours!

Wait a minute! How is that possible?

VDM tested his 1,200W-rated power station, and it lasted 5 hours and 17 minutes under the conditions of his demonstration.

Now Blord is claiming his 1,000W unit can run all those appliances for 12 hours, without showing a complete, uninterrupted test to back up the claim.

Blord, Nigerians need evidence, not just words!

However, there is something important here. A power station's watt rating tells us how much power it can deliver at once, while its battery capacity, measured in watt-hours (Wh), determines how long it can run appliances. So battery life cannot be judged from the 1,000W and 1,200W ratings alone.

Blord, show us the battery capacity, connect all those appliances and let the test run for 12 hours. No editing, no shortcuts!

VDM has shown his demonstration. Nigerians are waiting to see yours.

Do you believe Blord's 12-hour claim, or should he prove it with a proper live test?

All is fair in war, but has VeryDarkMan gone too far with Davido?

9 Nov 2026, 17:57 GMT

Amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VeryDarkMan has reportedly brought up the deaths of the singer's late friends, a painful chapter long surrounded by unproven rumours. Criticism is fair, but should personal tragedy ever be used as ammunition?

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) does not seem to believe in fighting fairly. When he has a disagreement with someone, he appears willing to go to any length to make his point, even if it means bringing up some of the most painful moments in that person's life.

A painful chapter

Over the years, Davido has lost several close friends and associates in tragic circumstances. Their deaths set off all kinds of rumours and conspiracy theories online.

Some people even claimed the deaths were connected to rituals or sacrifices for fame and success. None of those claims was ever proven, and there is no credible evidence linking Davido to any of their deaths.

Imagine losing people who were very close to you, only to have strangers turn their deaths into accusations against you. Davido has lived with those memories for years. He has carried on with his music career, built his family and moved forward with his life, but moving forward does not mean the pain has disappeared.

Reopening old wounds

Now, amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VDM has reportedly brought those painful memories back into public discussion. This is where things appear to be getting out of hand.

You can criticise Davido's music, lifestyle, public statements or behaviour. You can disagree with him and challenge his opinions. That is perfectly normal. But bringing up the deaths of his close friends because of a disagreement raises serious questions.

What exactly is the intention? Is it to expose wrongdoing, to make a legitimate point, or simply to hurt someone where he is most vulnerable?

Where are the limits?

VDM has built a reputation for confronting people without holding back, and his supporters may argue he is only giving his opponents the same treatment they give others. But surely there should be limits.

Davido is not above criticism, and neither is VeryDarkMan. Both are public figures who should be held accountable for their actions. Using someone's personal tragedies as ammunition in a public quarrel, however, is something else entirely.

There are some experiences people never fully recover from. They may laugh and smile in public, but that does not mean they have forgotten. If winning an argument requires reopening another person's deepest wounds, perhaps the argument has already gone too far.

Over to you

People say all is fair in love and war, but does that mean we should abandon all compassion when we disagree with someone? Has VDM crossed the line with Davido, or is this just two public figures fighting without limits?

US Congress to hold hearing on Nigeria's protection of Christians and the Middle Belt

10 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

A US House subcommittee chaired by Congressman Chris Smith will hold a hearing on Thursday, 12 November, to assess Nigeria's efforts to protect Christians and curb violence in the Middle Belt, with State Department officials due to testify.

Read more

A United States congressional committee will hold a hearing this week to review Nigeria's record on protecting Christians and tackling violence in the Middle Belt.

The hearing

The House Foreign Affairs Africa Subcommittee will hold the session on Thursday, 12 November 2026, according to a notice from the office of its chairman, Republican Congressman Chris Smith of New Jersey, issued on Monday. The hearing is titled 'One Year Later: Assessing Nigeria's Efforts to Protect Christians and Secure the Middle Belt'.

What will be discussed

The hearing will examine religious violence by extremist groups, including Boko Haram, and how effectively the Nigerian government has responded to attacks on vulnerable communities. It will also consider what measures the United States could press for to make sure perpetrators are held accountable and to prevent future attacks on Christians and non-radical Muslims. The first panel will hear from two senior State Department officials, Frank Garcia, assistant secretary of state for African affairs, and Riley Barnes, assistant secretary of state for democracy, human rights and labour, followed by outside experts.

Background

The title refers to developments around a year ago, when the US designated Nigeria a 'Country of Particular Concern' over religious freedom. The Nigerian government has rejected claims of a targeted genocide against Christians, saying insecurity affects people of all faiths, and has pointed to military operations against armed groups. Attacks have continued in states such as Plateau and Benue, including a deadly attack in Barkin Ladi at the weekend.

Republican congressman files articles of impeachment against Pete Hegseth over Iran war

10 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Republican Congressman Thomas Massie has introduced articles of impeachment against US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, accusing him of waging war against Iran without congressional authorisation, as criticism of the conflict grows after the midterms.

Read more

Articles of impeachment have been introduced in the US House of Representatives against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

What happened

Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky brought the resolution on Monday, 9 November 2026, as Congress returned for a 'lame-duck' session after the midterm elections. Massie accused Hegseth of high crimes and misdemeanours and abuse of power, alleging that he violated the War Powers Resolution by carrying out military operations against Iran and other countries without a declaration of war or specific authorisation from Congress.

Background

The United States has been in a conflict with Iran since late February, with US forces carrying out strikes on Iranian targets as recently as last week. President Donald Trump has insisted the US is not at war, but has acknowledged that the conflict contributed to his party's heavy losses in the midterms, partly by pushing up petrol prices.

What happens next

Impeachment would require a majority vote in the House, followed by a trial in the Senate.
