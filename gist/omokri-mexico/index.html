"Buhari promised me APC presidential ticket in 2022, I regret listening to him": Rotimi Amaechi

8 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

Former Rivers governor and ex-Transport Minister Rotimi Amaechi has reportedly said the late President Muhammadu Buhari promised him the APC presidential ticket in 2022, a promise he now regrets believing. Amaechi went on to lose the primary to Bola Tinubu.

Read more

Former Rivers State Governor and ex-Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi has reportedly expressed regret over his decision to trust former President Muhammadu Buhari during the race for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential ticket in 2022.

The promise

According to the statement attributed to Amaechi, Buhari had promised him the party's presidential ticket, a promise he now regrets believing.

The claim raises fresh questions about the political arrangements and behind-the-scenes discussions that took place before the APC primary that eventually produced Bola Ahmed Tinubu as the party's candidate.

Buhari died in July 2025, so he is no longer able to respond to Amaechi's account.

How the 2022 race played out

Amaechi was among the prominent politicians who contested the ticket in 2022. Despite being a serving minister in Buhari's government and a close associate of the President, he lost the primary to Tinubu.

As the sitting President and leader of the ruling party at the time, Buhari's influence was considerable, although the final decision rested with the party's delegates.

Loyalty without reward

For Amaechi, the disappointment appears to go beyond losing an election. His comments suggest he believed he had received an assurance from someone whose word carried enormous political weight.

That points to one of the oldest lessons in Nigerian politics: loyalty does not always guarantee reward.

Amaechi served under Buhari for nearly seven years as Minister of Transportation, and he was one of the key figures who helped build the coalition that brought the APC to power in 2015. Yet when his chance came to seek the presidency, things did not turn out as he had apparently expected.

The questions that remain

Did Buhari actually make such a commitment, and if he did, what happened between that promise and the primary?

Was Amaechi politically outplayed, or did he place too much confidence in a promise that was never guaranteed?

Whatever the answer, his regret offers another glimpse into the complicated world of Nigerian party politics, where today's allies can have very different interests when power is at stake.

Amaechi says Buhari promised in writing to back him at 2022 APC primary

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

ADC vice-presidential candidate Rotimi Amaechi says former President Buhari assured him in writing that he would name him as his preferred successor in 2022, and that relying on the promise was his biggest mistake. He lost the APC ticket to Tinubu.

Read more

Rotimi Amaechi, the vice-presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) for 2027, says former President Muhammadu Buhari promised to back him as his preferred candidate at the 2022 All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential primary.

What he said

Speaking in a radio interview in Lagos on Friday, 6 November 2026, Amaechi said Buhari had given him the assurance in writing. Because of it, he said, he held back from campaigning, lobbying delegates or making political deals before the primary, which he now considers his biggest mistake. He said his refusal to promise positions such as the vice-presidency and the post of Secretary to the Government of the Federation to governors cost him support.

Claims about religion

Amaechi also accused President Bola Tinubu's camp of appealing to religious identity to win Northern delegates, alleging that a delegation of Muslim politicians from the South-West urged governors in the North-West to back Tinubu on religious grounds. He said he would never use religion to win an election. Tinubu's camp had not responded to the claims at the time of writing.

Background

Tinubu won the June 2022 APC primary at Eagle Square in Abuja with 1,271 votes to Amaechi's 316, and went on to win the 2023 presidential election. Amaechi, a former Rivers governor, directed Buhari's successful 2015 campaign and served as his Minister of Transportation. Buhari, who died in 2025, did not publicly endorse any aspirant at the primary. Amaechi is now Atiku Abubakar's running mate, though a recent court judgment voided Atiku's nomination, a ruling the ADC has appealed.

Tinubu says naira would have been worthless without his reforms, rules out subsidy return

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Tinubu told a reception in Uyo that the naira would have become valueless if he had not removed the fuel subsidy, and that Nigeria will never go back to it. He said 27 states could not pay salaries when he took office.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has defended his removal of the fuel subsidy, saying the naira would have become worthless if his government had kept it.

What he said

Speaking at a state reception in his honour in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State, on Friday, 6 November 2026, after an APC rally at the Godswill Akpabio International Stadium, Tinubu said 27 of the 36 state governors could not pay salaries when he took office, let alone pensions and gratuities. He argued that his reforms had moved the country away from the risk of bankruptcy, and said that without them Nigerians would by now need wheelbarrows to carry their money. He put the exchange rate at about ₦1,300 to the US dollar and said Nigeria would never go back to the subsidy.

The visit

The reception followed a day in which the President inaugurated several projects in Akwa Ibom, including a new terminal at Victor Attah International Airport, and approved a request by Governor Umo Eno for the Ibom Deep Seaport. Vice-President Kashim Shettima used the same visit to criticise NDC candidate Peter Obi.

Background

Tinubu announced the end of the petrol subsidy in his inauguration speech in May 2023, and his government also unified the exchange rate. The reforms led to sharp rises in fuel and food prices. Opposition candidates, including Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi, have criticised the way the reforms were carried out, while the government says they were needed to save the economy and has introduced relief measures such as the ₦50,000 monthly grant to selected households.

Why it matters

The cost of living is expected to be one of the main issues in the January 2027 election.

Obasanjo says foreign leaders' warnings saved him from Abacha's plot

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Former President Olusegun Obasanjo says Sani Abacha had planned to kill him, Shehu Yar'Adua and MKO Abiola, and credits Jimmy Carter's intervention and a warning from former British prime minister James Callaghan for his survival.

Read more

Former President Olusegun Obasanjo has spoken about how he survived imprisonment under the military regime of the late General Sani Abacha.

What he said

Speaking in Yoruba at a book event at the University of Ibadan, in a video shared on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Obasanjo said Abacha had decided to kill him along with the late General Shehu Musa Yar'Adua and Chief MKO Abiola, the acknowledged winner of the annulled 12 June 1993 presidential election. He said Abacha had confided the plan to the late Ibadan philanthropist Chief Alao Arisekola.

Foreign intervention

Obasanjo said former US President Jimmy Carter travelled to Nigeria and spoke to Abacha on his behalf, while former British prime minister James Callaghan went further, warning Abacha that he would face death himself if he went ahead. Obasanjo also said prayer played a central role in his survival, describing himself as a committed prayer warrior in detention.

Background

Obasanjo, a former military head of state, was jailed in 1995 over an alleged coup plot, which he always denied. Yar'Adua died in detention in 1997, and Abiola died in custody in July 1998, a month after Abacha's own death. Obasanjo was released after Abacha died and was elected president in 1999, serving two terms. At the same event, he also warned against sending children into armed conflict, drawing on his experience in the civil war.
