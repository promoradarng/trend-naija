Ebonyi women parade man in wheelbarrow over alleged repeated beating of his wife

7 Nov 2026, 18:58 GMT

Women in Ishiagu, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly paraded a man around the community in a wheelbarrow over claims that he repeatedly beat his wife. The unusual punishment has sparked debate about domestic violence and community justice.

Read more

Women in Ishiagu community, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly taken an unusual step to show their anger over domestic violence, publicly parading a man accused of repeatedly beating his wife.

Pushed around in a wheelbarrow

According to reports circulating online, the women placed the man in a wheelbarrow and pushed him around the community, apparently determined to make an example of him over his alleged treatment of his wife.

Photos show a large group of women surrounding the man, who sits in the wheelbarrow in a yellow T-shirt as several women push and guide it along a dirt path, with one carrying a calabash.

"Enough is enough"

The incident has drawn attention because of the unusual punishment the women chose. They appeared to have reached a point where they could no longer tolerate the alleged abuse.

In many communities, domestic violence is still treated as a private family matter, and victims are sometimes encouraged to endure abuse for the sake of their marriage. The women of Ishiagu reportedly decided that enough was enough.

Justice or humiliation?

Their action raises an important question about how communities should respond when a woman is repeatedly subjected to violence in her own home.

Many people may applaud the women for standing up against domestic abuse. Others may question whether publicly humiliating an accused person, who has not been tried, is the right approach.

The full circumstances of the incident, including the man's side of the story and whether the authorities have stepped in, remain unclear.

Jerry Eze's protocol officer sparks outrage at Ibiyeomie's church dedication

7 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A video of a member of Pastor Jerry Eze's security team reacting roughly to a man who knelt to greet the cleric at the Hand of God Cathedral dedication in Port Harcourt has drawn criticism online.

Read more

A video from the dedication of Pastor David Ibiyeomie's 120,000-seat Hand of God Cathedral in Port Harcourt has sparked debate online, after a member of Pastor Jerry Eze's security team was seen reacting roughly to a worshipper.

What the video shows

The footage, shared on Facebook on Friday, 6 November 2026, shows the founder of Streams of Joy International arriving at the event. A man dropped to his knees as a sign of respect as the cleric walked past, and Pastor Eze briefly acknowledged him. However, one of his protocol officers then reportedly acted towards the man in a way many viewers felt was unnecessarily aggressive and disrespectful.

Background

The dedication of the Hand of God Cathedral, led by Bishop David Oyedepo, drew many of Nigeria's best-known pastors. Another widely shared video from the same event showed Jerry Eze kneeling to greet Oyedepo, Ibiyeomie and other senior ministers. Eze is known for his popular online prayer programme, 'New Season Prophetic Prayers and Declarations', which draws large audiences on YouTube and social media.

How people reacted

Many Nigerians criticised the protocol officer's behaviour, saying it was out of place at a celebratory religious event and that church leaders' security teams should treat members of the public with respect. Pastor Eze had not publicly commented at the time of writing.

Bandits reportedly kill 12 members of one family in Zamfara village attack

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Twelve members of the same family were reportedly killed when bandits set fire to the building where they had taken refuge during an attack on Gidan Taba village in Maradun LGA, Zamfara State. Other residents were said to have been kidnapped.

Read more

Twelve members of one family have reportedly been killed in an attack by bandits on a village in Zamfara State.

What happened

The attack took place late on Saturday, 7 November 2026, in Gidan Taba village, Dosara Birnin Kaya ward, in Maradun Local Government Area, according to residents. A resident said the head of the family had gathered his relatives into a single room to try to stop them from being kidnapped. When the attackers found the family and they refused to come out, the bandits reportedly set the building on fire, and all 12 people inside died. An unknown number of other residents were reportedly abducted during the raid. The victims were buried according to Islamic rites on Sunday morning.

Official response

The police and the Zamfara State Government had not publicly commented on the attack at the time of writing.

Background

Zamfara is one of the states worst affected by banditry in Nigeria's North-West, with armed groups carrying out killings, mass kidnappings and attacks on farming communities. Last week, bandits ambushed a bus carrying police officers on the Sokoto–Gusau road, reportedly killing and abducting some of them. Security forces have carried out repeated operations in the state's forests, but attacks on villages continue.

Amaechi warns court ruling against ADC could trigger unrest if mishandled

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

ADC vice-presidential candidate Rotimi Amaechi has warned that the court judgment removing the party's leadership and voiding Atiku Abubakar's nomination could lead to serious unrest, even civil war, if the dispute is not properly managed.

Read more

Rotimi Amaechi, the vice-presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has warned that the court ruling against the party's leadership could cause serious unrest if it is not handled carefully.

What he said

In comments reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Amaechi said the judgment that nullified the party's leadership and the process that produced Atiku Abubakar as presidential candidate could lead to civil war if the dispute is not properly managed. He did not set out in detail how he believed the crisis should be resolved.

The ruling

On Thursday, Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja ruled that the national working committee led by former Senate President David Mark was invalid. The judgment also nullified the emergence of former Interior Minister Rauf Aregbesola as national secretary, and decisions taken by the Mark-led leadership, including the process that produced Atiku as candidate. The court directed INEC not to recognise decisions taken at the party's national executive committee meeting of 27 July 2025. The suit was filed by Nafiu Gombe, a former deputy national chairman of the party.

Background

The ADC has appealed the judgment and asked INEC not to act on it until the appeal is heard. Atiku has vowed to stay in the race and accused the government of using the courts against the opposition. Amaechi, a former Rivers governor and transport minister, this week also said former President Muhammadu Buhari had promised in writing to back him in 2022.

What happens next

Attention is now on the Court of Appeal and on how INEC responds before the presidential election on 16 January 2027.
