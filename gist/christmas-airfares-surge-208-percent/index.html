Christmas airfares to South-East and South-South jump by up to 208%

5 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Nigerians planning to fly home for Christmas and the New Year face sharply higher fares, according to booking data reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The new prices

Economy tickets for flights departing on 20 and 21 December on routes linking Lagos and Abuja to cities in the South-East and South-South have reportedly risen to between ₦196,000 and ₦337,500, with some fares up by as much as 208 per cent. One airline was reportedly charging about ₦310,500 for a one-way Lagos–Benin flight, up from about ₦120,000. Air Peace, Aero Contractors and United Nigeria Airlines were among the carriers with the steepest increases.

Why fares rise

Demand for flights to the South-East and South-South surges every December as many people travel home for the holidays. Airlines say high costs of aviation fuel, foreign exchange for maintenance and leasing, and limited aircraft numbers push up fares. Senate President Godswill Akpabio said this week that even politicians are struggling to find aircraft to hire.

Background

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) previously accused some domestic airlines of fixing prices during the 2025 festive season. Airlines denied wrongdoing and blamed operating costs. Many travellers choose road travel instead, despite security concerns on some highways.

What it means

Consumer groups are urging regulators to monitor fares, while travel experts advise booking early to avoid the highest prices.

  • Some fares have risen by up to 208%.
  • Lagos–Benin now costs about ₦310,500.
  • The FCCPC previously accused airlines of price-fixing.

FG orders ministries and agencies to freeze staff numbers for 2027

17 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The federal government has reportedly told ministries, departments and agencies to keep their workforce at current levels in 2027. Agencies that ignore the new reporting rules risk losing budget provisions.

Read more

The federal government has reportedly directed all ministries, departments and agencies (MDAs) to keep their workforce at current levels for the 2027 fiscal year.

The circular

The directive is contained in a circular dated 9 November 2026 from the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, titled the 2027 Workforce Budget Call Circular. It was addressed to ministers, permanent secretaries, service chiefs and heads of federal institutions.

The new rules

Under the circular, MDAs may not create new departments, units or other structures without clearance from the Head of Service. Every workforce budget proposal must include the institution's approved organisational structure, and no vacancies may be created in structures that have not been officially approved.

The government is said to have singled out posts on Grade Levels 15 to 17, the senior ranks of the federal civil service, warning agencies against creating unauthorised vacancies at those levels. Agencies that can show a genuine staffing need must back their requests with evidence.

Penalties

Institutions that fail to comply with the new reporting requirements reportedly risk having their budget provisions cut off.

Gas explosion at Lagos filling station spreads fire to neighbouring school, no casualties

16 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A gas explosion at an NNPC filling station in Ejigbo, Lagos, on Sunday reportedly started a fire that spread to a school next door. Firefighters contained it and no one was hurt, as the school was closed for the weekend.

Read more

A gas explosion at an NNPC filling station at Savannah Bus Stop, Command, in the Ejigbo area of Lagos State reportedly caused a fire that spread to a neighbouring private school on Sunday, 15 November 2026.

What happened

The explosion is said to have happened at about 1pm. The Controller-General of the Lagos State Fire and Rescue Service, Margaret Adeseye, confirmed the incident and said the force of the blast caused the fire to spread quickly to Saint Lawrence Greater Height School, which shares a boundary with the station, affecting parts of the premises.

The response

Adeseye said the service received a distress call at 12:50pm and reached the scene at 1:13pm. Firefighters from the Ejigbo, Ikeja and Bolade fire stations were deployed, and they stopped the fire from spreading to other parts of the school.

No casualties

No one was injured or killed, as the school was closed for the weekend. The fire service said preliminary investigations into the cause of the explosion were under way.

Police say 78 arrested over Tinubu convoy incident, now being released after presidential clemency

16 Nov 2026, 17:45 GMT

The FCT Police Command says it arrested 78 people after the president's motorcade was reportedly pelted with stones in Garki, Abuja, on Friday. It says the suspects are now being warned and released after the Presidency extended clemency to them.

Read more

The Federal Capital Territory Police Command has reportedly said that 78 people were arrested in connection with an incident involving President Bola Tinubu's motorcade in Abuja last Friday, and that all of them are now being released on the Presidency's directive.

What the police said

In a statement on Monday, 16 November 2026, the command's spokesperson, CSP Josephine Adeh, said the incident happened on Friday, 13 November, shortly after the President observed Friday prayers at a mosque in the Garki District. According to the police account, some people threw stones and other objects at the departing convoy, damaging several vehicles and injuring some security personnel.

The police said a joint security team responded at the scene and that later intelligence-led raids in the area brought the total number of people arrested to 78.

Clemency and release

The statement said the Presidency had extended clemency to those involved and directed that the suspects be warned and released. The command said they were going through the release process.

Earlier denial

The Presidency had earlier dismissed reports that the convoy was attacked, while some opposition figures, including former senator Dino Melaye, insisted the incident happened and shared videos online. The police statement is the first official account to describe arrests in connection with it.

Call for calm

The FCT Commissioner of Police, CP Ahmed Mohammed Sanusi, warned against violence and destruction of property as campaigns for the 2027 general elections intensify. He urged political parties, politicians and their supporters to avoid intimidation and inflammatory statements, and directed area commanders and tactical units to step up security operations across the territory.

Nigeria's inflation reportedly eases to 14.06% in October; Imo highest, Taraba lowest

16 Nov 2026, 17:45 GMT

Nigeria's headline inflation reportedly fell to 14.06% in October 2026. State figures show Imo with the highest annual rate at 28.66% and Taraba the lowest at 6.12%, while Ondo and Lagos topped food inflation.

Read more

Nigeria's headline inflation rate reportedly eased to 14.06% in October 2026, according to the latest figures from the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), with wide differences in price increases across the states.

Highest and lowest states

Imo recorded the highest annual headline inflation in October at 28.66%, followed by Enugu at 22.81% and Kaduna at 21.55%. At the other end, Taraba had the lowest rate at 6.12%, followed by Sokoto at 6.68% and Ekiti at 7.64%.

On a month-on-month basis, Bauchi, Borno and Yobe recorded the biggest increases, while Oyo, Osun and Akwa Ibom saw average prices fall compared with September.

Food prices

Food inflation remained high in several states. Ondo had the highest annual food inflation rate at 31.74%, followed by Lagos at 31.43% and Imo at 29.75%. Nasarawa, Bayelsa and Kano recorded the lowest annual food inflation.

Month on month, Yobe, Kebbi and Borno saw the sharpest food price increases, while Lagos, Plateau and Benue recorded declines in average food prices compared with the previous month.

Why it matters

The figures come as the cost of living remains a central issue in campaigns for the 2027 elections. The federal government recently announced new support for farmers, including a guaranteed minimum price scheme meant to give growers assured markets for their produce.
