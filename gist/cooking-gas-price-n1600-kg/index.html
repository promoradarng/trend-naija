Cooking gas now sells for up to ₦1,600 per kg as demand rises

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Nigerian households are paying between about ₦1,200 and ₦1,600 for a kilogramme of cooking gas, according to the latest data from the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (NMDPRA) for September 2026.

The prices

The regulator's survey found the highest price in Bauchi, at ₦1,600 per kg, and the lowest in Abuja, at about ₦1,200. In Lagos, prices ranged from ₦1,250 to ₦1,400. That means refilling a 12.5kg cylinder now costs roughly ₦15,000 to ₦20,000 depending on location.

Demand and supply

Daily consumption of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) rose by about 8 per cent to 5.2 kilotonnes in September, well above the official benchmark of 3.9 kilotonnes. Supply also rose, to about 4.7 kilotonnes a day, but still fell short of consumption by roughly 500 tonnes daily. Local production now provides almost all the gas, with NLNG and Seplat supplying more than half, while imports fell sharply. The Dangote Refinery supplied about 15 per cent. Stocks were enough for about 16 days, against a required 30, although the regulator said the figures did not point to a nationwide shortage.

Background

Gas prices rose by up to ₦75 per kg in two days earlier this month as international crude prices climbed, with some suppliers raising their rates from around ₦1,175 to ₦1,250 per kg. More families have switched to gas from kerosene and charcoal in recent years, pushing up demand.

What it means

For many families already under pressure from food and transport costs, the price of a refill has become another monthly worry. The regulator says the rise in local supply has helped shield buyers from sharper swings in world prices.

  • Bauchi has the highest price at ₦1,600/kg; Abuja the lowest.
  • A 12.5kg refill costs about ₦15,000–₦20,000.
  • Stocks cover about 16 days against a 30-day target.

Oyedepo arrives in Port Harcourt to dedicate Ibiyeomie's 120,000-seat Hand of God Cathedral

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Bishop David Oyedepo has arrived in Port Harcourt to lead the dedication of Salvation Ministries' Hand of God Cathedral, which is reported to seat up to 120,000 worshippers. Pastor David Ibiyeomie received him on Thursday evening.

Read more

Bishop David Oyedepo, founder of Living Faith Church Worldwide, has arrived in Port Harcourt to lead the dedication of a giant new church auditorium built by Pastor David Ibiyeomie's Salvation Ministries.

The arrival

Oyedepo reportedly landed in Rivers State by private jet on the evening of Thursday, 5 November 2026, and was received by Ibiyeomie and a delegation of ministers. The group then travelled in a motorcade to the cathedral complex, where church leaders welcomed them with cheers. Videos of the arrival have been widely shared online.

The cathedral

The dedication of the Hand of God Cathedral was scheduled for 10am on Friday, 6 November, at the ministry's headquarters on the Port Harcourt International Airport Road in Igwuruta. According to reports, the auditorium can seat up to 120,000 people and covers about 56,000 square metres, with a five-wing design meant to resemble an open hand. Reports have described it as bigger than the 109,000-capacity Ark auditorium built by Oyedepo's church. The church invited worshippers from across Nigeria and beyond to attend.

Background

Ibiyeomie founded Salvation Ministries in Port Harcourt and is widely seen as a spiritual son of Oyedepo, which has given the event special meaning for members of both churches. The pastor has been in the news this week over a sermon urging Christians to pay their tithes before spending money on Christmas clothes.

How people reacted

Church members celebrated the occasion online, calling it a historic moment. Others debated the cost of building such large church buildings at a time of economic hardship, while supporters said the project shows what a faith community can achieve.

Two killed, including choirmaster, and 20 abducted in Kaduna community attack

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Gunmen have reportedly killed two people, including a church choirmaster, and abducted 20 others in an attack on Akwando community in Kachia Local Government Area of Kaduna State, according to a local development association.

Read more

Suspected terrorists have reportedly killed two people, including a church choirmaster, and abducted 20 others in an attack on a community in southern Kaduna State.

What happened

The attack on Akwando community in Kachia Local Government Area took place on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, according to the Kuturmi Unity Development Association (KUDA). In a statement on Thursday, the association's president, Dr Joseph Ariko, and its publicity secretary said the attack had heightened fear among residents. The association released a list of those it said had been abducted, which includes several members of the same families.

Earlier attacks

KUDA said the attack was the latest in a series on communities in Kuturmi land. It recalled an attack on Ariko community on Easter Sunday, in which five people were reportedly killed at the scene and 17 others later died in captivity, as well as an attack on Awon community in which two people were killed and 11 abducted. Police and the military had not publicly commented on the latest incident at the time of writing.

Background

Parts of Kaduna State have suffered repeated attacks by armed groups for years, with killings and kidnapping for ransom common in rural communities. This week, bandits also demanded more than ₦100 million for the release of Islamiyya students abducted in neighbouring Niger State.

What happens next

The association has called on the federal and state governments and security agencies to rescue the abducted residents and protect communities in the area.

Enugu Air gets approval for Lagos–London Gatwick flights

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Enugu State-owned Enugu Air says it has secured approval to fly between Lagos and London Gatwick, with Omni-Blu Aviation as its designated operator. No start date, frequency or fares have been announced.

Read more

Enugu Air, the airline owned by the Enugu State government, says it has secured approval to operate flights between Lagos and London Gatwick Airport.

What was announced

In an announcement reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, the airline said Omni-Blu Aviation had been appointed as the designated operator for the route under a partnership to support its international expansion. It said the service would improve links between Nigeria and the United Kingdom and create opportunities for business travel and trade.

What is still unknown

The airline has not announced a start date, how many flights a week it plans to run, ticket prices or how to book. The approval does not mean commercial flights will begin immediately, as further operational requirements may still need to be met.

Background

Air Peace began flying between Lagos and London Gatwick in 2024 after years in which the route was dominated by foreign carriers. Nigerian airlines have been trying to expand on the busy Nigeria–UK corridor, which is used by students, families and business travellers. Enugu Air is part of the Enugu State government's plans to improve transport links in the South-East. The announcement comes as domestic fares to the South-East and South-South for Christmas have risen sharply.

Why it matters

More Nigerian airlines on the route could increase competition and give travellers more choice, although new international services often take time to launch.

Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Residents of Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo are facing worsening power cuts. Power Minister Joseph Tegbe has reportedly blamed gas shortages, maintenance work and low water levels at hydro plants, saying there has been no national grid collapse.

Read more

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Public frustration

The outages have drawn complaints from well-known Nigerians. On Friday, comedian The Cute Abiola posted a video addressed to President Bola Tinubu, saying his solar system had run down after days of poor supply, that everywhere was hot and that the suffering was too much.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.
