Cooking gas now sells for up to ₦1,600 per kg as demand rises

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Nigerian households are paying between about ₦1,200 and ₦1,600 for a kilogramme of cooking gas, according to the latest data from the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (NMDPRA) for September 2026.

The prices

The regulator's survey found the highest price in Bauchi, at ₦1,600 per kg, and the lowest in Abuja, at about ₦1,200. In Lagos, prices ranged from ₦1,250 to ₦1,400. That means refilling a 12.5kg cylinder now costs roughly ₦15,000 to ₦20,000 depending on location.

Demand and supply

Daily consumption of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) rose by about 8 per cent to 5.2 kilotonnes in September, well above the official benchmark of 3.9 kilotonnes. Supply also rose, to about 4.7 kilotonnes a day, but still fell short of consumption by roughly 500 tonnes daily. Local production now provides almost all the gas, with NLNG and Seplat supplying more than half, while imports fell sharply. The Dangote Refinery supplied about 15 per cent. Stocks were enough for about 16 days, against a required 30, although the regulator said the figures did not point to a nationwide shortage.

Background

Gas prices rose by up to ₦75 per kg in two days earlier this month as international crude prices climbed, with some suppliers raising their rates from around ₦1,175 to ₦1,250 per kg. More families have switched to gas from kerosene and charcoal in recent years, pushing up demand.

What it means

For many families already under pressure from food and transport costs, the price of a refill has become another monthly worry. The regulator says the rise in local supply has helped shield buyers from sharper swings in world prices.

  • Stocks cover about 16 days against a 30-day target.

Osun government offices shut as NLC begins three-day strike over council funds

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Government offices and public schools in Osogbo were shut on Monday as workers in Osun began a three-day strike in solidarity with local government employees over withheld statutory allocations to the state's councils.

Read more

Government offices in Osun State were shut on Monday, 9 November 2026, as workers began a three-day strike over withheld allocations to local governments.

What happened

The gates of the state government secretariat and the House of Assembly in Osogbo were locked, with no workers or business activity at either, according to reports. Local government secretariats in Olorunda and Osogbo were also closed, and pupils in public schools in Osogbo were sent home after teachers joined the strike.

Why workers are striking

In a statement on Sunday, the state chapter of the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC), led by its chairman, Christopher Arapasopo, directed all affiliated unions to join the strike in solidarity with the Nigeria Union of Local Government Employees (NULGE). The NLC said the action followed the expiry of a seven-day ultimatum to the federal and Osun state governments over the continued withholding of statutory allocations to local councils in the state, which it said remained unresolved.

Background

Osun's local governments have been at the centre of a long legal and political dispute over who controls the councils, which has led to federal allocations being withheld. Osun is governed by Ademola Adeleke, who has backed President Bola Tinubu's re-election.

Naira up about 6% against the dollar in a year as reserves reach $55bn

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The naira has strengthened by about 6.3% against the US dollar over the past year, trading at about ₦1,347 on 6 November compared with ₦1,437 a year earlier, while Nigeria's foreign reserves have risen to about $55.1 billion.

Read more

The naira has gained ground against the US dollar over the past year, according to official exchange rate data.

The figures

The naira traded at about ₦1,346.79 to the dollar on 6 November 2026, compared with ₦1,436.74 on the same date in 2025, a gain of about ₦90, or 6.26 per cent, according to reports on Monday, 9 November. Nigeria's external reserves have risen to about $55.13 billion.

Why it matters

A stronger naira could reduce the cost of imported raw materials, machinery, medicines and other goods priced in foreign currency. However, how quickly that feeds through to lower prices for consumers depends on businesses passing on the savings.

Background

The naira lost much of its value after the government unified the exchange rate in 2023, when the official rate was about ₦461 to the dollar. The Central Bank of Nigeria has since taken steps to stabilise the foreign exchange market and improve dollar supply. The naira's performance has become a campaign issue: President Bola Tinubu said last week that it would have become worthless without his reforms, while the campaign of APM candidate Seyi Makinde said the President could not claim credit for a recovery after policies that weakened it.

Gunmen have reportedly killed a soldier and three residents, including a 15-year-old boy, in an attack on a village in Plateau State.

What happened

The attack took place at about 9pm on Sunday, 8 November 2026, in Bell village, Sho community, in Barkin Ladi Local Government Area. A resident said the attackers stormed the village and opened fire on residents, leaving some dead and others injured. A local youth association confirmed the killings, identifying three of the victims, aged 15, 28 and 32.

Official response

The police and the military had not publicly commented on the attack at the time of writing.

Background

Barkin Ladi and other parts of Plateau State have suffered repeated deadly attacks on rural communities in recent years. On Sunday, APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde visited Angwan Rukuba in Jos North to console families of 33 people killed in an attack in March. Bishop David Oyedepo also warned politicians last week about the failure to stop killings and kidnappings.

Kano APC council chairman freed after 68 days in kidnappers' hands

9 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Kano police say they have rescued Abdullahi Alasan Beli, APC chairman in Rogo LGA, after 68 days in captivity. He says his abductors moved him through parts of Kaduna and Zamfara states.

Read more

Kano State police say they have rescued the All Progressives Congress (APC) chairman in Rogo Local Government Area, Abdullahi Alasan Beli, popularly known as Abdullahi Fanka, after 68 days in captivity.

The rescue

Commissioner of Police Adamu Bakori announced the rescue while briefing Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf at Government House, Kano, on Sunday, 8 November 2026, according to a statement by the state Commissioner for Information, Sanusi Bature Dawakin Tofa. The police chief said the rescue was achieved through coordinated tactical operations, and that the command would step up efforts against kidnapping.

His ordeal

Beli was abducted by armed men from his home in Beli community, Rogo LGA. After his release, he said his kidnappers moved him through several places, including Danja, Ikara and Giwa in Kaduna State, as well as parts of Zamfara State.

Reactions

Governor Yusuf commended the police for securing the release of the APC chieftain.

Background

Kidnapping for ransom has affected parts of Kano, especially areas bordering Katsina and Kaduna states. It was not disclosed whether any ransom was paid.
