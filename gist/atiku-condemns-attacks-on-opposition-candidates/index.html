Atiku condemns attacks on Sowore and Sokoto ADC candidate, demands probe

31 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has spoken out against a string of reported attacks on opposition politicians, accusing supporters of the ruling party of trying to intimidate rivals ahead of the 2027 elections.

What he said

In a statement on X on Saturday, 31 October 2026, Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), condemned the assault on AAC presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore in Ado-Ekiti and the attack on the convoy of Manir Muhammad Dan'iya, the ADC governorship candidate in Sokoto State. He also criticised what he said was harassment of residents in Sokoto for wearing ADC-branded clothing, and described the unexplained disappearance of some social media influencers as a warning sign.

Who he blamed

Atiku blamed loyalists and enforcers of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC), noting that the attacks happened while President Bola Tinubu was abroad on a religious pilgrimage. He described the violence as a sign of desperation and said the ADC would resist any attempt to wipe out political opposition.

His demands

He called on the federal government to order immediate and transparent investigations into the attacks on Sowore and Dan'iya, and to guarantee a free, fair and competitive environment for the 2027 elections.

Background

Sowore says he and his team were attacked at a restaurant in Ado-Ekiti on Friday; police deny he was kidnapped and have ordered an investigation. Dan'iya says his convoy was blocked and shot at near the Sokoto airport on Friday night. The APC and the Sokoto State Government had not responded to Atiku's statement at the time of the report.

  • Atiku blames ruling-party loyalists for the attacks.
  • He wants transparent investigations into the Sowore and Dan'iya incidents.
  • The APC had not responded at the time of reporting.

Adamu Garba denies endorsing Peter Obi for 2027, says he has quit partisan politics

19 Nov 2026, 17:04 GMT

Former APC presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has dismissed reports that he endorsed Peter Obi for the 2027 presidential election, saying he has withdrawn from partisan politics to focus on his businesses and Africa's digital economy.

Read more

Former All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has denied reports that he endorsed Peter Obi ahead of Nigeria's 2027 presidential election.

"The claim is false"

In a public statement dated 18 November 2026, Garba described the claim circulating online as false.

He said he has withdrawn from partisan politics and is now focusing on his business ventures and on Africa's digital economy.

His new focus

Garba named Crowwe, Tablars and Justitia SI among the projects currently receiving his attention.

He also urged the public to rely only on his official channels for accurate information about his activities.

Where did the story come from?

The origin of the endorsement claim remains unclear. No post, interview or public statement showing Garba endorsing Obi has been found.

What is clear is that Garba has publicly disputed the claim.

A familiar pattern

With the 2027 elections approaching, false claims about endorsements are spreading quickly on social media. It is the second time this week that a story about backing for Obi has been corrected, after Obi himself clarified that his Washington visit was not an endorsement from the US Congress.

Six presidential candidates have reportedly been invited to a policy debate in Abuja on 10 January 2027, six days before the presidential election.

The announcement

The convener of the organising group, Lai Omotola, announced the plan at a press conference in Lagos on Friday, 20 November 2026. He said the debate is designed to push the candidates beyond campaign slogans and into detailed discussions of their policies.

Who is invited

The six candidates invited are President Bola Tinubu of the APC, Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, Peter Obi of the NDC, Oyo Governor Seyi Makinde, former Cross River governor Donald Duke and the SDP's Adewole Adebayo.

Will they attend?

It was not clear from the reports whether all the candidates have confirmed they will take part.

The election

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Anambra State Governor Charles Soludo has approved a one-off payment of ₦50,000 for all civil servants in the state, to be added to their December 2026 salaries.

The announcement

Soludo announced the bonus at the 2026 Civil Service Day celebration at the Jerome Udoji State Secretariat in Awka on Friday, 20 November. He also gave on-the-spot orders to improve working conditions at the secretariat.

Pay concerns

Workers had raised concerns about how Anambra's pay compares with other states, and the governor reportedly responded to those comparisons during the event.

Background

The announcement comes in a busy week for Soludo, whose party, APGA, has adopted President Bola Tinubu as its candidate for the 2027 election.
