Atiku condemns attacks on Sowore and Sokoto ADC candidate, demands probe

31 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has spoken out against a string of reported attacks on opposition politicians, accusing supporters of the ruling party of trying to intimidate rivals ahead of the 2027 elections.

What he said

In a statement on X on Saturday, 31 October 2026, Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), condemned the assault on AAC presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore in Ado-Ekiti and the attack on the convoy of Manir Muhammad Dan'iya, the ADC governorship candidate in Sokoto State. He also criticised what he said was harassment of residents in Sokoto for wearing ADC-branded clothing, and described the unexplained disappearance of some social media influencers as a warning sign.

Who he blamed

Atiku blamed loyalists and enforcers of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC), noting that the attacks happened while President Bola Tinubu was abroad on a religious pilgrimage. He described the violence as a sign of desperation and said the ADC would resist any attempt to wipe out political opposition.

His demands

He called on the federal government to order immediate and transparent investigations into the attacks on Sowore and Dan'iya, and to guarantee a free, fair and competitive environment for the 2027 elections.

Background

Sowore says he and his team were attacked at a restaurant in Ado-Ekiti on Friday; police deny he was kidnapped and have ordered an investigation. Dan'iya says his convoy was blocked and shot at near the Sokoto airport on Friday night. The APC and the Sokoto State Government had not responded to Atiku's statement at the time of the report.

  • Atiku blames ruling-party loyalists for the attacks.
  • He wants transparent investigations into the Sowore and Dan'iya incidents.
  • The APC had not responded at the time of reporting.

INEC warns 'rig and die' slogan could endanger polling staff and voters

24 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

INEC has cautioned politicians and supporters against the popular 'rig and die' slogan ahead of 2027. A national commissioner said linking an electoral offence to death could incite mob attacks at polling units.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has warned political actors and their supporters against using the slogan 'rig and die' ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What INEC said

Speaking on Monday, 23 November 2026, at a media engagement in Abuja on ethical election reporting, National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, represented by an INEC official, said that whatever its originators intended, a slogan that links an electoral offence to death is dangerous. He warned that it could encourage mob attacks at voting centres.

Who is at risk

The commissioner reportedly stressed that ordinary voters and ad-hoc election staff at polling units, rather than senior politicians, are the most physically exposed if such slogans lead to violence.

Background

The phrase has become widely repeated on social media and at rallies, with some saying it is aimed only at those who would rig the vote. INEC's warning comes amid growing concern about election-related violence, including Monday's deadly attack on an NDC campaign event in Delta State.

Tinubu formally declares for second term, claims Otti and four South-East states back him

24 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

At a town hall with Igbo leaders in Lagos, President Tinubu formally declared he is seeking a second term. He also claimed Imo, Enugu, Ebonyi and Anambra, as well as Abia Governor Alex Otti, are with him.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has formally declared his intention to seek a second term, while claiming the support of Abia State Governor Alex Otti and four South-East states.

The declaration

Tinubu spoke on Monday, 23 November 2026, at a town hall meeting with Igbo leaders, traders and youths, organised by the Ndigbo National Dialogue and Development Initiatives at the Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Creative Arts in Iganmu, Lagos. He said the constitution allowed him to ask for a second term and that he wanted one, adding that the large turnout made him happy.

He framed his appeal around national unity, saying no Nigerian chose their ethnicity and all citizens share a common destiny.

The South-East claim

The president listed Imo, Enugu, Ebonyi and Anambra as states he said were firmly behind him, and singled out Governor Otti, who was elected on the Labour Party platform, as a supporter. He said the absence of some of those he named from the event should not be read as displeasure.

The other side

No response from Governor Otti was included in the reports. The South-East is widely regarded as a stronghold of the NDC's Peter Obi, who is from Anambra State.

Four killed as gunmen attack Omo-Agege's NDC campaign in Delta

23 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Gunmen opened fire on former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege and his campaign team in Uduere, Ughelli North, Delta State, on Monday. His campaign says four people were killed and four others critically injured, and that the senator escaped unhurt.

Read more

Four people were killed and several others injured when gunmen attacked a Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) campaign event in Uduere, Ughelli North Local Government Area of Delta State, on Monday, 23 November 2026.

What happened

According to his campaign organisation, armed men opened fire at close range on former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege, the NDC's Delta Central senatorial candidate, and his entourage while he was addressing a crowd that had gathered to welcome him. The campaign said the senator was not hit, although bullets passed close to him.

The victims

The Omo-Agege Campaign Organisation said four people died and four others were critically injured and receiving treatment. It named two of the dead as aides of the campaign.

'Assassination attempt'

In a statement by its media adviser, the campaign described the incident as a targeted attempt on the senator's life rather than a political clash, citing eyewitnesses who said the attackers made clear he was their target.

What happens next

There was no word in the reports on arrests or a police statement. The attack comes as campaigns for the 2027 elections gather pace.

Omo-Agege speaks

In a television interview on Monday night, Omo-Agege said the gunmen started shooting less than five minutes after he began addressing supporters, and that they shouted his name. He said a chance movement, stepping to the side of his vehicle to keep eye contact with the crowd, saved his life.

Reactions

NDC chieftain Pat Utomi condemned the attack, saying violence would not cow Delta residents, and accused the ruling party of being behind political violence in the state. No response from the APC to that claim was included in the reports.
