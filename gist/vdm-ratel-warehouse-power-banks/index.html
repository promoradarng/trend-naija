VeryDarkMan inspects warehouse of his 40ft container of power banks from China, targets students, barbers and makeup artists

28 Oct 2026, 18:07 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and philanthropist Martins Vincent Otse, widely known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again attracted public attention after visiting his warehouse to inspect a shipment of power banks and portable power stations imported from China in a 40-foot container.

During the visit, VDM explained that the products were brought into Nigeria with students, barbers, makeup artists and other small business owners in mind, particularly those who struggle daily with Nigeria's unreliable electricity supply.

According to him, many Nigerians are unable to study comfortably or run their businesses efficiently because of constant power outages.

For students, having a reliable source of electricity can make a difference when preparing for examinations, attending online classes or studying at night.

For barbers and makeup artists, electricity is equally important. Many depend on electrical equipment to earn their daily income, and every hour without power can mean losing customers and money.

VDM believes that making portable power solutions available to these groups could help reduce some of the difficulties they face.

From social media activism to business

Over the years, VeryDarkMan has built a reputation for speaking out on issues affecting ordinary Nigerians, particularly people who struggle to make ends meet. His latest business venture appears to reflect that same interest in the challenges facing low-income earners.

Rather than concentrating only on luxury products, VDM says he wants to provide practical solutions that students, artisans and small business owners can use in their everyday lives.

However, the prices, capacities and affordability of the imported products will ultimately determine how accessible they are to the people he hopes to serve.

A business idea that addresses a real Nigerian problem

Nigeria's electricity challenges have continued to place enormous pressure on households and small businesses. Many artisans spend a significant portion of their earnings on fuel, generator maintenance and alternative power sources just to stay in business.

If VDM can offer reliable portable power stations at affordable prices, the venture could become a useful option for people who cannot afford expensive solar installations or large generators.

What makes this development particularly interesting is his stated intention to combine business with solutions to everyday problems. VDM has made it clear that he intends to make money from the venture while providing products that could make life easier for Nigerians.

VDM continues to show an interest in the struggles of ordinary Nigerians, whether through his activism, humanitarian activities or business ventures. His latest move deserves attention, especially if the products eventually reach students and low-income workers at prices they can genuinely afford.

Social media personality and relationship coach Blessing CEO has admitted that she did not have breast cancer, months after telling the public she was battling the disease.

What she said

In an interview with activist VeryDarkMan (VDM), reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, she said a medical check-up had found a lump in her breast, but that it was not cancerous. She admitted that she used the discovery to tell people it was cancer because she wanted financial support. The Nigeria Police Force has launched an investigation into officers seen in a video providing an escort for businessman and social media personality Linus Williams, popularly known as Blord.

What the police said

In a post on X on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the Force Public Relations Officer described the situation as unlawful and illegal, added the hashtag #NoToImpunity, and confirmed that a probe had begun. The statement did not make clear exactly which part of the incident is being investigated.

INEC says it will obey final court ruling on Atiku, begins moving election materials

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

INEC chairman Joash Amupitan says the commission will abide by the final court decision on Atiku Abubakar's candidacy. The commission has also started moving non-sensitive election materials to states and says PVC production is complete.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) says it will be bound by the final court decision on whether Atiku Abubakar can contest the 2027 presidential election.

What INEC said about Atiku

Speaking in London on Thursday, 12 November 2026, where he was briefing an audience at Chatham House on election preparations, INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan said the commission would follow whatever final ruling the courts give in the dispute over Atiku's nomination. A Federal High Court in Abuja on 5 November nullified the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and reportedly voided the primary that produced Atiku as its candidate. The ADC has appealed, and its lawyer has argued that the judgment contained no express order invalidating Atiku's candidacy.

Materials on the move

Separately, INEC National Commissioner Professor Rhoda Gumus said on Thursday that the commission had begun moving non-sensitive election materials to some geopolitical zones. Speaking while receiving a delegation from Kenya's electoral commission, she said INEC had completed nine of the 14 activities on its election timetable, finished producing Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs) and sent the first two batches to most states. She urged registered voters to collect their cards and said staff training was under way.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027. Amupitan recently completed a tour of the six geopolitical zones to assess readiness. Separately, a Federal High Court in Kaduna on Thursday nullified an ADC primary in the state, adding to the party's legal troubles.

Obi pledges 10,000MW of electricity in four years at Lagos town hall

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has promised to generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000 megawatts of electricity within four years if elected, and to declare emergencies on security, education and power. He spoke at the Lagos town hall that was attacked by thugs earlier in the day.

Read more

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has promised to deliver at least 10,000 megawatts of electricity within four years if he wins the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at the party's town hall meeting in Oregun, Lagos, on Thursday, 12 November 2026, which went ahead despite an attack on the venue by suspected thugs that morning, Obi said unreliable electricity was one of the biggest obstacles to economic growth and industrialisation. He said his government would declare an immediate state of emergency on security, education and electricity, and would generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000MW. He said Nigeria currently generates and distributes roughly 4,000MW, and compared the country's power supply unfavourably with South Africa's.

Background

Power supply has long been one of Nigeria's most persistent problems. President Bola Tinubu's government has begun a ₦30bn programme to distribute free prepaid meters. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
