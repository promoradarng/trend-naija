VeryDarkMan inspects warehouse of his 40ft container of power banks from China, targets students, barbers and makeup artists

28 Oct 2026, 18:07 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and philanthropist Martins Vincent Otse, widely known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again attracted public attention after visiting his warehouse to inspect a shipment of power banks and portable power stations imported from China in a 40-foot container.

During the visit, VDM explained that the products were brought into Nigeria with students, barbers, makeup artists and other small business owners in mind, particularly those who struggle daily with Nigeria's unreliable electricity supply.

According to him, many Nigerians are unable to study comfortably or run their businesses efficiently because of constant power outages.

For students, having a reliable source of electricity can make a difference when preparing for examinations, attending online classes or studying at night.

For barbers and makeup artists, electricity is equally important. Many depend on electrical equipment to earn their daily income, and every hour without power can mean losing customers and money.

VDM believes that making portable power solutions available to these groups could help reduce some of the difficulties they face.

From social media activism to business

Over the years, VeryDarkMan has built a reputation for speaking out on issues affecting ordinary Nigerians, particularly people who struggle to make ends meet. His latest business venture appears to reflect that same interest in the challenges facing low-income earners.

Rather than concentrating only on luxury products, VDM says he wants to provide practical solutions that students, artisans and small business owners can use in their everyday lives.

However, the prices, capacities and affordability of the imported products will ultimately determine how accessible they are to the people he hopes to serve.

A business idea that addresses a real Nigerian problem

Nigeria's electricity challenges have continued to place enormous pressure on households and small businesses. Many artisans spend a significant portion of their earnings on fuel, generator maintenance and alternative power sources just to stay in business.

If VDM can offer reliable portable power stations at affordable prices, the venture could become a useful option for people who cannot afford expensive solar installations or large generators.

What makes this development particularly interesting is his stated intention to combine business with solutions to everyday problems. VDM has made it clear that he intends to make money from the venture while providing products that could make life easier for Nigerians.

VDM continues to show an interest in the struggles of ordinary Nigerians, whether through his activism, humanitarian activities or business ventures. His latest move deserves attention, especially if the products eventually reach students and low-income workers at prices they can genuinely afford.

VeryDarkMan says he will register his own Ratel party and fund it himself

28 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Activist VeryDarkMan has announced plans to register the Ratel Political Party before 2032, saying he will pay the registration costs from his business earnings. He said the movement will start with grassroots offices.

Read more

Social media activist Martins Vincent Otse, popularly known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has announced plans to register his own political party, the Ratel Political Party, before 2032.

Paying his own way

In a video, VDM said he would fund the project with his own earnings from his businesses, rather than relying on supporters. He said he had heard the registration fee was over ₦50 million and that he would raise it. INEC's party registration guidelines set a non-refundable administrative fee of ₦50 million, and payment does not guarantee approval.

Grassroots first

VDM said the Ratel movement would begin by focusing on grassroots positions, such as local government chairmen and councillors, rather than seats in the National Assembly.

Background

'Ratel' is the name VDM uses for his followers. This week he also urged opposition leaders Peter Obi, Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde to unite under one party against President Tinubu.

Omotola Jalade opens up on difficult bond with late mother and Nollywood blacklist

28 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Veteran actress Omotola Jalade-Ekeinde has spoken about her strained relationship with her late mother and her regrets since her death. She also said she was once blacklisted in Nollywood for demanding better standards.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actress Omotola Jalade-Ekeinde has spoken openly about her complicated relationship with her late mother and about being blacklisted in the film industry early in her career.

A difficult bond

In an interview released on Friday, 27 November 2026, Omotola said she and her mother did not always get along. Her mother worried that the film industry would expose her daughter to immorality, and the tension grew when Omotola began dating her husband, Captain Matthew Ekeinde. When he visited to formally ask for her hand, her mother reportedly questioned whether her daughter was ready for marriage.

Omotola said the relationship later improved and the two became much closer. She admitted that she still has regrets about the care and support she never got to show her mother, and said she now finds herself giving that care to other people's mothers. She added that the experience shaped her decision to make her directorial debut.

'Troublesome Eight'

Turning to her career, the actress said she was blacklisted after pushing for better standards and professionalism in Nollywood. She said she was one of a group of actors labelled the 'Troublesome Eight' because they demanded improvements. She also said she had a manager as far back as the 1990s, when professional management was not widely understood in the industry.

Motherhood

The mother of four also spoke about her approach to parenting and the lessons motherhood has taught her.
