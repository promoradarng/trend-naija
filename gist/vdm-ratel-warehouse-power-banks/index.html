VeryDarkMan inspects warehouse of his 40ft container of power banks from China, targets students, barbers and makeup artists

28 Oct 2026, 18:07 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and philanthropist Martins Vincent Otse, widely known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again attracted public attention after visiting his warehouse to inspect a shipment of power banks and portable power stations imported from China in a 40-foot container.

During the visit, VDM explained that the products were brought into Nigeria with students, barbers, makeup artists and other small business owners in mind, particularly those who struggle daily with Nigeria's unreliable electricity supply.

According to him, many Nigerians are unable to study comfortably or run their businesses efficiently because of constant power outages.

For students, having a reliable source of electricity can make a difference when preparing for examinations, attending online classes or studying at night.

For barbers and makeup artists, electricity is equally important. Many depend on electrical equipment to earn their daily income, and every hour without power can mean losing customers and money.

VDM believes that making portable power solutions available to these groups could help reduce some of the difficulties they face.

From social media activism to business

Over the years, VeryDarkMan has built a reputation for speaking out on issues affecting ordinary Nigerians, particularly people who struggle to make ends meet. His latest business venture appears to reflect that same interest in the challenges facing low-income earners.

Rather than concentrating only on luxury products, VDM says he wants to provide practical solutions that students, artisans and small business owners can use in their everyday lives.

However, the prices, capacities and affordability of the imported products will ultimately determine how accessible they are to the people he hopes to serve.

A business idea that addresses a real Nigerian problem

Nigeria's electricity challenges have continued to place enormous pressure on households and small businesses. Many artisans spend a significant portion of their earnings on fuel, generator maintenance and alternative power sources just to stay in business.

If VDM can offer reliable portable power stations at affordable prices, the venture could become a useful option for people who cannot afford expensive solar installations or large generators.

What makes this development particularly interesting is his stated intention to combine business with solutions to everyday problems. VDM has made it clear that he intends to make money from the venture while providing products that could make life easier for Nigerians.

VDM continues to show an interest in the struggles of ordinary Nigerians, whether through his activism, humanitarian activities or business ventures. His latest move deserves attention, especially if the products eventually reach students and low-income workers at prices they can genuinely afford.

CBN and NNPC sign new agreement to tighten oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue

18 Nov 2026, 14:54 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement covering foreign-exchange inflows, remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing and more. It promises a more structured system, but the full agreement has not been released and no revenue targets have been set.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement aimed at tightening oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue.

What the agreement covers

The agreement covers foreign-exchange inflows, the remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing, digital payments, regulatory compliance and cybersecurity.

Officials say it will replace the old practice of dealing with issues one transaction at a time with a more structured system, including joint committees and performance monitoring.

Why it matters

Oil remains Nigeria's biggest source of foreign exchange and a major part of government revenue. For years, questions have been raised about how much oil money actually reaches government accounts, and how quickly.

Stronger coordination between the central bank and the national oil company could, in theory, improve transparency, boost foreign-exchange supply and help stabilise the naira.

Promises versus results

It sounds promising, but Nigerians will need to see measurable results. The full agreement has not been made public, and no target has been given for additional revenue or foreign-exchange inflows.

Without published targets and regular public reporting, it will be difficult for citizens to judge whether the new arrangement is working.

The real test

The real test is simple: will more oil money reach government accounts, and will Nigerians be able to see it?

State police bill moves to Tinubu's table after all 36 state assemblies approve it

18 Nov 2026, 14:51 GMT

The Senate has authorised its Clerk to send the constitutional amendment creating state police to President Bola Tinubu for assent, after all 36 state Houses of Assembly approved it. But state police will not start immediately: a separate National Policing Bill is still needed.

Read more

Nigeria has moved a step closer to state police. The Senate has authorised its Clerk to transmit the constitutional amendment establishing state police to President Bola Tinubu for his assent.

All 36 states on board

All 36 state Houses of Assembly have approved the proposal, completing the state-legislature stage of the constitutional amendment process.

The bill was originally sent to the National Assembly by President Tinubu himself, as part of efforts to tackle the country's worsening insecurity, so his assent is widely expected.

Not an overnight change

However, state police will not begin operating immediately once the President signs the amendment.

According to the Senate, a separate National Policing Bill is still needed to settle the key details: how state police will be funded, how officers will be recruited, who will command them and who will oversee them.

Why it matters

For years, many Nigerians, especially in states hit hard by banditry, kidnapping and communal violence, have argued that a single, centrally controlled police force is too stretched to protect every community.

Supporters say state police will bring security closer to the people, with officers who know the local terrain, language and culture.

Critics worry that governors could use state police against political opponents, journalists and critics, particularly at election time, and that poorer states may struggle to fund them properly.

The real test

Nigeria is now closer to decentralised policing, but the most important safeguards, the rules that will decide whether state police protect citizens or serve politicians, are still to be written.
