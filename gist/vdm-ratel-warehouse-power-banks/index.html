VeryDarkMan inspects warehouse of his 40ft container of power banks from China, targets students, barbers and makeup artists

28 Oct 2026, 18:07 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and philanthropist Martins Vincent Otse, widely known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again attracted public attention after visiting his warehouse to inspect a shipment of power banks and portable power stations imported from China in a 40-foot container.

During the visit, VDM explained that the products were brought into Nigeria with students, barbers, makeup artists and other small business owners in mind, particularly those who struggle daily with Nigeria's unreliable electricity supply.

According to him, many Nigerians are unable to study comfortably or run their businesses efficiently because of constant power outages.

For students, having a reliable source of electricity can make a difference when preparing for examinations, attending online classes or studying at night.

For barbers and makeup artists, electricity is equally important. Many depend on electrical equipment to earn their daily income, and every hour without power can mean losing customers and money.

VDM believes that making portable power solutions available to these groups could help reduce some of the difficulties they face.

From social media activism to business

Over the years, VeryDarkMan has built a reputation for speaking out on issues affecting ordinary Nigerians, particularly people who struggle to make ends meet. His latest business venture appears to reflect that same interest in the challenges facing low-income earners.

Rather than concentrating only on luxury products, VDM says he wants to provide practical solutions that students, artisans and small business owners can use in their everyday lives.

However, the prices, capacities and affordability of the imported products will ultimately determine how accessible they are to the people he hopes to serve.

A business idea that addresses a real Nigerian problem

Nigeria's electricity challenges have continued to place enormous pressure on households and small businesses. Many artisans spend a significant portion of their earnings on fuel, generator maintenance and alternative power sources just to stay in business.

If VDM can offer reliable portable power stations at affordable prices, the venture could become a useful option for people who cannot afford expensive solar installations or large generators.

What makes this development particularly interesting is his stated intention to combine business with solutions to everyday problems. VDM has made it clear that he intends to make money from the venture while providing products that could make life easier for Nigerians.

VDM continues to show an interest in the struggles of ordinary Nigerians, whether through his activism, humanitarian activities or business ventures. His latest move deserves attention, especially if the products eventually reach students and low-income workers at prices they can genuinely afford.

Nollywood actor Aremu Afolayan has become a talking point online after his appearance on comedian Isbae U's interview show, Curiosity Made Me Ask, reportedly ended in a heated exchange.

What happened

Isbae U, known for his sarcastic interview style, is said to have questioned whether Aremu was still active in Nollywood and made jokes about his body and how he earns a living given his few recent film roles.

Aremu reportedly explained that he has stayed away from film sets because of insecurity, saying travelling for shoots had become dangerous and he was not willing to risk his life for a role paying ₦2 million or ₦3 million.

Where it went wrong

The mood reportedly changed when Isbae U brought up his own late father, actor Sir K. Kamoru, and compared him with Aremu's late father, the veteran filmmaker Ade Love Afolayan. Aremu is said to have taken offence and warned the host against such comparisons, insisting his father's contribution to the film industry was not up for debate. He also reportedly questioned Isbae U's own achievements as an actor.

Tinubu announces home loans of up to ₦100m at 9.75% interest over 20 years

17 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Tinubu says eligible Nigerians can get home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed 9.75% interest rate, repayable over 20 years. Buyers must pay at least 10% of the property's value upfront.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has announced that eligible Nigerians can now access home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed interest rate of 9.75%, repayable over 20 years.

How it works

In a post on his X account on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the President reportedly said the loans are available through the ₦1 trillion MOFI Real Estate Investment Fund (MREIF). Applicants must pay a deposit of at least 10% of the property's value to qualify.

Using a ₦10 million home as an example, he said the buyer would pay ₦1 million upfront and then about ₦85,400 a month over the 20 years.

Extra options

He added that workers in the Contributory Pension Scheme can use up to 25% of their retirement savings towards the deposit, while married couples can apply jointly to qualify for bigger loans.

What happens next

Full eligibility rules and the application process were not detailed in the reports.

Nigeria's Minister of Defence, Christopher Musa, has reportedly said the United States is providing the Nigerian military with satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help locate militant leaders ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What the minister said

Speaking to journalists on Monday, 16 November 2026, Musa said troops had killed a number of bandit and terrorist commanders in overnight operations across the north-west and north-east. He described the operations as happening almost daily and said they were backed by much better intelligence.

He said the partnership with Washington had improved the military's ability to find and strike targets.

More equipment

The minister also said Nigeria is expecting more helicopters from the United States and Italy to support operations against insurgents in the north-east and armed bandits in the north-west.
