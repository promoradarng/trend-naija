VeryDarkMan inspects warehouse of his 40ft container of power banks from China, targets students, barbers and makeup artists

28 Oct 2026, 18:07 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and philanthropist Martins Vincent Otse, widely known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again attracted public attention after visiting his warehouse to inspect a shipment of power banks and portable power stations imported from China in a 40-foot container.

During the visit, VDM explained that the products were brought into Nigeria with students, barbers, makeup artists and other small business owners in mind, particularly those who struggle daily with Nigeria's unreliable electricity supply.

According to him, many Nigerians are unable to study comfortably or run their businesses efficiently because of constant power outages.

For students, having a reliable source of electricity can make a difference when preparing for examinations, attending online classes or studying at night.

For barbers and makeup artists, electricity is equally important. Many depend on electrical equipment to earn their daily income, and every hour without power can mean losing customers and money.

VDM believes that making portable power solutions available to these groups could help reduce some of the difficulties they face.

From social media activism to business

Over the years, VeryDarkMan has built a reputation for speaking out on issues affecting ordinary Nigerians, particularly people who struggle to make ends meet. His latest business venture appears to reflect that same interest in the challenges facing low-income earners.

Rather than concentrating only on luxury products, VDM says he wants to provide practical solutions that students, artisans and small business owners can use in their everyday lives.

However, the prices, capacities and affordability of the imported products will ultimately determine how accessible they are to the people he hopes to serve.

A business idea that addresses a real Nigerian problem

Nigeria's electricity challenges have continued to place enormous pressure on households and small businesses. Many artisans spend a significant portion of their earnings on fuel, generator maintenance and alternative power sources just to stay in business.

If VDM can offer reliable portable power stations at affordable prices, the venture could become a useful option for people who cannot afford expensive solar installations or large generators.

What makes this development particularly interesting is his stated intention to combine business with solutions to everyday problems. VDM has made it clear that he intends to make money from the venture while providing products that could make life easier for Nigerians.

VDM continues to show an interest in the struggles of ordinary Nigerians, whether through his activism, humanitarian activities or business ventures. His latest move deserves attention, especially if the products eventually reach students and low-income workers at prices they can genuinely afford.

Sanusi urges Tinubu government to stop treating talk of poverty as an attack

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Emir of Kano Muhammadu Sanusi II has reportedly told the Tinubu administration to stop issuing statements from the Villa against people who raise concerns about poverty. He said Nigeria was close to hyperinflation in 2023, but that critics of hardship are not enemies of the President.

Read more

The Emir of Kano and former Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) governor, Muhammadu Sanusi II, has urged President Bola Tinubu's government to listen to concerns about poverty rather than treating critics as enemies.

What he said

Speaking at the Oxford Global Think Tank conference convened by former World Bank vice-president Arunma Oteh, in comments reported on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Sanusi reportedly said the government's habit of issuing statements from the Presidential Villa to condemn people who raise economic concerns was counterproductive. He argued that citizens who talk about poverty are not attacking the President personally, and that the government should distinguish between criticism and hostility.

On the economy

Sanusi also said Nigeria was dangerously close to economic collapse in 2023 and on the verge of hyperinflation, a view he said he had held and expressed at the time. His comments suggest he sees the need for reform, while insisting that the hardship many people feel should be acknowledged.

Background

Sanusi was CBN governor from 2009 to 2014 and is a well-known commentator on the economy. He was reinstated as Emir of Kano in 2024. The Tinubu government removed the petrol subsidy and unified exchange rates in 2023, and says these moves rescued the economy, pointing to falling inflation and rising reserves. Critics, including investment banker Atedo Peterside this week, say the poor have become poorer. Presidency officials have often responded sharply to such criticism, accusing some critics of playing politics ahead of 2027.

What happens next

The Presidency had not responded to Sanusi's comments at the time of reporting.

El-Rufai tells supporters to vote ADC at every level in 2027

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Former Kaduna governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly directed his supporters to back the African Democratic Congress in all elections in 2027. The message was delivered by an ally at a coalition meeting in Kaduna.

Read more

Former Kaduna State governor Nasir El-Rufai has instructed his supporters to vote for the African Democratic Congress (ADC) at every level in the 2027 elections, according to a close ally.

The message

Hajiya Hafsat Baba, the ADC's national vice-chairman for the North-West, reportedly conveyed the message at a meeting in Kaduna that brought together various El-Rufai support groups. She said the directive had been verified by El-Rufai himself and covered federal, state and local elections. The meeting was held to unite the groups and coordinate their activities ahead of the polls.

'Base still strong'

Another ally, Ibrahim Hussaini, rejected claims that El-Rufai had lost his political base, saying his loyalists remain strong across Kaduna State and the wider North.

Background

El-Rufai served two terms as governor of Kaduna from 2015 to 2023 as a member of the APC and was a key figure in President Bola Tinubu's 2023 campaign. He later fell out with the party and joined the opposition coalition that adopted the ADC, which has fielded former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as its presidential candidate. His relationship with his successor, Governor Uba Sani of the APC, has been tense. El-Rufai has also been involved in legal disputes in recent months and has appealed for a fair trial.

Why it matters

Kaduna is one of the most populous states in the North-West, a region with a large share of Nigeria's voters. The APC says it remains strong in the state, while the ADC hopes El-Rufai's support will help it make gains there.

Soludo says his family and Obi's are linked as godparents despite political rift

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Anambra governor Charles Soludo has reportedly said Peter Obi's wife is godmother to one of his children, and his own wife is confirmation godmother to Obi's daughter. He said their political differences have not broken their friendship.

Read more

Anambra State governor Charles Soludo has spoken about his close family ties with former governor and NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, despite their recent political differences.

What he said

In an interview with a BBC Igbo-language service on Monday, 2 November 2026, Soludo reportedly said that Obi's wife, Margaret, is the godmother of one of his children, and that his own wife, Nonye, is the confirmation godmother of Obi's daughter. He described Obi as a close friend and said no political disagreement had been enough to break the bond between their families.

Their differences

Soludo said the two men had disagreed on political matters, mainly over who should govern Anambra State. Their relationship became a subject of public debate after a dispute in September, and Soludo has said that his party, APGA, would not automatically support Obi's presidential bid. Soludo has also said he backs President Bola Tinubu for 2027.

Background

Obi governed Anambra from 2006 to 2014 as a member of APGA, the same party on which Soludo was elected in 2021. Both men are among the most prominent politicians in the South East. Soludo's son, Ozonna, recently released a song supporting the NDC, which drew attention because of his father's backing for Tinubu.

How people reacted

Many people welcomed the comments as a sign that politics should not destroy friendships, while others said the two men's public differences were more important for voters.

Wike aide says there is hunger but people are still building houses, sparking debate

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Lere Olayinka, an aide to FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, has reportedly questioned how young Nigerians describe the level of hardship, pointing to ongoing construction as a sign of economic activity. His comments drew criticism online.

Read more

Lere Olayinka, Senior Special Assistant on Public Communications and Social Media to FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, has sparked debate with comments on poverty and hunger in Nigeria.

What he said

Appearing on a television politics programme, in comments reported on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Olayinka reportedly pushed back against what he described as the narrative of widespread poverty and hunger promoted by young Nigerians, often referred to as Gen Z. While he acknowledged that people were facing economic pressure, he pointed to private building projects and construction in communities as signs that economic activity was continuing. He said there is hunger, but people are still building houses.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the comments, saying they showed officials were out of touch with ordinary people struggling with high food and transport costs. Others agreed that the economy was not as bad as some critics suggest and said the government's reforms needed time.

Background

The cost of living is one of the most debated issues ahead of the 2027 elections. On the same day, the Emir of Kano, Muhammadu Sanusi II, urged the government to stop treating talk of poverty as an attack, while investment banker Atedo Peterside said the reforms were making the rich richer and the poor poorer. The government points to falling inflation, a stronger stock market and higher reserves as signs of progress. Wike, a former PDP governor of Rivers State, is a key ally of President Bola Tinubu and leads the Rainbow Coalition backing his re-election.
