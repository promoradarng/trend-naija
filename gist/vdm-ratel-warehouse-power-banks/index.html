VeryDarkMan inspects warehouse of his 40ft container of power banks from China, targets students, barbers and makeup artists

28 Oct 2026, 18:07 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and philanthropist Martins Vincent Otse, widely known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again attracted public attention after visiting his warehouse to inspect a shipment of power banks and portable power stations imported from China in a 40-foot container.

During the visit, VDM explained that the products were brought into Nigeria with students, barbers, makeup artists and other small business owners in mind, particularly those who struggle daily with Nigeria's unreliable electricity supply.

According to him, many Nigerians are unable to study comfortably or run their businesses efficiently because of constant power outages.

For students, having a reliable source of electricity can make a difference when preparing for examinations, attending online classes or studying at night.

For barbers and makeup artists, electricity is equally important. Many depend on electrical equipment to earn their daily income, and every hour without power can mean losing customers and money.

VDM believes that making portable power solutions available to these groups could help reduce some of the difficulties they face.

From social media activism to business

Over the years, VeryDarkMan has built a reputation for speaking out on issues affecting ordinary Nigerians, particularly people who struggle to make ends meet. His latest business venture appears to reflect that same interest in the challenges facing low-income earners.

Rather than concentrating only on luxury products, VDM says he wants to provide practical solutions that students, artisans and small business owners can use in their everyday lives.

However, the prices, capacities and affordability of the imported products will ultimately determine how accessible they are to the people he hopes to serve.

A business idea that addresses a real Nigerian problem

Nigeria's electricity challenges have continued to place enormous pressure on households and small businesses. Many artisans spend a significant portion of their earnings on fuel, generator maintenance and alternative power sources just to stay in business.

If VDM can offer reliable portable power stations at affordable prices, the venture could become a useful option for people who cannot afford expensive solar installations or large generators.

What makes this development particularly interesting is his stated intention to combine business with solutions to everyday problems. VDM has made it clear that he intends to make money from the venture while providing products that could make life easier for Nigerians.

VDM continues to show an interest in the struggles of ordinary Nigerians, whether through his activism, humanitarian activities or business ventures. His latest move deserves attention, especially if the products eventually reach students and low-income workers at prices they can genuinely afford.

"Just come out and say it's a lie"

The feud began after a video of Davido singing a line from Zlatan's song INFERNO at Zlatan's wedding welcome party in Morocco, a lyric VDM believes was aimed at him. VDM then warned Zlatan that he would "learn the hard way".

Another Ghanaian found dead in USA as body of 24-year-old church musician Joseph Owusu is discovered dumped near trash can in New York

9 Nov 2026, 06:11 GMT

The body of Joseph Owusu, a 24-year-old Ghanaian church organist from the Bronx, was found in a large black bag left near a trash can on a Brooklyn sidewalk. CCTV footage shows two people taking the bag from a white car. Ghana's government has demanded justice.

Read more

The Ghanaian community in the United States has been thrown into mourning again following the disturbing discovery of the body of 24-year-old Joseph Owusu, a talented church musician who lived in the Bronx, New York.

Found in a bag on the sidewalk

Owusu's body was discovered inside a large black bag left near a trash can on a sidewalk in Brooklyn on 30 October 2026.

CCTV footage shows two unidentified people taking the bag out of a white car, leaving it by the roadside and driving away.

Who was Joseph Owusu?

The young man was the organist at the Ghana United Methodist Church in the Bronx. He was also studying computer science and worked as a caregiver for people living with disabilities. Friends and church members have described him as gifted, gentle and devoted to his faith.

A family demands answers

His devastated family is demanding justice as investigators work to establish how he died and who abandoned his body. Ghana's embassy in the United States has confirmed his death, and the Ghanaian government has called on US authorities to ensure those responsible are found and brought to justice.

Two young lives in days

The tragedy came just a day after another young Ghanaian, 15-year-old Javits Frimpong, was shot dead while walking to school in the Bronx.

Two young Ghanaians, two heartbreaking deaths in New York within days. Both families are now waiting for answers.

A group of suspected thieves in South Africa has become the talk of social media after they were reportedly caught and subjected to an unusual form of punishment.

The viral video

The suspects were made to lie face down on the ground in a single line by the side of a road, with each person's face pressed against the backside of the man in front of him. Their hands appear to be tied behind them.

Reports say they were caught by locals, although a uniformed officer can also be seen standing beside the men in the footage, so it is not clear whether the police or members of the public arranged them that way.

Laughter and concern

The incident has sparked mixed reactions online. Many people found the punishment hilarious, joking that South Africans seem to have invented a brand-new style of discipline.

Others were less amused, questioning whether suspects should be humiliated in public before any court has found them guilty, and whether members of the public should ever take the law into their own hands.

What we don't know

The circumstances of the incident, including where and when it happened and what the men are accused of stealing, have not been independently verified.

Amnesty International condemns Zamfara killings, says burning of 12 family members alive exposes failure of Nigerian authorities

8 Nov 2026, 21:28 GMT

Amnesty International has condemned the reported killing of 12 members of one family in Zamfara State, who were said to have been burned alive, calling it another sign of the authorities' failure to protect communities from armed attacks.

Read more

Amnesty International has strongly condemned the reported killing of 12 members of a family in Zamfara State, describing the horrifying incident as another painful reminder of the Nigerian authorities' failure to protect innocent citizens from violent attacks.

An entire family wiped out

Imagine an entire family wiped out in such a cruel way. Twelve human beings, reportedly burned alive. People who had their own dreams, responsibilities and hopes for tomorrow, only to have their lives cut short in circumstances no family should ever experience.

The tragedy has once again raised serious questions about the worsening security situation in parts of northern Nigeria, where many families live under the constant threat of armed attacks, kidnappings and killings.

A failure to protect

For Amnesty International, the incident reflects a much bigger problem: the continued inability of the authorities to guarantee the safety of communities that have suffered attack after attack.

For years, residents of Zamfara and neighbouring states have endured violence from armed groups. Villages have been raided, homes destroyed, residents abducted and families forced to abandon communities where they have lived for generations.

Despite repeated assurances from government officials and security agencies, reports of fresh atrocities keep emerging.

Every citizen deserves protection

The Nigerian government has a constitutional duty to protect lives and property. That duty extends to every citizen, whether they live in Abuja, Lagos, Zamfara or a remote village far from the attention of the national media.

The victims deserve justice, and their surviving relatives deserve more than another round of official condolences. There must be a thorough investigation, those responsible must be brought to justice, and the authorities must take meaningful steps to prevent similar tragedies.

A country cannot claim to be making progress when families are being burned alive in their homes and communities are left wondering who will be attacked next.
