VeryDarkMan inspects warehouse of his 40ft container of power banks from China, targets students, barbers and makeup artists

28 Oct 2026, 18:07 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and philanthropist Martins Vincent Otse, widely known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again attracted public attention after visiting his warehouse to inspect a shipment of power banks and portable power stations imported from China in a 40-foot container.

During the visit, VDM explained that the products were brought into Nigeria with students, barbers, makeup artists and other small business owners in mind, particularly those who struggle daily with Nigeria's unreliable electricity supply.

According to him, many Nigerians are unable to study comfortably or run their businesses efficiently because of constant power outages.

For students, having a reliable source of electricity can make a difference when preparing for examinations, attending online classes or studying at night.

For barbers and makeup artists, electricity is equally important. Many depend on electrical equipment to earn their daily income, and every hour without power can mean losing customers and money.

VDM believes that making portable power solutions available to these groups could help reduce some of the difficulties they face.

From social media activism to business

Over the years, VeryDarkMan has built a reputation for speaking out on issues affecting ordinary Nigerians, particularly people who struggle to make ends meet. His latest business venture appears to reflect that same interest in the challenges facing low-income earners.

Rather than concentrating only on luxury products, VDM says he wants to provide practical solutions that students, artisans and small business owners can use in their everyday lives.

However, the prices, capacities and affordability of the imported products will ultimately determine how accessible they are to the people he hopes to serve.

A business idea that addresses a real Nigerian problem

Nigeria's electricity challenges have continued to place enormous pressure on households and small businesses. Many artisans spend a significant portion of their earnings on fuel, generator maintenance and alternative power sources just to stay in business.

If VDM can offer reliable portable power stations at affordable prices, the venture could become a useful option for people who cannot afford expensive solar installations or large generators.

What makes this development particularly interesting is his stated intention to combine business with solutions to everyday problems. VDM has made it clear that he intends to make money from the venture while providing products that could make life easier for Nigerians.

VDM continues to show an interest in the struggles of ordinary Nigerians, whether through his activism, humanitarian activities or business ventures. His latest move deserves attention, especially if the products eventually reach students and low-income workers at prices they can genuinely afford.

"Go and work, I have children to take care of!": GehGeh sends strong message to his siblings and critics

3 Nov 2026, 20:34 GMT

Social media personality and financial adviser GehGeh has told his brothers and sisters to work hard and stop depending entirely on him, saying he has children to care for. His message has reopened a familiar debate about the pressure Nigerian families put on relatives who become successful.

Read more

Popular Nigerian social media personality and financial adviser GehGeh has stirred reactions online after sending a strong message to his family members and to those who believe he should carry the financial responsibilities of everyone around him.

"Go and work"

Speaking openly about the pressure that comes with financial success, GehGeh made it clear that having money does not mean one person should become the permanent provider for an entire family.

According to him, his brothers and sisters must learn to work hard and take responsibility for their own lives rather than depending entirely on him.

"If you're my brother or my sister, go and work. Don't depend only on me because I have children I need to take care of. I can't do more than my power for family members," he said.

A familiar Nigerian story

His message touches on something many Nigerians can relate to, especially those who have become financially stable while other relatives continue to struggle.

In many families, once one person starts making money, expectations begin to grow. School fees, house rent, feeding allowances, business capital and even personal expenses suddenly become that person's responsibility. Sometimes the demands never seem to end.

Drawing the line

GehGeh appears determined to draw a line. He did not suggest that helping relatives is wrong, but his message was clear: family support should have limits, particularly when someone has children and responsibilities of their own.

His remarks also highlight the pressure successful people face from relatives who believe family ties automatically guarantee financial support.

Family members should help one another whenever they can. But should one person's success become an excuse for everyone else to stop trying?

Over to you

Is GehGeh being too harsh on his family, or is he simply saying what many successful people are afraid to tell their relatives?

ADC names venue as Atiku kicks off presidential campaign in Sokoto

4 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The ADC says Atiku Abubakar's 2027 presidential campaign will be flagged off at its secretariat on Ilorin Road, Sokoto, on Wednesday. The party had kept the venue quiet after alleging intimidation, a convoy attack and the sealing of an office.

Read more

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has named the venue for the official launch of Atiku Abubakar's 2027 presidential campaign in Sokoto.

The venue

According to reports, the campaign will be flagged off at the party's secretariat on Ilorin Road, Sokoto, on Wednesday, 4 November 2026. Senator Ibrahim Lamido, who coordinates the ADC presidential campaign in the state, said Atiku was expected to arrive at Sultan Abubakar III International Airport at about 9am, and invited supporters and members of the public to welcome him before moving to the venue. He urged party executives in all 23 local government areas of the state to mobilise supporters.

Why the venue was kept quiet

The party had earlier withheld details of the venue, citing alleged intimidation, an attack on the convoy of its governorship candidate and the sealing of one of its campaign offices. On Tuesday, Atiku accused the Department of State Services of recruiting protesters to disrupt the rally, a claim the DSS had not publicly answered. The Sokoto State Government said it sealed the office over questions about who owns the building and to prevent further disturbances.

Background

Atiku, a former vice-president, is the ADC's candidate after the party became the platform of an opposition coalition. Sokoto is in the North-West, a region with a large share of Nigeria's voters, and is governed by the APC. Other candidates, including President Bola Tinubu of the APC and Peter Obi of the NDC, have also been holding rallies and town halls across the country.

What happens next

Security agencies are expected to be deployed in Sokoto for the event, and the ADC plans further rallies in other states.

Education minister says Tinubu graduated with first class, calls him a genius

4 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Education Minister Tunji Alausa has reportedly said President Tinubu graduated with a first class in accounting from Chicago State University and that he has met the President's classmates. He urged Nigerians to ignore opposition doubts about the President's credentials.

Read more

The Minister of Education, Tunji Alausa, has defended President Bola Tinubu's academic record, describing him as a genius.

What he said

Speaking on a television politics programme on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Alausa reportedly said Tinubu graduated with a first class degree in accounting from Chicago State University in the United States. He said he knew the university well, had visited it many times, and had met many of the President's former classmates in person. He urged Nigerians to disregard what he described as the opposition's claims about the President's credentials, and called Tinubu the first internationally exposed president.

Background

Questions about the President's academic records were raised by opponents before and after the 2023 election. Chicago State University released records confirming that Tinubu graduated in 1979, and the Supreme Court dismissed petitions challenging his election, including claims about his certificates. Opposition figures have continued to raise the issue in political debates. Supporters say the matter has been settled by the courts and the university.

Background on the minister

Alausa, a medical doctor who practised in the United States, was appointed Minister of Education in 2024. He has introduced several policy changes in the sector.

How people reacted

APC supporters welcomed the comments, while critics said the minister's remarks would not end the debate and that voters care more about the economy than academic records.

57 Nigerians at risk as Gambia orders banks to replace foreign staff

4 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigerians in Diaspora Commission says 57 Nigerian professionals in four Nigerian-owned banks in The Gambia could lose their jobs after the Gambian central bank ordered banks to replace foreign staff with Gambians by 31 December. The FG has urged a rethink.

Read more

The federal government has raised concern over a directive by The Gambia's central bank ordering commercial banks to replace foreign employees with Gambian nationals, a move that could cost dozens of Nigerians their jobs.

The directive

According to reports, the Central Bank of The Gambia issued a circular dated 16 September 2026 giving banks until 31 December 2026 to replace non-Gambian staff with qualified Gambians.

Nigeria's concern

The Nigerians in Diaspora Commission (NiDCOM) said 57 Nigerian professionals working in four Nigerian-owned banks in The Gambia could be affected. It urged the Gambian authorities to reconsider the directive and to engage with the banks and other stakeholders to reach a fair solution.

Background

Nigerian banks have expanded across West Africa over the past two decades, with several operating subsidiaries in The Gambia. Both countries are members of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS), whose protocols promote the free movement of people and the right to work across member states. Many West African countries, however, have rules encouraging the employment of their own citizens in key sectors.

Why it matters

The move comes as several countries tighten rules on foreign workers. Nigerians abroad have recently faced stricter immigration policies in the UK, Portugal and elsewhere.

What happens next

It is not yet clear whether The Gambia will change or delay the policy.
