Tinubu prays inside the Kaaba as Umrah trip ends, critics respond

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has wrapped up his visit to Saudi Arabia, saying he and members of his delegation were granted the rare privilege of entering the Holy Kaaba in Mecca to pray during their Umrah, the lesser pilgrimage.

What the President said

In a statement on his official social media account on Monday, 2 November 2026, Tinubu thanked King Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman for the invitation and for their hospitality. He said that inside the Kaaba he prayed for peace, unity and prosperity for Nigeria and all Nigerians, and described the gesture as a sign of the long-standing bonds of faith and friendship between the two countries.

In a further statement released through his adviser Bayo Onanuga, Tinubu said he had asked God for wisdom and resilience to keep serving the people. Referring to a verse of the Quran, he said all power belongs to God, who can give leadership to anyone and take it away, and described being chosen as president out of about 200 million people as a great favour.

Why it matters

The Kaaba, inside the Grand Mosque in Mecca, is Islam's most sacred site. Entry into the structure itself is rarely allowed and is usually granted only to heads of state and other dignitaries invited by the Saudi royal family.

How people reacted

Supporters praised the President and prayed for him to lead well. Critics questioned the timing of the trip so close to the 2027 elections. Former senator Dino Melaye, an opposition figure, challenged Tinubu on X to a Quran recitation contest when he returns. Last week, Oyo governor and APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde asked the Presidency to be more open about the purpose of the trip, while activist Omoyele Sowore claimed it was for medical treatment. The Presidency rejected that claim, saying the President travelled for Umrah, as he did twice in 2022.

What happens next

The President is expected back in Abuja in the coming days as campaigning for the January 2027 elections intensifies.

  • Entry into the Kaaba is a rare honour usually given to heads of state.
  • Dino Melaye challenged him to a Quran recitation contest.

Sanusi urges Tinubu government to stop treating talk of poverty as an attack

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Emir of Kano Muhammadu Sanusi II has reportedly told the Tinubu administration to stop issuing statements from the Villa against people who raise concerns about poverty. He said Nigeria was close to hyperinflation in 2023, but that critics of hardship are not enemies of the President.

Read more

The Emir of Kano and former Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) governor, Muhammadu Sanusi II, has urged President Bola Tinubu's government to listen to concerns about poverty rather than treating critics as enemies.

What he said

Speaking at the Oxford Global Think Tank conference convened by former World Bank vice-president Arunma Oteh, in comments reported on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Sanusi reportedly said the government's habit of issuing statements from the Presidential Villa to condemn people who raise economic concerns was counterproductive. He argued that citizens who talk about poverty are not attacking the President personally, and that the government should distinguish between criticism and hostility.

On the economy

Sanusi also said Nigeria was dangerously close to economic collapse in 2023 and on the verge of hyperinflation, a view he said he had held and expressed at the time. His comments suggest he sees the need for reform, while insisting that the hardship many people feel should be acknowledged.

Background

Sanusi was CBN governor from 2009 to 2014 and is a well-known commentator on the economy. He was reinstated as Emir of Kano in 2024. The Tinubu government removed the petrol subsidy and unified exchange rates in 2023, and says these moves rescued the economy, pointing to falling inflation and rising reserves. Critics, including investment banker Atedo Peterside this week, say the poor have become poorer. Presidency officials have often responded sharply to such criticism, accusing some critics of playing politics ahead of 2027.

What happens next

The Presidency had not responded to Sanusi's comments at the time of reporting.

Wike aide says there is hunger but people are still building houses, sparking debate

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Lere Olayinka, an aide to FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, has reportedly questioned how young Nigerians describe the level of hardship, pointing to ongoing construction as a sign of economic activity. His comments drew criticism online.

Read more

Lere Olayinka, Senior Special Assistant on Public Communications and Social Media to FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, has sparked debate with comments on poverty and hunger in Nigeria.

What he said

Appearing on a television politics programme, in comments reported on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Olayinka reportedly pushed back against what he described as the narrative of widespread poverty and hunger promoted by young Nigerians, often referred to as Gen Z. While he acknowledged that people were facing economic pressure, he pointed to private building projects and construction in communities as signs that economic activity was continuing. He said there is hunger, but people are still building houses.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the comments, saying they showed officials were out of touch with ordinary people struggling with high food and transport costs. Others agreed that the economy was not as bad as some critics suggest and said the government's reforms needed time.

Background

The cost of living is one of the most debated issues ahead of the 2027 elections. On the same day, the Emir of Kano, Muhammadu Sanusi II, urged the government to stop treating talk of poverty as an attack, while investment banker Atedo Peterside said the reforms were making the rich richer and the poor poorer. The government points to falling inflation, a stronger stock market and higher reserves as signs of progress. Wike, a former PDP governor of Rivers State, is a key ally of President Bola Tinubu and leads the Rainbow Coalition backing his re-election.

Oyo commissioner and two other officials resign from Makinde government in one day

3 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Oyo's Commissioner for Budget and Economic Planning, a special adviser and an electoral commission member reportedly resigned on Monday. One said he was no longer comfortable with the working environment, adding to a wave of departures before 2027.

Read more

Three officials serving in Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde's administration resigned on Monday, 2 November 2026, according to reports, in the latest of a series of departures from the state government.

Who resigned

Those who left were reportedly the Commissioner for Budget and Economic Planning, Ayobami Ojo; the Special Adviser on Budget and Economic Planning, Adegbola Oyeleke; and Sunday Falana, a member of the Oyo State Independent Electoral Commission. Ojo, who is from Ago-Are in Atisbo Local Government Area, submitted his letter through the Secretary to the State Government, said his exit was with immediate effect and thanked the governor for the opportunity to serve, without giving a reason. Oyeleke reportedly said he was no longer comfortable with the working environment in the administration.

Background

Makinde is serving his second and final term as governor and is now the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) for 2027. Several officials have left his government in recent months as politicians reposition ahead of the elections, with some moving to rival parties. The governor has not publicly commented on the latest resignations.

Why it matters

The departures come as Makinde campaigns nationally, including in the South East, and as parties compete for control of Oyo State, where the governorship seat will be open in 2027.

Deposed Ipetumodu monarch sues Adeleke to stop appointment of successor

3 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Oba Joseph Oloyede, removed as Apetumodu of Ipetumodu after his conviction in the US for fraud, has reportedly asked an Osun High Court to stop Governor Ademola Adeleke from filling the stool. The case was adjourned to 21 January 2027.

Read more

The deposed Apetumodu of Ipetumodu in Osun State, Oba Joseph Oloyede, has gone to court to stop Governor Ademola Adeleke from appointing a new traditional ruler for the town.

The case

According to reports, Oloyede filed a motion at the Osun State High Court in Ile-Ife asking the court to restrain the governor and other officials from filling the stool until his main case is decided. In the application, dated 16 September 2026, he argued that he was not formally told of any wrongdoing before he was removed, and challenged the legality of his deposition. Members of a branch of the town's ruling house joined him as claimants. The defendants include the governor, the state Attorney-General, the Commissioner for Local Government and Chieftaincy Affairs, local chiefs and Ife North Local Government.

What happened in court

Proceedings stalled on Monday, 2 November, after some defendants said they had not received hearing notices. Justice Dapo Adeniji adjourned the case to 21 January 2027 and ordered that fresh notices be served.

Background

Oloyede was removed from the throne after he was convicted in the United States on charges related to tax fraud and money laundering. Governor Adeleke reportedly signed the order removing him in May 2026.

What happens next

The court will hear the case in January. Until then, it is not clear whether the state will go ahead with appointing a new monarch.
