EFCC uncovers suspected illegal oil refinery allegedly operated by Chinese nationals in Ogun State

3 Nov 2026, 20:28 GMT

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has uncovered a suspected illegal oil refinery in Ogere, Ogun State, allegedly linked to Chinese nationals.

The discovery has raised fresh concerns about illegal petroleum processing in Nigeria, especially the possibility that some operations are running quietly in places far from the country's major oil-producing areas.

The raid

According to reports, EFCC operatives raided the facility on Saturday, 31 October 2026, but nobody was found on the premises when they arrived.

During the operation, investigators discovered underground storage tanks holding substances suspected to be diesel, suggesting the site may have been used for petroleum processing and storage.

Sludge oil from Warri and Port Harcourt

Preliminary investigations reportedly showed that sludge oil was transported from Warri in Delta State and Port Harcourt in Rivers State to the Ogun State facility, where it was allegedly processed into petroleum products.

That detail raises an important question: how was petroleum waste moved across several states to a facility allegedly operating outside the law without attracting the attention of the relevant authorities?

Questions for investigators

Given the size and nature of such an operation, investigators will also need to establish how long the facility had been running, who financed it and whether other individuals or companies were involved.

The EFCC has reportedly taken control of the premises while investigations continue to identify those responsible, including the alleged owners and operators.

Although Chinese nationals have been linked to the facility, the identities of those involved have not been publicly established, and the allegations remain under investigation.

A wider problem

For many Nigerians, the discovery points to a familiar and troubling issue. While the country continues to battle crude oil theft, illegal refining and lost revenue in the petroleum sector, questions remain about how such operations are set up and kept running.

The authorities are now expected to determine the full extent of what went on at the Ogere facility and whether any laws were broken.

The big question

Who owns this refinery, how long has it been operating, and who has been supplying the materials used there?

As the EFCC investigation continues, Nigerians will be watching to see whether those behind the suspected operation are identified and brought before the law.

  • The EFCC raided the Ogere facility on 31 October.
  • No one was found on the premises.
  • Sludge oil was reportedly brought in from Warri and Port Harcourt.

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has shut down Zuba International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

The operation

The closure followed a joint enforcement operation with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services, which reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome products worth more than ₦300 million. The exercise covered more than 160 shops in the market.

Why now

Naira gains ₦19 to ₦1,378 per dollar after CBN raises BDC limit

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The naira strengthened by ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar on the official market on Thursday, after the CBN raised the weekly dollar sales limit for bureaux de change. It had lost over ₦43 in the previous two weeks.

Read more

The naira recovered strongly against the US dollar on the official foreign exchange market on Thursday, 26 November 2026.

The rebound

The currency gained ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar, up from ₦1,397 on Wednesday. The recovery reversed some of the losses it suffered between 13 and 25 November, when it fell by ₦43.50 amid rising demand for dollars.

Why

The gain followed the Central Bank of Nigeria's decision to raise the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators from $150,000 to $1 million, aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the market.

Background

The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 23%.

Dangote cuts cooking gas price to ₦1,000 per kg, but retail prices stay high

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its cooking gas gantry price to ₦1,000 per kg, with its marketers selling at ₦1,100. Consumers, however, are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kg at retail outlets.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery has reportedly reduced the wholesale price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), commonly known as cooking gas, to ₦1,000 per kilogram.

The new prices

Marketers linked to the refinery have lowered their selling price to ₦1,100 per kilogram, according to a market survey.

The retail gap

Despite the cuts, consumers are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kilogram at retail outlets, raising concerns about the gap between wholesale and retail prices. Earlier this week, cooking gas was reported to be selling for up to ₦2,000 per kg in parts of Abuja.

Background

The refinery has also cut its petrol and diesel prices in recent weeks, most recently reducing diesel by ₦125 to ₦1,595 per litre.
