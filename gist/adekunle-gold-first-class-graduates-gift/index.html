Davido has publicly backed Zlatan Ibile's new song 'Inferno', in a move that fans say has reignited his own cold war with social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM).

What happened

In an Instagram post reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, Davido congratulated Zlatan, Peruzzi and Shoday on the song, thanking God for its success. 'Inferno' reportedly reached the top of the Apple Music Nigeria chart within 12 hours of release, as well as the Nigerian iTunes chart and the chart in Sierra Leone. VDM's response

The bitter dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried has taken a new turn, after his younger brother reportedly spoke out in defence of the singer's widow.

What the brother said

In a voice note said to be his, reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, the brother said he wanted to clear the air because people were blaming the wrong person. He insisted that the widow had no role in the dispute and should be left alone. He blamed the singer's maternal relatives for turning him against his father's side of the family from childhood, and said their father had supported the singer financially during his school years. He also described a disagreement in which the singer's grandmother asked him for a Sallah ram, which he declined because he is a Christian, leaving some relatives upset.

Others weigh in

Media personality Nancy Umeh reportedly criticised the singer's family and said the widow has every right to decide where her husband is buried. Actor Yomi Fabiyi also sent a message to the widow, according to reports. On social media, many fans praised the brother for speaking up and said the widow had been unfairly attacked.

Background

Toluwanising died suddenly on 14 October 2026. Since then, members of his family have disagreed publicly about his burial. A family spokesman said in a viral video this week that relatives would remove his body if he was buried in a cemetery, and insisted he be laid beside his mother in the family compound. His widow had reportedly objected to a burial at the house they shared in Ibadan.

What happens next

No burial date has been announced, and fans are calling on both sides to resolve the matter privately so the singer can be laid to rest.

Mohbad's family asks for DNA test delay, wants details of US lab

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

The family of late singer Mohbad has asked for the DNA test on his son to be postponed until it is told which US laboratory will analyse the samples. The family says it is not opposing the test but wants transparency. The test was scheduled for Saturday in Lagos.

Read more

The family of the late singer Ilerioluwa 'Mohbad' Aloba has asked for the planned DNA test on his son, Liam, to be put on hold until it receives more information about how the samples will be handled.

What the family wants

In a statement signed by family head Samuel Aloba on Thursday, 29 October 2026, the family asked for the identity, address and accreditation of the laboratory in the United States that will analyse the samples. It also asked whether the Lagos State DNA and Forensic Centre would carry out the test itself or only collect samples to be sent abroad, and requested clear procedures to protect the samples on their journey. The family asked that all parties be given proper notice and that the court give further directions if needed.

Not opposing the test

The family said it was not against the paternity test and was not claiming that anything improper had happened. It said it only wanted transparency and safeguards to prevent any possible mishandling. The test had been scheduled to take place on Saturday, 31 October, at a hospital in Yaba, Lagos. It is not yet clear whether it will go ahead as planned.

Background

Mohbad died on 12 September 2023 at the age of 27, and his death sparked nationwide protests and calls for justice. The paternity of his son has since been the subject of a bitter dispute between his father, Joseph Aloba, and his widow, Omowunmi. A Lagos court ordered in May 2026 that the test be carried out by the Lagos State DNA and Forensic Centre.

What happens next

The widow's camp had not responded to the latest demand at the time of the report. Further court directions may be needed if the test is delayed.
