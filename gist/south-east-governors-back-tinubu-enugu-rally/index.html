South-East governors urge region to back Tinubu at APC women's rally in Enugu

1 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

Four South-East state governments have thrown their weight behind President Bola Tinubu's re-election bid, in a show of support in a region widely seen as Peter Obi's stronghold.

What happened

At an APC Women Mega Rally and Empowerment Programme for the South-East at Okpara Square, Enugu, on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the governors reportedly urged residents to back the President and APC candidates at all levels in the January 2027 elections.

What the governors said

Enugu Governor Peter Mbah said the region should build on the government's economic reforms rather than change course, comparing it to a sailor who does not abandon his ship mid-sea. He also described an empowerment programme for 5,000 women across the five South-East states. Imo Governor Hope Uzodimma said Igbo people were now seeing real inclusion and development under Tinubu, and that improved federal allocations had helped his state pay salaries and pensions regularly. Ebonyi Governor Francis Nwifuru praised the APC's skills programmes and denied links to any coalition outside the party. Anambra Governor Charles Soludo was represented by his deputy, Gilbert Ibezim, who said the region should repay the administration with votes. Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu also spoke, noting that no Igbo person had been president in six decades and praising the South East Development Commission.

Background

Peter Obi, the NDC presidential candidate and a former Anambra governor, won most of the South East in the 2023 presidential election. He has said he would serve only one term if elected, while some APC figures argue the region should aim for a longer stay in power through alliances. Obi had not responded to the rally at the time of the report.

What happens next

Both the APC and the NDC are expected to intensify their campaigns in the South East, which is likely to be one of the most closely watched regions in the election.

  • The South East is seen as Peter Obi's stronghold.

Representatives of former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde have reportedly been meeting to discuss a possible alliance ahead of the 2027 presidential election.

The talks

According to reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, sources from both camps say the prospect of an alliance is looking increasingly likely. An ADC leader in the South-West, speaking anonymously, described the discussions as fresh, purposeful and promising, and suggested an announcement would come before the 16 January election. The talks are said to be separate from wider opposition efforts to agree on a single candidate. Neither Atiku nor Makinde has publicly confirmed any deal.

Background

Atiku is the candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), while Makinde is running on the platform of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM). Makinde hosted an opposition summit in Ibadan in April to discuss a joint candidate, but no agreement emerged, with Atiku, Makinde and Peter Obi each pressing ahead with their own campaigns. Makinde said this week that talks with both Atiku and Obi remained open. ADC running mate Rotimi Amaechi also told a rally in Bauchi that the APM would win one South-West state.

Judgment details: why the court sacked the Mark-led ADC leadership

14 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The certified copy of the judgment that voided the David Mark-led ADC leadership shows the judge found a key resignation letter was fabricated and ruled that the party's executive committee could not appoint national officers without an election.

Read more

Details of the Federal High Court judgment that nullified the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) have emerged from the certified true copy of the ruling.

The resignation letter

Justice Peter Lifu, who delivered the judgment on 5 November 2026, examined three main issues. The first was a letter said to show that the party's deputy national chairman, Nafiu Bala Gombe, had resigned in May 2025, before the meeting that installed the new leadership. After comparing signatures, the judge found clear differences, described the letter as fictitious or fabricated and rejected it as evidence.

Powers of the executive committee

The ADC argued that its constitution allowed the National Executive Committee to dissolve the old leadership and appoint a new one. The judge accepted that parties may run their internal affairs, but held that this cannot override the Constitution and the Electoral Act, which require parties to elect their principal officers through periodic democratic elections. He ruled that the committee's administrative powers did not allow it to appoint national executives in place of an election.

No proof of a vote

The judge also found that the party could not show that members or delegates had been given the chance to vote for candidates of their choice, and said notifying INEC of a meeting was not the same as complying with the law.

Background

The judgment also voided the primary that produced Atiku Abubakar as the ADC's presidential candidate. The party has appealed, and INEC says it will obey the final court decision. Atiku and his running mate Rotimi Amaechi are continuing to campaign.
