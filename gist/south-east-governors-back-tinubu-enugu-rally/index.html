South-East governors urge region to back Tinubu at APC women's rally in Enugu

1 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

Four South-East state governments have thrown their weight behind President Bola Tinubu's re-election bid, in a show of support in a region widely seen as Peter Obi's stronghold.

What happened

At an APC Women Mega Rally and Empowerment Programme for the South-East at Okpara Square, Enugu, on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the governors reportedly urged residents to back the President and APC candidates at all levels in the January 2027 elections.

What the governors said

Enugu Governor Peter Mbah said the region should build on the government's economic reforms rather than change course, comparing it to a sailor who does not abandon his ship mid-sea. He also described an empowerment programme for 5,000 women across the five South-East states. Imo Governor Hope Uzodimma said Igbo people were now seeing real inclusion and development under Tinubu, and that improved federal allocations had helped his state pay salaries and pensions regularly. Ebonyi Governor Francis Nwifuru praised the APC's skills programmes and denied links to any coalition outside the party. Anambra Governor Charles Soludo was represented by his deputy, Gilbert Ibezim, who said the region should repay the administration with votes. Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu also spoke, noting that no Igbo person had been president in six decades and praising the South East Development Commission.

Background

Peter Obi, the NDC presidential candidate and a former Anambra governor, won most of the South East in the 2023 presidential election. He has said he would serve only one term if elected, while some APC figures argue the region should aim for a longer stay in power through alliances. Obi had not responded to the rally at the time of the report.

What happens next

Both the APC and the NDC are expected to intensify their campaigns in the South East, which is likely to be one of the most closely watched regions in the election.

  • They urged the region to back Tinubu in January 2027.
  • The South East is seen as Peter Obi's stronghold.

DSS says it found no evidence it planned to disrupt ADC campaign in Sokoto, asks Atiku to retract or provide proof

18 Nov 2026, 07:57 GMT

The Department of State Services says its internal investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off in Sokoto. It has asked Atiku Abubakar to retract or clarify his allegation, or provide evidence.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) says its investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential campaign in Sokoto.

How it started

Earlier this month, former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar accused the leadership of the DSS of recruiting protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto.

The allegation drew wide attention, and the DSS responded by ordering an investigation into the claim.

The DSS findings

The agency now says its investigation found nothing to support the allegation that it planned to recruit protesters or disrupt the ADC campaign.

It has asked Atiku to either retract or clarify his claim, or to provide evidence that could support further investigation.

Not the final word

It is important to note that this is the DSS's own preliminary finding. It was not an independent investigation, which means the agency effectively investigated an allegation made against itself.

Atiku has not issued a fresh public response to the DSS findings.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections approaching, allegations that security agencies are being used against opposition parties are likely to keep coming up. Public trust depends on such claims being properly examined, whether they turn out to be true or not.

Kwara APC governorship rally in Offa reportedly turns violent over unpaid mobilisation funds

16 Nov 2026, 11:21 GMT

An APC governorship rally in Offa, Kwara State, reportedly descended into chaos after claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised. The incident has revived debate about paying crowds to attend political events.

Read more

A governorship campaign rally organised by the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Offa, Kwara State, has reportedly descended into chaos following claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised.

What reportedly happened

According to reports, the rally drew a large crowd of supporters to the streets of Offa. But tension rose when some of those who had turned up complained that they had not received the money they were allegedly promised for attending.

The situation reportedly turned violent as angry participants confronted organisers, disrupting the event.

The full details, including whether anyone was injured and how much money was involved, have not been independently confirmed, and the party has not publicly commented on the incident.

The business of crowds

The incident shines a light on a practice many Nigerians already know about: paying people, often called "mobilisation", to attend political rallies.

Across parties, campaign organisers frequently provide transport, food, branded caps and T-shirts, and sometimes cash, to fill venues and create the impression of massive support. When those promises are not kept, frustration can quickly boil over.

It also raises a deeper question. If people have to be paid to attend, how much do the crowds at political rallies really tell us about genuine support?

Petrol prices fall at filling stations after Dangote cuts rate by ₦51

18 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Major marketers including AA Rano, NIPCO and AP Ardova have reportedly reduced pump prices to between ₦1,350 and ₦1,385 per litre. It follows a ₦51 per litre cut by Dangote Refinery.

Read more

Motorists are beginning to see some relief at filling stations as major marketers reduce petrol prices following a ₦51 per litre cut by the Dangote Petroleum Refinery.

The new prices

A market survey on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, reportedly showed that AA Rano, the Nigerian Independent Petroleum Company (NIPCO) and AP Ardova had lowered their petrol prices to between ₦1,350 and ₦1,385 per litre. Several filling stations are said to have cut prices by between ₦20 and ₦60 per litre, with NNPC and other marketers also adjusting their rates.

Why prices are falling

The reductions come as the cost of importing petrol has dropped, with the landing cost of imported fuel reportedly falling to about ₦1,067 per litre, below Dangote Refinery's own rate. This has put pressure on local prices.

The survey suggested more stations could follow with price cuts in the coming days.

The celebration

The P-Square star shared a video of himself sitting alone at a dining table filled with food, with a caption expressing gratitude and pride.

The solo celebration follows Peter Okoye's announcement in April 2026 that he would no longer share their birth date. Peter, who performs as Mr P, said on X that 30 November would now be his official birthday, describing it as a personal decision and thanking fans for understanding.

How fans reacted

The birthday post drew many reactions, with numerous fans expressing sadness that the twins, who rose to fame together as P-Square, now mark their birthdays separately.
