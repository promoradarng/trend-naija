South-East governors urge region to back Tinubu at APC women's rally in Enugu

1 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

Four South-East state governments have thrown their weight behind President Bola Tinubu's re-election bid, in a show of support in a region widely seen as Peter Obi's stronghold.

What happened

At an APC Women Mega Rally and Empowerment Programme for the South-East at Okpara Square, Enugu, on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the governors reportedly urged residents to back the President and APC candidates at all levels in the January 2027 elections.

What the governors said

Enugu Governor Peter Mbah said the region should build on the government's economic reforms rather than change course, comparing it to a sailor who does not abandon his ship mid-sea. He also described an empowerment programme for 5,000 women across the five South-East states. Imo Governor Hope Uzodimma said Igbo people were now seeing real inclusion and development under Tinubu, and that improved federal allocations had helped his state pay salaries and pensions regularly. Ebonyi Governor Francis Nwifuru praised the APC's skills programmes and denied links to any coalition outside the party. Anambra Governor Charles Soludo was represented by his deputy, Gilbert Ibezim, who said the region should repay the administration with votes. Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu also spoke, noting that no Igbo person had been president in six decades and praising the South East Development Commission.

Background

Peter Obi, the NDC presidential candidate and a former Anambra governor, won most of the South East in the 2023 presidential election. He has said he would serve only one term if elected, while some APC figures argue the region should aim for a longer stay in power through alliances. Obi had not responded to the rally at the time of the report.

What happens next

Both the APC and the NDC are expected to intensify their campaigns in the South East, which is likely to be one of the most closely watched regions in the election.

  • Governors Mbah, Uzodimma and Nwifuru spoke at the rally; Soludo was represented.
  • They urged the region to back Tinubu in January 2027.
  • The South East is seen as Peter Obi's stronghold.

Fashola says Tinubu stands out because he has kept his allies for 16 years

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Former Lagos Governor Babatunde Fashola has argued that President Tinubu differs from Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi because he has kept the loyalty of his political allies for about 16 years. He said even former opponents now work with him.

Read more

Former Lagos State Governor Babatunde Fashola has made a case for President Bola Tinubu's re-election, arguing that he stands apart from his main rivals by keeping his political allies for about 16 years.

The comparison

Speaking in a television interview on Friday, 27 November 2026, Fashola noted that Tinubu, Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi all began their political rise as state governors, although Atiku was elected governor but did not serve. He framed his argument as a job reference question, saying that Tinubu's long-time allies have stayed with him.

He also said that even some of the president's fiercest former opponents now work with him.

Background

Fashola, who succeeded Tinubu as Lagos governor and later served as a federal minister, also condemned the 'rig and die' slogan in the same interview. Atiku and Obi are expected to sign an opposition cooperation pact at a summit in Abuja on Monday.

The other side

No response from the Atiku or Obi campaigns was included in the reports.

Obi urges mothers to pray for Nigeria and guard their votes

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has appealed to Nigerian mothers to pray for the country and to stay behind after voting to make sure their votes are counted. He spoke at an ECWA women's fellowship in Abuja.

Read more

Peter Obi, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate, has appealed to Nigerian mothers to pray for the country and to protect their votes at the 2027 elections.

What he said

Speaking at an ECWA Women Fellowship event in Abuja, Obi expressed concern about rising poverty, unemployment and insecurity. He said Nigeria now has the highest number of people living in poverty in the world, that millions of young people are unemployed, and that mothers are suffering because of insecurity.

He urged the women, after voting for the candidate of their choice on election day, to stay behind at polling units to make sure their votes are counted.

A warm welcome

Videos from the convention showed women rushing excitedly to greet him.

Background

Obi is expected to attend an opposition summit in Abuja on Monday, where he, Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde are due to sign a cooperation pact.
