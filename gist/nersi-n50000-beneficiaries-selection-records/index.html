Government says ₦50,000 NERSI beneficiaries will be chosen from official records, not applications

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The federal government has explained how it will choose beneficiaries for its National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention (NERSI), which pays ₦50,000 a month to vulnerable Nigerians.

How people will be selected

According to reports on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, the government said beneficiaries will be identified strictly through verified official records and defined eligibility criteria, rather than through applications or nominations. Only people who meet the set criteria will be considered.

Scam warnings

Officials have warned Nigerians not to share their PINs or one-time passwords with anyone who calls claiming to represent the programme, and have explained who may collect the payment on a beneficiary's behalf. Several digital banks and payment platforms have also published guidance on how to check eligibility.

Payments and SMS delays

The government has started paying the grant and has told people who have not yet received a text message not to assume they have been left out. In a frequently asked questions page on the programme's website, viewed on Thursday, 5 November, it said payment providers are contacting confirmed beneficiaries in batches, so some people will be notified later than others. It also said not everyone will be included in the current round, as the scheme covers a limited number of households at a time. People can check their status by dialling their payment provider's USSD code; those not selected receive a message saying they are currently not selected to receive the relief support. The ₦50,000 is paid into a mobile wallet through one of five approved providers.

Background

NERSI is one of several government schemes aimed at cushioning the effect of high living costs, alongside the HoPE cash transfer programme, which pays ₦25,000 a month for three months to eligible households, and the CREDICORP consumer credit scheme. The government says it is using the national social register to target the poorest households. Critics have questioned whether the payments reach the right people and say the amounts are small compared with rising prices.

What it means

People who have not been contacted through official channels are being advised to treat any request for payment or personal details with suspicion.

  • Beneficiaries will come from verified official records.
  • There is no application or nomination process.
  • Nigerians are warned not to share PINs or OTPs.

NAFDAC shuts Abuja's Zuba International Market over fake products worth ₦300m

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has shut down Zuba International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

The operation

The closure followed a joint enforcement operation with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services, which reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome products worth more than ₦300 million. The exercise covered more than 160 shops in the market.

Why now

NAFDAC said the closure was part of a wider crackdown on substandard and fake goods ahead of the festive season.

Naira gains ₦19 to ₦1,378 per dollar after CBN raises BDC limit

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The naira strengthened by ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar on the official market on Thursday, after the CBN raised the weekly dollar sales limit for bureaux de change. It had lost over ₦43 in the previous two weeks.

Read more

The naira recovered strongly against the US dollar on the official foreign exchange market on Thursday, 26 November 2026.

The rebound

The currency gained ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar, up from ₦1,397 on Wednesday. The recovery reversed some of the losses it suffered between 13 and 25 November, when it fell by ₦43.50 amid rising demand for dollars.

Why

The gain followed the Central Bank of Nigeria's decision to raise the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators from $150,000 to $1 million, aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the market.

Background

The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 23%.

Dangote cuts cooking gas price to ₦1,000 per kg, but retail prices stay high

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its cooking gas gantry price to ₦1,000 per kg, with its marketers selling at ₦1,100. Consumers, however, are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kg at retail outlets.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery has reportedly reduced the wholesale price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), commonly known as cooking gas, to ₦1,000 per kilogram.

The new prices

Marketers linked to the refinery have lowered their selling price to ₦1,100 per kilogram, according to a market survey.

The retail gap

Despite the cuts, consumers are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kilogram at retail outlets, raising concerns about the gap between wholesale and retail prices. Earlier this week, cooking gas was reported to be selling for up to ₦2,000 per kg in parts of Abuja.

Background

The refinery has also cut its petrol and diesel prices in recent weeks, most recently reducing diesel by ₦125 to ₦1,595 per litre.
