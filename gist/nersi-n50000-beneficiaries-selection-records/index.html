Government says ₦50,000 NERSI beneficiaries will be chosen from official records, not applications

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The federal government has explained how it will choose beneficiaries for its National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention (NERSI), which pays ₦50,000 a month to vulnerable Nigerians.

How people will be selected

According to reports on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, the government said beneficiaries will be identified strictly through verified official records and defined eligibility criteria, rather than through applications or nominations. Only people who meet the set criteria will be considered.

Scam warnings

Officials have warned Nigerians not to share their PINs or one-time passwords with anyone who calls claiming to represent the programme, and have explained who may collect the payment on a beneficiary's behalf. Several digital banks and payment platforms have also published guidance on how to check eligibility.

Payments and SMS delays

The government has started paying the grant and has told people who have not yet received a text message not to assume they have been left out. In a frequently asked questions page on the programme's website, viewed on Thursday, 5 November, it said payment providers are contacting confirmed beneficiaries in batches, so some people will be notified later than others. It also said not everyone will be included in the current round, as the scheme covers a limited number of households at a time. People can check their status by dialling their payment provider's USSD code; those not selected receive a message saying they are currently not selected to receive the relief support. The ₦50,000 is paid into a mobile wallet through one of five approved providers.

Background

NERSI is one of several government schemes aimed at cushioning the effect of high living costs, alongside the HoPE cash transfer programme, which pays ₦25,000 a month for three months to eligible households, and the CREDICORP consumer credit scheme. The government says it is using the national social register to target the poorest households. Critics have questioned whether the payments reach the right people and say the amounts are small compared with rising prices.

What it means

People who have not been contacted through official channels are being advised to treat any request for payment or personal details with suspicion.

  • Beneficiaries will come from verified official records.
  • There is no application or nomination process.
  • Nigerians are warned not to share PINs or OTPs.

Christmas return air tickets climb as high as ₦822,000 on some routes

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Return tickets on some domestic routes for the Christmas and New Year period have reached ₦822,000, with Air Peace listing about ₦676,000 for Lagos–Anambra return between 24 December and 2 January.

Read more

Nigerians planning to fly home for Christmas and the New Year are facing steep fares, with return tickets on some domestic routes reaching ₦822,000.

The fares

According to checks reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, major carriers including Air Peace, United Nigeria Airlines and ValueJet have listed much higher fares for December 2026 and early January 2027, especially on routes linking Lagos and Abuja to the South-East and South-South. Air Peace listed a Lagos–Anambra return ticket between 24 December and 2 January at about ₦676,000, with the outbound flight at ₦341,500 and the return at ₦335,500. An Abuja–Anambra return costs about ₦677,000, and Lagos–Owerri about ₦671,000. The highest return fares found reached ₦822,000.

Why fares rise

Demand for flights to the South-East and South-South surges every December as many Nigerians travel home for the holidays, weddings and other ceremonies. Airlines say the high cost of aviation fuel, foreign exchange for maintenance and leasing, and a shortage of aircraft push up prices.

Background

Last week, one-way economy fares for flights around 20 and 21 December were reported at up to ₦337,500, increases of as much as 208 per cent. The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission previously accused some airlines of price-fixing during the 2025 festive season, which the airlines denied. Many travellers turn to road transport instead, despite security concerns on some highways.

What it means

Travellers are being advised to book early to avoid the highest prices.

Woman confronts herder after cattle destroy her farm crops in Nasarawa State

10 Nov 2026, 00:44 GMT

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle strayed onto her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months growing. The incident has reopened the question of who pays when a farmer's livelihood is wiped out.

Read more

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle entered her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months cultivating.

Months of work gone

For someone who depends on farming to survive, watching cattle destroy months of hard work can be heartbreaking.

Imagine spending your money on seeds, preparing the land, planting and waiting patiently for harvest, only to wake up one day and find your crops destroyed. Worse still, many farmers borrow money to cultivate their land, hoping to repay the loan after harvest.

An old source of tension

Incidents like this remain one of the main causes of tension between farmers and herders in parts of Nigeria, and some have escalated into violence in the past.

The question is: who compensates the woman for her losses? Who takes responsibility for the destroyed crops?

Protection for everyone

Farmers deserve protection, just as herders have a right to carry out their lawful activities. But nobody should be allowed to destroy another person's livelihood without consequences.

Clear rules on grazing, quick ways to settle disputes and fair compensation could help prevent confrontations like this from turning into something worse.

Osun government offices shut as NLC begins three-day strike over council funds

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Government offices and public schools in Osogbo were shut on Monday as workers in Osun began a three-day strike in solidarity with local government employees over withheld statutory allocations to the state's councils.

Read more

Government offices in Osun State were shut on Monday, 9 November 2026, as workers began a three-day strike over withheld allocations to local governments.

What happened

The gates of the state government secretariat and the House of Assembly in Osogbo were locked, with no workers or business activity at either, according to reports. Local government secretariats in Olorunda and Osogbo were also closed, and pupils in public schools in Osogbo were sent home after teachers joined the strike.

Why workers are striking

In a statement on Sunday, the state chapter of the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC), led by its chairman, Christopher Arapasopo, directed all affiliated unions to join the strike in solidarity with the Nigeria Union of Local Government Employees (NULGE). The NLC said the action followed the expiry of a seven-day ultimatum to the federal and Osun state governments over the continued withholding of statutory allocations to local councils in the state, which it said remained unresolved.

Background

Osun's local governments have been at the centre of a long legal and political dispute over who controls the councils, which has led to federal allocations being withheld. Osun is governed by Ademola Adeleke, who has backed President Bola Tinubu's re-election.
