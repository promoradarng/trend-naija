EFCC uncovers illegal refinery in Ogun allegedly run by foreign nationals

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) says it has uncovered an illegal oil refinery in Ogere, Ogun State, allegedly run by Chinese nationals.

The raid

In a statement on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, the EFCC said operatives of its Lagos Zonal Directorate 2 raided the facility on Saturday, 31 October, after intelligence and surveillance of illegal sludge oil processing in the area. The site was empty when officers arrived, but there were signs of recent activity, including underground tanks containing a substance suspected to be diesel.

What investigators found

According to the commission, preliminary investigations showed that the operators sourced sludge oil from Delta and Rivers states and brought it to the Ogere site for processing. The EFCC said it has taken over the site and is investigating the operators, the owner of the property and possible economic sabotage. No arrests were announced.

Background

Illegal refining is a major problem in Nigeria, especially in the Niger Delta, where crude oil is stolen from pipelines and processed in makeshift refineries. The practice causes huge losses to the country, pollutes land and water, and has led to deadly fires. Security agencies say illegal refining has also spread to other parts of the country. The government has stepped up efforts against oil theft.

What happens next

The EFCC says investigations are continuing and suspects will be prosecuted if found.

  • The site in Ogere was deserted but had tanks of suspected diesel.
  • Operators allegedly sourced sludge oil from Delta and Rivers.
  • The EFCC is investigating the operators and property owner.

At least 30 feared dead, about 300 missing as boat carrying mourners capsizes in Bayelsa

20 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

At least 30 people are feared dead and about 300 missing after an overloaded boat carrying mourners to a burial capsized near Oweikorogha in Southern Ijaw, Bayelsa State. Police marine units are leading rescue efforts.

Read more

A boat said to be carrying more than 300 passengers has capsized in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of Bayelsa State, with many people feared dead or missing.

What happened

The boat was travelling from Ayama community to Ukubie for a burial ceremony when it capsized at about noon on Friday, 20 November 2026, shortly after passing Oweikorogha community.

The exact number of passengers on board and the number of casualties had not been established at the time of the reports, but residents fear several people may have lost their lives.

Rescue efforts

The Bayelsa State Police Command spokesperson, DSP Musa Muhammad, confirmed the incident and said the Commissioner of Police had deployed the Marine Police Unit and the local divisional police officer for rescue operations. He said further details on casualties and missing persons would be released.

A former state chairman of the Maritime Workers Union also confirmed the incident.

Death toll rises

Later reports put the number feared dead at at least 30, with around 300 others unaccounted for. Witnesses described the boat as heavily overloaded, and a community source said some people had raised concerns as passengers boarded.

What happens next

Rescue and recovery efforts are continuing, and official casualty figures are expected once they are confirmed.
