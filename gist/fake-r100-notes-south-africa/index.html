Nigerians allegedly busted for producing fake R100 notes in South Africa

27 Oct 2026, 07:20 GMT

A suspected counterfeit currency operation involving Nigerian nationals in South Africa has sparked reactions following reports that individuals were caught allegedly producing fake R100 banknotes.

The incident has raised fresh concerns about counterfeit money circulating in South Africa and the possible consequences for unsuspecting businesses and members of the public.

According to claims surrounding the incident, the individuals were allegedly involved in producing counterfeit South African R100 notes, which could potentially be passed off as genuine currency during everyday transactions.

However, the exact location of the alleged operation, the number of suspects involved, and the circumstances surrounding their reported arrest have yet to be independently verified.

Counterfeit money poses a serious threat to businesses, particularly small traders, street vendors, transport operators and shop owners who handle cash transactions daily. When fake banknotes enter circulation, unsuspecting individuals may accept them as payment, only to discover later that the money has no value.

The alleged involvement of foreign nationals could also fuel tensions in a country where immigration and crime remain sensitive public issues. However, criminal responsibility rests with the individuals involved, and allegations against particular suspects should not be used to generalise about Nigerians living and working legally in South Africa.

Under South African law, producing, possessing or distributing counterfeit currency can attract serious criminal penalties. If the allegations are confirmed, those involved could face prosecution.

No official statement from the authorities detailing the alleged operation, the evidence recovered or any charges filed has been independently confirmed.

The reported incident has prompted questions about how counterfeit currency operations are detected and what measures can protect ordinary people from accepting fake money. It also highlights the importance of checking banknotes carefully, particularly when handling large amounts of cash.

Guinea deports 1,557 West Africans convicted of illegal gold mining

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Guinea has deported 1,557 West Africans, mostly from Burkina Faso and Mali, who had been jailed for illegal gold mining, as it steps up a crackdown on unauthorised mining.

Read more

Guinea has expelled 1,557 West African nationals who were arrested and convicted for illegal gold mining.

The deportation

According to judicial and security sources quoted in reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, the deportees, mostly citizens of Burkina Faso and Mali, were arrested at mining sites during security operations. After serving prison sentences of 18 months to two years, they were taken in a convoy of 44 vehicles from the capital, Conakry, to the border town of Kouremale on the Mali border, arriving on Saturday.

The crackdown

Guinean authorities stepped up operations against illegal mining in early November, particularly in Siguiri, a major gold-producing region in the north-east. The government says the aim is to stop illegal exploitation of mineral resources and limit environmental damage from unregulated mining.

Background

The crackdown has also raised tensions between miners and the authorities.

Jordan sentences three men to death over deadly church attack

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

A Jordanian court has sentenced three men to death for a gun attack on a Greek Orthodox church funeral in October that killed nine people and wounded 25.

Read more

A court in Jordan has sentenced three men to death by hanging for a deadly attack on a church in the south of the country.

The verdict

The State Security Court on Sunday, 15 November 2026, found the men, two brothers and a relative by marriage, guilty of terrorist attacks resulting in death, weapons offences, conspiracy to commit terrorist acts and promoting the ideas of a terrorist organisation, according to a statement from the Jordanian military.

The attack

Gunmen killed nine people and wounded 25 when they stormed a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud village, in the Karak governorate, on 23 October 2026. The three suspects fled after the attack and were arrested three days later.

Background

The attack shocked Jordan, a Muslim-majority country where Christians make up around three per cent of the population.

Zambia and Congo pull out of Miss Universe over US travel rules; Nigeria's queen arrives

15 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Zambia and the Republic of the Congo have withdrawn from Miss Universe 2026 in Puerto Rico after their contestants could not get US travel permission. Nigeria's Danielle Adewusi has arrived and joined preliminary events.

Read more

Two African countries have withdrawn from the 75th Miss Universe pageant because their contestants could not get permission to travel to Puerto Rico, a US territory.

Who is affected

According to reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Zambia and the Republic of the Congo have pulled out, while representatives from Zimbabwe and South Sudan were still awaiting decisions ahead of the final on 24 November. Miss Universe Zambia said its representative, a 25-year-old registered nurse who won the national title in October, could not secure the required travel authorisation, and blamed US travel restrictions rather than any failing by her or the organisers. The Republic of the Congo had been due to send its first ever Miss Universe contestant.

Nigeria's contestant

Nigeria is also affected by partial US entry restrictions, but its representative, Danielle Adewusi, arrived in San Juan on 9 November and has joined the preliminary activities.

Background

Because Puerto Rico is a US territory, contestants travelling there must meet US immigration requirements.

Davido, Tems and Ayra Starr all nominated for 2027 Grammy Best Global Music Album

16 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Davido's 'ORIADÉ', Tems' 'Love Is a Kingdom' and Ayra Starr's 'Starrgirl' are among the five nominees for Best Global Music Album at the 2027 Grammy Awards, alongside Anitta and Angélique Kidjo. The ceremony is on 7 February 2027.

Read more

Three Nigerian stars, Davido, Tems and Ayra Starr, have been nominated in the same category at the 2027 Grammy Awards.

The nominees

The Recording Academy announced on Monday, 16 November 2026, that the nominees for Best Global Music Album are 'EQUILIBRIVM' by Brazilian star Anitta, 'ORIADÉ' by Davido, 'HOPE!!' by Beninese legend Angélique Kidjo, 'STARRGIRL' by Ayra Starr and 'LOVE IS A KINGDOM' by Tems. The 69th Grammy Awards will be held on 7 February 2027.

Davido's album

'ORIADÉ', Davido's sixth studio album, was released on 31 July 2026 and marks his 15 years in music. The 13-track project was submitted in several categories, including Album of the Year, but received its nomination in the global music category. Davido has been nominated before, including for his album 'Timeless' and the songs 'Unavailable' and 'Feel' in 2024, but has yet to win.

A strong year for Nigeria

Tems, who has already won Grammys, is also executive music producer of the soundtrack for the Hollywood film 'Children of Blood and Bone', while Ayra Starr was recently announced for Coachella 2027. Kidjo is a five-time Grammy winner.
