Nigerians allegedly busted for producing fake R100 notes in South Africa

27 Oct 2026, 07:20 GMT

A suspected counterfeit currency operation involving Nigerian nationals in South Africa has sparked reactions following reports that individuals were caught allegedly producing fake R100 banknotes.

The incident has raised fresh concerns about counterfeit money circulating in South Africa and the possible consequences for unsuspecting businesses and members of the public.

According to claims surrounding the incident, the individuals were allegedly involved in producing counterfeit South African R100 notes, which could potentially be passed off as genuine currency during everyday transactions.

However, the exact location of the alleged operation, the number of suspects involved, and the circumstances surrounding their reported arrest have yet to be independently verified.

Counterfeit money poses a serious threat to businesses, particularly small traders, street vendors, transport operators and shop owners who handle cash transactions daily. When fake banknotes enter circulation, unsuspecting individuals may accept them as payment, only to discover later that the money has no value.

The alleged involvement of foreign nationals could also fuel tensions in a country where immigration and crime remain sensitive public issues. However, criminal responsibility rests with the individuals involved, and allegations against particular suspects should not be used to generalise about Nigerians living and working legally in South Africa.

Under South African law, producing, possessing or distributing counterfeit currency can attract serious criminal penalties. If the allegations are confirmed, those involved could face prosecution.

No official statement from the authorities detailing the alleged operation, the evidence recovered or any charges filed has been independently confirmed.

The reported incident has prompted questions about how counterfeit currency operations are detected and what measures can protect ordinary people from accepting fake money. It also highlights the importance of checking banknotes carefully, particularly when handling large amounts of cash.

US adds 24 more Nigerians to 'worst of the worst' deportation list

24 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The US Department of Homeland Security has reportedly added 24 Nigerians to its list of foreign nationals arrested for serious crimes, bringing the number of Nigerians on it to 157. The allegations range from fraud to drug offences.

Read more

The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has reportedly added 24 Nigerians to its deportation list, bringing the total number of Nigerian nationals named to 157.

The list

DHS updated its 'Worst of the Worst' initiative on Monday, 23 November 2026. The list identifies foreign nationals arrested by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) for what it describes as serious crimes, and now includes more than 45,000 people.

The allegations

The people listed face allegations ranging from drug and weapons offences and violent crimes to fraud and the misuse of public benefits. Many of the Nigerians named were arrested over alleged wire fraud, mail fraud or impersonation in states including Texas, Virginia, New Jersey, Georgia and Indiana.

Background

Being named on the list does not mean a person has been convicted.

A fire broke out early on Monday, 23 November 2026, at Will Smith and Jada Pinkett Smith's $42 million estate in Calabasas, California.

What happened

Sheriff's deputies were called shortly after midnight when security staff reported flames on the 150-acre property. They found a maintenance shed fully ablaze. Firefighters spent about two hours putting out the fire, which officials said did not spread to any other buildings.

No injuries

A sheriff's spokesperson said no one was injured and that the cause of the fire was accidental.

Background

It is the latest in a string of fires to affect the family's properties over the past eight years. Their biggest loss came in January 2025, when the Palisades Fire tore through parts of Malibu.

Democrats win biggest US House majority in more than three decades

23 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

US Democrats now hold 258 of 435 House seats after a recount in North Carolina, their largest majority since 1992. They also won the Senate in this month's midterm elections.

Read more

The Democratic Party has secured its largest majority in the US House of Representatives in more than 30 years, according to media projections.

The deciding race

US networks projected on Sunday, 22 November 2026, that Democrat Chuck Hubbard had defeated Republican incumbent Virginia Foxx in North Carolina after a recount requested by Foxx. The win gives Democrats 258 seats in the 435-member chamber, matching the number they won in 1992, the year Bill Clinton was elected president. Hubbard said Foxx had conceded and thanked her for her decades of service.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries celebrated on X, saying the party now held the largest majority of any party in Congress this century.

Senate too

Democrats also won control of the Senate in this month's midterm elections, a major setback for President Donald Trump and the Republican Party.

What it means

Control of both chambers gives Democrats more power to block Trump's legislative agenda and launch investigations during the final two years of his presidency. The results were widely seen as reflecting voter frustration over living costs, tariffs and the conflict with Iran.

Thousands evacuated from Don Toliver concert in Manchester after unknown substance released

23 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Police declared a major incident at Manchester's Co-op Live arena after an unknown substance made concertgoers unwell during a Don Toliver show. More than 50 people were assessed and one was taken to hospital.

Read more

Thousands of fans were evacuated from Manchester's Co-op Live arena on Sunday night after an unknown substance reportedly made several concertgoers unwell.

What happened

About 12,000 people were at a performance by American rapper Don Toliver, part of the UK leg of his Octane World Tour, when emergency services were called. Greater Manchester Police declared a major incident after reports of people falling ill in the crowd.

The response

The ambulance service said it assessed more than 50 people, some of whom reported eye and breathing symptoms, and decontamination was carried out at the venue. Police said on Monday that several people were treated at the scene and one was taken to hospital.

Investigation

Officers said they were working to find out what the substance was. The arena, which describes itself as the UK's largest indoor entertainment venue, said it was cooperating with the police.
