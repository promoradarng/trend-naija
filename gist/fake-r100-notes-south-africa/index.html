Nigerians allegedly busted for producing fake R100 notes in South Africa

27 Oct 2026, 07:20 GMT

A suspected counterfeit currency operation involving Nigerian nationals in South Africa has sparked reactions following reports that individuals were caught allegedly producing fake R100 banknotes.

The incident has raised fresh concerns about counterfeit money circulating in South Africa and the possible consequences for unsuspecting businesses and members of the public.

According to claims surrounding the incident, the individuals were allegedly involved in producing counterfeit South African R100 notes, which could potentially be passed off as genuine currency during everyday transactions.

However, the exact location of the alleged operation, the number of suspects involved, and the circumstances surrounding their reported arrest have yet to be independently verified.

Counterfeit money poses a serious threat to businesses, particularly small traders, street vendors, transport operators and shop owners who handle cash transactions daily. When fake banknotes enter circulation, unsuspecting individuals may accept them as payment, only to discover later that the money has no value.

The alleged involvement of foreign nationals could also fuel tensions in a country where immigration and crime remain sensitive public issues. However, criminal responsibility rests with the individuals involved, and allegations against particular suspects should not be used to generalise about Nigerians living and working legally in South Africa.

Under South African law, producing, possessing or distributing counterfeit currency can attract serious criminal penalties. If the allegations are confirmed, those involved could face prosecution.

No official statement from the authorities detailing the alleged operation, the evidence recovered or any charges filed has been independently confirmed.

The reported incident has prompted questions about how counterfeit currency operations are detected and what measures can protect ordinary people from accepting fake money. It also highlights the importance of checking banknotes carefully, particularly when handling large amounts of cash.

Bomb threats hit at least 25 passenger flights across Europe

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

At least 25 flights across Europe were targeted by bomb threats on Thursday, forcing diversions, emergency landings and fighter jet escorts. No explosives were found on the aircraft searched. It was the second wave of threats this week.

Read more

At least 25 passenger flights across Europe were hit by bomb threats on Thursday, 5 November 2026, causing diversions, emergency landings and security checks at several airports.

What happened

According to reports, an Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome carrying 151 passengers and six crew was diverted to a French military air base at Évreux, where bomb disposal experts inspected it. Three Ryanair flights to Bulgaria from Copenhagen, Madrid and London were also targeted. Two landed in Sofia and the third was diverted to Plovdiv. Italian fighter jets escorted a Finnair flight from Helsinki to Rome after a bomb alert. Flights operated by Wizz Air, LOT Polish Airlines and Aegean Airlines were also affected, with incidents reported in countries including France, Spain, Poland, Denmark, Italy, the Czech Republic, Austria and Romania.

No explosives found

No explosives were found on any of the aircraft searched so far, and all the affected planes landed safely. The threats followed another wave on Tuesday, when at least seven flights across Europe were targeted.

Background

Hoax bomb threats against airlines cause major disruption and cost, as pilots and authorities must treat every threat as real. Investigators have not said publicly who is behind the latest threats or whether the incidents are linked. European security agencies have warned in recent years of attempts to disrupt transport and infrastructure.

What it means for travellers

Passengers flying in Europe, including Nigerians travelling for the holidays, may face extra security checks and delays while investigations continue.

UK says Putin will not be invited to 2027 G20 summit, breaking with Trump

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has reportedly said Russian President Vladimir Putin will not attend the G20 summit the UK hosts in Manchester in November 2027, because of sanctions. President Trump has invited Putin to this year's G20 in Miami.

Read more

The United Kingdom says it will not invite Russian President Vladimir Putin to the G20 summit it is hosting in 2027, setting it apart from the United States.

What was said

In an interview reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026, Prime Minister Andy Burnham was reportedly asked whether Putin would be invited to the summit in Manchester in November 2027, and replied that he would not. He said the guest list was handled by the G20, but that Putin would not be coming because of an outstanding sanction.

A contrast with Washington

President Donald Trump has invited Putin to this year's G20 summit in Miami in December. The Kremlin has not confirmed whether the Russian leader will attend.

Background

Britain has avoided direct engagement with Russian ministers since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and has imposed wide sanctions on Russian officials. Tensions over Russia's role in the G20 were on display at a meeting of foreign ministers in October, when a planned group photo was cancelled after European ministers refused to pose with Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov. The G20 includes the world's largest economies, and the African Union became a full member in 2023.

Why it matters

The disagreement highlights divisions among Western allies over how to deal with Russia as the war in Ukraine continues.

American rapper Nicki Minaj has doubled down on her support for President Donald Trump after his party's heavy losses in the US midterm elections.

What she posted

In a series of posts on X on Wednesday night, 4 November 2026, Minaj reportedly shared a numbered list of messages, declaring that Trump is still the president and, in a reference to herself, that 'the Queen' is still people's obsession. Trump said he was not on the ballot and later admitted the Iran war had contributed to the losses. Minaj, who was born in Trinidad and Tobago, has in recent years become an outspoken supporter of Trump, which has divided her fan base.

How people reacted

Her posts drew mixed reactions, with some fans applauding her and others criticising her political views.

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña resigns over bank collapse scandal

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned after the lower house voted to begin impeachment proceedings over the collapse of a bank he was once linked to as a shareholder. He apologised to Paraguayans.

Read more

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned amid a political crisis caused by the collapse of a bank he was previously linked to.

What happened

In a video message posted on X, Peña said he was stepping down to protect the country's stability and democratic institutions, a day after opposition lawmakers requested his impeachment and the lower house of Congress voted to begin proceedings, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. He apologised to the people if he had failed them at any point, saying it was never his intention to betray their trust.

The bank collapse

The crisis began when Ueno Bank collapsed and froze its operations, reportedly leaving large numbers of customers unable to access their accounts. Lawmakers accused Peña of conflicts of interest, pointing to his past shareholding in the company that owned the bank.

Background

Peña, an economist and former finance minister, took office in August 2023 as a member of the long-dominant Colorado Party. Under Paraguay's constitution, the vice-president takes over when a president leaves office before the end of the term.

What happens next

Attention now turns to who will lead the country for the rest of the term, and to investigations into the bank's collapse and whether depositors will recover their money.
