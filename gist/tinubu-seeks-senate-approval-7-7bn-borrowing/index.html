Tinubu asks Senate to approve $7.7bn foreign borrowing for 2026 budget

4 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has asked the Senate to approve fresh external borrowing of $7.7 billion to help finance the 2026 budget.

The request

The request was contained in a letter read by Senate President Godswill Akpabio during plenary on Wednesday, 4 November 2026. At the budget exchange rate of ₦1,400 to the dollar, the amount is equal to about ₦10.79 trillion. According to the letter, the money would be raised mainly through Eurobond issuance, as well as loans from multilateral and bilateral lenders, international financial institutions and syndicated loans. The President said the borrowing would help finance the budget, strengthen foreign reserves and support the stability of the naira. He also asked the Senate to authorise the Minister of Finance and the Debt Management Office to carry out the plan.

What the Senate did

Akpabio said the new request appeared to replace an earlier borrowing proposal and gave more detail on where the money would come from. He referred it to the Senate Committee on Local and Foreign Debts for consideration and a report.

Background

Nigeria's public debt has risen sharply in recent years, and borrowing has become a major political issue ahead of the 2027 elections. Last week, former President Olusegun Obasanjo described the government's borrowing as 'absolute nonsense', while opposition candidates have warned that debt servicing is taking up too much of government revenue. The government says borrowing is needed to fund infrastructure and that its reforms have improved Nigeria's credit rating and ability to raise money abroad.

Atiku urges rejection

ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has called on the Senate to reject the request. In a statement issued through his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu, he warned against giving the government a blank cheque on foreign debt and urged senators to demand a full list of projects, the loan terms and a repayment plan before considering approval. His campaign noted that the request came before the National Assembly had finished considering an earlier external borrowing proposal of $20.52 billion and €1.045 billion.

What happens next

The Senate committee is expected to review the request before the full Senate votes on it.

  • The request is worth about ₦10.79 trillion.
  • Funds would come mainly from Eurobonds and international loans.
  • The Senate referred it to its debt committee.

Microsoft outage disrupts Outlook, Teams and Copilot in 74 countries

13 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

A global Microsoft 365 outage that began on Thursday has disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot for users in 74 countries and territories, with nearly 140,000 problem reports logged at its peak. Microsoft says it is working to restore services.

Read more

Microsoft is working to restore its services after a global outage disrupted Outlook email, Teams and its Copilot AI assistant for users around the world.

What happened

The outage began on Thursday, 12 November 2026, and affected Microsoft 365 services in 74 countries and territories, according to reports on Friday. Many users were unable to load their inboxes or send and receive emails, while others had trouble with Teams calls and Copilot. Reports of Outlook problems on an outage-tracking website peaked at nearly 140,000 worldwide.

Microsoft's explanation

Microsoft initially blamed the problem on unusually high traffic across parts of its infrastructure, which caused connection errors. The company said it had paused some deployments while it worked on a fix.

Why it matters

Outlook and Teams are widely used by businesses and organisations around the world, including in Nigeria.

Read more

Super Eagles striker Victor Boniface has won praise online after donating ₦1 million to a woman with cancer during a TikTok livestream.

What happened

In a clip that spread widely on Friday, 13 November 2026, the woman joined Boniface's livestream and told him she needed about ₦4 million for her treatment. After listening to her, the Bayer Leverkusen forward asked for her account details and sent the money immediately, leaving her visibly emotional. Many Nigerians commended him for his generosity.

His career

Away from the gesture, Boniface is going through a difficult spell. Injuries have held back his progress at Leverkusen, and a loan spell at Werder Bremen last season did not revive his form. He has played only a few minutes of Bundesliga football this season and is reportedly keen to leave in the January transfer window. Leverkusen, where he is under contract until 2028, are said to be open to a sale or a loan.
