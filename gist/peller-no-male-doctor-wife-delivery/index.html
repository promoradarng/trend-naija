Peller says no male doctor will attend his wife's delivery, sparking debate

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

Popular streamer and content creator Peller has got social media talking after saying he will not allow a male doctor to attend to his wife during childbirth.

What he said

In a viral Instagram video posted on Thursday, 29 October 2026, Peller reportedly said that only a woman would help his wife during labour and that he would go into the delivery room himself if he saw a man there. He said he did not trust men in such settings and challenged a person sitting beside him to say whether they would be comfortable with a male doctor delivering their own wife's baby. The moment was met with laughter.

How people reacted

Reactions online ranged from amusement to concern. Many users laughed at his tone, while others warned that labour is unpredictable and that a male doctor may be needed in an emergency, such as a caesarean section. Several commenters noted that the couple reportedly live in Canada, where hospitals typically assign whichever doctor or midwife is on duty, and questioned how his demand would work in practice.

Background

Peller is one of Nigeria's best-known streamers, with a large following on TikTok, and is often in the news for his livestreams and personal life. The comments came as he and his wife prepare to welcome their baby. He was also the subject of romance rumours with fellow streamer Jarvis this week.

What happens next

Fans are now waiting for news of the baby's arrival, with many joking that they hope the hospital is warned in advance.

  • Peller says only female staff should attend his wife's delivery.
  • Fans warned that emergencies may require any available doctor.
  • The couple reportedly live in Canada.

VeryDarkMan questions INEC chairman's photo with APC UK chapter chairman

14 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Activist VeryDarkMan has questioned why INEC chairman Joash Amupitan was photographed with the APC's UK chapter chairman during his London trip, asking whether other parties' UK leaders were given similar access.

Read more

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has raised questions about a photograph showing the chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) with a leader of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) in the UK.

What he said

In a post reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, VDM clarified that the man wearing an APC-branded cap in the photo was not INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan, but Tunde Doherty, chairman of the APC's UK chapter. He asked what the APC official was doing with the INEC chairman and whether the UK leaders of other parties, including the NDC, Labour Party, PDP and ADC, had been given the same opportunity to meet him. He went on to claim that INEC was compromised.

Background

Amupitan was in London this week to speak at Chatham House about preparations for the 2027 elections. Protesters confronted him outside the venue, accusing the commission of bias, and he pledged to reject any political appointment after leaving office to show his independence. INEC has also denied reports that he plans to resign. INEC had not responded to VDM's post at the time of writing, and the circumstances of the photo are not clear.

APC UK chairman responds

Tunde Doherty, chairman of the APC in the UK, later rejected claims that he was part of the INEC chairman's entourage at Chatham House. He said opposition figures had deliberately misrepresented a photo of him taken outside the venue, that he had never held any position or role in INEC and had no affiliation with it, and that as a party leader he had a right to attend public policy discussions there.

Goodluck Jonathan calls for review of Nigeria's democracy and institutions

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a broad review of Nigeria's democratic structures, how politicians are recruited and the state of public institutions, saying the country owes a debt to its founding nationalists.

Read more

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a wide-ranging review of Nigeria's democratic structures, political recruitment and public institutions.

What he said

Chairing a colloquium at the Nigerian Institute of International Affairs in Lagos to mark the 162nd birth anniversary of nationalist Herbert Macaulay, reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Jonathan said the country's founding nationalists did not merely fight to end colonial rule, but imagined a sovereign, united and prosperous Nigeria where every citizen would enjoy equal rights, dignity and opportunity. He said independence was also meant to bring economic freedom, industrialisation, quality education, healthcare and social justice.

Honesty about failures

While acknowledging progress in some areas, Jonathan said true patriotism requires honesty about the country's failures. He pointed to leadership failures and weak institutions, and urged a review of how the country's democracy works and how leaders emerge.

Background

Jonathan, who governed from 2010 to 2015, is widely remembered for conceding defeat to Muhammadu Buhari in the 2015 election, a first for a sitting Nigerian president. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Yoruba Muslim clerics' league says it will not endorse any 2027 candidate

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland says it will not back any politician or party in the 2027 elections, insisting its role is to pray for Nigeria and guide the people, not to campaign.

Read more

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland has declared that it will not endorse any politician or party ahead of the 2027 elections.

What the clerics said

At a gathering in Ibadan on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the chairman of the league's board of trustees, Sheikh Muhammad Habibullah Adam-Abdullahi El-Ilory, said the league had neither the capacity nor the intention to name a preferred candidate for any office. He said Islamic clerics are not politicians and their duty does not include campaigning for any individual or party. League president Sheikh Jamiu Kewulere said imams must stay focused on religious guidance.

Background

The statement came after Oyo governor and APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde made a donation to the league. The lecturers' union ASUU also said this week that it would not endorse any candidate. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
