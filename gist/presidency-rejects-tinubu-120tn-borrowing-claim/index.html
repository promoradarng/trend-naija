Presidency rejects claim that Tinubu borrowed ₦120 trillion in three years

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

The Presidency has rejected viral social media claims that President Bola Tinubu's government has added ₦120 trillion to Nigeria's debt in its first three years.

What the Presidency said

In a statement on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the President's special assistant on social media, Dada Olusegun, described the figures as a distorted picture of the country's public finances. The claim came from a widely shared graphic saying that 16 previous Nigerian leaders borrowed a total of ₦46 trillion over 63 years, compared with ₦120 trillion under Tinubu.

The Presidency's argument

Olusegun said the ₦46 trillion figure, from late 2022, left out ₦22.7 trillion in Central Bank of Nigeria 'Ways and Means' overdrafts to the previous government, which were only formally added to the official public debt in May 2023. He also said earlier valuations of foreign debt used an official exchange rate of about ₦448 to the dollar, which understated the true naira value of those debts; when the exchange rate was unified, the naira value of existing foreign debt rose sharply even without new borrowing.

Background

The rebuttal came after NDC candidate Peter Obi challenged Tinubu to account for subsidy savings and more than $100 billion he said the government had borrowed. The World Bank reported last week that Nigeria's total external debt, including private debt, rose to about $145 billion in 2025. Tinubu is also seeking Senate approval for about $7.7 billion in new external loans, which opposition figures have urged lawmakers to reject.

Why it matters

Debt and the cost of living are expected to be major issues in the 16 January 2027 presidential election.

  • The Presidency calls the ₦120 trillion claim distorted.
  • It cites ₦22.7 trillion in old CBN overdrafts.
  • It says exchange rate unification raised the naira value of old debt.

INEC asks court to cancel subpoena for Tinubu's 1999 and 2023 election records

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

INEC has asked the Federal High Court in Abuja to set aside a subpoena ordering its chairman to appear and produce President Tinubu's 1999 and 2023 nomination documents in the trial of activist Omoyele Sowore.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has asked the Federal High Court in Abuja to set aside a subpoena requiring its chairman to produce President Bola Tinubu's election documents.

The application

INEC's lawyer, Alex Izinyon, a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, filed the motion on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, the day before INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan was due to appear as a defence witness in the criminal trial of activist Omoyele Sowore. The subpoena, issued by Justice Mohammed Umar, had directed the INEC chairman to produce Tinubu's nomination forms from the 1999 Lagos governorship election, including an affidavit relating to lost certificates, and from the 2023 presidential election.

The case

Sowore is being prosecuted by the State Security Service over social media posts in which he described the President as a criminal. His defence team asked the court to call the INEC chairman and Joe Igbokwe, a former APC publicity secretary in Lagos, as witnesses.

Background

Questions about the President's academic records have been raised by critics for years and were the subject of legal challenges after the 2023 election, which the Supreme Court dismissed. The President and his supporters have consistently rejected claims of wrongdoing.

APC campaign council reportedly sacks spokespersons after embarrassing 112 emergency number incident on live TV

11 Nov 2026, 20:22 GMT

The APC Presidential Campaign Council has reportedly removed its spokespersons after APC official Alwan Hassan's attempt to demonstrate the 112 emergency number on live television failed twice. Only three officials are now authorised to speak for the council.

Read more

The All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council has reportedly removed some of its spokespersons following the controversy over a failed attempt to demonstrate Nigeria's 112 emergency number on live television.

Only three can now speak

According to reports, President Bola Tinubu's Chief of Staff, Femi Gbajabiamila, announced on Wednesday that the spokespersons earlier named by the campaign council had been removed.

He said only three officials are now authorised to speak on behalf of the council: Senator Abdulaziz Yari, the Director General; Minister of Solid Minerals Dele Alake; and Minister of Information Mohammed Idris.

What happened on live TV

It all started on Tuesday, when APC official Alwan Hassan appeared as a guest on a live television politics programme.

While discussing the achievements of the APC-led government, Hassan pointed to the 112 emergency number as an example of a working public service. He explained that Nigerians could dial it whenever they needed urgent help, whether in a medical emergency, a security threat or a road accident.

To prove his point, he challenged the host to dial 112 right there in the studio.

That was where things took an unexpected turn. The host tried the number, but the call failed to connect. He tried again, with the same result.

Hassan insisted the line was working and suggested network problems might have stopped the calls going through. His explanation did little to stop the criticism that followed.

The backlash

Clips of the interview spread quickly online, with Nigerians questioning the reliability of a service people are meant to depend on in life-threatening situations. Opposition figures also seized on the incident to challenge the government's claims about improved public services.

The real question

Is removing spokespersons really the answer, or should the government be more concerned about finding out why the emergency calls failed?

Someone calling 112 during an armed robbery, a serious accident or a medical emergency may not have the luxury of trying again and again until the network connects.

One failed demonstration does not prove the whole emergency system is broken. But it raises questions that deserve proper answers, and for many Nigerians, those answers matter far more than who is allowed to speak for a political campaign.

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso visit Yelewata community in Benue State, offer support to vulnerable children

11 Nov 2026, 19:53 GMT

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have visited Yelewata in Benue State, where Obi laid a wreath at the memorial to victims of the community's deadly 2025 attack. The two leaders donated ₦10 million for vulnerable children and promised a motorised borehole.

Read more

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have visited Yelewata community in Benue State, bringing some hope to residents who have endured years of hardship and neglect.

A moment of remembrance

Yelewata was the scene of a deadly attack in June 2025 that left many residents dead and shocked the nation. During the visit, Obi knelt to lay a wreath at the memorial bearing the names of the victims, as a large crowd looked on.

Donations and promises

The two political leaders promised to provide a motorised borehole to help address the community's water needs. They also donated ₦10 million to support vulnerable children, while Professor Shija contributed another ₦2 million towards the education of orphans.

The people speak

Beyond the donations, the people of Yelewata had their own stories to tell. They spoke about years of neglect, the difficulties they face every day and the lack of meaningful government support.

Imagine living for years without some of the basic things that should make life easier, while those elected to serve appear distant from your suffering. For residents, the visit was a chance to make their voices heard and draw attention to their situation.

Action, not just visits

Promises must be followed by action. The community needs lasting solutions, not just occasional visits.

Leadership should be about compassion, listening to the people and doing something practical to improve their lives.

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu becomes New Jersey's lieutenant governor

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu has been appointed lieutenant governor of the US state of New Jersey, becoming the first immigrant to hold the post. She will also serve as Secretary of State.

Read more

Shirley U. Emehelu, a lawyer born in Nigeria, has been appointed lieutenant governor of New Jersey in the United States.

The appointment

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill appointed Emehelu on Monday, 9 November 2026, making her the state's fifth lieutenant governor and the first immigrant to hold the office. She was sworn in at the Statehouse in Trenton by the state's Chief Justice, Stuart Rabner. The appointment also means that, for the first time in New Jersey's history, women hold the state's two highest executive offices at the same time. Emehelu will also serve as Secretary of State, a role that covers government administration and oversight of elections.

Why she was chosen

Sherrill described Emehelu as an experienced public servant and pointed to her record prosecuting financial crimes, protecting consumers, defending civil rights and tackling government waste. The governor also praised her leadership as acting Secretary of State, including overseeing the state's recent elections.

Her Nigerian roots

Emehelu, who moved from Nigeria to the United States as a child, said she was proud of what the appointment means for Black women, immigrants and people who share her Nigerian heritage, and expressed hope that it would inspire young people to serve. She said she would work with the governor on the cost of living and economic opportunity.
