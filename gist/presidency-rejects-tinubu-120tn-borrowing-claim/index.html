Presidency rejects claim that Tinubu borrowed ₦120 trillion in three years

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

The Presidency has rejected viral social media claims that President Bola Tinubu's government has added ₦120 trillion to Nigeria's debt in its first three years.

What the Presidency said

In a statement on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the President's special assistant on social media, Dada Olusegun, described the figures as a distorted picture of the country's public finances. The claim came from a widely shared graphic saying that 16 previous Nigerian leaders borrowed a total of ₦46 trillion over 63 years, compared with ₦120 trillion under Tinubu.

The Presidency's argument

Olusegun said the ₦46 trillion figure, from late 2022, left out ₦22.7 trillion in Central Bank of Nigeria 'Ways and Means' overdrafts to the previous government, which were only formally added to the official public debt in May 2023. He also said earlier valuations of foreign debt used an official exchange rate of about ₦448 to the dollar, which understated the true naira value of those debts; when the exchange rate was unified, the naira value of existing foreign debt rose sharply even without new borrowing.

Background

The rebuttal came after NDC candidate Peter Obi challenged Tinubu to account for subsidy savings and more than $100 billion he said the government had borrowed. The World Bank reported last week that Nigeria's total external debt, including private debt, rose to about $145 billion in 2025. Tinubu is also seeking Senate approval for about $7.7 billion in new external loans, which opposition figures have urged lawmakers to reject.

Why it matters

Debt and the cost of living are expected to be major issues in the 16 January 2027 presidential election.

  • The Presidency calls the ₦120 trillion claim distorted.
  • It cites ₦22.7 trillion in old CBN overdrafts.
  • It says exchange rate unification raised the naira value of old debt.

Speaking in Anambra alongside the former Kano governor, Obi reportedly said their administration would prioritise accountability, transparency and the responsible management of public funds.

Fact check: Viral video claiming bandits attacked Tinubu is old and misleading

20 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A video shared on Facebook claiming bandits tried to attack President Tinubu is false. The footage reportedly dates back to a June 2025 incident in Kaduna and does not show a bandit attack.

Read more

A video circulating on Facebook since 27 October 2026, claiming that bandits attempted to attack President Bola Tinubu, has been found to be misleading.

The claim

The video shows Tinubu speaking at a podium and flinching as a man runs beside him, while two uniformed soldiers move in to shield him. A caption on the clip reads that a bandit attacked the president, and a narrator repeats the claim.

The facts

A fact-check found that the footage dates back to a June 2025 incident in Kaduna and does not show an attack by bandits. The video has been recirculated with a false caption ahead of the 2027 elections.

Why it matters

Misleading videos and AI-generated images have become a growing concern in the 2027 campaign, with the main parties accusing each other of spreading false content online.

Court rejects Atiku's bid to add new evidence to suit against Tinubu's eligibility

20 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has dismissed an application by Atiku Abubakar to introduce fresh evidence into his suit challenging President Tinubu's eligibility for 2027. The judge said the constitutional 14-day window had passed.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has thrown out an application by ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar to bring new evidence into his suit challenging President Bola Tinubu's eligibility to contest the 2027 election.

The ruling

Justice Inyang Ekwo held that Section 285(9) of the Constitution allows only 14 days for evidence to be filed in a pre-election case. He found that Atiku's request to amend his originating summons would effectively bring in fresh evidence after that period had expired, which would breach the Constitution.

What it means

The ruling is a setback for Atiku's legal challenge, although the main suit itself was not decided in the reports. Atiku's camp had not responded publicly to the ruling in the reports.

Background

Atiku is also involved in a separate legal battle over his ADC candidacy, after a court on 5 November voided the process that produced him as the party's candidate. INEC has said he will remain on its list until that dispute is settled.
