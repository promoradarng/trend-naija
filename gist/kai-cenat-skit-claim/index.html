Popular Nigerian relationship therapist and social media personality Blessing CEO has strongly denied allegations of drug use, insisting that she has never been involved in smoking, drinking alcohol or taking illegal substances.

Blessing made the clarification during a recent interview following her release from custody, where she addressed several rumours and allegations that circulated on social media while she was detained.

During the interview, she was confronted with claims suggesting that she had been struggling with substance use and needed rehabilitation or professional help rather than business opportunities.

However, the controversial relationship therapist dismissed the allegations, describing them as false and misleading.

"I am not a drug addict. I do not smoke, drink, and I do not even party," she stated.

According to Blessing, many of the stories circulated about her on social media have little or no connection to her actual lifestyle.

She expressed frustration over what she described as the growing habit of internet users creating and spreading damaging narratives about public figures without verifying their claims.

Blessing maintained that being controversial or frequently discussed online should not give people the freedom to make unfounded accusations about her personal life.

Her remarks have once again drawn attention to the challenges celebrities and internet personalities face when rumours about their private lives spread across social media.

While Blessing has firmly rejected the allegations, the claims remain unsubstantiated, and no verified evidence of illegal drug use was presented in the account of the interview.

Nigerians allegedly busted for producing fake R100 notes in South Africa

27 Oct 2026, 07:20 GMT

Reports claim that Nigerian nationals in South Africa were caught allegedly producing counterfeit R100 banknotes, sparking concern about fake money in circulation. The location, number of suspects and details of any arrest have not been independently verified, and no official statement has been confirmed.

Read more

A suspected counterfeit currency operation involving Nigerian nationals in South Africa has sparked reactions following reports that individuals were caught allegedly producing fake R100 banknotes.

The incident has raised fresh concerns about counterfeit money circulating in South Africa and the possible consequences for unsuspecting businesses and members of the public.

According to claims surrounding the incident, the individuals were allegedly involved in producing counterfeit South African R100 notes, which could potentially be passed off as genuine currency during everyday transactions.

However, the exact location of the alleged operation, the number of suspects involved, and the circumstances surrounding their reported arrest have yet to be independently verified.

Counterfeit money poses a serious threat to businesses, particularly small traders, street vendors, transport operators and shop owners who handle cash transactions daily. When fake banknotes enter circulation, unsuspecting individuals may accept them as payment, only to discover later that the money has no value.

The alleged involvement of foreign nationals could also fuel tensions in a country where immigration and crime remain sensitive public issues. However, criminal responsibility rests with the individuals involved, and allegations against particular suspects should not be used to generalise about Nigerians living and working legally in South Africa.

Under South African law, producing, possessing or distributing counterfeit currency can attract serious criminal penalties. If the allegations are confirmed, those involved could face prosecution.

No official statement from the authorities detailing the alleged operation, the evidence recovered or any charges filed has been independently confirmed.

The reported incident has prompted questions about how counterfeit currency operations are detected and what measures can protect ordinary people from accepting fake money. It also highlights the importance of checking banknotes carefully, particularly when handling large amounts of cash.
