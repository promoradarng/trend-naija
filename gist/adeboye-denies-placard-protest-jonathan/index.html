Adeboye says he never carried a placard against Jonathan, calls it a march against corruption

1 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The General Overseer of the Redeemed Christian Church of God (RCCG), Pastor Enoch Adeboye, has responded to claims that he once protested against former President Goodluck Jonathan's government.

What he said

Speaking at the church's monthly Holy Ghost Service, in comments reported on Sunday, 1 November 2026, Adeboye said he did not carry any placard against Jonathan. He explained that the event was a prayer walk and a march against corruption, aimed at drawing attention to the country's problems. He added that he has never protested against any government and that his role is to pray for those in authority.

Why it came up

Clips and posts claiming that the cleric had carried a placard against the Jonathan administration have circulated online in recent weeks, as supporters of different candidates debate the role of religious leaders in politics ahead of the 2027 elections. Some critics argued that church leaders who spoke out under Jonathan have been quieter under the current government.

Background

Adeboye leads one of Nigeria's largest churches, with branches around the world, and his statements are closely followed. Religious leaders have played a prominent part in national debates on insecurity and the economy. Several pastors and Islamic clerics have also spoken out this week about the elections and religious freedom.

How people reacted

His explanation drew mixed reactions. Some members welcomed the clarification, while others insisted the march was widely seen as a protest at the time.

  • He says he has never protested against any government.

Alaafin of Oyo says he is the foremost traditional ruler in Yorubaland

6 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Alaafin of Oyo, Oba Abimbola Owoade I, has said history makes him the number one traditional ruler in Yorubaland, while insisting he is not competing with the Ooni of Ife. The two stools clashed last year over a chieftaincy title.

Read more

The Alaafin of Oyo, Oba Abimbola Akeem Owoade I, has described himself as the foremost traditional ruler in Yorubaland.

What he said

In a television interview reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, the monarch said that, going by history and the contribution of the old Oyo Empire to Yoruba civilisation, the Alaafin is number one. He denied that he was in a contest for supremacy with any other Yoruba ruler, including the Ooni of Ife, Oba Enitan Ogunwusi. He said he regularly prays for the progress of the Yoruba people and believes that when Oyo succeeds, all Yoruba people are honoured.

The earlier dispute

His comments follow a dispute between the two thrones in August 2025, after the Ooni conferred the title of Okanlomo of Yorubaland on Ibadan businessman Dotun Sanusi. The Alaafin gave the Ooni 48 hours to withdraw the title, arguing that the Ooni's authority was limited to a few local government areas in Osun State and that the title was unlawful. The Ooni's spokesman later said the monarch had told him not to respond to what he called an empty threat.

Background

The question of which monarch ranks highest in Yorubaland has been debated for many years. The Alaafin's supporters point to the power of the old Oyo Empire, while the Ooni's supporters point to Ile-Ife's place as the traditional cradle of the Yoruba people. Oba Owoade was crowned Alaafin in 2025, succeeding the late Oba Lamidi Adeyemi III.

Three dead, 14 in hospital after drinking herbal mixtures in Benue

6 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

At least three people have reportedly died and 14 others are receiving treatment after drinking herbal mixtures sold by hawkers in Oju Local Government Area of Benue State. The local government chairman says suspects have been arrested.

Read more

At least three people have reportedly died and 14 others have been taken to hospital after drinking herbal mixtures suspected to contain harmful substances in Benue State.

What happened

According to reports on Friday, 6 November 2026, three men hawking the herbal preparations sold them to residents during a market day in Oju Township, Oju Local Government Area, last weekend. A resident said panic broke out on Monday, 2 November, when several people who had taken the mixtures began showing similar symptoms. One person died on Tuesday, a second early on Wednesday and a third on Thursday. Fourteen others are being treated at the General Hospital in Oju, four of them reportedly in a critical condition.

Suspects arrested

The chairman of Oju Local Government Area, Jackson Ominyi, confirmed the incident and said the suspected sellers had been arrested and taken to the state police headquarters in Makurdi. He said three deaths had been recorded as of Thursday, but that he did not yet have a final figure. The suspects have not been charged in court, and the substances in the mixtures have not been officially identified.

Background

Unregulated herbal mixtures are widely sold in markets and on the streets across Nigeria, often with claims that they cure many illnesses. Health authorities, including the food and drug regulator NAFDAC, have repeatedly warned Nigerians against buying unregistered medicines from hawkers.

What happens next

Residents are urging the authorities to investigate and to stop the sale of unregulated herbal products in the area.

Nigeria's external debt rises to $145 billion, World Bank report says

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Nigeria's external debt rose 6.6% to about $145 billion in 2025, according to the World Bank's latest International Debt Report. Some debt ratios improved, but repayments took a bigger share of export earnings.

Read more

Nigeria's external debt reached about $145.0 billion in 2025, according to the World Bank's International Debt Report 2026.

The figures

According to reports on Friday, 6 November 2026, the total was 6.6 per cent higher than the year before and about 74 per cent higher than the $83.2 billion recorded in 2015. Public and publicly guaranteed debt made up about $62.4 billion of the total, private debt without government guarantees about $54.5 billion, and short-term debt about $28.1 billion. Multilateral lenders were the biggest creditors of the government, at about $31.4 billion, including about $18.5 billion owed to the World Bank's International Development Association. China remained Nigeria's largest single-country lender, owed about $5.7 billion.

Burden of repayments

Some measures of the debt burden improved: external debt fell to 212 per cent of export earnings and to 51 per cent of gross national income. But debt service took a bigger share of income, rising to 19 per cent of exports and 5 per cent of gross national income.

The global picture

The World Bank said debt service payments by low- and middle-income countries reached $1.4 trillion in 2025, the fifth yearly rise in a row, driven largely by payments to private lenders such as bondholders. It noted that loans from official lenders carried an average interest rate of 1.9 per cent, compared with 9.6 per cent from private creditors.

Why it matters

The figures come as President Bola Tinubu seeks Senate approval for new external borrowing of about $7.7 billion, a request opposition figures, including Atiku Abubakar, have urged lawmakers to reject. The government says borrowing is needed to fund infrastructure and that its reforms are improving the economy.

Uzbekistan reportedly suspends visas and entry for Nigerians

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Uzbekistan has reportedly introduced temporary restrictions on issuing visas to Nigerians and on their entry into the country, according to a notice on an exhibition website. Ghana, Kenya, Egypt, Iraq and Bangladesh are said to be affected too.

Read more

Uzbekistan has reportedly introduced temporary restrictions on visas and entry for Nigerian citizens.

What was announced

The restrictions came to light through a notice published on the website of the Green Energy Technologies Central Asia exhibition, reported on Friday, 6 November 2026. The notice said temporary restrictions had been introduced on issuing visas and on entry into Uzbekistan for citizens of the affected countries. It advised Nigerians who need to travel in exceptional circumstances to contact the consular section of the Uzbek embassy. Similar measures were reportedly listed for citizens of Ghana, Kenya, Egypt, Iraq and Bangladesh.

No official explanation

Uzbekistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs has not issued a public statement explaining the reasons for the restrictions or how long they will last, and its main visa portal does not mention them. The Nigerian government had not publicly reacted at the time of writing.

Background

Uzbekistan, a former Soviet republic in Central Asia, has been trying to attract more tourists and investors in recent years. Nigerians already face strict visa requirements for many countries, and travel restrictions affecting Nigerian passport holders often spark debate at home about the country's image abroad.

What it means for travellers

Nigerians planning trips to Uzbekistan, including for business events, are advised to check with the Uzbek embassy before making travel arrangements.
