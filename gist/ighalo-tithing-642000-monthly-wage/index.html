Odion Ighalo says he has paid tithe since his first ₦15,000 salary, sparks debate

3 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Former Super Eagles striker Odion Ighalo has started a lively online debate after speaking about his long commitment to paying tithes.

What he said

In a podcast-style interview, reportedly shared on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Ighalo said he began paying tithe in 2005 when he received his first professional salary of ₦15,000, after his pastor introduced him to the practice, and that he has not missed it since. He admitted that the habit became harder when his income grew. When he was earning a reported $642,000 a month in Saudi Arabia, about ₦900 million at today's exchange rate, he said he thought about it for around a month before deciding to continue paying 10 per cent. He said some friends had questioned his decision, but he stood by it.

Background

Ighalo, 37, played for clubs including Watford and Manchester United before moving to Saudi Arabia, where he became one of the best-paid African players. He was top scorer at the 2019 Africa Cup of Nations and has long spoken publicly about his Christian faith.

How people reacted

Opinion was divided. Many Christians praised his faith and discipline and said his success was a blessing. Others argued that giving such large sums to churches was unnecessary and that the money could do more good if given directly to people in need. Some said it was his personal choice and others should respect it.

Why it matters

Tithing is a frequent topic of debate in Nigeria, where churches play a large role in public life and some pastors have faced criticism over their wealth.

  • He earned a reported $642,000 a month in Saudi Arabia.
  • His comments divided opinion online.

Aubameyang crashes Ferrari on way to training in Spain, no serious injuries

26 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former Arsenal striker Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang was involved in a crash on his way to training with Deportivo La Coruña. His Ferrari collided with another car, but no one was seriously hurt.

Read more

Former Arsenal and Chelsea striker Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang was involved in a road accident on his way to training with his Spanish club, Deportivo La Coruña, on Thursday, 26 November 2026.

The crash

The 37-year-old Gabon international was driving a Ferrari, reportedly worth around €380,000, when it collided with a BMW on the AP-9 motorway in north-west Spain at about 9:30am local time. The BMW overturned in the impact and had to be towed away.

No serious injuries

Emergency services, including the Civil Guard, firefighters and paramedics, attended the scene. Despite the force of the collision, no one was seriously injured.

FIFA says hackers stole personal and business information in cyberattack

26 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

FIFA has confirmed it was the victim of a cyberattack in which criminals gained access to a limited set of personal and business information. It is working with law enforcement.

Read more

World football's governing body FIFA has confirmed that it was hit by a cyberattack in which hackers gained unauthorised access to personal and business information.

What FIFA said

In a statement on Thursday, 26 November 2026, FIFA said it had been the victim of a criminal cyberattack that gave access to a limited set of personal and business information. It said it was working with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement, to investigate.

The organisation strongly condemned the unlawful taking and publication of the information, particularly personal data, describing it as a serious violation of privacy that could have significant consequences for the people affected.

Unanswered questions

FIFA did not say when the attack happened, what kind of information was accessed or how many people were affected.

NAFDAC shuts Abuja's Zuba International Market over fake products worth ₦300m

27 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

NAFDAC has shut Zuba International Market in Abuja after a joint operation with police and the DSS reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome goods worth more than ₦300 million. Over 160 shops were inspected.

Read more

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has shut down Zuba International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

The operation

The closure followed a joint enforcement operation with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services, which reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome products worth more than ₦300 million. The exercise covered more than 160 shops in the market.

Why now

NAFDAC said the closure was part of a wider crackdown on substandard and fake goods ahead of the festive season.
