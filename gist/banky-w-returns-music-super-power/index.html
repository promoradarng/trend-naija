Banky W returns to music on Victor Thompson's 'Super Power'

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

Banky W, the R&B singer turned pastor and former boss of Wizkid's old label, is making a return to music.

What he announced

On Friday, 30 October 2026, Banky W revealed on Instagram that he features on gospel singer Victor Thompson's upcoming single, 'Super Power', and shared a preview of the music video. The song is due to be released on Friday, 6 November. A clip of the two singers enjoying the song together was also shared online.

Why he returned

Banky W reportedly said he had accepted that his recording career had ended, and described the song as an act of obedience rather than a career move. He said he had prayed that if a return was meant to happen, the songs would come. About three weeks later, Thompson sent him a message saying they were meant to make music together.

Background

Banky W, whose real name is Olubankole Wellington, was one of Nigeria's leading R&B stars in the 2000s and early 2010s and founded the Empire Mates Entertainment label, which launched Wizkid's career. His last full project was the 2017 album 'Songs About U'. He has since focused on ministry and public life, and is married to actress Adesua Etomi-Wellington. Victor Thompson is known for the gospel hit 'This Year (Blessings)'.

How fans reacted

Fans welcomed the news with excitement, with many saying they had missed his voice. Some said they hoped the song would lead to a full gospel album.

  • 'Super Power' is due out on 6 November.
  • Banky W says the song is 'an act of obedience'.
  • His last full project was 'Songs About U' in 2017.

Portable reveals how 'Zazoo Zehh' royalties were shared with Olamide and Poco Lee

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Singer Portable says he gave Olamide 20% of the royalties from his hit 'Zazoo Zehh', up from the 16% the rapper asked for, while Poco Lee got 30%, the producer 20% and he kept 30%.

Read more

Singer Portable has revealed how royalties from his breakthrough hit 'Zazoo Zehh' were shared.

What he said

In a video reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, Portable said rapper Olamide, who featured on the song, initially asked for 16 per cent because he contributed 16 bars, while the producer received 20 per cent. He said he later raised Olamide's share to 20 per cent because he felt 16 per cent was too small for an artiste of Olamide's status, and credited Olamide with helping him secure his royalties when the song came out. He said dancer Poco Lee received 30 per cent, and he kept 30 per cent for himself.

Background

'Zazoo Zehh', released in late 2021, became a massive street anthem and turned Portable, whose real name is Habeeb Okikiola, into one of Nigeria's most talked-about artistes. The remix featured Olamide and Poco Lee. Portable has since been involved in several controversies and public disputes, but remains popular with fans of street-pop music.

Naira Marley announces Marlians Fest comeback for 12 December

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

Singer Naira Marley has announced the return of Marlians Fest on 12 December at Eagles Club Field in Surulere, Lagos, saying he stepped back to heal after difficult years. Davido was among those who liked the post.

Read more

Singer Naira Marley has announced that his Marlians Fest concert is returning this December.

What he announced

In a long post on his social media pages on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the Marlian Records founder said the show will hold on 12 December at the Eagles Club Field in Surulere, Lagos, as part of the 'Detty December' season. He spoke about a difficult period in his life without going into detail, saying rumours and negative stories had circulated about him and that he had deliberately stepped back to find peace and heal. He described the concert as an event about growth, healing and moving forward, and dedicated it partly to everyone 'we have lost along the way'. He said friends would join him on stage and thanked supporters who stood by him.

Background

Naira Marley became one of Nigeria's most popular street-pop stars with songs such as 'Soapy' and 'Issa Goal'. He faced intense public criticism after the death of his former signee, Mohbad, in September 2023, when questions were raised about Mohbad's earlier complaints against the label. Naira Marley was arrested and questioned by police over the death and was later released; he has denied any wrongdoing, and no charges against him have been reported in connection with Mohbad's death. A coroner's inquest was held into the circumstances. This week, VeryDarkMan has also revived discussion of the Mohbad case during his feud with Davido, while Davido's aide denied any link between the singer and Naira Marley's troubles.

How people reacted

Davido was among those who liked the announcement on Instagram. Fans were divided, with some welcoming his return and others saying the wounds from Mohbad's death were still fresh.

Ghanaian singer Efya marries Nigerian singer Tómi Thomas in Accra

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

Ghanaian singer Efya has married Nigerian musician Tómi Thomas in a traditional ceremony in Accra, six years after saying publicly that she would never marry. Ghana's culture minister was among the guests.

Read more

Ghanaian singer Efya has married Nigerian singer Tómi Thomas in a traditional ceremony in Accra.

The wedding

The 39-year-old singer and her 33-year-old partner married on Friday, 6 November 2026, surrounded by family, friends and well-known figures from Ghana's entertainment industry, according to reports. Guests reportedly included Ghana's Minister for Tourism, Culture and Creative Arts, Abla Dzifa Gomashie, as well as Sister Derby, Fella Makafui, Hajia4Reall, Efia Odo and Kafui Danku.

Background

The wedding comes six years after Efya said publicly that she would never get married. The couple's relationship became public in February 2026, when their families held a knocking ceremony on Valentine's Day; their management later said that ceremony did not complete the traditional marriage rites. Efya, whose real name is Jane Awindor, is one of Ghana's best-known female vocalists. Tómi Thomas is a Nigerian singer and songwriter known for R&B and Afro-soul music.

Trump admits immigration crackdown was 'too tough' after midterm defeat

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

President Donald Trump has said his administration's immigration crackdown was too tough and hurt Republicans in the midterm elections, particularly the deportation of people with stable jobs whose only offence was breaking immigration rules.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has acknowledged that his administration's immigration crackdown went too far, in a notable change of tone after his party's heavy losses in the midterm elections.

What he said

In an interview broadcast by the CBS programme '60 Minutes' on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Trump defended his record on border security and on removing dangerous criminals, but said he thought the crackdown had been too tough. He said the administration had suffered politically by deporting people who had stable jobs and had only committed immigration violations, and that this had hurt Republican candidates at the ballot box.

The election

Exit polls showed immigration was the third most important issue for voters, after the economy and the state of democracy. In traditionally conservative states such as Texas, a shift among Latino voters towards the Democrats contributed to their gains in Congress, where they are projected to control both the House of Representatives and the Senate.

Background

Mass deportations became a defining policy of Trump's second term, drawing protests and legal challenges. Only days ago he promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of the deportation programme, to White House chief of staff, so it is unclear how far policy will change in practice. Groups of Nigerians have been among those deported, including six who arrived in Lagos last week.
