"I established my university before becoming Vice-President": Atiku fires back at Tinubu

8 Nov 2026, 03:13 GMT

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has responded to President Bola Tinubu over questions about his investment in education, insisting that the foundation of his educational institution was laid long before he became Vice-President.

Atiku's response

Atiku, who served as Vice-President from 1999 to 2007, said the suggestion that he established a university with resources acquired while in public office was misleading.

According to him, his journey into education began in 1993, when he established ABTI Academy, six years before he took office as Vice-President under former President Olusegun Obasanjo.

He made the clarification after remarks attributed to President Tinubu questioning how he was able to set up a university while serving in government.

Why the dates matter

Atiku maintained that his investment in education was part of his business activities before he entered the country's second-highest office. By the time he became Vice-President in May 1999, he says, he had already begun building what would eventually grow into a major private institution.

That institution later became the American University of Nigeria (AUN) in Yola, Adamawa State. The university itself was established in the mid-2000s, during his time as Vice-President, which is why questions about its funding and expansion continue to come up.

Atiku also insisted that his business activities and investments are open to public scrutiny, and that anyone genuinely interested in the source and history of his wealth can examine his record.

A growing rivalry

The exchange adds another chapter to the rivalry between President Tinubu and the former Vice-President, two prominent figures whose differences have shaped several national debates.

Beyond the personal disagreement, the controversy raises a question Nigerians have long asked of their leaders: how did those who held public office acquire their wealth, and can they give a clear account of their investments?

The fact that an educational project began before someone took office does not, on its own, settle every question about its funding or later expansion. Those questions would require documentary evidence and a proper look at the institution's financial history.

The road to 2027

As activities ahead of the 2027 general election gather pace, exchanges like this between leading political figures are likely to attract even more attention.

For now, Atiku has made his position clear: his educational investment began in 1993, well before he became Vice-President, and he is prepared to have his business record examined.

Suspected thugs attack Kwankwaso's convoy in Kano, smash about seven vehicles

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Suspected thugs reportedly attacked the campaign convoy of NDC vice-presidential candidate Rabiu Kwankwaso and Kano governorship candidate Aminu Abdussalam Gwarzo in Bichi on Sunday evening, injuring team members and damaging about seven vehicles.

Read more

The campaign convoy of Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) vice-presidential candidate and former Kano governor, was reportedly attacked by suspected thugs in Kano State on Sunday evening, 15 November 2026.

What happened

According to Ibrahim Garba Shu'aibu, spokesperson for the NDC's Kano governorship candidate, Aminu Abdussalam Gwarzo, the convoy had passed peacefully through Tofa, Rimin Gado, Kabo, Gwarzo, Shanono and Bagwai local government areas before reaching Bichi. He said the team was warned that its route into Bichi had been blocked by people opposed to the rally, and that stones were thrown when the delegation arrived. The meeting went ahead, but as the convoy left, a group of armed youths near the local government secretariat reportedly attacked it, injuring some members of the team and smashing about seven vehicles. A media aide to Kwankwaso also confirmed the incident on social media.

Response

The police had not publicly commented on the incident at the time of writing, and it is not clear who was behind the attack.

Background

The attack is the latest in a series of incidents of political violence ahead of the 2027 elections. Suspected thugs stormed an NDC town hall in Lagos last week, and supporters of other parties, including the APC, have also been attacked at rallies in several states. President Bola Tinubu has ordered security agencies to deal firmly with thuggery, and the House of Representatives is investigating attacks on political events. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Soludo tells politicians to keep campaigns out of churches

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Anambra governor Charles Soludo has urged politicians not to turn places of worship into campaign grounds, saying churches should be neutral spaces where people of all political views can worship in peace.

Read more

Anambra State governor Charles Soludo has called on politicians to stop turning churches into campaign platforms.

What he said

Speaking at the 30th ministry anniversary of Prophet Chukwuemeka Ohanaemere, popularly known as Odumeje, in Onitsha, reported on Monday, 16 November 2026, Soludo said places of worship should be holy places where people of all political persuasions can worship God in peace. He said politicians have their own venues for politics and urged them to keep the two clearly separate. The governor said he had personally followed a policy of not using religious venues for campaigning.

Background

Earlier this month, a Catholic priest apologised for singing Peter Obi's campaign song in church, and Yoruba Muslim clerics said they would not endorse any candidate. Soludo, whose party APGA has endorsed President Bola Tinubu for 2027, has pledged to deliver half of Anambra's votes to the President.

Yoruba Muslim clerics' league says it will not endorse any 2027 candidate

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland says it will not back any politician or party in the 2027 elections, insisting its role is to pray for Nigeria and guide the people, not to campaign.

Read more

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland has declared that it will not endorse any politician or party ahead of the 2027 elections.

What the clerics said

At a gathering in Ibadan on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the chairman of the league's board of trustees, Sheikh Muhammad Habibullah Adam-Abdullahi El-Ilory, said the league had neither the capacity nor the intention to name a preferred candidate for any office. He said Islamic clerics are not politicians and their duty does not include campaigning for any individual or party. League president Sheikh Jamiu Kewulere said imams must stay focused on religious guidance.

Background

The statement came after Oyo governor and APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde made a donation to the league. The lecturers' union ASUU also said this week that it would not endorse any candidate. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Tinubu and Aregbesola have met three times since their rift, says APC chieftain

15 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Professor Sola Adeyeye, an APC campaign director in the South-West, says President Tinubu and former Osun governor Rauf Aregbesola have met at least three times since their political falling-out, and that he has tried to reconcile them.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu and his former close ally Rauf Aregbesola have met at least three times since their political falling-out, according to a senior figure in the ruling party.

What he said

Professor Sola Adeyeye, director of electioneering planning for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign in the South-West, said in an interview reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, that he knew the two men had met about three times since the rift began. He described them as former very close friends, said the hurt is deeper when people who care about each other feel betrayed, and suggested time could heal the relationship. He said he had personally tried to broker a reconciliation but did not give details.

Background

Aregbesola, a former Osun governor and minister, was one of Tinubu's closest political allies for decades before they fell out. He is now national secretary of the Mark-led faction of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), whose leadership was voided by a Federal High Court this month, a ruling the party has appealed. Aregbesola has not commented on Adeyeye's claim.
