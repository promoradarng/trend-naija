"I established my university before becoming Vice-President": Atiku fires back at Tinubu

8 Nov 2026, 03:13 GMT

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has responded to President Bola Tinubu over questions about his investment in education, insisting that the foundation of his educational institution was laid long before he became Vice-President.

Atiku's response

Atiku, who served as Vice-President from 1999 to 2007, said the suggestion that he established a university with resources acquired while in public office was misleading.

According to him, his journey into education began in 1993, when he established ABTI Academy, six years before he took office as Vice-President under former President Olusegun Obasanjo.

He made the clarification after remarks attributed to President Tinubu questioning how he was able to set up a university while serving in government.

Why the dates matter

Atiku maintained that his investment in education was part of his business activities before he entered the country's second-highest office. By the time he became Vice-President in May 1999, he says, he had already begun building what would eventually grow into a major private institution.

That institution later became the American University of Nigeria (AUN) in Yola, Adamawa State. The university itself was established in the mid-2000s, during his time as Vice-President, which is why questions about its funding and expansion continue to come up.

Atiku also insisted that his business activities and investments are open to public scrutiny, and that anyone genuinely interested in the source and history of his wealth can examine his record.

A growing rivalry

The exchange adds another chapter to the rivalry between President Tinubu and the former Vice-President, two prominent figures whose differences have shaped several national debates.

Beyond the personal disagreement, the controversy raises a question Nigerians have long asked of their leaders: how did those who held public office acquire their wealth, and can they give a clear account of their investments?

The fact that an educational project began before someone took office does not, on its own, settle every question about its funding or later expansion. Those questions would require documentary evidence and a proper look at the institution's financial history.

The road to 2027

As activities ahead of the 2027 general election gather pace, exchanges like this between leading political figures are likely to attract even more attention.

For now, Atiku has made his position clear: his educational investment began in 1993, well before he became Vice-President, and he is prepared to have his business record examined.

Makinde gives four reasons he is challenging Tinubu, whom he backed in 2023

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Oyo governor Seyi Makinde, the APM presidential candidate, says he is running against President Tinubu because economic gains have not reached ordinary Nigerians, the government keeps blaming inherited problems, alliances serve politics rather than the nation, and reforms lacked cushions.

Read more

Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde has explained why he is running against President Bola Tinubu in the 2027 presidential election, three years after supporting him.

His reasons

In a newsletter published on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Makinde, the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), said his decision was not about personal rivalry or a desire to live in Aso Rock. He said he backed Tinubu in 2023 because he believed the President's experience and relationships could unite the country. He listed four concerns: that improved economic figures have not translated into better lives for ordinary Nigerians; that the government keeps blaming problems it inherited; that political alliances appear to serve political rather than national interests, while opposition parties have been weakened; and that fuel subsidy removal and currency reforms came without enough protection for citizens.

What he would have done

Makinde said he would have used the transition period to coordinate with governors through the National Economic Council and prepare support measures before removing the subsidy. He pointed to Oyo's response, saying his government deployed extra buses, froze fares and introduced half-price travel for students and the elderly within 11 days, and later delivered food relief to 200,000 households. He also noted that Tinubu had opposed an abrupt subsidy removal under President Goodluck Jonathan in 2012.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027. The President has defended his reforms, saying ending the subsidy freed money for infrastructure and other investments. His campaign had not responded to the newsletter at the time of writing.

Armed thugs storm NDC town hall in Lagos; supporters fight back

12 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Suspected thugs armed with cutlasses, cudgels and stones stormed a Nigeria Democratic Congress town hall meeting at Oregun, Lagos, on Thursday morning, scattering attendees before party supporters regrouped and drove them out.

Read more

A town hall meeting of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) in Lagos was disrupted on Thursday morning, 12 November 2026, when suspected thugs stormed the venue.

What happened

The attackers, armed with cutlasses, cudgels and stones, arrived at an event centre in Oregun, Ikeja, where supporters of NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi and his running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso had gathered. The atmosphere had been calm, with supporters singing and dancing before the meeting began. According to a journalist at the scene, the group was heard saying the NDC supporters were few and could be overpowered, and some members were heard asking for clearance to enter with guns.

Supporters fight back

Many people inside the hall fled when the attackers forced their way in, while motorists on Kudirat Abiola Way turned back to avoid the area. NDC supporters then regrouped, reportedly chased the attackers out of the hall and caught one of them in the car park. It is not clear how many people were injured, who sent the attackers, or whether anyone has been arrested. The police had not publicly commented at the time of writing.

Background

Obi won Lagos in the 2023 presidential election as the Labour Party candidate, with more than 580,000 votes, in a state seen as a stronghold of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC). Campaign-related violence has been reported against several parties in recent weeks, and on Wednesday the Inspector-General of Police warned officers not to aid thuggery, while the European Union raised concerns about violence ahead of the 16 January 2027 election.

Tinubu promises interest-free loans for small farmers, unveils APC manifesto

12 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Tinubu has promised interest-free loans for small-scale farmers and unveiled the APC's 2027 manifesto, 'From Reforms to Prosperity', at a rally in Jigawa, where he also defended fuel subsidy removal.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has promised interest-free loans for small-scale farmers as part of his re-election pitch.

What he announced

Speaking at Dutse Township Stadium in Jigawa State on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, where he also commissioned 400 tractors and other farm machinery bought by the state government, the President said the loans would help boost food production and food security. He also unveiled the All Progressives Congress (APC) campaign manifesto, titled 'From Reforms to Prosperity', which he described as a plan to build on his economic reforms and spread prosperity. He promised better welfare and pay for the armed forces and police.

Defending subsidy removal

Tinubu defended his decision to end the petrol subsidy in May 2023, saying the money saved had been freed up for infrastructure and other investments, and questioned how opposition politicians who promise to restore the subsidy would pay for it. The removal led to sharp rises in fuel, transport and food prices, which remain a major concern for households ahead of the election.

Background

The President urged Jigawa residents to vote for the APC in the 16 January 2027 election. During the same visit, former Jigawa governor Sule Lamido denied a claim by Vice-President Kashim Shettima that he supports the APC.

Crowd chants 'Obi' as Deputy Speaker Kalu campaigns for Tinubu at Abia festival

12 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

A crowd at a cultural festival in Bende, Abia State, reportedly drowned out Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu with chants of 'Obi, Obi' as he tried to rally support for President Tinubu's re-election.

Read more

Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives Benjamin Kalu was reportedly met with chants of Peter Obi's name as he campaigned for President Bola Tinubu at a festival in Abia State.

What happened

Kalu, an All Progressives Congress (APC) lawmaker who represents Bende Federal Constituency, was addressing the Item Okpi Festival, an annual cultural celebration in Bende Local Government Area, on Saturday. According to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the audience responded with loud and sustained chants of Obi's name rather than applause. When a woman on stage began a pro-Obi song, Kalu reportedly asked her to switch to a pro-Tinubu song, but the chanting continued. The Deputy Speaker was said to have remarked in Igbo that the crowd did not seem to want a university the President plans to build for them, and suggested some of those chanting were not from Item.

Background

Abia is governed by Alex Otti of the Labour Party, a former ally of Obi who has said he supports Tinubu as the sitting president while acknowledging that his party has its own candidate. Obi, now the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), remains popular in the South-East, where the APC is working hard to win support ahead of the 16 January 2027 election. Kalu has not publicly commented on the video.
