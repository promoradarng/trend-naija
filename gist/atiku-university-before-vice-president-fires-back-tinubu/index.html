"I established my university before becoming Vice-President": Atiku fires back at Tinubu

8 Nov 2026, 03:13 GMT

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has responded to President Bola Tinubu over questions about his investment in education, insisting that the foundation of his educational institution was laid long before he became Vice-President.

Atiku's response

Atiku, who served as Vice-President from 1999 to 2007, said the suggestion that he established a university with resources acquired while in public office was misleading.

According to him, his journey into education began in 1993, when he established ABTI Academy, six years before he took office as Vice-President under former President Olusegun Obasanjo.

He made the clarification after remarks attributed to President Tinubu questioning how he was able to set up a university while serving in government.

Why the dates matter

Atiku maintained that his investment in education was part of his business activities before he entered the country's second-highest office. By the time he became Vice-President in May 1999, he says, he had already begun building what would eventually grow into a major private institution.

That institution later became the American University of Nigeria (AUN) in Yola, Adamawa State. The university itself was established in the mid-2000s, during his time as Vice-President, which is why questions about its funding and expansion continue to come up.

Atiku also insisted that his business activities and investments are open to public scrutiny, and that anyone genuinely interested in the source and history of his wealth can examine his record.

A growing rivalry

The exchange adds another chapter to the rivalry between President Tinubu and the former Vice-President, two prominent figures whose differences have shaped several national debates.

Beyond the personal disagreement, the controversy raises a question Nigerians have long asked of their leaders: how did those who held public office acquire their wealth, and can they give a clear account of their investments?

The fact that an educational project began before someone took office does not, on its own, settle every question about its funding or later expansion. Those questions would require documentary evidence and a proper look at the institution's financial history.

The road to 2027

As activities ahead of the 2027 general election gather pace, exchanges like this between leading political figures are likely to attract even more attention.

For now, Atiku has made his position clear: his educational investment began in 1993, well before he became Vice-President, and he is prepared to have his business record examined.

INEC says its staff in about 10 states uploaded AI photos to voters' register

11 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

INEC says it has identified its own officials in about 10 states who uploaded AI-generated photographs to the voters' register. The images have been removed and the commission says the staff involved will face disciplinary action.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) says it has identified officials in about 10 states who uploaded photographs generated by artificial intelligence to Nigeria's voters' register.

What INEC said

Speaking on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, at a webinar on AI, misinformation and the 2027 elections, INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, who chairs the commission's Information and Voter Education Committee, said a large number of AI-generated pictures had been posted onto the register and had since been taken down. He said the commission had the names of the staff involved across about 10 states and would take disciplinary action against them.

Limits on what INEC can do

Haruna said it could be difficult for the commission to stop members of the public from digitally altering their own photographs before submitting them, but that INEC could act directly where its own staff were responsible.

Background

The issue of AI-generated images on the register had already drawn public attention, raising concerns about the accuracy of the voter roll ahead of the general elections. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Uzodimma insists he will keep Imo West Senate ticket despite court ruling

11 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Imo governor Hope Uzodimma says he will not give up the APC ticket for Imo West senatorial district, despite a Federal High Court ruling that named his predecessor Rochas Okorocha as the party's candidate.

Read more

Imo State governor Hope Uzodimma has vowed to continue his bid for the Imo West Senate seat, despite a court judgment that removed him as the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate.

What he said

Speaking after Mass at the Government House chapel in Owerri, reported on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, Uzodimma dismissed suggestions that he would surrender the ticket, telling supporters that anyone who says someone else will take it from him is lying. He urged residents to campaign for APC candidates in state and federal races, naming the party's candidates for the Okigwe and Owerri zones, and continued to present himself as the party's candidate for Orlu, the Imo West zone.

The court ruling

A Federal High Court recently ruled that former governor Rochas Okorocha, not Uzodimma, is the APC's rightful candidate for Imo West. Okorocha, who represented the district in the Senate from 2019 to 2023, was congratulated on the ruling by former APC chairman Abdullahi Ganduje.

Background

Uzodimma, who is serving his second term as governor and cannot run again, is a key ally of President Bola Tinubu in the South-East. FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has denied having any role in the judgment.

Owerri event centre that hosted Obi town hall marked by Imo government

11 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

An event centre in Owerri that hosted a Nigeria Democratic Congress town hall meeting with Peter Obi has reportedly been marked by the Imo State government, which demanded its title documents within 48 hours. Some Nigerians link the move to the event; others say the owners should simply produce the papers.

Read more

An event centre in Owerri, Imo State, has reportedly been marked by the state government days after hosting a town hall meeting of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC).

What happened

Photos shared online on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, show a message sprayed in red paint on the property, attributed to the Imo State government, asking the owners to show their title document and ground rent receipts within 48 hours. The venue, on Port Harcourt Road in New Owerri, hosted a town hall meeting with Peter Obi, the NDC presidential candidate, and his allies on 31 October.

How people reacted

Some social media users questioned whether the venue was being punished for hosting an opposition event, while others said the owners should simply present the requested documents and urged critics not to politicise the matter. The state government had not publicly explained the reason for the notice at the time of writing.

Background

Imo is governed by Hope Uzodimma of the All Progressives Congress (APC), a strong supporter of President Bola Tinubu's re-election. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Adamawa governor Fintiri denies threatening to detain 'Bamayi' chanters

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Adamawa governor Ahmadu Fintiri's media team says a report claiming he threatened to detain anyone who shouts 'Bamayi' until after the 2027 elections is false, and has demanded a retraction from the online blog that published it.

Read more

The media office of Adamawa State governor Ahmadu Umaru Fintiri has rejected a report that the governor threatened to detain people who shout the slogan 'Bamayi'.

The claim

A report circulating on social media alleged that Fintiri had said anyone chanting 'Bamayi', a Hausa political slogan, would be detained until after the 2027 general elections. In a statement reported on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the governor's aide on electronic media said Fintiri never made such a statement, publicly or privately, and described the report as fabricated and a deliberate misrepresentation of his position.

Demand for retraction

The statement named a Hausa-language online blog based in Jigawa State as the source and asked it to withdraw the report and apologise, or produce evidence to support it. It warned that legal action could follow if it did neither. The governor's team said the report was designed to stoke political tension and damage his reputation ahead of the elections.

Background

Fintiri has governed Adamawa since 2019 and is in his second term. Political tension has been rising across the country ahead of the 2027 general elections, with false reports and misinformation becoming a growing concern for parties and candidates on all sides.
