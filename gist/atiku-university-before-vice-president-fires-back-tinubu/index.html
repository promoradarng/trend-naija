"I established my university before becoming Vice-President": Atiku fires back at Tinubu

8 Nov 2026, 03:13 GMT

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has responded to President Bola Tinubu over questions about his investment in education, insisting that the foundation of his educational institution was laid long before he became Vice-President.

Atiku's response

Atiku, who served as Vice-President from 1999 to 2007, said the suggestion that he established a university with resources acquired while in public office was misleading.

According to him, his journey into education began in 1993, when he established ABTI Academy, six years before he took office as Vice-President under former President Olusegun Obasanjo.

He made the clarification after remarks attributed to President Tinubu questioning how he was able to set up a university while serving in government.

Why the dates matter

Atiku maintained that his investment in education was part of his business activities before he entered the country's second-highest office. By the time he became Vice-President in May 1999, he says, he had already begun building what would eventually grow into a major private institution.

That institution later became the American University of Nigeria (AUN) in Yola, Adamawa State. The university itself was established in the mid-2000s, during his time as Vice-President, which is why questions about its funding and expansion continue to come up.

Atiku also insisted that his business activities and investments are open to public scrutiny, and that anyone genuinely interested in the source and history of his wealth can examine his record.

A growing rivalry

The exchange adds another chapter to the rivalry between President Tinubu and the former Vice-President, two prominent figures whose differences have shaped several national debates.

Beyond the personal disagreement, the controversy raises a question Nigerians have long asked of their leaders: how did those who held public office acquire their wealth, and can they give a clear account of their investments?

The fact that an educational project began before someone took office does not, on its own, settle every question about its funding or later expansion. Those questions would require documentary evidence and a proper look at the institution's financial history.

The road to 2027

As activities ahead of the 2027 general election gather pace, exchanges like this between leading political figures are likely to attract even more attention.

For now, Atiku has made his position clear: his educational investment began in 1993, well before he became Vice-President, and he is prepared to have his business record examined.

Abia APC chairman urges Igbo voters to back Tinubu, says 2023 approach was an error

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Abia APC chairman Chijioke Chukwu says it is wrong to believe the Igbo are not supporting President Tinubu, and urged them not to repeat what he called the political error of 2023, when many voted for Peter Obi.

Read more

The chairman of the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Abia State, Chijioke Chukwu, has called on Igbo people to rally behind President Bola Tinubu in 2027.

What he said

In a Facebook post on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Chukwu said it was wrong to believe that the Igbo were not supporting Tinubu and were backing Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC). He argued that many Igbo voters acted out of political naivety in 2023 by believing that their numbers alone could produce a president of Igbo origin without building alliances with other regions, and warned against repeating that approach.

Background

The debate over how the Igbo should vote in 2027 has intensified this week. Senator Victor Umeh of the NDC said any Igbo person who works against Obi is an enemy of the Igbo, a remark criticised by actor Yul Edochie. Several South-East governors have declared support for Tinubu, including Ebonyi's Francis Nwifuru, who promised him 80 per cent of the state's votes, while Abia's governor, Alex Otti, was elected on the Labour Party platform.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Fact check: Obasanjo did not ask Nigerians to reject Tinubu's second term

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

A viral Facebook post claiming Olusegun Obasanjo urged Nigerians to reject President Tinubu's re-election is misleading, a fact-check found. Obasanjo criticised the government's borrowing but did not call for Tinubu to be voted out.

Read more

A viral social media post claiming that former President Olusegun Obasanjo called on Nigerians to reject President Bola Tinubu's second-term bid has been found to be misleading.

The claim

The post, shared by a Facebook page, had gathered thousands of likes and hundreds of shares by Tuesday, 10 November 2026. It circulated as attention grew on the government's supplementary borrowing plan of about $20.5 billion and €1 billion, sent to the National Assembly in October to fund infrastructure, agriculture, health, education, security and other areas.

What Obasanjo actually said

According to the fact-check, Obasanjo's remarks were a response to Tinubu's comment that 'borrowing is not leprosy', which Obasanjo described as nonsense, warning that excessive debt could destroy generations. Reports of his comments by several media organisations show he did not ask Nigerians to reject the President or vote against his re-election. His criticism was aimed at the government's approach to borrowing.

Background

Borrowing has become a major campaign issue. The Presidency has rejected claims that it has added ₦120 trillion to the national debt, while opposition candidates have criticised new loan requests, including a planned $500 million World Bank facility.

Amaechi says Nigerians now miss Buhari because life was better under him

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

ADC vice-presidential candidate Rotimi Amaechi says Nigerians miss former President Buhari, urging them to compare the exchange rate and fuel prices under Buhari with today. The APC says the reforms were needed to save the economy.

Read more

Rotimi Amaechi, the vice-presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), says Nigerians miss former President Muhammadu Buhari because life was better under his government.

What he said

Speaking on a television programme on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Amaechi recalled Buhari's prediction that people would one day call him back, and said Nigerians were now saying they miss him. He urged people to compare their lives under Buhari with their circumstances today, pointing to the exchange rate, fuel prices and what he described as corruption.

Background

Amaechi served as Buhari's Minister of Transportation and directed his 2015 campaign, before losing the APC presidential ticket to Bola Tinubu in 2022. He said last week that Buhari had promised in writing to back him at that primary. Buhari died in 2025. The naira traded at about ₦461 to the dollar when Tinubu took office in May 2023 and petrol was subsidised; both changed sharply after the President removed the subsidy and unified the exchange rate. Tinubu says those reforms saved Nigeria from economic collapse and that the naira has since recovered some value.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Imo Catholic priest apologises for singing Peter Obi campaign song in church

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

Rev. Fr. Finbarr Chukwu, a Catholic priest in Imo State, has apologised after a viral video showed him singing a campaign song associated with Peter Obi during a church service, saying he joined in spontaneously.

Read more

A Catholic priest in Imo State has apologised after a video of him singing a campaign song associated with Peter Obi during a church service went viral.

The apology

Rev. Fr. Finbarr Chukwu, who is attached to St. Dominic's Secondary School, Ugiri, in Isiala Mbano Local Government Area, explained himself in a letter dated 4 November 2026, addressed to the Catholic Bishop of Okigwe Diocese, Most Rev. Solomon Amatu, fellow priests, the faithful and the public. He said the incident happened during a thanksgiving service attended by a family and some people linked to a political campaign. When some of them began singing campaign songs, he said, he joined in spontaneously without considering how it would be seen.

Regret

He said he later recognised the implications of his action and had advised those present to respect the sanctity of church functions by keeping politics out of religious events.

Background

Obi, now the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), is a Catholic and has a strong following in the South-East. The video spread widely amid heated campaigning ahead of the 16 January 2027 election.
