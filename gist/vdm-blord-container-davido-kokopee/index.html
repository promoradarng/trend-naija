VeryDarkMan takes a swipe at Blord over power station business as Kokopee speaks on his rift with Davido

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Social media activist Martins Vincent Otse, better known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), is again at the centre of online debate, this time over his new power station business and his broken friendship with Davido.

The swipe at Blord

In a video shared on Thursday, 29 October 2026, VDM boasted about the container power stations he has been importing from China under his Ratel brand. He said people first claimed the project would never work but were now asking how he managed it, adding that his critics had been complaining since he unveiled the container. He aimed the comments at businessman Blord, claiming he was the first to talk about the idea and that Blord rushed into a similar venture afterwards. Blord had not publicly responded at the time of writing. VDM also said each buyer would be limited to two units, and that he would not sell in bulk.

Kokopee on the Davido rift

Separately, content creator Kokopee, a close friend of VDM, said in an Instagram video on Wednesday that he acted as a go-between when the relationship between VDM and Davido began to fall apart. He said VDM felt let down by the singer and that a sense of betrayal was at the heart of the quarrel, while Davido disagreed with VDM's view of events. Kokopee declined to share the private details of their conversations and said his support for VDM had always been private advice rather than public backing. He also promised to speak later on a separate matter involving the rapper Zlatan.

How people reacted

Reactions online were mixed. Some fans praised VDM for turning to business, while others questioned whether he could sustain it. On the Davido rift, many fans urged both men to settle their differences, while others shared unproven theories about what caused the split.

Background

VDM and Davido were once seen as allies, but their friendship cooled publicly earlier this year. The activist has spent recent weeks promoting the Ratel power stations, which he showed off at a warehouse and in demonstration videos.

"Go and work, I have children to take care of!": GehGeh sends strong message to his siblings and critics

3 Nov 2026, 20:34 GMT

Social media personality and financial adviser GehGeh has told his brothers and sisters to work hard and stop depending entirely on him, saying he has children to care for. His message has reopened a familiar debate about the pressure Nigerian families put on relatives who become successful.

Read more

Popular Nigerian social media personality and financial adviser GehGeh has stirred reactions online after sending a strong message to his family members and to those who believe he should carry the financial responsibilities of everyone around him.

"Go and work"

Speaking openly about the pressure that comes with financial success, GehGeh made it clear that having money does not mean one person should become the permanent provider for an entire family.

According to him, his brothers and sisters must learn to work hard and take responsibility for their own lives rather than depending entirely on him.

"If you're my brother or my sister, go and work. Don't depend only on me because I have children I need to take care of. I can't do more than my power for family members," he said.

A familiar Nigerian story

His message touches on something many Nigerians can relate to, especially those who have become financially stable while other relatives continue to struggle.

In many families, once one person starts making money, expectations begin to grow. School fees, house rent, feeding allowances, business capital and even personal expenses suddenly become that person's responsibility. Sometimes the demands never seem to end.

Drawing the line

GehGeh appears determined to draw a line. He did not suggest that helping relatives is wrong, but his message was clear: family support should have limits, particularly when someone has children and responsibilities of their own.

His remarks also highlight the pressure successful people face from relatives who believe family ties automatically guarantee financial support.

Family members should help one another whenever they can. But should one person's success become an excuse for everyone else to stop trying?

Over to you

Is GehGeh being too harsh on his family, or is he simply saying what many successful people are afraid to tell their relatives?

Four Nigerian-Americans win seats in US midterm elections

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Four Nigerian-Americans reportedly won state and local races in the US midterms, including Will Jawando as Montgomery County executive in Maryland and Onitsha-born Junior Ezeonu in the Texas House. A fifth, Georgia's Segun Adeyina, was leading in an uncalled race.

Read more

Nigerian-Americans have recorded a string of wins in the United States midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, according to reports.

Who won

Four Nigerian-Americans reportedly won state and local offices in Maryland, Texas, Washington DC and Pennsylvania. In Maryland, Democrat Will Jawando, whose father is Nigerian, defeated Republican Esther Wells to become Montgomery County executive, winning about 78 per cent of the vote with most ballots counted. He previously served on the Montgomery County Council. In Texas, 27-year-old Democrat Junior Ezeonu won the District 101 seat in the state House of Representatives. Born in Onitsha, Anambra State, he moved to Texas at the age of two, was elected to the Grand Prairie City Council in 2021 and became the city's youngest mayor pro tempore. He had defeated the incumbent in the Democratic primary and faced no Republican opponent. In Washington DC, Democrat Oye Owolewa won an at-large seat on the District of Columbia Council.

Still counting

A fifth Nigerian-American, Georgia state Representative Segun Adeyina, was reportedly leading in his race, which had not yet been called.

Background

The wins came on a night when Democrats were projected to take control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate. Nigerian-Americans are one of the most highly educated immigrant groups in the United States, and a growing number have entered politics at local, state and national level in recent years.

How people reacted

Many Nigerians at home and abroad celebrated the results online, describing them as a proud moment for the diaspora.

First Muslim senator and first Black woman governor among US midterm history-makers

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Abdul El-Sayed has reportedly become the first Muslim US senator after winning in Michigan, Keisha Lance Bottoms is the first Black woman elected governor of a US state, and Gina Hinojosa is set to be Texas's first Latina governor after defeating Greg Abbott.

Read more

The 2026 US midterm elections produced several historic firsts, according to results and projections reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

First Muslim senator

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed reportedly defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race, becoming the first Muslim member of the US Senate. A former Detroit health director, he ran on a progressive platform including Medicare for All and higher taxes on billionaires, and won the seat left by retiring Democrat Gary Peters. His campaign survived a bitter contest in which a Democratic rival he beat in the primary endorsed his Republican opponent.

First Black woman governor

In Georgia, former Atlanta mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms, 56, defeated Republican businessman Rick Jackson, becoming the first Black woman elected governor of any US state and Georgia's first female governor. Her victory reportedly ends 24 years of Republican control of the office. Atlanta-born Nigerian singer Davido shared the news on his Instagram Story.

Texas turns blue

In Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa reportedly defeated Republican governor Greg Abbott, who was seeking a fourth term, by about 53.5 per cent to 46.5 per cent. When she takes office in January 2027, the 52-year-old will be the state's first Latina governor and its first Democratic governor since Ann Richards left office in 1995.

Background

The results came as Democrats were projected to win control of both the House and the Senate, in what many analysts described as a rebuke of President Donald Trump's second-term agenda amid concern over the economy, tariffs and the conflict with Iran.

Lege Miami weeps at Tinubu rally as Eniola Badmus appears to snub him

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Comic actor Lege Miami reportedly broke down in tears while thanking MC Oluomo and others at a rally he organised for President Tinubu's re-election. A separate clip from the same day appeared to show actress Eniola Badmus ignoring him.

Read more

Nollywood actor Adams Kehinde, popularly known as Lege Miami, became emotional at a campaign event for President Bola Tinubu's re-election on Tuesday, 3 November 2026.

What happened

According to reports, the actor organised a rally attended by transport union leader Musiliu Akinsanya, known as MC Oluomo, and other personalities. In a video shared online, he broke down in tears while thanking those who had supported the event financially and taken time out of their busy schedules to attend.

The Eniola Badmus moment

In another clip that went viral the same day, actress Eniola Badmus, who also backs the President, appeared to ignore Lege Miami at a pro-Tinubu gathering. She reportedly took a seat across the room after walking in, and kept her distance even after he made a light-hearted attempt to ease the tension. The pair have had a public disagreement since 2024, linked to a dispute over matchmaking comments made online.

Background

Several Nollywood stars and entertainers have openly declared support for candidates ahead of the January 2027 elections. Eniola Badmus has been a visible supporter of the President. Supporters of other candidates, including Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar, have also held events with entertainers in recent weeks.

How people reacted

Fans were divided over the tears, with some saying they were genuine and others mocking the display. Many joked about the frosty moment with Eniola Badmus, asking why the two always seem to be at odds.
