VeryDarkMan takes a swipe at Blord over power station business as Kokopee speaks on his rift with Davido

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Social media activist Martins Vincent Otse, better known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), is again at the centre of online debate, this time over his new power station business and his broken friendship with Davido.

The swipe at Blord

In a video shared on Thursday, 29 October 2026, VDM boasted about the container power stations he has been importing from China under his Ratel brand. He said people first claimed the project would never work but were now asking how he managed it, adding that his critics had been complaining since he unveiled the container. He aimed the comments at businessman Blord, claiming he was the first to talk about the idea and that Blord rushed into a similar venture afterwards. Blord had not publicly responded at the time of writing. VDM also said each buyer would be limited to two units, and that he would not sell in bulk.

Kokopee on the Davido rift

Separately, content creator Kokopee, a close friend of VDM, said in an Instagram video on Wednesday that he acted as a go-between when the relationship between VDM and Davido began to fall apart. He said VDM felt let down by the singer and that a sense of betrayal was at the heart of the quarrel, while Davido disagreed with VDM's view of events. Kokopee declined to share the private details of their conversations and said his support for VDM had always been private advice rather than public backing. He also promised to speak later on a separate matter involving the rapper Zlatan.

How people reacted

Reactions online were mixed. Some fans praised VDM for turning to business, while others questioned whether he could sustain it. On the Davido rift, many fans urged both men to settle their differences, while others shared unproven theories about what caused the split.

Background

VDM and Davido were once seen as allies, but their friendship cooled publicly earlier this year. The activist has spent recent weeks promoting the Ratel power stations, which he showed off at a warehouse and in demonstration videos.

VDM praised as Oyo corps members finally receive delayed allowance

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Oyo State Batch 25C corps members have reportedly received their delayed final ₦77,000 allowance, two days after VeryDarkMan raised the alarm. He shared a video of a corps member celebrating the payment.

Read more

Corps members of the Oyo State Batch 25C have reportedly received their long-delayed final allowance, after social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM), whose real name is Martins Vincent Otse, publicly called out the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC).

What happened

On Wednesday, 18 November 2026, VDM shared a video on Instagram showing a corps member celebrating after receiving a credit alert and thanking him for taking up the matter.

How it started

VDM first raised the issue on 16 November, after receiving messages from more than 100 affected corps members. He said they had completed their service and attended their passing-out parade at the end of October, but had not been paid their final ₦77,000 allowance. He called on the NYSC Director-General to act.

How people reacted

Many social media users praised the activist for taking up the corps members' complaint.

Dangote Refinery considers bigger IPO after ₦6 trillion in subscriptions

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering enlarging its share sale by up to 30% after investors put in nearly ₦6 trillion. That could take the offer to about $2.1 billion, one of Africa's largest.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering expanding its initial public offering (IPO) by as much as 30%, after strong investor demand pushed subscriptions to nearly ₦6 trillion, or about $4.5 billion.

The numbers

The refinery, owned by billionaire Aliko Dangote, initially planned to sell 4.1 billion shares. Demand is said to have exceeded the shares on offer by nearly three times, prompting talks about increasing the allocation.

If the expansion goes ahead, the value of the share sale could rise to about $2.1 billion, potentially making it one of the biggest stock market offerings on the continent.

Why it matters

The refinery is the largest in Africa, and its pricing decisions have recently driven changes in pump prices across Nigeria.

Air Peace offers ₦80,000 domestic fares to serving and retired military personnel

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Air Peace chairman Allen Onyema has announced a special ₦80,000 one-way domestic fare for serving and retired members of the armed forces. He said it recognises their sacrifices for the country.

Read more

Air Peace has announced a special one-way domestic fare of ₦80,000 for serving and retired military personnel.

The announcement

The airline's chairman, Allen Onyema, disclosed the initiative on Monday, 16 November 2026, while delivering a lecture at the Chief of Defence Intelligence Annual Conference in Abuja.

He said the discounted fare would apply across all of Air Peace's domestic routes, and would cover serving officers and veterans who have served Nigeria since independence in 1960.

Why the airline is doing it

Onyema said the initiative was meant to recognise the sacrifices made by members of the armed forces in protecting the country.

Atiku asks Tinubu to release full Villa interview, raises questions over $4.5m lobbying deal

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has challenged President Tinubu to release the unedited recording of a recent Villa interview. His campaign questioned whether it was linked to a $4.5 million US lobbying contract.

Read more

ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has challenged President Bola Tinubu to release the full, unedited recording of a recent interview at the Presidential Villa.

The claims

In a statement issued on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, through his campaign's director of strategic communication, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku raised questions about whether the interview was funded through a $4.5 million foreign lobbying contract paid on Nigeria's behalf.

He pointed to US filings that reportedly list a consultant working with the lobbying firm DCI Group on work for the Republic of Nigeria. The filings are said to record a $4.5 million retainer paid to the firm, but do not identify the interview as one of the services covered.

What Atiku wants

The former vice president called for the unedited recording to be made public and for the government to explain the lobbying arrangement.

The other side

No response from the Presidency or the APC campaign was included in the reports.
