Africa Energy Bank begins operations in Abuja with $5bn capital

1 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

A new pan-African bank dedicated to financing energy projects has officially started work in Nigeria's capital.

What happened

The Africa Energy Bank held its first general meeting in Abuja and named Helmy Ghazi as its first president, according to a statement by the African Export-Import Bank (Afreximbank) reported on Sunday, 1 November 2026. The bank has an authorised capital of $5 billion.

What the bank will do

The bank was set up by Afreximbank and the African Petroleum Producers' Organisation (APPO) to fund oil, gas and other energy infrastructure on the continent. Afreximbank estimates that Africa needs about $200 billion in energy investment to meet its development needs. Supporters of the bank say it will help African producers finance projects at a time when many Western lenders have pulled back from funding oil and gas.

Background

Nigeria won the bid to host the bank's headquarters, beating other African countries, and the Federal Government has promoted the institution as a sign of investor confidence. African oil producers have long complained that it is difficult to raise money for fossil fuel projects as global banks shift towards renewable energy. The bank is expected to fund a mix of projects, including gas processing, refineries and power generation.

What it means

For Nigeria, hosting the bank could bring jobs, international meetings and closer ties with energy investors, though analysts say its success will depend on how quickly it can raise funds and start lending.

  • The bank has $5bn authorised capital and is based in Abuja.
  • Helmy Ghazi is its first president.
  • It aims to help close a $200bn energy investment gap.

Chinese national and police escort killed in ambush in Bauchi

16 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Suspected terrorists have killed a Chinese national working on a drilling project and the police inspector escorting him in an ambush in Alkaleri, Bauchi State. Another man was also reportedly killed.

Suspected terrorists have killed a Chinese national and a police inspector in an attack in Bauchi State.

What happened

According to security sources, the attack happened on Saturday, 14 November 2026, at Ruga village in Alkaleri Local Government Area. The Chinese national, who was reportedly working on core drilling in the area, was travelling in a company vehicle when it was ambushed. A police inspector from a mobile police unit, who was deployed as his escort, was also killed. A local source said the attackers killed another man in the village.

Response

Troops of the Nigerian Army's 3 Division were informed of the attack late on Saturday night, and security sources said efforts were under way to track down the attackers and establish the circumstances of the ambush.

Background

Atiku Abubakar's campaign was in Bauchi State over the weekend, and insecurity remains a key issue ahead of the 2027 elections.

Court warns AGF Fagbemi of jail over detained Okuama community leaders

16 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Warri has warned Attorney-General Lateef Fagbemi that he faces imprisonment if he keeps defying an order to release detained leaders of the Okuama community in Delta State.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Warri, Delta State, has warned the Attorney-General of the Federation, Lateef Fagbemi, that he could be jailed for contempt over the continued detention of leaders of the Okuama community.

The notice

The warning was contained in a formal notice of consequences of disobedience to a court order, issued on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, and reported on Sunday. It relates to an order made on 10 June 2026 by Justice Hyeladzira Nganjiwa directing the immediate release of the applicants, six community leaders who brought a fundamental rights case. The respondents include the federal government, the AGF and the armed forces.

Background

Okuama, in Ughelli South Local Government Area of Delta State, was the scene of the killing of 17 soldiers in March 2024, after which the military carried out operations in the area and detained several community members. The AGF's office had not responded to the court notice at the time of writing.

Dangote Refinery cuts petrol gantry price by ₦51 to ₦1,159

16 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its petrol gantry price by ₦51 to ₦1,159 per litre, its latest reduction, as depot owners also lower prices.

Read more

Dangote Refinery has reduced the price at which it sells petrol at its gantry by ₦51 per litre, according to reports on Monday, 16 November 2026.

The new price

The new rate is ₦1,159 per litre, down from ₦1,210, a cut of about 4.2 per cent. The reduction gives marketers and filling station owners room to lower pump prices, although retail prices also depend on transport, distribution costs and each retailer's margins.

Depot prices

Depot owners have also been cutting their prices. Market data from Friday showed several Lagos depots selling at about ₦1,185 per litre, with one of the larger operators cutting its price by ₦35.

Background

Petrol prices have fallen steadily in recent days after the refinery moved to pricing linked to international petrol prices. NNPC cut its pump prices in Lagos to ₦1,289 per litre at the weekend, and MRS stations in Abuja reduced theirs to ₦1,399. Marketers have warned, however, that renewed tension between Iran and the United States could push global prices up again.

NAFDAC halves product registration fees for small businesses

16 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

NAFDAC has cut product registration fees by 50 per cent for small businesses with five or fewer employees, as part of six measures to reduce the cost and delay of regulatory approval.

Read more

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has cut product registration fees in half for small businesses.

The announcement

Speaking at a SMEDAN–NAFDAC summit in Lagos, reported on Monday, 16 November 2026, NAFDAC Director-General Professor Mojisola Adeyeye said the reduction applies to businesses with five or fewer employees and is part of a package of six support measures. She said the aim was to bring more entrepreneurs into the formal economy and make locally made products more competitive at home and abroad.

Addressing complaints

Adeyeye acknowledged that many small business owners see the agency as a source of high costs, long delays and difficult paperwork, and said the new measures were meant to address those concerns. She said many delays are caused by incomplete documents, poor labelling and facilities that are not ready for inspection, and urged business owners to engage with the agency before applying.

Background

The move comes as the government rolls out other support schemes for small businesses, including free business registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission. Last week, NAFDAC uncovered a suspected illegal cosmetics operation at the Lagos Trade Fair Complex.
