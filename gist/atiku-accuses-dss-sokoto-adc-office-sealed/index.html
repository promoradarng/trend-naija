Atiku accuses DSS of plotting to disrupt ADC rally as Sokoto seals party office

3 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Tension is rising in Sokoto State ahead of a planned campaign rally by the African Democratic Congress (ADC), with presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar accusing the Department of State Services (DSS) of plotting to disrupt it.

Atiku's allegation

In a statement on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Atiku alleged that the leadership of the DSS was recruiting people to stage protests in Sokoto in order to create disorder. He claimed hired protesters were being gathered at an office on Koko Road and sent out in trucks to key locations in the city. He also said public facilities had been blocked and logistical support withheld to frustrate the ADC's campaign, and warned that security officials who help suppress political freedom would be held to account under the law. He said the rally would go ahead. The DSS had not responded publicly to the claims at the time of reporting.

Campaign office sealed

Separately, the Sokoto State Government sealed an ADC campaign office on Ahmadu Bello Way on Monday, 2 November. Commissioner for Land and Housing Nasiru Dantsoho said records showed the building, once used by the defunct Savannah Bank and previously by the PDP, still belonged to the bank, and that the state had written to the Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation to clarify its ownership. He said the premises were sealed after a disturbance involving party supporters and others, to prevent further trouble. A media aide to the ADC governorship candidate confirmed the closure.

Background

Sokoto is governed by the APC. Opposition parties have complained of attacks and obstacles to their campaigns in several states, and an ADC candidate's convoy was attacked in Sokoto in recent weeks. The APC and state governments have generally denied using state agencies against opponents.

What happens next

Security agencies are expected to be on alert as the ADC presses ahead with its rally.

  • Sokoto sealed an ADC office over ownership questions.
  • The DSS has not responded.

Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly removed the David Mark-led caretaker leadership of the ADC and nullified its decisions, including the nomination of Atiku Abubakar as presidential candidate. The ruling came a day after Atiku's Sokoto rally.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign. Neither Atiku nor the party had responded publicly at the time of reporting.

Aisha Yesufu launches citizens' fundraising campaign for Peter Obi

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Activist Aisha Yesufu has called on Nigerians to donate to Peter Obi's 2027 campaign, saying it is not sustainable for Obi to fund most activities himself. She said the citizens' fund is separate from the NDC's account and promised transparency.

Read more

Activist Aisha Yesufu has launched a fundraising drive asking ordinary Nigerians to contribute to Peter Obi's 2027 presidential campaign.

What she said

In a video posted on X on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Yesufu said Obi cannot do it alone, and that people who want to take back their country must take ownership of the campaign. She argued that the arrangement in which Obi personally funds most campaign activities is no longer sustainable, and urged supporters to give whatever they can afford. She said the citizens' fund is separate from the account of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Obi's party, and promised full transparency in how the money is used.

Background

Yesufu is a co-founder of the Bring Back Our Girls movement and one of Obi's most prominent supporters. Obi, a former Anambra governor, is running with Rabiu Kwankwaso on the NDC ticket. His campaign has presented itself as funded by ordinary citizens in contrast to what it describes as money politics. His convoy was attacked by thugs in Calabar on Wednesday. Other candidates, including President Bola Tinubu of the APC and Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, are backed by large party structures and support groups.

Rules on campaign funding

Nigeria's Electoral Act sets limits on how much candidates can spend and how much individuals can donate, and requires parties to account for campaign funds.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters welcomed the appeal and shared it widely, while critics questioned how the fund would be managed and audited.

Governor Peter Mbah claims he started the 'APC Ka Mma' campaign song

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Enugu governor Peter Mbah has reportedly said he was the first to sing the trending 'APC Ka Mma' jingle, joking that other parties may need copyright permission to use it. Critics said he should focus on more pressing issues.

Read more

Enugu State governor Peter Mbah has sparked reactions after claiming credit for a popular campaign song.

What he said

In comments shared on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Mbah reportedly said he was the first person to start singing 'APC Ka Mma', an Igbo phrase meaning 'APC is better'. He added that he was not sure other political parties had been given copyright permission to sing it. The song has become popular at campaign events, and supporters of other parties have adapted it, including a recent viral version in which a Catholic priest led a congregation in singing that Peter Obi's party is better.

Background

Mbah was elected governor of Enugu State in 2023 on the platform of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) and later joined the All Progressives Congress (APC). He is one of the South East governors who have declared support for President Bola Tinubu's re-election, and Enugu has hosted major APC rallies. The South East is expected to be a key battleground between Tinubu and Obi, a former Anambra governor.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the comment, saying a campaign song should not be used to restrict political expression and that the governor should focus on economic and security problems. Others saw it as a joke and said the song's popularity showed the APC was gaining ground in the region.

Former Osun governor Oyinlola says his endorsement of Tinubu 'has expired'

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Former Osun governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has reportedly withdrawn his 2025 endorsement of President Tinubu for 2027, saying it was a political move to resolve Osun's local government crisis that did not work.

Read more

Former Osun State governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has said his endorsement of President Bola Tinubu for the 2027 election is no longer valid.

What he said

Asked in a television interview, reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026, whether he still stood by the endorsement, Oyinlola reportedly replied that it had expired. He explained that he and other Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) stakeholders in Osun endorsed the President on 20 July 2025 as a strategic move, because of problems in the state that they believed only the President could solve, including missing local government funds and a dispute over who should occupy the local council offices. He said the endorsement was meant to help resolve those issues, but the solution had not come.

Background

Oyinlola governed Osun State from 2003 to 2010. Osun has been at the centre of a long-running dispute over control of its local governments, involving the PDP-led state government of Governor Ademola Adeleke and APC-backed council officials, with court rulings and disagreements over the release of council funds. The PDP itself is divided, with some members backing Tinubu, others joining opposition coalitions, and a court battle over its national leadership.

Why it matters

The withdrawal is a setback for the President's campaign in Osun, a swing state in the South West. The APC says it remains confident of winning the state, while opposition parties hope to benefit from discontent there.

What happens next

Oyinlola did not say which candidate, if any, he would now support.
