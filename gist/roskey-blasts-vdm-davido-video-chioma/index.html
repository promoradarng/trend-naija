"VeryDarkMan, you have gone too far. Respect Davido's marriage and leave Chioma out of this": Content creator Roskey blasts VDM

4 Nov 2026, 22:57 GMT

Content creator Roskey has called out social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) over his decision to resurface a video involving Nigerian music superstar Davido, popularly known as OBO.

According to Roskey, some things should be left alone, especially when a married man and his family could be dragged into unnecessary controversy.

"You went too far"

Roskey questioned why VDM would bring back a video of Davido enjoying Zlatan's song, knowing fully well that the singer is now married to Chioma.

What appears to have upset him even more is that VDM attended Davido's wedding. In Roskey's view, that alone should have been reason enough for VDM to show some restraint.

"VeryDarkMan, you went too far by posting that video of Davido. You know OBO is married now, and his wife, Chioma, won't be happy seeing that video circulating again," Roskey said.

He argued that marriage comes with responsibilities, and that people who have access to celebrities should know when to draw the line.

"He barely danced"

Roskey also questioned what Davido actually did in the video to deserve so much attention. According to him, Davido barely danced in the footage. He simply raised his hand while Zlatan's song was playing.

So why make such a big issue out of something that looked so harmless?

The Carter Efe question

Roskey went further and questioned VDM's priorities, bringing up his clash with fellow entertainer Carter Efe, who recently made insulting remarks about VDM's mother in a video.

"Why haven't you gone after Carter Efe, who insulted your mother? Why are you targeting Davido? Or is there something you're not telling us?" he asked.

That last question has added another layer to the controversy. Roskey appears to believe there may be more behind VDM's decision to revisit the Davido video, although he presented no evidence of any hidden motive.

What about Davido's marriage?

Roskey's bigger concern is the possible effect of these online fights on Davido's marriage. Celebrities are constantly under public scrutiny, but he believes there should be some consideration for their spouses and families, particularly when old videos are brought back into circulation.

Whether the video poses any real threat to Davido's marriage remains a matter of speculation, and there is no sign that Chioma has publicly expressed displeasure over the footage.

For now, Roskey's message to VDM is clear: whatever point he is trying to make, Davido's marriage should not become collateral damage.

Peter Obi corrects claims that his Washington visit was an endorsement from the US Congress

18 Nov 2026, 16:43 GMT

Peter Obi says his recent trip to Washington involved talks on Nigeria's development, citizens' welfare and democracy, and that he neither sought nor announced any endorsement, after social media posts claimed the US Congress had backed him.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has corrected claims that his recent visit to Washington amounted to an endorsement from the United States Congress.

What Obi said

According to Obi, the trip involved discussions about Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and the state of democracy. He said he neither sought nor announced any endorsement.

He said he met serving and former US lawmakers and other stakeholders during the visit.

How the confusion started

The confusion followed a viral video in which retired US Army Colonel Albert Short praised Obi.

Some social media posts presented those personal remarks as if they were official backing from the US Congress, and the claim spread quickly online.

What the facts show

There is no public evidence that the US Congress as an institution has endorsed Obi's 2027 presidential bid. Congress does not endorse candidates in other countries' elections.

A meeting with politicians, or kind words from an individual, is not the same as an endorsement from their institution.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections drawing closer, claims about foreign support for candidates are likely to keep spreading on social media. Obi's correction is a reminder for supporters on all sides to check claims before sharing them.

CBN and NNPC sign new agreement to tighten oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue

18 Nov 2026, 14:54 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement covering foreign-exchange inflows, remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing and more. It promises a more structured system, but the full agreement has not been released and no revenue targets have been set.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement aimed at tightening oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue.

What the agreement covers

The agreement covers foreign-exchange inflows, the remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing, digital payments, regulatory compliance and cybersecurity.

Officials say it will replace the old practice of dealing with issues one transaction at a time with a more structured system, including joint committees and performance monitoring.

Why it matters

Oil remains Nigeria's biggest source of foreign exchange and a major part of government revenue. For years, questions have been raised about how much oil money actually reaches government accounts, and how quickly.

Stronger coordination between the central bank and the national oil company could, in theory, improve transparency, boost foreign-exchange supply and help stabilise the naira.

Promises versus results

It sounds promising, but Nigerians will need to see measurable results. The full agreement has not been made public, and no target has been given for additional revenue or foreign-exchange inflows.

Without published targets and regular public reporting, it will be difficult for citizens to judge whether the new arrangement is working.

The real test

The real test is simple: will more oil money reach government accounts, and will Nigerians be able to see it?
