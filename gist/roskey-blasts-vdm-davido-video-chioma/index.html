"VeryDarkMan, you have gone too far. Respect Davido's marriage and leave Chioma out of this": Content creator Roskey blasts VDM

4 Nov 2026, 22:57 GMT

Content creator Roskey has called out social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) over his decision to resurface a video involving Nigerian music superstar Davido, popularly known as OBO.

According to Roskey, some things should be left alone, especially when a married man and his family could be dragged into unnecessary controversy.

"You went too far"

Roskey questioned why VDM would bring back a video of Davido enjoying Zlatan's song, knowing fully well that the singer is now married to Chioma.

What appears to have upset him even more is that VDM attended Davido's wedding. In Roskey's view, that alone should have been reason enough for VDM to show some restraint.

"VeryDarkMan, you went too far by posting that video of Davido. You know OBO is married now, and his wife, Chioma, won't be happy seeing that video circulating again," Roskey said.

He argued that marriage comes with responsibilities, and that people who have access to celebrities should know when to draw the line.

"He barely danced"

Roskey also questioned what Davido actually did in the video to deserve so much attention. According to him, Davido barely danced in the footage. He simply raised his hand while Zlatan's song was playing.

So why make such a big issue out of something that looked so harmless?

The Carter Efe question

Roskey went further and questioned VDM's priorities, bringing up his clash with fellow entertainer Carter Efe, who recently made insulting remarks about VDM's mother in a video.

"Why haven't you gone after Carter Efe, who insulted your mother? Why are you targeting Davido? Or is there something you're not telling us?" he asked.

That last question has added another layer to the controversy. Roskey appears to believe there may be more behind VDM's decision to revisit the Davido video, although he presented no evidence of any hidden motive.

What about Davido's marriage?

Roskey's bigger concern is the possible effect of these online fights on Davido's marriage. Celebrities are constantly under public scrutiny, but he believes there should be some consideration for their spouses and families, particularly when old videos are brought back into circulation.

Whether the video poses any real threat to Davido's marriage remains a matter of speculation, and there is no sign that Chioma has publicly expressed displeasure over the footage.

For now, Roskey's message to VDM is clear: whatever point he is trying to make, Davido's marriage should not become collateral damage.

TikToker gets suspended jail term for defamatory videos about NDA commandant

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

A Kaduna High Court has given a TikToker a suspended prison sentence and a ₦500,000 fine after she pleaded guilty to posting defamatory videos about the commandant of the Nigerian Defence Academy. She was also ordered to publish an apology.

Read more

A TikTok content creator has been given a suspended prison sentence by a Kaduna High Court over defamatory videos about the commandant of the Nigerian Defence Academy (NDA), Major General Yemi Olatoye.

The case

The creator faced five counts filed by the Inspector-General of Police and pleaded guilty to two of them, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. Justice Rilwanu Aikawa sentenced her to one year in prison on each count, to run concurrently, and fined her ₦250,000 per count, ₦500,000 in total. The prison terms were suspended for three years under a plea bargain. The court also ordered her to publish a public apology in two national newspapers.

The videos

The videos included captions blaming the general for the creator's possible death, among other claims.

Background

The case is one of a number of prosecutions in Nigeria over social media posts about public figures.

Businessman and social media personality Linus Williams, popularly known as Blord, has drawn attention after being seen in a cap linked to Senator Rabiu Kwankwaso.

What happened

During a visit to Kano State, reported on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Blord was seen wearing a cap with the words 'Nigeria Must Be OK', a slogan associated with Kwankwaso, the NDC's vice-presidential candidate on Peter Obi's ticket. Many social media users took it as a possible sign of support for the Obi–Kwankwaso campaign.

'Nigeria Must Be OK' caption

Blord later shared a photo of himself in the cap with a caption praising Kano and ending with the phrase 'Nigeria Must Be OK', associated with the Obi–Kwankwaso campaign, further fuelling speculation that he has shifted his support from Sowore, who is running for president on a different platform.

Background

The appearance drew attention because Blord had earlier declared support for activist Omoyele Sowore after Sowore was released from Kuje Prison in April 2026. At the time, Blord praised Sowore for intervening in his own legal case, described himself as a member of the Revolution Now movement and was presented with a branded beret. Blord has not publicly announced any change in his political allegiance.

All is fair in war, but has VeryDarkMan gone too far with Davido?

9 Nov 2026, 17:57 GMT

Amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VeryDarkMan has reportedly brought up the deaths of the singer's late friends, a painful chapter long surrounded by unproven rumours. Criticism is fair, but should personal tragedy ever be used as ammunition?

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) does not seem to believe in fighting fairly. When he has a disagreement with someone, he appears willing to go to any length to make his point, even if it means bringing up some of the most painful moments in that person's life.

A painful chapter

Over the years, Davido has lost several close friends and associates in tragic circumstances. Their deaths set off all kinds of rumours and conspiracy theories online.

Some people even claimed the deaths were connected to rituals or sacrifices for fame and success. None of those claims was ever proven, and there is no credible evidence linking Davido to any of their deaths.

Imagine losing people who were very close to you, only to have strangers turn their deaths into accusations against you. Davido has lived with those memories for years. He has carried on with his music career, built his family and moved forward with his life, but moving forward does not mean the pain has disappeared.

Reopening old wounds

Now, amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VDM has reportedly brought those painful memories back into public discussion. This is where things appear to be getting out of hand.

You can criticise Davido's music, lifestyle, public statements or behaviour. You can disagree with him and challenge his opinions. That is perfectly normal. But bringing up the deaths of his close friends because of a disagreement raises serious questions.

What exactly is the intention? Is it to expose wrongdoing, to make a legitimate point, or simply to hurt someone where he is most vulnerable?

Where are the limits?

VDM has built a reputation for confronting people without holding back, and his supporters may argue he is only giving his opponents the same treatment they give others. But surely there should be limits.

Davido is not above criticism, and neither is VeryDarkMan. Both are public figures who should be held accountable for their actions. Using someone's personal tragedies as ammunition in a public quarrel, however, is something else entirely.

There are some experiences people never fully recover from. They may laugh and smile in public, but that does not mean they have forgotten. If winning an argument requires reopening another person's deepest wounds, perhaps the argument has already gone too far.
