FG signs Port Harcourt airport concession deal, unions say no jobs will be lost

14 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

The federal government has signed a concession agreement for the Port Harcourt International Airport with a private operator.

The deal

The agreement with PH Airport Limited was signed at the office of the Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, according to reports on Saturday, 14 November 2026. The concessionaire was represented by its group managing director, Eyo Asuquo. The heads of the Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority and the Federal Airports Authority of Nigeria, ministry officials and aviation union leaders attended the signing. The company reportedly plans to recruit more workers.

Unions back the deal

Ben Nnabue, president of the National Union of Air Transport Employees, said organised labour supported the agreement after receiving assurances that no aviation worker would lose their job because of the concession, adding that protecting jobs remained the unions' top priority.

Background

The government has been seeking private investors to run and upgrade major airports.

  • PH Airport Limited will run the airport.
  • Unions say jobs are protected.
  • The firm plans to hire more workers.

Naira weakens to ₦1,363 per dollar in official market

19 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The naira lost ₦7.43 against the dollar on Wednesday to close at ₦1,363.23 in the official market, and fell to ₦1,430 on the parallel market. Analysts point to reduced dollar inflows and rising demand.

Read more

The naira weakened against the US dollar in both the official and parallel foreign exchange markets on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, amid reduced foreign exchange inflows and rising demand for dollars.

Official market

Data from the Nigerian Autonomous Foreign Exchange Market (NAFEM) showed the naira fell by ₦7.43, or 0.54%, to close at ₦1,363.23 to the dollar, from ₦1,355.80 the previous day. It also lost ground against the euro, closing at ₦1,495.60, but gained slightly against the British pound to ₦1,795.24.

Parallel market

On the parallel market, the naira slipped by ₦10 to trade at ₦1,430 to the dollar, while at one major bank's foreign exchange desk it closed at ₦1,368.

Why it matters

The fall comes a day after the naira lost about ₦25 against the dollar, and as the Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC move to boost foreign exchange inflows.

FG selects eight startups for over ₦1 billion iDICE funding

19 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The federal government has named eight Nigerian startups to receive about ₦134 million each in equity investment under the iDICE programme. They will also get mentorship and business support.

Read more

The federal government has unveiled eight Nigerian startups selected to receive about ₦134 million each under the Investment in Digital and Creative Enterprises (iDICE) programme.

The funding

The companies will share a combined investment of more than ₦1 billion through Growth Lab, the second phase of the iDICE Startup Bridge initiative. Each startup will receive $100,000 in equity investment, along with business development support, mentorship and access to industry experts.

The aim

The programme is designed to help Nigerian entrepreneurs grow their businesses, create jobs and compete more effectively in the digital and creative economy, and to help the startups attract further investment.

Cement hits ₦15,000 a bag, up over 300% in five years

19 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

A 50kg bag of cement now reportedly costs between ₦13,000 and ₦15,000, up about 329% from 2021 prices. Homebuilders and small developers are the hardest hit.

Read more

The price of a 50kg bag of cement in Nigeria has reportedly reached between ₦13,000 and ₦15,000, about 329% higher than in 2021, according to market price data.

How prices rose

A bag cost between ₦3,300 and ₦3,500 in 2021, rising to about ₦4,600 in 2022 and ₦5,000 in 2023. By 2024 buyers were paying ₦7,500 to ₦8,500, and prices climbed to ₦9,500 to ₦10,500 through most of 2025. There was brief relief at the start of 2026 before prices rose again.

Who is affected

The steep increase has put heavy pressure on construction budgets, hitting individual homebuilders and small-scale developers hardest.

FG, states and councils share ₦2.44 trillion October revenue

19 Nov 2026, 06:41 GMT

The Federation Account Allocation Committee has shared ₦2.441 trillion from October 2026 revenue among the federal, state and local governments. Both oil-related revenue and VAT dipped slightly from September.

Read more

The Federation Account Allocation Committee (FAAC) has shared ₦2.441 trillion from October 2026 revenue among the federal government, states and local government councils.

The breakdown

According to a statement on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, from the Office of the Accountant-General of the Federation, the pool was made up of ₦1.688 trillion in statutory revenue and ₦752.488 billion from Value Added Tax (VAT).

The federal government received ₦856.065 billion, the states ₦809.909 billion and local government councils ₦568.701 billion. A further ₦206.384 billion made up the rest of the pool.

Revenue dips

Total gross revenue for October was ₦3.811 trillion. Gross statutory revenue fell by about ₦97 billion to ₦2.999 trillion, compared with September, while gross VAT revenue dipped slightly to ₦812.446 billion. Around ₦128.8 billion was deducted as cost of collection and ₦1.241 trillion for transfers, refunds and savings.
