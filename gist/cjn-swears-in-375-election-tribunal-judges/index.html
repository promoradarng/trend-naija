Chief Justice swears in 375 judges for 2027 election tribunals

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

The Chief Justice of Nigeria, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, has sworn in 375 judges who will serve on the election petition tribunals for the 2027 general elections.

The ceremony

The swearing-in took place on Monday, 9 November 2026, at the Body of Benchers auditorium in Abuja, alongside the launch of the second edition of the Judicial Electoral Manual and a training workshop for judicial officers.

The CJN's warning

Justice Kekere-Ekun charged the judges to uphold judicial independence, resist political pressure and deliver justice without fear or favour, in line with the Constitution, judicial ethics and the rule of law. She also warned political parties, lawyers, the media and the public against behaviour aimed at undermining the judicial process, and specifically cautioned against the abuse of ex parte applications, which are orders granted without hearing the other side.

Background

Election petition tribunals hear challenges to the results of governorship, National Assembly and state assembly elections. Courts have played a major role in recent Nigerian elections, and pre-election cases are already shaping the 2027 contest, including a Federal High Court ruling that voided Atiku Abubakar's ADC nomination, which the party has appealed, and a ruling on the Imo West Senate ticket. Concerns about conflicting court orders, especially ex parte orders, have been raised repeatedly in recent years.

  • The CJN warned against abuse of ex parte orders.
  • A new Judicial Electoral Manual was launched.

Atiku says nearly 40% of bread makers have shut down under Tinubu

14 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Campaigning in Bauchi, ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar said the Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria has lost close to 40 per cent of its members in two years, blaming soaring energy and financing costs under President Tinubu.

Read more

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has accused the Tinubu administration of pushing Nigeria's baking industry to the brink.

What he said

In a statement released in Bauchi on Saturday, 14 November 2026, by Phrank Shaibu, director of strategic communication for his campaign, Atiku said the Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria had lost close to 40 per cent of its members in two years. He was in the state as part of what his campaign calls its 'affordable life' tour.

Rising costs

According to the statement, energy accounts for roughly half of most bakeries' production costs. It said one operator's monthly diesel bill had risen from ₦9 million to ₦19 million, and that switching to alternative power could cost a small bakery ₦40 million to ₦50 million, money most cannot raise. The statement blamed high energy prices and expensive financing for the closures.

Background

Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has made the cost of living a central theme of his campaign. The government says its reforms, including the removal of fuel subsidy, were necessary to stabilise the economy and free up money for infrastructure. The Presidency had not responded to the statement at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
