Chief Justice swears in 375 judges for 2027 election tribunals

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

The Chief Justice of Nigeria, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, has sworn in 375 judges who will serve on the election petition tribunals for the 2027 general elections.

The ceremony

The swearing-in took place on Monday, 9 November 2026, at the Body of Benchers auditorium in Abuja, alongside the launch of the second edition of the Judicial Electoral Manual and a training workshop for judicial officers.

The CJN's warning

Justice Kekere-Ekun charged the judges to uphold judicial independence, resist political pressure and deliver justice without fear or favour, in line with the Constitution, judicial ethics and the rule of law. She also warned political parties, lawyers, the media and the public against behaviour aimed at undermining the judicial process, and specifically cautioned against the abuse of ex parte applications, which are orders granted without hearing the other side.

Background

Election petition tribunals hear challenges to the results of governorship, National Assembly and state assembly elections. Courts have played a major role in recent Nigerian elections, and pre-election cases are already shaping the 2027 contest, including a Federal High Court ruling that voided Atiku Abubakar's ADC nomination, which the party has appealed, and a ruling on the Imo West Senate ticket. Concerns about conflicting court orders, especially ex parte orders, have been raised repeatedly in recent years.

  • 375 judges were sworn in for election tribunals.
  • The CJN warned against abuse of ex parte orders.
  • A new Judicial Electoral Manual was launched.

Donald Duke says he will step down if opposition agrees on single candidate

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

PRP presidential candidate Donald Duke says he is willing to step aside for a stronger opposition candidate, such as Atiku Abubakar or Peter Obi, if opposition parties agree through a credible process on who should face President Tinubu.

Read more

Donald Duke, the presidential candidate of the Peoples Redemption Party (PRP), says he is ready to step aside for a stronger opposition candidate in the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking in Abuja on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, at the unveiling of his running mate, Malam Sule Mohammed, and the launch of the PRP's manifesto, the former Cross River governor said he would respect any democratic decision by opposition parties to rally behind one candidate to challenge President Bola Tinubu, whether Atiku Abubakar of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) or Peter Obi of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), as long as the choice came through a credible collective process. He said a genuine coalition could not simply gather around a name, and needed a shared agenda.

Background

Duke governed Cross River State from 1999 to 2007 and is known for developing the Obudu Ranch Resort and the Tinapa project. He was a presidential candidate in 2023. There are 18 presidential candidates for the 16 January 2027 election. Talks about a single opposition candidate have repeatedly stalled, with Atiku and Obi both insisting on running.

Sule Lamido denies Shettima's claim that he backs the APC

Former Jigawa governor Sule Lamido has rejected Vice-President Kashim Shettima's claim that he is among Jigawa leaders supporting the APC, asking why he would join the party in 2026 when he did not in 2014.

Former Jigawa State governor Sule Lamido has denied claims by Vice-President Kashim Shettima that he supports the All Progressives Congress (APC).

What happened

In a video shared online, Shettima said prominent politicians in Jigawa, including Lamido, were backing the APC. Responding in Hausa in the comments under a Facebook post of the video, reported on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, Lamido asked why he would be with the APC in 2026 when he was not with the party in 2014, and described the claim as a lie.

Background

Lamido, a former foreign affairs minister, governed Jigawa from 2007 to 2015 as a member of the PDP and has remained a prominent opposition figure. Shettima's comments came around President Bola Tinubu's visit to Jigawa on Tuesday, when the President held a campaign rally and inaugurated projects in Dutse. Jigawa is governed by the APC's Umar Namadi.

Seun Kuti questions Peter Obi's education record in resurfaced interview

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

In a resurfaced interview clip, musician Seun Kuti alleges that Peter Obi's policies as Anambra governor kept poor children out of good public schools, and says high exam results mean little if access is limited. Obi's supporters dispute the claim.

Read more

Afrobeat musician and activist Seun Kuti has sparked debate after a resurfaced interview clip in which he criticises Peter Obi's education record as Anambra governor.

What he said

In the clip, reported on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, Kuti challenged what he called a selective retelling of history about governors praised for their track records. He alleged that Obi's policies in Anambra pushed children of low-income families out of public schools, and argued that the state's strong results in external exams such as WAEC meant little if access to quality education depended on families' economic status.

Background

Obi, now the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), governed Anambra from 2006 to 2014. His supporters often point to the state's improved exam results during his time in office. Kuti, the youngest son of Afrobeat legend Fela Anikulapo-Kuti, has been a critic of several major politicians. Obi's campaign had not responded to the clip at the time of writing.

Abia APC chairman urges Igbo voters to back Tinubu, says 2023 approach was an error

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Abia APC chairman Chijioke Chukwu says it is wrong to believe the Igbo are not supporting President Tinubu, and urged them not to repeat what he called the political error of 2023, when many voted for Peter Obi.

Read more

The chairman of the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Abia State, Chijioke Chukwu, has called on Igbo people to rally behind President Bola Tinubu in 2027.

What he said

In a Facebook post on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Chukwu said it was wrong to believe that the Igbo were not supporting Tinubu and were backing Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC). He argued that many Igbo voters acted out of political naivety in 2023 by believing that their numbers alone could produce a president of Igbo origin without building alliances with other regions, and warned against repeating that approach.

Background

The debate over how the Igbo should vote in 2027 has intensified this week. Senator Victor Umeh of the NDC said any Igbo person who works against Obi is an enemy of the Igbo, a remark criticised by actor Yul Edochie. Several South-East governors have declared support for Tinubu, including Ebonyi's Francis Nwifuru, who promised him 80 per cent of the state's votes, while Abia's governor, Alex Otti, was elected on the Labour Party platform.
