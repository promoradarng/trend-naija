Blord spotted in Kwankwaso-linked 'Nigeria Must Be OK' cap in Kano

10 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Businessman and social media personality Linus Williams, popularly known as Blord, has drawn attention after being seen in a cap linked to Senator Rabiu Kwankwaso.

What happened

During a visit to Kano State, reported on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Blord was seen wearing a cap with the words 'Nigeria Must Be OK', a slogan associated with Kwankwaso, the NDC's vice-presidential candidate on Peter Obi's ticket. Many social media users took it as a possible sign of support for the Obi–Kwankwaso campaign.

'Nigeria Must Be OK' caption

Blord later shared a photo of himself in the cap with a caption praising Kano and ending with the phrase 'Nigeria Must Be OK', associated with the Obi–Kwankwaso campaign, further fuelling speculation that he has shifted his support from Sowore, who is running for president on a different platform.

Background

The appearance drew attention because Blord had earlier declared support for activist Omoyele Sowore after Sowore was released from Kuje Prison in April 2026. At the time, Blord praised Sowore for intervening in his own legal case, described himself as a member of the Revolution Now movement and was presented with a branded beret. Blord has not publicly announced any change in his political allegiance.

Blessing CEO admits she did not have breast cancer in interview with VeryDarkMan

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Relationship coach Blessing CEO has admitted in an interview with VeryDarkMan that she did not have breast cancer, months after announcing that she was battling the illness and receiving donations from the public.

Read more

Social media personality and relationship coach Blessing CEO has admitted that she did not have breast cancer, months after telling the public she was battling the disease.

What she said

In an interview with activist VeryDarkMan (VDM), reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, she said a medical check-up had found a lump in her breast, but that it was not cancerous. She admitted that she used the discovery to tell people it was cancer because she wanted financial support. She said she first gave the impression that she was thinking of selling her car and house, before fans encouraged her to share her account details so they could donate.

How the donations ended

She said money started coming in from members of the public until VeryDarkMan questioned the fundraising and demanded proof of her diagnosis, after which the donations stopped.

Police open probe after video shows officers escorting Blord

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigeria Police Force says it has opened an investigation after a video showed police officers escorting businessman and influencer Blord, describing the arrangement as unlawful. Some social media users have defended him.

Read more

The Nigeria Police Force has launched an investigation into officers seen in a video providing an escort for businessman and social media personality Linus Williams, popularly known as Blord.

What the police said

In a post on X on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the Force Public Relations Officer described the situation as unlawful and illegal, added the hashtag #NoToImpunity, and confirmed that a probe had begun. The statement did not make clear exactly which part of the incident is being investigated.

Background

The video drew attention after social media users pointed out that Blord had recently been seen in caps associated with Rabiu Kwankwaso, running mate to Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), fuelling speculation about his political leanings. Blord was recently released from detention and publicly thanked activist Omoyele Sowore, now a presidential candidate himself, for his intervention. In late 2025, President Bola Tinubu ordered the withdrawal of police officers attached to VIPs.

How people reacted

The police announcement drew strong reactions online, with some users defending Blord. Blord had not publicly responded at the time of writing.

Reps order probe into attacks on political rallies ahead of 2027 polls

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The House of Representatives has ordered an investigation into violence at political rallies and meetings across the country, citing deaths and injuries in Delta, Nasarawa, Katsina and Enugu. Committees are to report back within three weeks.

Read more

The House of Representatives has resolved to investigate violence at political rallies, meetings and processions ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The motion

On Thursday, 12 November 2026, the House adopted a motion of urgent public importance sponsored by Minority Leader Frederick Agbedi and 16 other lawmakers. It directed its committees on Police Affairs and on National Security and Intelligence to examine the causes of the violence and recommend ways to prevent further attacks. A principal officer of the House will lead the inquiry, and the committees are to report within three weeks.

Incidents cited

Presenting the motion, Agbedi pointed to an attack during a political procession in Asaba, Delta State, on 7 November, which police said left one person dead and five injured; violence at an APC rally in Lafia, Nasarawa State, on 4 November; an attack on APC supporters travelling to a rally in Katsina State on 28 October, after which police reportedly arrested 14 suspects; and the disruption of political meetings in Enugu State in September.

Background

The motion came on the same day suspected thugs stormed a Nigeria Democratic Congress town hall in Lagos. The APC presidential campaign council condemned that attack and other recent attacks on supporters across party lines, and urged security agencies to arrest those responsible. The Inspector-General of Police and the European Union have also raised concerns about rising political violence. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Protesters heckle INEC chairman Amupitan in London; he denies running pro-APC X account

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Diaspora protesters surrounded INEC chairman Joash Amupitan's car outside Chatham House in London, accusing the commission of bias. Amupitan, who pledged to refuse any political appointment after his tenure, has again denied operating an X account that once backed the APC.

Read more

Protesters confronted the chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Amupitan, in London on Thursday, 12 November 2026.

What happened

Amupitan was in London to speak at a panel on Nigeria's preparations for the 2027 elections, hosted by Chatham House and the Kimpact Development Initiative. Nigerian diaspora activists who had mobilised on social media gathered outside the venue and reportedly surrounded his vehicle, accusing the commission of favouring President Bola Tinubu and the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) and demanding his resignation. They cited duplicate entries and AI-generated images in the preliminary voters' register and alleged fake polling units.

Amupitan's response

At the panel, Amupitan pledged to turn down any political or diplomatic appointment after leaving INEC, saying he wanted to demonstrate his impartiality, and said the commission was introducing technical safeguards. INEC has said it removed 1.6 million invalid records from the register, including entries with manipulated images, and flagged officials in 10 states. Amupitan also again denied operating an X account that had allegedly posted support for the APC during the 2023 elections, saying he had never had an X account and that it was never linked to his email. INEC said in April that the claims were fabricated and that fake accounts had been created in his name.

Background

At the same event, Amupitan said INEC would obey the final court ruling on Atiku Abubakar's candidacy. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
