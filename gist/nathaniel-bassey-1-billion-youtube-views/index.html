Nathaniel Bassey becomes first African gospel singer to pass 1 billion YouTube views

2 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Nigerian gospel singer and pastor Nathaniel Bassey has reached a milestone no other African gospel artist has achieved, according to reports on Monday, 2 November 2026.

The milestone

His main YouTube channel had passed 1.014 billion views at the time of reporting, with about 5.24 million subscribers. The channel has been active since 2011. Among his most popular songs are 'Imela', 'Onise Iyanu', 'Olowogbogboro' and 'You Are God', which are sung in churches across Nigeria and the diaspora.

The Hallelujah Challenge

The milestone came during the October 2026 edition of the Hallelujah Challenge, the midnight prayer and worship programme Bassey started in 2017. It runs twice a year and draws huge online audiences, with many viewers joining live on Instagram and YouTube. Earlier this week, a plane crash survivor shared a testimony during the programme, and a Muslim woman's decision to join drew attention online.

A non-commercial approach

Some of Bassey's followers have suggested he should make money from the large audience. He has previously described the Hallelujah Challenge as a spiritual exercise focused on devotion and worship rather than revenue. He did not give a public statement about the milestone at the time of reporting.

Background

Bassey, who is also a trumpeter, has become one of the best-known voices in Nigerian worship music. His success shows the growing global reach of Nigerian gospel music, which has produced several international stars in recent years.

How fans reacted

Fans celebrated the news online, describing it as a proud moment for African gospel music.

  • His channel has more than 1.01 billion views and 5.24 million subscribers.
  • The milestone came during the Hallelujah Challenge.
  • He is the first African gospel artist to reach the mark.

Odumodublvck says he refused to settle with man suing him over alleged assault

9 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Rapper Odumodublvck says a man who repeatedly filmed him without permission has taken him to court, accusing him of assault, and that he rejected his lawyers' advice to settle out of court.

Rapper Odumodublvck has spoken about a court case brought against him by a man he says repeatedly filmed him without permission.

His account

According to reports on Monday, 9 November 2026, the rapper said the man gained access to his home through a mutual friend and began filming him without his consent, continuing even after warnings from him and his bouncer. He said the disagreement continued at a party when the man brought out his camera again, and that he confronted him on stage, but that they later settled their differences, with the man even dancing with him while still filming. Odumodublvck said he was surprised when the man later took him to court, accusing him of assault.

Refusing to settle

The rapper said his lawyers advised him to settle the matter out of court, but he refused. The man's side of the story has not been reported, and the allegations have not been tested in court.

Background

Odumodublvck, is an Abuja-based rapper known for hits such as 'Declan Rice'. He went viral last week after a brief encounter with Peter Obi at Abuja airport.

Gospel singer Toluwanisings buried in Ibadan after family dispute

9 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Gospel singer Olayemi Bolaji, known as Toluwanisings, is being buried in Ibadan on Monday, almost four weeks after his death, following a dispute between his widow and his family that police helped resolve.

Read more

Gospel singer Olayemi Bolaji, popularly known as Toluwanisings, is being laid to rest in Ibadan, Oyo State, on Monday, 9 November 2026.

The arrangements

According to funeral details shared on the singer's official Instagram page, the lying-in-state was scheduled for 9am to 10am, followed by a burial service from 10am to 11am at an event centre on Hope Road, Alakia, Ibadan. The announcement did not say where he would be interred.

The dispute

Toluwanisings died in the early hours of 14 October 2026. His burial was delayed by weeks of disagreement between his widow and his family over the arrangements, reportedly including where he should be buried. The Oyo State Police Command stepped in after learning of the dispute on 28 October, with Commissioner of Police Olugbenga Abimbola helping the two sides reach an agreement.

The burial

Videos from Monday's ceremony showed pallbearers in black suits carrying the singer's white casket, led by trumpeters, before he was taken to his home, where a private burial was held. Some fans remarked on the absence of fellow gospel artistes at the ceremony.

Background

Toluwanisings was a Nigerian gospel singer. The public dispute over his burial attracted wide attention.

Portable reveals how 'Zazoo Zehh' royalties were shared with Olamide and Poco Lee

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Singer Portable says he gave Olamide 20% of the royalties from his hit 'Zazoo Zehh', up from the 16% the rapper asked for, while Poco Lee got 30%, the producer 20% and he kept 30%.

Read more

Singer Portable has revealed how royalties from his breakthrough hit 'Zazoo Zehh' were shared.

What he said

In a video reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, Portable said rapper Olamide, who featured on the song, initially asked for 16 per cent because he contributed 16 bars, while the producer received 20 per cent. He said he later raised Olamide's share to 20 per cent because he felt 16 per cent was too small for an artiste of Olamide's status, and credited Olamide with helping him secure his royalties when the song came out. He said dancer Poco Lee received 30 per cent, and he kept 30 per cent for himself.

Background

'Zazoo Zehh', released in late 2021, became a massive street anthem and turned Portable, whose real name is Habeeb Okikiola, into one of Nigeria's most talked-about artistes. The remix featured Olamide and Poco Lee. Portable has since been involved in several controversies and public disputes, but remains popular with fans of street-pop music.

Naira Marley announces Marlians Fest comeback for 12 December

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

Singer Naira Marley has announced the return of Marlians Fest on 12 December at Eagles Club Field in Surulere, Lagos, saying he stepped back to heal after difficult years. Davido was among those who liked the post.

Read more

Singer Naira Marley has announced that his Marlians Fest concert is returning this December.

What he announced

In a long post on his social media pages on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the Marlian Records founder said the show will hold on 12 December at the Eagles Club Field in Surulere, Lagos, as part of the 'Detty December' season. He spoke about a difficult period in his life without going into detail, saying rumours and negative stories had circulated about him and that he had deliberately stepped back to find peace and heal. He described the concert as an event about growth, healing and moving forward, and dedicated it partly to everyone 'we have lost along the way'. He said friends would join him on stage and thanked supporters who stood by him.

Background

Naira Marley became one of Nigeria's most popular street-pop stars with songs such as 'Soapy' and 'Issa Goal'. He faced intense public criticism after the death of his former signee, Mohbad, in September 2023, when questions were raised about Mohbad's earlier complaints against the label. Naira Marley was arrested and questioned by police over the death and was later released; he has denied any wrongdoing, and no charges against him have been reported in connection with Mohbad's death. A coroner's inquest was held into the circumstances. This week, VeryDarkMan has also revived discussion of the Mohbad case during his feud with Davido, while Davido's aide denied any link between the singer and Naira Marley's troubles.

How people reacted

Davido was among those who liked the announcement on Instagram. Fans were divided, with some welcoming his return and others saying the wounds from Mohbad's death were still fresh.
