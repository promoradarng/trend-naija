Nathaniel Bassey becomes first African gospel singer to pass 1 billion YouTube views

2 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Nigerian gospel singer and pastor Nathaniel Bassey has reached a milestone no other African gospel artist has achieved, according to reports on Monday, 2 November 2026.

The milestone

His main YouTube channel had passed 1.014 billion views at the time of reporting, with about 5.24 million subscribers. The channel has been active since 2011. Among his most popular songs are 'Imela', 'Onise Iyanu', 'Olowogbogboro' and 'You Are God', which are sung in churches across Nigeria and the diaspora.

The Hallelujah Challenge

The milestone came during the October 2026 edition of the Hallelujah Challenge, the midnight prayer and worship programme Bassey started in 2017. It runs twice a year and draws huge online audiences, with many viewers joining live on Instagram and YouTube. Earlier this week, a plane crash survivor shared a testimony during the programme, and a Muslim woman's decision to join drew attention online.

A non-commercial approach

Some of Bassey's followers have suggested he should make money from the large audience. He has previously described the Hallelujah Challenge as a spiritual exercise focused on devotion and worship rather than revenue. He did not give a public statement about the milestone at the time of reporting.

Background

Bassey, who is also a trumpeter, has become one of the best-known voices in Nigerian worship music. His success shows the growing global reach of Nigerian gospel music, which has produced several international stars in recent years.

How fans reacted

Fans celebrated the news online, describing it as a proud moment for African gospel music.

  • His channel has more than 1.01 billion views and 5.24 million subscribers.
  • The milestone came during the Hallelujah Challenge.
  • He is the first African gospel artist to reach the mark.

Skales says Burna Boy once asked Davido for space on his private jet

4 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Singer Skales says Burna Boy once asked Davido to let them travel on his private jet to a show in Yola where they performed their song 'Temper'. He said the jet was so full that one person sat on the toilet seat.

Read more

Singer Skales has shared a story from the early days of his career involving two of Nigeria's biggest stars, Burna Boy and Davido.

What he said

In comments that spread on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Skales said he and Burna Boy were booked to perform their collaboration 'Temper' at a show in Yola. According to him, Burna Boy contacted Davido and asked if they could travel on his private jet, and Davido agreed. Because the plane was already full, Davido reportedly asked one of his team members to stay behind and fly the next day. Skales said the jet was still so crowded that one person had to sit on the toilet seat during the flight.

A lucky break

Skales reportedly said the Yola trip led to an important connection for him with the international act Major Lazer, which helped his career.

Background

'Temper', released in 2014, was one of Skales's biggest hits and featured Burna Boy. Burna Boy and Davido have had a complicated relationship over the years, with periods of rivalry among their fans, although they have also shown respect for each other in public. Both are now global stars, with Burna Boy winning a Grammy in 2021 and Davido receiving multiple Grammy nominations.

How people reacted

Fans enjoyed the throwback, with some joking about the toilet seat and others saying it showed that the stars once helped each other.

Olamide defends Yoruba culture, says black soap is not diabolical

4 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Rapper Olamide has said he would happily use Ose Dudu, the traditional black soap often linked to spiritual practices, insisting Yoruba culture should not be demonised. He spoke at a private screening of a film of the same name by actress Sotayo Gaga.

Read more

YBNL boss Olamide Adedeji, popularly known as Olamide, has spoken in defence of Yoruba culture and tradition.

What he said

At a private screening of 'Ose Dudu', a new film produced by his friend, actress Sotayo Gaga, on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Olamide was asked about the traditional black soap of the same name, which some people associate with spiritual or diabolical practices. In a video from the event, he said Yoruba culture and tradition are not diabolical and should not be demonised, and that people should be proud of who they are and where they come from. He said he would not hesitate to use the soap if someone offered it to him.

Background

Ose dudu, or black soap, is widely used across West Africa for skin care and is made from natural ingredients such as plantain skins, cocoa pods and palm oil. In some contexts it is also prepared with herbs for traditional spiritual purposes, which has led to suspicion among some religious people. Olamide is one of Nigeria's most influential rappers and record label owners, and has launched the careers of several stars.

How people reacted

Many fans praised Olamide for standing up for African heritage, while others said they would still be careful with traditional products associated with spiritual rites.

American hip-hop artist Rayven Tyler has sparked conversation about relationships after sharing her experience of dating women and admitting that she now understands some of the challenges men face.

What she said

According to the artist, her personal experiences have given her a different perspective on dating, and made her appreciate what many men go through when trying to keep their relationships going.

"After dating women for so long, I just want to say I get it now, men, and I'm sorry. Now I know what you guys are passing through," she reportedly said.

Why it struck a chord

For years, men have complained about the pressures they face in relationships, from managing emotional expectations to dealing with misunderstandings and trying to keep their partners happy.

Tyler's statement suggests that her own dating experiences have helped her see some of those complaints from a different angle. What makes her comments particularly interesting is that she is speaking from her own experience of dating women, a perspective that has drawn plenty of attention.

Her remarks have opened a wider discussion about emotional expectations, communication and the difficulties that sometimes come with understanding a partner.

Every relationship is different

Relationship experiences differ from person to person, and her comments reflect her own perspective rather than the behaviour of all women.
