Akpabio says politicians cannot find enough private jets to hire ahead of 2027

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Senate President Godswill Akpabio has sparked reactions after saying that politicians are struggling to find private jets to hire as campaigning for the 2027 elections gathers pace.

What he said

Speaking during a Senate debate on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Akpabio reportedly said there were not enough private jets available for hire, and linked the problem to the failure of domestic airlines to keep up with rising demand for air travel. He pointed to the December festive season as a time when the country's aviation capacity is badly stretched, and urged the federal government to take urgent steps to address the problems facing local airlines.

The debate

The comments came during debate on a motion sponsored by Senator Tahir Monguno of Borno North, which urged the government to renegotiate the Bilateral Air Services Agreement between Nigeria and China so that Nigerian airlines can fly directly to China.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the remark, saying it showed how far some politicians are from the everyday struggles of Nigerians facing high costs of food and transport. Others said Akpabio was making a broader point about the poor state of domestic aviation, which often leaves ordinary passengers stranded by delays and cancellations.

Background

Nigeria's domestic airlines have long faced problems including ageing fleets, high fuel costs, foreign exchange shortages and frequent flight disruptions. The government has promised reforms to strengthen the sector. Akpabio has been Senate President since 2023 and is a leading member of the APC.

The Coalition for Democracy, an alliance of opposition parties ahead of the 2027 elections, has reportedly postponed its second summit by one week.

The new date

The coalition's Central Leadership Committee announced on Friday, 20 November 2026, that the summit would now hold on Monday, 30 November, instead of Monday, 23 November.

Why it was moved

The committee said the request came from member parties whose presidential candidates and campaign teams are busy with intensive campaigns across the country. It said it was more important for every party, presidential candidate and national chairman to attend than to keep the original date.

Who is involved

The coalition brings together parties linked to ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar, NDC candidate Peter Obi and Oyo Governor Seyi Makinde. Member parties are expected to pause their nationwide campaigns to attend.

Soludo says Tinubu doesn't need to rig 2027; Dele Momodu says Obi will beat him in Anambra

20 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Anambra Governor Charles Soludo told an APC rally in Awka that the 2027 election is already decided and Tinubu does not need to rig it. ADC chieftain Dele Momodu fired back that Peter Obi will win Anambra and embarrass the governor.

Read more

Anambra State Governor Charles Soludo and ADC chieftain Dele Momodu have clashed over the 2027 presidential race in Anambra, a day after Soludo's party, APGA, adopted President Bola Tinubu as its candidate.

Soludo's claim

Speaking at the APC presidential campaign rally at Alex Ekwueme Square in Awka on Thursday, 19 November 2026, Soludo said the election was already won and lost and that President Tinubu does not need to rig it to secure a second term.

He argued that opposition candidates had strong support only in some parts of the country, while the Tinubu-Shettima ticket had a presence in every state.

Momodu's response

Reacting the same evening on a television programme, Dele Momodu dismissed Soludo's prediction and said Peter Obi, the NDC candidate, would defeat Tinubu in Anambra and embarrass the governor. He pointed to Obi's 2023 performance, when he won both Anambra and Lagos States.

Background

Anambra is Obi's home state, where he served as governor. APGA, which controls the state, formally adopted Tinubu as its candidate on Thursday, with Soludo handing the party's flag to Vice President Kashim Shettima.

The election

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

INEC says Atiku stays on candidates' list until courts settle ADC dispute

19 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

INEC has explained that Atiku Abubakar's name will remain on its list as the ADC presidential candidate until the courts resolve the party's leadership dispute. The ADC has appealed the judgment that voided his nomination.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has explained why Atiku Abubakar's name still appears on its website as the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential candidate for the 2027 election.

INEC's position

A senior INEC official is reported to have said the commission will not make any changes until the courts settle the leadership dispute within the party.

The court case

On 5 November 2026, Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court delivered a judgment that affected the David Mark-led leadership of the ADC and voided the process through which Atiku became the party's candidate. The party has filed an appeal raising 17 grounds and asked the court to suspend the judgment's implementation until the matter is decided.

What it means

For now, Atiku and his running mate, former Rivers governor Rotimi Amaechi, remain on INEC's list ahead of the 16 January 2027 election. The outcome of the appeal will determine whether that changes.
