What the US said

In a statement shared on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, by the US State Department's Bureau of African Affairs, Washington commended recent operations that led to the rescue of the victims, the dismantling of terrorist camps, the recovery of weapons and the clearing of explosive devices.

Growing partnership

The statement comes days after Defence Minister Christopher Musa said the US was providing Nigeria with satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help track down militant leaders ahead of the 2027 elections.

Many Nigerians on social media welcomed the commendation and praised the military's efforts, while some others were sceptical about the statement and the wider security situation.

Nine dead, 46 injured as overloaded truck crashes in Cross River

18 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

At least nine people died and 46 were injured when a truck carrying 85 people crashed near the Yahe Bridge in Cross River State. The FRSC blamed excessive speed.

Read more

At least nine people have died and 46 others were injured after a truck crashed near the Yahe Bridge area of Cross River State on Wednesday, 18 November 2026.

What happened

The Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) unit commander in Yahe, Pascal Anigbo, said the crash involved a DAF truck carrying 85 people as well as goods including palm oil, refrigerators and motorbikes.

He said the driver was speeding and lost control while trying to avoid a head-on collision with another truck coming from the opposite direction, causing the vehicle to veer off the road into the bush.

Casualties

According to the FRSC, 46 people were injured and taken to hospital, while 30 others escaped unhurt. Three bodies were taken to the mortuary after being confirmed dead by a doctor, while six others were collected by their families after being identified.

NIMC to let Nigerians start NIN registration on WhatsApp

18 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The National Identity Management Commission plans a WhatsApp platform for NIN pre-enrolment and booking appointments. It comes as NIN becomes compulsory for domestic flights from January.

Read more

Nigerians may soon be able to start their National Identification Number (NIN) registration through WhatsApp, as the National Identity Management Commission (NIMC) expands its digital services.

How it will work

The planned platform will allow people to complete pre-enrolment and book appointments for biometric capture before visiting a registration centre.

NIMC's Director-General, Abisoye Coker-Odusote, disclosed the plan at the 2026 International Students' Day celebration in Abuja. She said the commission chose WhatsApp because of how widely it is used by Nigerians.

Why it matters

The service is expected to reduce crowding at NIMC offices, cut waiting times and make registration easier, especially for students and young people.

Timing

The plan comes as the federal government has announced that passengers without a valid NIN will not be allowed on domestic flights from 1 January 2027. No launch date for the WhatsApp service was given.
