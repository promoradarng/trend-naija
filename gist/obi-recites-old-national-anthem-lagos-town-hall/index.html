Obi and NDC supporters recite old national anthem instead of new one at Lagos town hall

13 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has drawn attention after reportedly leading supporters in reciting Nigeria's former national anthem rather than the current one.

What happened

A video from the party's town hall meeting in Oregun, Lagos, on Thursday, 12 November 2026, which went viral afterwards, shows Obi and the NDC's Lagos governorship candidate, Funso Doherty, choosing not to recite the current anthem when attendees were asked to stand for it. Instead, they and the crowd recited the old anthem, 'Arise O Compatriots', together.

The anthem change

Nigeria replaced 'Arise O Compatriots', which had been in use since 1978, with its original post-independence anthem, 'Nigeria We Hail Thee', in May 2024. President Bola Tinubu signed the change into law after the bill passed quickly through the National Assembly. The speed of the change drew criticism at the time.

Response

Neither Obi's campaign nor the APC had commented on the video at the time of writing.

Wike criticises Obi

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike criticised Obi over the incident on Friday, 13 November, at a town hall meeting of the pro-Tinubu Rainbow Coalition in Ogu-Bolo, Rivers State. He accused the NDC candidate of lawlessness, saying a man who wants to be president should not refuse to stand for the national anthem while standing for one that is no longer in use, and argued that anyone who disregards national symbols cannot be trusted to lead the country. Obi's campaign had not responded to Wike's remarks at the time of writing.

NDC hits back at Wike

The NDC responded in a statement by Chiogo Constance Ikokwu, spokesperson of its national campaign council, saying Wike lacked the moral standing to lecture Obi or other Nigerians on tolerance, leadership or national unity, and pointing to his role in political crises in Rivers State.

Background

The same town hall meeting was disrupted earlier in the day by suspected thugs, before it went ahead. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

  • Obi and supporters recited 'Arise O Compatriots'.
  • The current anthem is 'Nigeria We Hail Thee'.
  • The change was signed into law in May 2024.

25 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has reportedly asked President Tinubu to explain a ₦1.44 trillion fall in oil revenue in the first half of 2026, citing Budget Office figures. His campaign says collections were far below target.

Read more

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has reportedly challenged President Bola Tinubu to publicly explain a ₦1.44 trillion drop in Nigeria's gross oil revenue in the first half of 2026.

The figures

In a statement on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, by the director of strategic communication for the ADC Presidential Campaign Council, Atiku's campaign cited figures from the government's own Budget Office. It said the government collected ₦7.88 trillion in oil revenue between January and June 2026, well below its half-year target of ₦18.65 trillion, leaving a shortfall of about ₦10.78 trillion.

The campaign argued that the drop came even as international crude prices rose in the second quarter of the year.

The other side

No response from the presidency or the APC campaign was included in the reports.

Background

Atiku, who turned 80 on Wednesday, is the ADC's candidate in the January 2027 election. President Tinubu sent him a birthday message despite their rivalry.

NDC says supporters were attacked in Benin as Obi arrived for campaign

25 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Nigeria Democratic Congress has alleged that gunmen attacked its supporters and campaign vehicles on Airport Road, Benin City, as Peter Obi arrived in Edo State on Wednesday. It said one supporter was critically wounded.

Read more

The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has alleged that its supporters were attacked in Benin City, Edo State, on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, as its presidential candidate Peter Obi arrived for a town hall meeting and other campaign events.

What the party says

According to the party, attackers who arrived in a pickup van opened fire on its campaign vehicles parked along Airport Road while supporters waited for Obi. A party spokesperson posted videos online showing damaged vehicles and bloodstains, and said one supporter was critically wounded and receiving hospital treatment.

In a statement, the NDC's National Publicity Secretary said the attackers also assaulted people in the crowd and destroyed buses, and described the attack as unprovoked. The party blamed thugs it said were linked to the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC).

The other side

No response from the APC or the police to the allegation was included in the reports.

Background

The incident comes two days after gunmen attacked an NDC campaign event in Delta State, killing several people, and on the same day the House of Representatives warned that a state of emergency might be needed in Delta if violence continues. The NDC has also boycotted a security meeting called by the police chief.

Tinubu holds rally in Maiduguri, orders fresh push against insurgents

25 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

President Tinubu visited Maiduguri on Wednesday, where he commissioned projects, held a campaign rally and directed security agencies to step up operations against insurgents. He promised to turn security gains in the North-East into economic recovery.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu visited Maiduguri, the Borno State capital, on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, where he held a campaign rally ahead of the January 2027 election.

Projects commissioned

On arrival, the president commissioned two projects: a female lodge for National Youth Service Corps members and the Borno International Conference Centre.

Security push

Addressing the crowd, Tinubu directed security agencies to intensify operations against retreating insurgents and promised support for the families of troops. He pledged that the peace gains recorded in the North-East would be turned into full economic recovery.

Background

Borno has been at the centre of the insurgency in the North-East for more than a decade. Earlier this week, state officials revealed that eight soldiers and 13 construction workers were killed during the rebuilding of the Mayanti community, which Governor Babagana Zulum has now reopened.
