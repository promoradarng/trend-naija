Christian woman reportedly jailed 14 years in Borno after Muslim woman converts to Christianity

31 Oct 2026, 14:05 GMT

A Christian woman identified as Liyatu Samuel Dawa has reportedly been sentenced to 14 years in prison by a Borno State High Court following the conversion of an adult Muslim woman to Christianity.

What the reports say

According to reports, the Muslim woman testified in court that she willingly embraced Christianity and was not forced or threatened by anyone.

Dawa was reportedly convicted under Section 210 of the Borno State Penal Code, which deals with depriving a parent of the lawful custody of a child. She reportedly received the maximum term of 14 years without the option of a fine, and is said to have pleaded for leniency.

What is still unclear

The full circumstances of the conviction remain unclear. It has not been explained publicly how a custody charge applies to the conversion of an adult, and there has been no statement yet from the Borno State judiciary or government. It is also not yet known whether Dawa will appeal.

Christian advocacy groups have described the sentence as punishment for sharing her faith and a miscarriage of justice. They say Dawa is in custody and that her family has gone into hiding after receiving threats.

Religious freedom and the Constitution

The case has raised concerns about religious freedom and how the law is being applied. Section 38 of Nigeria's Constitution guarantees every citizen the freedom to practise, change and share their religion or belief.

Many Nigerians argue that this freedom must apply equally to everyone, whether they are Christian, Muslim or of another faith, and are calling for the judgment to be reviewed by a higher court.

Court orders arrest of Lagos NURTW chairman Sego over alleged contempt

2 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

The National Industrial Court in Lagos has ordered the arrest of Lagos NURTW chairman Mustapha Adekunle, known as Sego, and the union's secretary, after they failed to appear to explain why they should not be jailed for contempt. The case arises from a dispute with tricycle operators.

Read more

The National Industrial Court in Lagos has ordered the arrest and detention of the Lagos State chairman of the National Union of Road Transport Workers (NURTW), Mustapha Adekunle, popularly known as Sego, and the union's secretary, Usman Teslim.

What the court said

Justice Isaac Essien gave the order on Monday, 2 November 2026, after the two officials failed to appear in court to show why they should not be sent to prison for contempt. According to reports, the judge described their conduct as brazen disobedience of court orders and warned that the authority of the courts must not be undermined. He directed the Lagos Commissioner of Police, the state commandant of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps and other security agencies to arrest the two men and hand them over to the officer in charge of the Kirikiri Correctional Centre.

Bailiff obstruction

The court also raised concern over an affidavit filed by its bailiff, who said he was prevented from serving court papers on 5 October by a security officer linked to one of the parties in the case. Justice Essien reportedly described the alleged obstruction as a dangerous development.

Background

The case stems from a long-running dispute between commercial tricycle operators and the NURTW in Lagos over control of operations and levies. The union has been at the centre of several leadership and legal battles in the state in recent years. Sego and the union have not publicly responded to the order.

What happens next

The officials can still appear before the court or challenge the order on appeal. It is not yet clear whether security agencies have acted on the directive.

Rufai Oseni says law letting states run their own electricity will never work

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

Broadcaster Rufai Oseni has reportedly said states lack the money, gas and grid to generate their own power, arguing that the government should fix the national grid instead.

Read more

Television presenter Rufai Oseni has dismissed the law that allows Nigeria's states to generate and distribute their own electricity, saying it cannot work in practice.

What he said

Speaking on a breakfast television programme on Monday, 2 November 2026, Oseni reportedly said the idea of decentralising power sounds good in theory, but most states lack the money, technical skill and infrastructure to carry it out. He said the power sector needs huge investment, steady gas supply, power plants, transmission lines and working distribution networks, and asked how many states could provide these.

His argument

Oseni noted that the federal government, after running the sector for more than four decades, still struggles to supply a steady 4,000 to 5,000 megawatts to a population of over 200 million, and that the national grid often collapses. He added that many states struggle to pay civil servants and depend on monthly federal allocations, so they cannot be expected to fund power plants costing billions of dollars. He questioned how the country could split supply into 36 centres when it could not provide 24-hour power from one, and urged the government to focus on fixing the grid and gas supply.

Background

The Electricity Act of 2023 allows states to regulate and develop their own electricity markets, and several states, including Lagos, Enugu and Ondo, have set up their own regulators. Supporters of the law say it will attract investment and bring power closer to consumers. Electricity has also become a campaign issue, with opposition candidate Atiku Abubakar accusing President Tinubu of failing to keep his power promises, while the government points to its grid expansion plans.

Ebonyi governor sends bill to make kidnapping punishable by death

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

Ebonyi governor Francis Nwifuru says he has sent a bill to the state House of Assembly to make kidnapping punishable by death by hanging. He announced it at the funeral of a monarch who was abducted and killed in March.

Read more

Ebonyi State governor Francis Nwifuru says he has sent a bill to the state House of Assembly that would make kidnapping punishable by death by hanging, with no other option.

Where he said it

Nwifuru made the announcement over the weekend at the funeral of HRH Eze Francis Igwe, the traditional ruler of Ndufu-Alike autonomous community in Ikwo Local Government Area. The monarch, the father of a former deputy governor of the state, Eric Igwe, was abducted on Sunday, 1 March 2026, while travelling to church, and was reportedly killed by his captors the next day.

What he said

The governor said the bill was meant to deter anyone planning a kidnapping, whether for money or for political reasons. He said he would not delay in carrying out any court judgment against those arrested over the monarch's killing, and that the murder must be the last of its kind in Ikwo and across the state. He also expressed concern about what he described as a moral decline among young people and urged them to turn away from crime.

Tributes

Former governors Sam Egwu and Martin Elechi and former Senate President Anyim Pius Anyim paid tribute to the late monarch, describing him as a peacemaker, educationist and good administrator who brought projects such as the Folk Technical College to his community. His eldest son said his father's last wish was for Governor Nwifuru to win a second term.

Background

Several Nigerian states already have laws that allow the death penalty for kidnapping, but executions are rare because governors must sign death warrants. Human rights groups oppose the death penalty, while supporters argue it is needed to deter a crime that has spread across the country.

What happens next

The bill must be passed by the House of Assembly and signed by the governor before it becomes law.

Two suspected Ebola cases in Adamawa test negative, NCDC says

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

The NCDC says two people isolated in Yola, Adamawa State, with suspected Ebola tested negative after two rounds of tests. The earlier case in the FCT remains Nigeria's only confirmed case.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says two people investigated for suspected Ebola virus disease in Adamawa State have tested negative.

What happened

NCDC Director-General Jide Idris said the Adamawa State Public Health Emergency Operations Centre received a report in the early hours of 30 October 2026 after two people in Yola developed symptoms that needed further investigation. The two were isolated, public health measures were started and samples were collected. Idris said the samples came back negative for Ebola after two rounds of testing.

Nigeria's only case

Idris said the case confirmed earlier in the Federal Capital Territory remained the only laboratory-confirmed Ebola case in Nigeria. That patient had recently travelled to Uganda and was later evacuated to Switzerland for treatment, while dozens of contacts were monitored. He praised the Adamawa Commissioner for Health and the state response team for acting quickly, and said the NCDC would keep supporting the state.

Contacts being monitored

In its latest situation report, published on Monday, 2 November, the NCDC said 62 contacts of the confirmed case had been identified and are being followed up. Their monitoring periods end on different dates between 31 October and 13 November 2026, so 13 November is the latest date on which follow-up of the current contacts is expected to finish. The agency said it had trained more contact tracers and that surveillance continues.

Advice to the public

The NCDC urged people to stay calm, avoid spreading unverified claims and rely on official channels for updates. Anyone with fever, severe weakness, headache, muscle pain, vomiting, diarrhoea or unexplained bleeding, especially after possible exposure, should seek medical help immediately, tell health workers about any travel or contact with sick people, and avoid self-medication.

Background

The current outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain, began in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May and has recorded more than 10,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,000 deaths, mostly in the DR Congo. Africa CDC has warned that the continent is not winning the fight, and African health ministers have agreed to strengthen border surveillance.
