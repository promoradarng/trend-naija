Christian woman reportedly jailed 14 years in Borno after Muslim woman converts to Christianity

31 Oct 2026, 14:05 GMT

A Christian woman identified as Liyatu Samuel Dawa has reportedly been sentenced to 14 years in prison by a Borno State High Court following the conversion of an adult Muslim woman to Christianity.

What the reports say

According to reports, the Muslim woman testified in court that she willingly embraced Christianity and was not forced or threatened by anyone.

Dawa was reportedly convicted under Section 210 of the Borno State Penal Code, which deals with depriving a parent of the lawful custody of a child. She reportedly received the maximum term of 14 years without the option of a fine, and is said to have pleaded for leniency.

What is still unclear

The full circumstances of the conviction remain unclear. It has not been explained publicly how a custody charge applies to the conversion of an adult, and there has been no statement yet from the Borno State judiciary or government. It is also not yet known whether Dawa will appeal.

Christian advocacy groups have described the sentence as punishment for sharing her faith and a miscarriage of justice. They say Dawa is in custody and that her family has gone into hiding after receiving threats.

Religious freedom and the Constitution

The case has raised concerns about religious freedom and how the law is being applied. Section 38 of Nigeria's Constitution guarantees every citizen the freedom to practise, change and share their religion or belief.

Many Nigerians argue that this freedom must apply equally to everyone, whether they are Christian, Muslim or of another faith, and are calling for the judgment to be reviewed by a higher court.

Six Nigerians deported from the US arrive in Lagos

8 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The National Emergency Management Agency says it received six Nigerians deported from the United States at Lagos airport on 4 November. The five men and one woman were profiled by immigration officers and given resettlement support.

Read more

Six Nigerians deported from the United States have arrived in Lagos, the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) says.

What happened

In a statement on its X account on Sunday, 8 November 2026, NEMA said the five men and one woman, all adults, landed at the cargo terminal of Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Ikeja at about 10.13pm on Wednesday, 4 November, aboard a small chartered aircraft. NEMA's Lagos office received them in partnership with the US Consulate, the Federal Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Nigeria Immigration Service and an aviation services company. Immigration officers documented and profiled each returnee before clearing them, and NEMA said it coordinated support to help them resettle.

Background

The deportations are part of the United States' wider crackdown on undocumented migrants under President Donald Trump, whose administration has carried out mass removals, often on chartered flights. Groups of Nigerians have been returned on several occasions over the past year. The agency did not give the reasons for the individual deportations. Trump this week named Stephen Miller, the architect of his deportation programme, as White House chief of staff.

What happens next

NEMA said the federal government would continue to work with other agencies to manage the return of Nigerians from abroad in an orderly and dignified way.

All 10 passengers abducted from commercial bus in Ogun regain freedom, one suspected kidnapper killed

8 Nov 2026, 18:52 GMT

All 10 passengers kidnapped from a commercial bus at Orita Fufu, Awa-Ijebu, Ogun State, on 3 November have been released unharmed, police say. One suspected kidnapper was killed in a follow-up operation, and an AK-47, ammunition and ₦1.37 million were recovered.

Read more

There is good news from Ogun State: all 10 passengers abducted from a commercial bus at Orita Fufu, Awa-Ijebu, have regained their freedom.

Released unharmed

The passengers were taken on Tuesday, 3 November 2026. Police say they were released unharmed on the night of Saturday, 7 November, following sustained security pressure and intelligence-led efforts.

Suspect killed, weapons and cash recovered

During a follow-up operation, police say one suspected kidnapper was killed. An AK-47 rifle, ammunition, ₦1.37 million in cash and a mobile phone were reportedly recovered.

Photos of the recovered items show bundles of naira notes, an AK-47 magazine and a mobile phone laid out on the ground in the bush.

Questions still unanswered

Police have not said whether any ransom was paid for the passengers' release.

Reports also differ on whether any of the passengers was a serving or recently discharged member of the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC).

Safe at last

The most important update is that all 10 passengers are alive and safely out of captivity, after five days that must have been terrifying for them and their families.

The incident is another reminder of the danger travellers face on Nigeria's highways, and of the need for security agencies to keep up pressure on kidnapping gangs.

Tinubu to inaugurate sections of Kaduna–Kano and Kano–Maradi railways on 3 December

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

President Tinubu will inaugurate completed sections of the Kaduna–Kano and Kano–Katsina–Maradi railway projects on 3 December, according to his campaign council. It says the Kaduna–Kano line is about 90% complete.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has fixed 3 December 2026 for the inauguration of completed sections of two major railway projects in northern Nigeria.

The projects

According to a statement on Sunday, 8 November 2026, by Dele Alake, spokesman of the APC Presidential Campaign Council, the 203-kilometre Kaduna–Kano Standard Gauge Railway is about 90 per cent complete, while the 400-kilometre Kano–Katsina–Maradi line, which will link Nigeria with the Republic of Niger, is about 70 per cent complete. The 190-kilometre Jaji-to-Kano segment of the Kaduna–Kano line and the 250-kilometre Dawanu–Kano–Kazaure–Daura–Katsina stretch of the Kano–Maradi line are due to be inaugurated on 3 December.

Progress claims

The statement said that when Tinubu took office in 2023, the Kano–Maradi project was barely 5 per cent complete and the Kaduna–Kano line under 30 per cent. It said a federal inspection team led by the Permanent Secretary of the Federal Ministry of Transportation, Funso Adebiyi, recently assessed both corridors and carried out a test run. The figures come from the President's campaign and have not been independently verified.

Background

The Kaduna–Kano line is part of the planned Lagos–Kano standard gauge railway, which already runs from Lagos to Ibadan and from Abuja to Kaduna. The Kano–Maradi project was started under former President Muhammadu Buhari.

Why it matters

The inauguration will come just weeks before the 16 January 2027 presidential election, and infrastructure is a key part of the President's re-election campaign.

Lagos seals Surulere church over noise pollution after resident's complaint

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Lagos State environment ministry has sealed All Christians Gathering Ministry in Surulere on Sunday morning after a resident complained online about noise. Commissioner Tokunbo Wahab said it will stay closed until further notice.

Read more

The Lagos State Government has sealed a church in Surulere over noise pollution.

What happened

The Commissioner for the Environment and Water Resources, Tokunbo Wahab, said on his X account on Sunday, 8 November 2026, that All Christians Gathering Ministry, located off Clegg Street in Surulere, had been shut and would remain closed until further notice. He said the action followed a complaint from a resident on X about noise from the church, and that officials investigated and confirmed the complaint was valid, finding the church in breach of environmental regulations. The sealing took place on Sunday morning.

No exceptions

Wahab said the government would not soften its position on such violations, whether the offender is a religious body, a business or anyone else.

Background

The Lagos State Environmental Protection Agency has sealed many churches, mosques, hotels, clubs and event centres in recent years over noise pollution, often after complaints on social media. Wahab is known for responding publicly to residents' complaints online. Just this week, another Lagos resident cried out to the commissioner over noise from a church at midnight.
