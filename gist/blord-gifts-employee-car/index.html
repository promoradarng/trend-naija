Cement hits ₦15,000 a bag, up over 300% in five years

19 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

A 50kg bag of cement now reportedly costs between ₦13,000 and ₦15,000, up about 329% from 2021 prices. Homebuilders and small developers are the hardest hit.

Read more

The price of a 50kg bag of cement in Nigeria has reportedly reached between ₦13,000 and ₦15,000, about 329% higher than in 2021, according to market price data.

How prices rose

A bag cost between ₦3,300 and ₦3,500 in 2021, rising to about ₦4,600 in 2022 and ₦5,000 in 2023. By 2024 buyers were paying ₦7,500 to ₦8,500, and prices climbed to ₦9,500 to ₦10,500 through most of 2025. There was brief relief at the start of 2026 before prices rose again.

Who is affected

The steep increase has put heavy pressure on construction budgets, hitting individual homebuilders and small-scale developers hardest.

FG, states and councils share ₦2.44 trillion October revenue

19 Nov 2026, 06:41 GMT

The Federation Account Allocation Committee has shared ₦2.441 trillion from October 2026 revenue among the federal, state and local governments. Both oil-related revenue and VAT dipped slightly from September.

Read more

The Federation Account Allocation Committee (FAAC) has shared ₦2.441 trillion from October 2026 revenue among the federal government, states and local government councils.

The breakdown

According to a statement on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, from the Office of the Accountant-General of the Federation, the pool was made up of ₦1.688 trillion in statutory revenue and ₦752.488 billion from Value Added Tax (VAT).

The federal government received ₦856.065 billion, the states ₦809.909 billion and local government councils ₦568.701 billion. A further ₦206.384 billion made up the rest of the pool.

Revenue dips

Total gross revenue for October was ₦3.811 trillion. Gross statutory revenue fell by about ₦97 billion to ₦2.999 trillion, compared with September, while gross VAT revenue dipped slightly to ₦812.446 billion. Around ₦128.8 billion was deducted as cost of collection and ₦1.241 trillion for transfers, refunds and savings.

The United States government has praised the Nigerian military for rescuing 125 kidnapping victims this month, describing the operations as a significant step towards ending violence in the country.

What the US said

In a statement shared on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, by the US State Department's Bureau of African Affairs, Washington commended recent operations that led to the rescue of the victims, the dismantling of terrorist camps, the recovery of weapons and the clearing of explosive devices.

Growing partnership

The statement comes days after Defence Minister Christopher Musa said the US was providing Nigeria with satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help track down militant leaders ahead of the 2027 elections.

How Nigerians reacted

Many Nigerians on social media welcomed the commendation and praised the military's efforts, while some others were sceptical about the statement and the wider security situation.

Nine dead, 46 injured as overloaded truck crashes in Cross River

18 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

At least nine people died and 46 were injured when a truck carrying 85 people crashed near the Yahe Bridge in Cross River State. The FRSC blamed excessive speed.

Read more

At least nine people have died and 46 others were injured after a truck crashed near the Yahe Bridge area of Cross River State on Wednesday, 18 November 2026.

What happened

The Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) unit commander in Yahe, Pascal Anigbo, said the crash involved a DAF truck carrying 85 people as well as goods including palm oil, refrigerators and motorbikes.

He said the driver was speeding and lost control while trying to avoid a head-on collision with another truck coming from the opposite direction, causing the vehicle to veer off the road into the bush.

Casualties

According to the FRSC, 46 people were injured and taken to hospital, while 30 others escaped unhurt. Three bodies were taken to the mortuary after being confirmed dead by a doctor, while six others were collected by their families after being identified.
