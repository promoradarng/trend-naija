Toluwanising's younger brother defends widow as burial dispute rages

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The bitter dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried has taken a new turn, after his younger brother reportedly spoke out in defence of the singer's widow.

What the brother said

In a voice note said to be his, reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, the brother said he wanted to clear the air because people were blaming the wrong person. He insisted that the widow had no role in the dispute and should be left alone. He blamed the singer's maternal relatives for turning him against his father's side of the family from childhood, and said their father had supported the singer financially during his school years. He also described a disagreement in which the singer's grandmother asked him for a Sallah ram, which he declined because he is a Christian, leaving some relatives upset.

Others weigh in

Media personality Nancy Umeh reportedly criticised the singer's family and said the widow has every right to decide where her husband is buried. Actor Yomi Fabiyi, however, reportedly urged the widow to allow the family to decide. On social media, many fans praised the brother for speaking up and said the widow had been unfairly attacked.

Background

Toluwanising died suddenly on 14 October 2026. Since then, members of his family have disagreed publicly about his burial. A family spokesman said in a viral video this week that relatives would remove his body if he was buried in a cemetery, and insisted he be laid beside his mother in the family compound. His widow had reportedly objected to a burial at the house they shared in Ibadan.

Mohbad's father weighs in

On Friday, Joseph Aloba, the father of the late singer Mohbad, shared a video in which he reportedly blamed Toluwanising's wife for the rift between the singer and his father's family, and urged people not to judge family matters they do not fully understand. Many social media users strongly criticised his comments and defended the widow.

The maternal uncle speaks

In a video interview that surfaced on Friday, a maternal uncle of the singer reportedly disputed the paternal family's account of his upbringing. He said Toluwanising's mother died when he was nine months old and that her family raised him, and claimed his father was absent for many years. He credited the widow with supporting the singer financially when he had little, and blamed an online interviewer for inflaming tensions. These claims have not been independently verified, and the paternal family has given a different account.

What happens next

No burial date has been announced, and fans are calling on both sides to resolve the matter privately so the singer can be laid to rest.

  • Nancy Umeh says the widow has the right to decide on the burial.
  • Yomi Fabiyi urged the widow to let the family decide.

Tems opens up about living with ADHD

Grammy-winning singer Tems has spoken about how ADHD affects her relationships, saying she finds it hard to keep in touch with people and sometimes switches topics mid-conversation.

Read more

Nigerian singer Temilade Openiyi, known as Tems, has opened up about living with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and how it affects her relationships with family and friends.

What she said

In a radio interview in Lagos, the singer said keeping in touch with people is something she finds difficult, even though she has many supportive people around her.

She explained that ADHD sometimes makes her switch topics suddenly during conversations, and that she often forgets to reach out to people. She admitted she has not always been consistent in communicating with people who matter to her, but said she is trying.

Background

Tems leads the Nigerian nominees for the 2027 Grammy Awards with four nominations.

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has reportedly called out Afrobeats star Davido after a photo of the singer with Seyi Tinubu, son of President Bola Tinubu, circulated online.

The photo

The picture showed Davido and Seyi Tinubu raising their hands in a gesture VDM interpreted as the APC's "4+4" slogan, which supporters use to call for a second term for President Tinubu in 2027.

What VDM said

VDM said the photo was significant given Davido's status and his previous statements that he would never support the All Progressives Congress (APC). He accused the singer of contradicting himself and questioned why he would pose in a picture that could be read as a political statement.

He also said that his past claims always end up being proved right, dismissing what he called propaganda against him.

Background

Davido had not responded to VDM's comments at the time of the reports.

Enugu businessman Festus Oshaba-Onuh reportedly dies in road crash

Billionaire businessman Chief Festus Oshaba-Onuh, CEO of Ferotex Group, has reportedly died in a crash on the Enugu-Port Harcourt Expressway. The business community in Enugu is in mourning.

Read more

The business community in Enugu State has been thrown into mourning following reports that billionaire businessman Chief Festus Oshaba-Onuh has died in a road accident.

What happened

Oshaba-Onuh, the chief executive of Ferotex Group and popularly known as Ferotex, reportedly died on the morning of Thursday, 19 November 2026, in a crash on the Enugu-Port Harcourt Expressway. He was said to be travelling back to Enugu at the time.

How the news spread

News of his death emerged after an Enugu-based social media influencer shared a video of the crash scene, showing a damaged SUV said to have been involved. The influencer described the death as a major loss to Enugu State and beyond.

No family statement yet

His family had not issued a public statement at the time of the reports.
