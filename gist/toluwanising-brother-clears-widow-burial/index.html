Toluwanising's younger brother defends widow as burial dispute rages

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The bitter dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried has taken a new turn, after his younger brother reportedly spoke out in defence of the singer's widow.

What the brother said

In a voice note said to be his, reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, the brother said he wanted to clear the air because people were blaming the wrong person. He insisted that the widow had no role in the dispute and should be left alone. He blamed the singer's maternal relatives for turning him against his father's side of the family from childhood, and said their father had supported the singer financially during his school years. He also described a disagreement in which the singer's grandmother asked him for a Sallah ram, which he declined because he is a Christian, leaving some relatives upset.

Others weigh in

Media personality Nancy Umeh reportedly criticised the singer's family and said the widow has every right to decide where her husband is buried. Actor Yomi Fabiyi, however, reportedly urged the widow to allow the family to decide. On social media, many fans praised the brother for speaking up and said the widow had been unfairly attacked.

Background

Toluwanising died suddenly on 14 October 2026. Since then, members of his family have disagreed publicly about his burial. A family spokesman said in a viral video this week that relatives would remove his body if he was buried in a cemetery, and insisted he be laid beside his mother in the family compound. His widow had reportedly objected to a burial at the house they shared in Ibadan.

Mohbad's father weighs in

On Friday, Joseph Aloba, the father of the late singer Mohbad, shared a video in which he reportedly blamed Toluwanising's wife for the rift between the singer and his father's family, and urged people not to judge family matters they do not fully understand. Many social media users strongly criticised his comments and defended the widow.

The maternal uncle speaks

In a video interview that surfaced on Friday, a maternal uncle of the singer reportedly disputed the paternal family's account of his upbringing. He said Toluwanising's mother died when he was nine months old and that her family raised him, and claimed his father was absent for many years. He credited the widow with supporting the singer financially when he had little, and blamed an online interviewer for inflaming tensions. These claims have not been independently verified, and the paternal family has given a different account.

What happens next

No burial date has been announced, and fans are calling on both sides to resolve the matter privately so the singer can be laid to rest.

  • Nancy Umeh says the widow has the right to decide on the burial.
  • Yomi Fabiyi urged the widow to let the family decide.

Street-pop singer Portable has stirred debate online after claiming he received ₦100 million following his involvement in campaigns for the All Progressives Congress (APC).

What he said

In a post shared on Friday, 13 November 2026, the singer, whose real name is Habeeb Okikiola, said he simply woke up and found ₦100 million had been paid to him. Tems to lead soundtrack for Hollywood film Children of Blood and Bone

Tems is executive music producer of the soundtrack for the Hollywood adaptation of Tomi Adeyemi's novel 'Children of Blood and Bone', featuring Burna Boy, Ayra Starr, Fireboy DML, BNXN and others. The album is out on 20 November.

Read more

Grammy-winning singer Tems has been named executive music producer of the soundtrack for the Hollywood film 'Children of Blood and Bone'.

The soundtrack

The announcement was made on Instagram on Thursday, 12 November 2026. The album brings together several of Africa's biggest stars, including Burna Boy, Ayra Starr, Fireboy DML, BNXN and Kold AF. Tems described the project as a profound honour and said she was excited for fans to hear what she had put together. The soundtrack is due for release on 20 November, ahead of the film's cinema debut.

The film

'Children of Blood and Bone' is directed by Gina Prince-Bythewood and adapted from the bestselling fantasy novel by Nigerian-American author Tomi Adeyemi.

Background

Tems has become one of the most successful Nigerian artists internationally, winning Grammy Awards and earning an Oscar nomination for co-writing 'Lift Me Up' for 'Black Panther: Wakanda Forever'.

Davido has signed on as the face of PUBG Mobile's new campaign across Africa, taking his brand into the continent's fast-growing gaming market.

The deal

The singer announced the partnership on Instagram on Thursday, 12 November 2026, sharing a promotional video in which he tells fans he is joining the battle royale game and that they do not need an expensive phone to play. He also invited fans to unlock an exclusive in-game 'King Set' outfit inspired by him.

The game

PUBG Mobile Flash, which launched on Friday, 13 November, is a lighter edition of the popular shooting game with a smaller download size, designed to run on a wider range of phones while keeping the main maps and gameplay. The campaign is aimed at expanding the game's audience in Africa, where many players use budget smartphones.

Background

Davido is one of Africa's biggest music stars and has a long list of brand partnerships. He has been in Tanzania this week, where he shot a project with Tanzanian star Diamond Platnumz.
