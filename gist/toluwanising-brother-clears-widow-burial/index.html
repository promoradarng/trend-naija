Toluwanising's younger brother defends widow as burial dispute rages

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The bitter dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried has taken a new turn, after his younger brother reportedly spoke out in defence of the singer's widow.

What the brother said

In a voice note said to be his, reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, the brother said he wanted to clear the air because people were blaming the wrong person. He insisted that the widow had no role in the dispute and should be left alone. He blamed the singer's maternal relatives for turning him against his father's side of the family from childhood, and said their father had supported the singer financially during his school years. He also described a disagreement in which the singer's grandmother asked him for a Sallah ram, which he declined because he is a Christian, leaving some relatives upset.

Others weigh in

Media personality Nancy Umeh reportedly criticised the singer's family and said the widow has every right to decide where her husband is buried. Actor Yomi Fabiyi, however, reportedly urged the widow to allow the family to decide. On social media, many fans praised the brother for speaking up and said the widow had been unfairly attacked.

Background

Toluwanising died suddenly on 14 October 2026. Since then, members of his family have disagreed publicly about his burial. A family spokesman said in a viral video this week that relatives would remove his body if he was buried in a cemetery, and insisted he be laid beside his mother in the family compound. His widow had reportedly objected to a burial at the house they shared in Ibadan.

Mohbad's father weighs in

On Friday, Joseph Aloba, the father of the late singer Mohbad, shared a video in which he reportedly blamed Toluwanising's wife for the rift between the singer and his father's family, and urged people not to judge family matters they do not fully understand. Many social media users strongly criticised his comments and defended the widow.

The maternal uncle speaks

In a video interview that surfaced on Friday, a maternal uncle of the singer reportedly disputed the paternal family's account of his upbringing. He said Toluwanising's mother died when he was nine months old and that her family raised him, and claimed his father was absent for many years. He credited the widow with supporting the singer financially when he had little, and blamed an online interviewer for inflaming tensions. These claims have not been independently verified, and the paternal family has given a different account.

What happens next

No burial date has been announced, and fans are calling on both sides to resolve the matter privately so the singer can be laid to rest.

  • Nancy Umeh says the widow has the right to decide on the burial.
  • Yomi Fabiyi urged the widow to let the family decide.

NDC boycotts IGP's security meeting, says its members are not safe

24 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Nigeria Democratic Congress has reportedly refused to attend a security meeting called by the Inspector-General of Police, accusing security agencies of failing to protect its members from attacks. It blamed thugs it said were loyal to the APC.

Read more

The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has announced that it will boycott a security meeting convened by the Inspector-General of Police, citing attacks on its members ahead of the 2027 elections.

The party's reasons

In a statement on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, signed by its National Publicity Secretary, the party said it no longer felt safe under the Nigeria Police Force and would not lend legitimacy to a security process that had failed to protect its members.

The NDC accused security agencies of failing to prevent repeated attacks it alleged were carried out by thugs loyal to the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC). It pointed to incidents in Benue, Edo, Cross River, Enugu and Delta states, including what it described as an attempt to stop its presidential candidate, Peter Obi, from entering Benue State.

The Delta attack

The boycott follows Monday's deadly attack on the campaign team of the party's Delta Central senatorial candidate, Ovie Omo-Agege, in Ughelli North. The party alleged that security personnel were complicit. Police have since invited Omo-Agege and others for questioning as part of their investigation.

The other side

No response from the APC or the police to the NDC's allegations was included in the reports.

Court voids APC's Bayelsa West senatorial candidacy

24 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly voided Fidelis Agbiki's candidacy as the APC's Bayelsa West senatorial candidate. The court upheld claims that his name was missing from the party's membership register.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly nullified the candidacy of High Chief Fidelis Agbiki, the All Progressives Congress (APC) senatorial candidate for Bayelsa West in the 2027 elections.

The case

The petitioners raised two main objections to his candidacy. First, they argued that his name did not appear in the APC membership register submitted to INEC, meaning he was not a recognised member of the party. Second, they claimed he did not resign from a political appointment before taking part in the senatorial primary.

The ruling

Justice Mohammed Garba Umar upheld the petition, finding that the primary breached the Electoral Act.

Background

The judgment came on the same day a Federal High Court in Warri voided the NDC candidacy of former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege for Delta Central on similar grounds relating to membership registers. Pre-election court cases are common in Nigeria, and both rulings can be appealed.
