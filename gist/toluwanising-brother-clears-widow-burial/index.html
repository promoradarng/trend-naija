Toluwanising's younger brother defends widow as burial dispute rages

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The bitter dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried has taken a new turn, after his younger brother reportedly spoke out in defence of the singer's widow.

What the brother said

In a voice note said to be his, reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, the brother said he wanted to clear the air because people were blaming the wrong person. He insisted that the widow had no role in the dispute and should be left alone. He blamed the singer's maternal relatives for turning him against his father's side of the family from childhood, and said their father had supported the singer financially during his school years. He also described a disagreement in which the singer's grandmother asked him for a Sallah ram, which he declined because he is a Christian, leaving some relatives upset.

Others weigh in

Media personality Nancy Umeh reportedly criticised the singer's family and said the widow has every right to decide where her husband is buried. Actor Yomi Fabiyi, however, reportedly urged the widow to allow the family to decide. On social media, many fans praised the brother for speaking up and said the widow had been unfairly attacked.

Background

Toluwanising died suddenly on 14 October 2026. Since then, members of his family have disagreed publicly about his burial. A family spokesman said in a viral video this week that relatives would remove his body if he was buried in a cemetery, and insisted he be laid beside his mother in the family compound. His widow had reportedly objected to a burial at the house they shared in Ibadan.

Mohbad's father weighs in

On Friday, Joseph Aloba, the father of the late singer Mohbad, shared a video in which he reportedly blamed Toluwanising's wife for the rift between the singer and his father's family, and urged people not to judge family matters they do not fully understand. Many social media users strongly criticised his comments and defended the widow.

The maternal uncle speaks

In a video interview that surfaced on Friday, a maternal uncle of the singer reportedly disputed the paternal family's account of his upbringing. He said Toluwanising's mother died when he was nine months old and that her family raised him, and claimed his father was absent for many years. He credited the widow with supporting the singer financially when he had little, and blamed an online interviewer for inflaming tensions. These claims have not been independently verified, and the paternal family has given a different account.

What happens next

No burial date has been announced, and fans are calling on both sides to resolve the matter privately so the singer can be laid to rest.

  • The brother says the widow had no role in the dispute.
  • Nancy Umeh says the widow has the right to decide on the burial.
  • Yomi Fabiyi urged the widow to let the family decide.

VeryDarkMan warns Tinubu over deadly attack on NDC campaign in Delta

24 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Activist VeryDarkMan has issued a strong warning to President Tinubu after gunmen killed four people, including NDC youth leader Efemena Umukoro, at Omo-Agege's campaign event in Delta. He said political violence could spiral out of control.

Read more

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has issued a fierce public warning to President Bola Tinubu following the deadly attack on an NDC campaign event in Uduere, Ughelli North, Delta State.

What he said

In a video posted on Instagram, VDM told the president that he would burn Nigeria down, arguing that targeting opposition figures with gunfire is a dangerous political strategy that could spiral far beyond anyone's control. He warned that even people close to the president would not be safe if the cycle of violence continued, and drew attention to the fact that the attack happened in Delta State.

The attack

Gunmen opened fire on former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege's campaign team on Monday, 23 November 2026, while he was addressing supporters. Four people were killed, according to his campaign, including Efemena Umukoro, an Urhobo youth leader popularly known as General Akima. Omo-Agege was unhurt.

Other reactions

Other social media commentators also condemned the killings, and Delta State Governor Sheriff Oborevwori called for everyone involved to be arrested regardless of their party. The Inspector-General of Police has ordered an investigation.

The other side

No response from the presidency or the APC to VDM's remarks was included in the reports.

Iran reportedly gives singer Parastoo Ahmadi 74 lashes for performing without hijab

24 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Iranian authorities have reportedly flogged singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her team over an online concert she performed without a headscarf. Rights groups say they also received two-year bans on artistic work.

Read more

Iranian authorities have reportedly administered 74 lashes to the singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her production team over a concert she performed without wearing the mandatory Islamic headscarf.

What happened

According to a human rights lawyer and monitoring groups, the punishment was carried out on Monday, 23 November 2026, at the prosecutor's office in the city of Qom.

The concert

The case stems from a virtual concert Ahmadi recorded in December 2024 at a historic caravanserai in Qom and streamed on YouTube. In the video, which was widely viewed, she performed traditional Persian music with male musicians while wearing a sleeveless dress with her hair uncovered, defying rules that ban women from singing solo in public and appearing without a hijab.

The sentence

A court found Ahmadi and four male musicians and crew members guilty of offending public decency by publishing what it called immoral content online. As well as the lashes, each of the five was reportedly given a two-year ban on artistic activity.
