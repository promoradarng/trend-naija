Toluwanising's younger brother defends widow as burial dispute rages

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The bitter dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried has taken a new turn, after his younger brother reportedly spoke out in defence of the singer's widow.

What the brother said

In a voice note said to be his, reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, the brother said he wanted to clear the air because people were blaming the wrong person. He insisted that the widow had no role in the dispute and should be left alone. He blamed the singer's maternal relatives for turning him against his father's side of the family from childhood, and said their father had supported the singer financially during his school years. He also described a disagreement in which the singer's grandmother asked him for a Sallah ram, which he declined because he is a Christian, leaving some relatives upset.

Others weigh in

Media personality Nancy Umeh reportedly criticised the singer's family and said the widow has every right to decide where her husband is buried. Actor Yomi Fabiyi, however, reportedly urged the widow to allow the family to decide. On social media, many fans praised the brother for speaking up and said the widow had been unfairly attacked.

Background

Toluwanising died suddenly on 14 October 2026. Since then, members of his family have disagreed publicly about his burial. A family spokesman said in a viral video this week that relatives would remove his body if he was buried in a cemetery, and insisted he be laid beside his mother in the family compound. His widow had reportedly objected to a burial at the house they shared in Ibadan.

Mohbad's father weighs in

On Friday, Joseph Aloba, the father of the late singer Mohbad, shared a video in which he reportedly blamed Toluwanising's wife for the rift between the singer and his father's family, and urged people not to judge family matters they do not fully understand. Many social media users strongly criticised his comments and defended the widow.

The maternal uncle speaks

In a video interview that surfaced on Friday, a maternal uncle of the singer reportedly disputed the paternal family's account of his upbringing. He said Toluwanising's mother died when he was nine months old and that her family raised him, and claimed his father was absent for many years. He credited the widow with supporting the singer financially when he had little, and blamed an online interviewer for inflaming tensions. These claims have not been independently verified, and the paternal family has given a different account.

What happens next

No burial date has been announced, and fans are calling on both sides to resolve the matter privately so the singer can be laid to rest.

  • Nancy Umeh says the widow has the right to decide on the burial.
  • Yomi Fabiyi urged the widow to let the family decide.

Skales says Burna Boy once asked Davido for space on his private jet

4 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Singer Skales says Burna Boy once asked Davido to let them travel on his private jet to a show in Yola where they performed their song 'Temper'. He said the jet was so full that one person sat on the toilet seat.

Read more

Singer Skales has shared a story from the early days of his career involving two of Nigeria's biggest stars, Burna Boy and Davido.

What he said

In comments that spread on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Skales said he and Burna Boy were booked to perform their collaboration 'Temper' at a show in Yola. According to him, Burna Boy contacted Davido and asked if they could travel on his private jet, and Davido agreed. Because the plane was already full, Davido reportedly asked one of his team members to stay behind and fly the next day. Skales said the jet was still so crowded that one person had to sit on the toilet seat during the flight.

A lucky break

Skales reportedly said the Yola trip led to an important connection for him with the international act Major Lazer, which helped his career.

Background

'Temper', released in 2014, was one of Skales's biggest hits and featured Burna Boy. Burna Boy and Davido have had a complicated relationship over the years, with periods of rivalry among their fans, although they have also shown respect for each other in public. Both are now global stars, with Burna Boy winning a Grammy in 2021 and Davido receiving multiple Grammy nominations.

How people reacted

Fans enjoyed the throwback, with some joking about the toilet seat and others saying it showed that the stars once helped each other.

American hip-hop artist Rayven Tyler has sparked conversation about relationships after sharing her experience of dating women and admitting that she now understands some of the challenges men face.

According to the artist, her personal experiences have given her a different perspective on dating, and made her appreciate what many men go through when trying to keep their relationships going.

For years, men have complained about the pressures they face in relationships, from managing emotional expectations to dealing with misunderstandings and trying to keep their partners happy.

Tyler's statement suggests that her own dating experiences have helped her see some of those complaints from a different angle. What makes her comments particularly interesting is that she is speaking from her own experience of dating women, a perspective that has drawn plenty of attention.

Her remarks have opened a wider discussion about emotional expectations, communication and the difficulties that sometimes come with understanding a partner.

Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly removed the David Mark-led caretaker leadership of the ADC and nullified its decisions, including the nomination of Atiku Abubakar as presidential candidate. The ruling came a day after Atiku's Sokoto rally.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign. Neither Atiku nor the party had responded publicly at the time of reporting.
