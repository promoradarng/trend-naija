Armed man arrested near White House after allegedly saying voices told him to kill Trump

7 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The US Secret Service has arrested a man near the White House after he allegedly told agents that voices in his head had instructed him to travel to Washington and kill President Donald Trump.

What happened

According to US officials, agents spotted the man behaving suspiciously in a car parked beside the White House on Thursday, 5 November 2026. During questioning, he allegedly said voices had directed him to come to the capital and assassinate the President. Agents found that the car had been reported stolen, and a search turned up a rifle, two handguns and dozens of rounds of ammunition.

The suspect

The Department of Homeland Security said the man is a Venezuelan national who entered the United States illegally in 2024. He has been charged with several firearms offences, as well as unauthorised use of a vehicle and receiving stolen property, and is being held by the US Marshals Service. He has not yet been tried.

Secret Service comments

Secret Service Director Sean Curran said the arrest showed the kind of security environment his agents deal with every day. Officials said the White House has seen a significant rise in incidents linked to mental health problems, as the presidency often becomes a focus for people experiencing such conditions.

Background

Trump survived two assassination attempts during the 2024 election campaign. The arrest came in the same week that the President's party suffered heavy losses in the midterm elections and he named Stephen Miller as his new chief of staff.

Hackers claim leaked Russian records show up to 450,000 war deaths in Ukraine

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Two hacking groups say records taken from Russia's state statistics agency show between about 394,000 and 450,000 Russian deaths linked to the war in Ukraine. The figures have not been verified and the agency denies any data was leaked.

Read more

Two hacking groups say they have obtained records from Russia's state statistics agency pointing to hundreds of thousands of Russian deaths in the war in Ukraine.

The claims

According to reports on Monday, 9 November 2026, the pro-Ukrainian group Silent Crow and the Belarusian Cyber Partisans said they accessed an internal database at Rosstat containing information from death certificates from February 2022 to August 2026. Silent Crow, which published the data, put the death toll at about 450,100, while the Belarusian Cyber Partisans, using a narrower method that counted only records naming military operations as the cause of death, put it at 393,881. The figures have not been independently verified.

Russia's response

Rosstat acknowledged that its systems were targeted between 29 October and 1 November but said no databases were compromised. Russia has banned both groups as 'extremist organisations'. Moscow has rarely given official figures for its military losses.

Background

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. Independent estimates of Russian military deaths have varied widely, and both sides have been accused of under-reporting their own losses. The Belarusian Cyber Partisans also oppose the government of Belarusian President Alexander Lukashenko, a close ally of Moscow.

Gunman in Krakow Main Square shooting that killed six dies in hospital

9 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The 24-year-old suspected gunman in a shooting in Krakow's Main Square that killed six people and injured about 16 has died in hospital, Polish police say. Three of his relatives were also found dead at his home.

Read more

The suspected gunman in a deadly shooting in the centre of Krakow, Poland, has died in hospital, police say.

What happened

The shooting took place in the city's Main Square on the afternoon of Saturday, 7 November 2026. Six people, four women and two men, were killed and about 16 others were injured, including visitors from the United Kingdom, Portugal, Russia, Germany and Austria. Police said the 24-year-old suspect was arrested on a nearby street after trying to escape in a car, and that two officers were injured in an exchange of fire. Three more bodies were found at his home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 150 kilometres from Krakow, and he is believed to have also killed three members of his own family.

The suspect dies

In an update on Monday, 9 November, regional police confirmed that the suspect had died in hospital that morning. He is believed to have used an AR-15-style rifle, and ammunition was recovered from his car. Authorities have not given a clear motive, but said he was fascinated by far-right ideologies.

Background

Mass shootings are rare in Poland, which has strict gun laws.

Trump admits immigration crackdown was 'too tough' after midterm defeat

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

President Donald Trump has said his administration's immigration crackdown was too tough and hurt Republicans in the midterm elections, particularly the deportation of people with stable jobs whose only offence was breaking immigration rules.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has acknowledged that his administration's immigration crackdown went too far, in a notable change of tone after his party's heavy losses in the midterm elections.

What he said

In an interview broadcast by the CBS programme '60 Minutes' on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Trump defended his record on border security and on removing dangerous criminals, but said he thought the crackdown had been too tough. He said the administration had suffered politically by deporting people who had stable jobs and had only committed immigration violations, and that this had hurt Republican candidates at the ballot box.

The election

Exit polls showed immigration was the third most important issue for voters, after the economy and the state of democracy. In traditionally conservative states such as Texas, a shift among Latino voters towards the Democrats contributed to their gains in Congress, where they are projected to control both the House of Representatives and the Senate.

Background

Mass deportations became a defining policy of Trump's second term, drawing protests and legal challenges. Only days ago he promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of the deportation programme, to White House chief of staff, so it is unclear how far policy will change in practice. Groups of Nigerians have been among those deported, including six who arrived in Lagos last week.

Another Ghanaian found dead in USA as body of 24-year-old church musician Joseph Owusu is discovered dumped near trash can in New York

9 Nov 2026, 06:11 GMT

The body of Joseph Owusu, a 24-year-old Ghanaian church organist from the Bronx, was found in a large black bag left near a trash can on a Brooklyn sidewalk. CCTV footage shows two people taking the bag from a white car. Ghana's government has demanded justice.

Read more

The Ghanaian community in the United States has been thrown into mourning again following the disturbing discovery of the body of 24-year-old Joseph Owusu, a talented church musician who lived in the Bronx, New York.

Found in a bag on the sidewalk

Owusu's body was discovered inside a large black bag left near a trash can on a sidewalk in Brooklyn on 30 October 2026.

CCTV footage shows two unidentified people taking the bag out of a white car, leaving it by the roadside and driving away.

Who was Joseph Owusu?

The young man was the organist at the Ghana United Methodist Church in the Bronx. He was also studying computer science and worked as a caregiver for people living with disabilities.

A family demands answers

His devastated family is demanding justice as investigators work to establish how he died and who abandoned his body. Ghana's embassy in the United States has confirmed his death, and the Ghanaian government has called on US authorities to ensure those responsible are found and brought to justice.

Two young lives in days

The tragedy came just a day after another young Ghanaian, 15-year-old Javits Frimpong, was shot dead while walking to school in the Bronx.

Two young Ghanaians, two heartbreaking deaths in New York within days. Both families are now waiting for answers.
