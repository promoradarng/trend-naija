Armed man arrested near White House after allegedly saying voices told him to kill Trump

7 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The US Secret Service has arrested a man near the White House after he allegedly told agents that voices in his head had instructed him to travel to Washington and kill President Donald Trump.

What happened

According to US officials, agents spotted the man behaving suspiciously in a car parked beside the White House on Thursday, 5 November 2026. During questioning, he allegedly said voices had directed him to come to the capital and assassinate the President. Agents found that the car had been reported stolen, and a search turned up a rifle, two handguns and dozens of rounds of ammunition.

The suspect

The Department of Homeland Security said the man is a Venezuelan national who entered the United States illegally in 2024. He has been charged with several firearms offences, as well as unauthorised use of a vehicle and receiving stolen property, and is being held by the US Marshals Service. He has not yet been tried.

Secret Service comments

Secret Service Director Sean Curran said the arrest showed the kind of security environment his agents deal with every day. Officials said the White House has seen a significant rise in incidents linked to mental health problems, as the presidency often becomes a focus for people experiencing such conditions.

Background

Trump survived two assassination attempts during the 2024 election campaign. The arrest came in the same week that the President's party suffered heavy losses in the midterm elections and he named Stephen Miller as his new chief of staff.

  • The suspect allegedly said voices told him to kill Trump.
  • He faces firearms and stolen vehicle charges.

Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as chief executive

23 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nvidia's board has unanimously voted to bring back Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and board member, weeks after he stepped down amid a controversy. The board said US government input assured it his return posed no national security risk.

Read more

Nvidia's board of directors has reinstated Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and a member of the board with immediate effect, the company announced on Monday, 23 November 2026.

The decision

The board said it voted unanimously after a series of discussions with Huang. It said it had received input from senior levels of the United States government and, based on that, was confident that his return posed no national security risk.

The board chair said Huang was the right person to lead the company and that his return was in the best interests of shareholders, employees, partners and customers.

Background

Huang co-founded Nvidia and built it into one of the world's most valuable companies on the back of demand for its AI chips. He stepped down earlier this month amid a controversy over his personal conduct.

Tyson Fury reveals he was diagnosed with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder

23 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Former heavyweight champion Tyson Fury has reportedly revealed he was diagnosed with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder after a severe mental health crisis. He speaks about it in an upcoming Netflix documentary.

Read more

Former world heavyweight boxing champion Tyson Fury has reportedly revealed that he was diagnosed with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder after a severe mental health crisis.

The documentary

The 38-year-old British boxer opens up about his struggles in an upcoming Netflix documentary titled Fury, which looks at his life, career and personal battles. It features interviews with Fury, his wife Paris and his father John.

His crisis

Fury and his wife recalled how, after he was hospitalised with a suspected heart attack in June 2016, he began hearing threatening voices. At the time he had sunk into depression and was struggling with drugs and alcohol. He described that period as an indescribable darkness and said he feared he would end up locked away.

Background

Fury has previously spoken publicly about his battle with bipolar disorder.

Zelensky says Ukrainian sea drones sank a Russian warship in Libya

23 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Ukraine says its naval drones sank a Russian corvette docked in Benghazi, Libya, and hit a Russian cargo ship off North Africa. President Zelensky released video of the operation on Monday.

Read more

Ukrainian naval drones have reportedly sunk a modern Russian warship docked in the eastern Libyan port of Benghazi and struck a Russian cargo ship off the North African coast, President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Monday, 23 November 2026.

The operation

In a statement on Telegram, released alongside video footage, Zelensky said Ukrainian forces used seven domestically made unmanned surface vessels to attack the corvette Merkury. The ship, commissioned in May 2023 and built with radar-evading features, caught fire and sank, and a combat helicopter on board was destroyed. Satellite images later reportedly showed the vessel capsized and mostly underwater beside the pier.

Zelensky described it as an exemplary operation in response to Russia's war and thanked Ukraine's Security Service and defence forces, saying the targets set had been achieved.

Why it matters

The strike marks a major expansion of Ukraine's attacks at sea, far beyond the Black Sea, where its drones have previously targeted Russian ships.

Russia's response

No response from Moscow was included in the initial reports.
