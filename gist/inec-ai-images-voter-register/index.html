INEC under fire over alleged AI-generated images in voter register as concerns grow over Nigeria's democracy

27 Oct 2026, 14:19 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the credibility of Nigeria's Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) following allegations that AI-generated images were found in the preliminary voter register, raising serious questions about the integrity of the country's electoral system.

The claims surfaced after INEC uploaded the preliminary register online, with users sharing screenshots of entries from several polling units in Edo State whose photographs they say look artificially generated. Some users have also alleged duplicate entries with the same photograph under different voter numbers. Economist and politician Pat Utomi has publicly asked INEC to explain the images. INEC had not publicly responded to the specific allegations at the time of writing.

The allegations have sparked concerns about the effectiveness of INEC's verification procedures, transparency and accountability, particularly as Nigeria prepares for future elections.

For an institution entrusted with protecting the voting rights of millions of Nigerians, the possibility of AI-generated photographs appearing in an official electoral database is a matter that deserves urgent investigation.

If confirmed, such irregularities could expose serious weaknesses in the processes used to register voters, authenticate their identities and maintain accurate electoral records.

How can Nigerians trust an electoral system if the authenticity of the photographs and identities contained in its register cannot be guaranteed?

INEC has a constitutional responsibility to conduct credible elections and maintain a reliable register of eligible voters. Any failure to detect manipulated images or questionable registrations could further weaken public confidence in an institution that has faced criticism over previous electoral exercises.

The growing sophistication of artificial intelligence also presents new challenges for electoral authorities worldwide. Images that appear genuine can now be generated or manipulated using readily available technology, making strong identity verification procedures increasingly important.

However, the existence of suspicious images alone would not establish electoral fraud. A proper investigation would need to determine whether the photographs were genuinely AI-generated, how they entered the register and whether they were connected to valid voter records.

Nigerians deserve clear answers from INEC. The commission should establish the facts, explain its verification procedures and disclose what safeguards are in place to prevent fraudulent registrations and manipulation of electoral information.

Where wrongdoing is established, those responsible should be identified and held accountable under the law.

Nigeria cannot afford another election overshadowed by doubts about the credibility of the electoral process. Democracy depends on public confidence that every legitimate vote will count and that electoral institutions can protect the integrity of the process.

With the 2027 general elections approaching, INEC must demonstrate that its systems are secure, its records are reliable and its operations are open to independent scrutiny.

The credibility of Nigeria's democracy is too important to be left to assumptions, political assurances or unanswered allegations.

The question Nigerians are asking is simple: if INEC cannot guarantee the authenticity of the identities in its electoral register, how can citizens be confident that it will deliver free, fair and credible elections in 2027?

South-East governors urge region to back Tinubu at APC women's rally in Enugu

1 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

The governors of Enugu, Imo and Ebonyi, and a representative of Anambra's governor, have reportedly urged South-East voters to support President Tinubu's re-election at an APC women's rally in Enugu. The call sets up a direct contest with Peter Obi in his home region.

Read more

Four South-East state governments have thrown their weight behind President Bola Tinubu's re-election bid, in a show of support in a region widely seen as Peter Obi's stronghold.

What happened

At an APC Women Mega Rally and Empowerment Programme for the South-East at Okpara Square, Enugu, on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the governors reportedly urged residents to back the President and APC candidates at all levels in the January 2027 elections.

What the governors said

Enugu Governor Peter Mbah said the region should build on the government's economic reforms rather than change course, comparing it to a sailor who does not abandon his ship mid-sea. He also described an empowerment programme for 5,000 women across the five South-East states. Imo Governor Hope Uzodimma said Igbo people were now seeing real inclusion and development under Tinubu, and that improved federal allocations had helped his state pay salaries and pensions regularly. Ebonyi Governor Francis Nwifuru praised the APC's skills programmes and denied links to any coalition outside the party. Anambra Governor Charles Soludo was represented by his deputy, Gilbert Ibezim, who said the region should repay the administration with votes. Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu also spoke, noting that no Igbo person had been president in six decades and praising the South East Development Commission.

Background

Peter Obi, the NDC presidential candidate and a former Anambra governor, won most of the South East in the 2023 presidential election. He has said he would serve only one term if elected, while some APC figures argue the region should aim for a longer stay in power through alliances. Obi had not responded to the rally at the time of the report.

What happens next

Both the APC and the NDC are expected to intensify their campaigns in the South East, which is likely to be one of the most closely watched regions in the election.

Pro-Tinubu group warns APC that Imo West row could cost it South East support

31 Oct 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Renewed Hope Foot Soldiers for Tinubu has warned that the fight over the APC's Imo West Senate ticket could weaken the party in the South East. The group wants Governor Hope Uzodimma to be allowed to pursue his ambitions and called for dialogue, after a court recognised Rochas Okorocha as the candidate.

Read more

A pro-Tinubu support group has warned the All Progressives Congress (APC) that the dispute over its candidate for the Imo West Senatorial District could hurt the party in the South East ahead of 2027.

What the group said

In a statement signed by its national president, Gbenga Owojori, and reported on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the Renewed Hope Foot Soldiers for Tinubu reacted to the Federal High Court ruling that recognised former governor Rochas Okorocha, rather than Governor Hope Uzodimma, as the APC candidate for Imo West. The group argued that shutting out candidates from the South East could weaken the coalition President Bola Tinubu needs for re-election.

Its demands

The group called on party leaders to resolve the matter through inclusive dialogue and transparent party processes, and said Uzodimma should be given a fair chance to pursue his ambitions, including a possible bid for the Senate presidency. It also made claims, which it did not support with evidence, that some senior political figures were working against Uzodimma. Those named have not responded to the claims.

Background

Okorocha went to court claiming he won the APC primary on 18 May, while the party's returning officer declared Uzodimma the winner. The court ordered INEC to publish Okorocha's name, and former APC national chairman Abdullahi Ganduje later visited Okorocha to congratulate him. The case also raises the question of whether a sitting governor can contest a Senate seat while still in office. Uzodimma has not said publicly whether he will appeal.

What happens next

The dispute is expected to continue in court and within the party, with both camps seeking the backing of the national leadership.

Atiku condemns attacks on Sowore and Sokoto ADC candidate, demands probe

31 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has condemned the recent attacks on Omoyele Sowore and Sokoto ADC governorship candidate Manir Dan'iya, blaming supporters of the ruling party. He demanded transparent investigations and a guarantee of a free and fair election.

Read more

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has spoken out against a string of reported attacks on opposition politicians, accusing supporters of the ruling party of trying to intimidate rivals ahead of the 2027 elections.

What he said

In a statement on X on Saturday, 31 October 2026, Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), condemned the assault on AAC presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore in Ado-Ekiti and the attack on the convoy of Manir Muhammad Dan'iya, the ADC governorship candidate in Sokoto State. He also criticised what he said was harassment of residents in Sokoto for wearing ADC-branded clothing, and described the unexplained disappearance of some social media influencers as a warning sign.

Who he blamed

Atiku blamed loyalists and enforcers of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC), noting that the attacks happened while President Bola Tinubu was abroad on a religious pilgrimage. He described the violence as a sign of desperation and said the ADC would resist any attempt to wipe out political opposition.

His demands

He called on the federal government to order immediate and transparent investigations into the attacks on Sowore and Dan'iya, and to guarantee a free, fair and competitive environment for the 2027 elections.

Background

Sowore says he and his team were attacked at a restaurant in Ado-Ekiti on Friday; police deny he was kidnapped and have ordered an investigation. Dan'iya says his convoy was blocked and shot at near the Sokoto airport on Friday night. The APC and the Sokoto State Government had not responded to Atiku's statement at the time of the report.

"2027 election has already been rigged": journalist Seun Okinbaloye raises alarm

31 Oct 2026, 14:12 GMT

Popular TV journalist Seun Okinbaloye has reportedly claimed that the 2027 general election has already been rigged, with reports linking his warning to concerns about flaws in the voter register. The claim has reopened the debate about whether INEC can deliver a credible poll.

Read more

With Nigeria's 2027 general elections still ahead, popular television journalist Seun Okinbaloye has raised a serious concern about the credibility of the electoral process.

What he said

In a statement now circulating widely online, Okinbaloye reportedly said the 2027 election has already been rigged. Reports link his warning to concerns about flaws in the national voter register.

The allegation raises troubling questions about Nigeria's democracy and about whether citizens will be able to freely choose their leaders.

Why it matters

The claim comes at a time when public confidence in Nigeria's electoral system remains a major issue, especially after the controversies that followed previous elections.

For millions of Nigerians, the concern goes beyond which political party wins or loses. The bigger question is whether the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) can guarantee a transparent, credible and fair election in 2027.

A claim, not proof

However, the allegation should not be mistaken for established evidence that the election has been manipulated. A claim this serious needs clear facts and proper investigation.

If Nigerians are already questioning the credibility of an election that has not yet taken place, INEC and the relevant authorities have a responsibility to address those concerns before voters head to the polls.

Democracy loses its meaning when citizens begin to believe that election results are decided before voting begins.
