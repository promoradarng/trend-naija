INEC under fire over alleged AI-generated images in voter register as concerns grow over Nigeria's democracy

27 Oct 2026, 14:19 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the credibility of Nigeria's Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) following allegations that AI-generated images were found in the preliminary voter register, raising serious questions about the integrity of the country's electoral system.

The claims surfaced after INEC uploaded the preliminary register online, with users sharing screenshots of entries from several polling units in Edo State whose photographs they say look artificially generated. Some users have also alleged duplicate entries with the same photograph under different voter numbers. Economist and politician Pat Utomi has publicly asked INEC to explain the images. INEC had not publicly responded to the specific allegations at the time of writing.

The allegations have sparked concerns about the effectiveness of INEC's verification procedures, transparency and accountability, particularly as Nigeria prepares for future elections.

For an institution entrusted with protecting the voting rights of millions of Nigerians, the possibility of AI-generated photographs appearing in an official electoral database is a matter that deserves urgent investigation.

If confirmed, such irregularities could expose serious weaknesses in the processes used to register voters, authenticate their identities and maintain accurate electoral records.

How can Nigerians trust an electoral system if the authenticity of the photographs and identities contained in its register cannot be guaranteed?

INEC has a constitutional responsibility to conduct credible elections and maintain a reliable register of eligible voters. Any failure to detect manipulated images or questionable registrations could further weaken public confidence in an institution that has faced criticism over previous electoral exercises.

The growing sophistication of artificial intelligence also presents new challenges for electoral authorities worldwide. Images that appear genuine can now be generated or manipulated using readily available technology, making strong identity verification procedures increasingly important.

However, the existence of suspicious images alone would not establish electoral fraud. A proper investigation would need to determine whether the photographs were genuinely AI-generated, how they entered the register and whether they were connected to valid voter records.

Nigerians deserve clear answers from INEC. The commission should establish the facts, explain its verification procedures and disclose what safeguards are in place to prevent fraudulent registrations and manipulation of electoral information.

Where wrongdoing is established, those responsible should be identified and held accountable under the law.

Nigeria cannot afford another election overshadowed by doubts about the credibility of the electoral process. Democracy depends on public confidence that every legitimate vote will count and that electoral institutions can protect the integrity of the process.

With the 2027 general elections approaching, INEC must demonstrate that its systems are secure, its records are reliable and its operations are open to independent scrutiny.

The credibility of Nigeria's democracy is too important to be left to assumptions, political assurances or unanswered allegations.

The question Nigerians are asking is simple: if INEC cannot guarantee the authenticity of the identities in its electoral register, how can citizens be confident that it will deliver free, fair and credible elections in 2027?

Lamido rules out Tinubu, backs Sandy Onor, warns divided opposition helps President

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Former Jigawa governor Sule Lamido says he will not support President Tinubu and has named Professor Sandy Onor as his choice for 2027, while warning that rivalry between Atiku and Obi is handing Tinubu an advantage.

Read more

Former Jigawa State governor Sule Lamido has declared that he will not back President Bola Tinubu's re-election and has named Professor Sandy Onor as his preferred presidential candidate.

His choice

Speaking in Dutse, Lamido reaffirmed his loyalty to the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) and named Onor outright as his candidate for the 16 January 2027 election, making a clear public break from the incumbent. Earlier this week, he denied a claim by Vice-President Kashim Shettima that he supported the APC.

Warning to the opposition

In a television interview on Friday, 13 November 2026, Lamido said the rivalry between Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi was working in Tinubu's favour. He pointed to the large number of serving and former governors in the APC and the failure of the two main opposition figures to cooperate, arguing that a joint ticket, with Atiku as president and Obi as running mate, would have posed a much stronger challenge. He also warned that whoever wins, Nigeria would lose if campaigns ignore national issues.

Background

Atiku is the ADC candidate and Obi the NDC candidate. Efforts to agree a single opposition candidate have repeatedly failed, though PRP candidate Donald Duke and APM candidate Seyi Makinde have both said they are open to collaboration.

Lalong, Dariye and other Plateau leaders endorse Tinubu for 2027

13 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A coalition of Plateau State political leaders, including former governors Simon Lalong and Joshua Dariye, has endorsed President Tinubu and other APC candidates for 2027 at a meeting in Jos.

Read more

Prominent political figures from Plateau State have endorsed President Bola Tinubu's re-election bid for 2027.

The meeting

The endorsement was made by voice vote at a meeting in Jos organised by Arewa for Asiwaju (A4A), a pro-Tinubu group, according to a statement released on Friday, 13 November 2026. Those present included former governors, ministers, senators, members of the House of Representatives, commissioners and council chairmen, who also adopted other All Progressives Congress (APC) candidates in the state.

What the leaders said

Former governor Simon Lalong, who chaired the meeting as deputy chairman of A4A's board of trustees, said those present believed the President was committed to Nigeria's development and that keeping him in office would speed up economic growth. Former governor Joshua Dariye credited the Tinubu administration with meaningful contributions to Plateau and the country.

Background

Plateau is a key battleground in the North Central. Peter Obi won the state in the 2023 presidential election. A Middle Belt youth group that backed Obi in 2023 also recently switched its support to Tinubu. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

ECOWAS names ex-Ghana president Akufo-Addo to lead observers for Nigeria's 2027 polls

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

ECOWAS has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Akufo-Addo to head its election observation mission for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections on 16 January 2027.

Read more

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to lead its election observation mission to Nigeria.

The appointment

The appointment was announced in a statement from Akufo-Addo's office on Thursday, 12 November 2026. He will oversee the deployment of ECOWAS observers for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections, scheduled for 16 January 2027. The mission will monitor the electoral process and report on how the polls are conducted.

Who is Akufo-Addo

Akufo-Addo served two terms as Ghana's president, from 2017 to January 2025.

Background

International attention on Nigeria's election is growing. The European Union has raised concerns about violence and hate speech, and a senior US State Department official told Congress this week that the vote could spur violence. INEC says preparations are on track, with non-sensitive materials already being moved to the states.

A senior US State Department official has told a congressional hearing that Nigeria's presidential election in January could spur violence, as a US lawmaker urged Washington to pay close attention to the vote.

Read more

The United States says it will keep a close watch on Nigeria's 2027 presidential election, with a senior official warning that it could trigger violence.

What was said

Frank Garcia, the Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs, made the remarks on Thursday, 12 November 2026, in prepared testimony before the House Foreign Affairs Subcommittee on Africa during a hearing on violence in Nigeria. He said he was realistic about the challenges ahead and that the January presidential election could spur violence. He also pointed to an overstretched security system, limited reach of the courts in the Middle Belt and a lack of resources as obstacles to tackling insecurity.

Congress weighs in

Congressman Riley Moore used the hearing to call for close American attention to how the election unfolds.
