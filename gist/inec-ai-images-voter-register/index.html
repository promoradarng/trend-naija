INEC under fire over alleged AI-generated images in voter register as concerns grow over Nigeria's democracy

27 Oct 2026, 14:19 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the credibility of Nigeria's Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) following allegations that AI-generated images were found in the preliminary voter register, raising serious questions about the integrity of the country's electoral system.

The claims surfaced after INEC uploaded the preliminary register online, with users sharing screenshots of entries from several polling units in Edo State whose photographs they say look artificially generated. Some users have also alleged duplicate entries with the same photograph under different voter numbers. Economist and politician Pat Utomi has publicly asked INEC to explain the images. INEC had not publicly responded to the specific allegations at the time of writing.

The allegations have sparked concerns about the effectiveness of INEC's verification procedures, transparency and accountability, particularly as Nigeria prepares for future elections.

For an institution entrusted with protecting the voting rights of millions of Nigerians, the possibility of AI-generated photographs appearing in an official electoral database is a matter that deserves urgent investigation.

If confirmed, such irregularities could expose serious weaknesses in the processes used to register voters, authenticate their identities and maintain accurate electoral records.

How can Nigerians trust an electoral system if the authenticity of the photographs and identities contained in its register cannot be guaranteed?

INEC has a constitutional responsibility to conduct credible elections and maintain a reliable register of eligible voters. Any failure to detect manipulated images or questionable registrations could further weaken public confidence in an institution that has faced criticism over previous electoral exercises.

The growing sophistication of artificial intelligence also presents new challenges for electoral authorities worldwide. Images that appear genuine can now be generated or manipulated using readily available technology, making strong identity verification procedures increasingly important.

However, the existence of suspicious images alone would not establish electoral fraud. A proper investigation would need to determine whether the photographs were genuinely AI-generated, how they entered the register and whether they were connected to valid voter records.

Nigerians deserve clear answers from INEC. The commission should establish the facts, explain its verification procedures and disclose what safeguards are in place to prevent fraudulent registrations and manipulation of electoral information.

Where wrongdoing is established, those responsible should be identified and held accountable under the law.

Nigeria cannot afford another election overshadowed by doubts about the credibility of the electoral process. Democracy depends on public confidence that every legitimate vote will count and that electoral institutions can protect the integrity of the process.

With the 2027 general elections approaching, INEC must demonstrate that its systems are secure, its records are reliable and its operations are open to independent scrutiny.

The credibility of Nigeria's democracy is too important to be left to assumptions, political assurances or unanswered allegations.

The question Nigerians are asking is simple: if INEC cannot guarantee the authenticity of the identities in its electoral register, how can citizens be confident that it will deliver free, fair and credible elections in 2027?

Makinde campaign says Tinubu cannot claim credit for naira recovery

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The campaign of APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde has accused President Tinubu of seeking praise for the naira's recent stability after policies that sharply weakened it, saying small exchange rate gains do little for Nigerians facing high prices.

Read more

The presidential campaign of Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde has criticised President Bola Tinubu's claims about the naira.

What the campaign said

In a statement reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, Richard Nnanna Ihediwa, director of strategic communications for the Makinde–Daura campaign, accused the President of trying to take credit for the naira's recent stability after policies that first caused a drastic fall in its value. He said the President could not preside over the destruction of the currency's purchasing power and then ask for applause. The campaign pointed out that the official exchange rate was about ₦461 to the dollar when Tinubu took office in May 2023, before the removal of the fuel subsidy and the unification of the exchange rate, and said marginal improvements offer little relief to people facing high inflation.

Background

The statement responds to Tinubu's remarks in Uyo last week, where he said the naira would have become worthless without his reforms and put the current rate at about ₦1,300 to the dollar. Makinde is the candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), with former DSS chief Lawal Musa Daura as his running mate, and recently launched his 'Reset Agenda' manifesto. The government has said the old official rate understated the true value of the dollar and that unification was needed.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Hamzat vows to enforce one-year rent limit as Lagos governorship candidates debate

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

Lagos APC governorship candidate Obafemi Hamzat says the state will enforce the legal limit on advance rent to one year, warning landlords against demanding two or three years upfront. He spoke at a televised governorship debate.

Read more

Lagos State Deputy Governor Obafemi Hamzat, the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate for governor, says the state government will enforce the legal limit on advance rent payments.

What he said

Speaking at a governorship debate organised by a coalition of media organisations on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Hamzat said the one-year cap on advance rent is already contained in the Lagos State Real Estate Registration Agency law passed in 2025, and that the focus now is making sure it is followed. He warned landlords against demanding two or three years' rent upfront, and said estate agents are required to register with the state and appear on an official database, urging residents to check it before dealing with agents.

The law

The Lagos State Tenancy Law of 2011 already makes it unlawful for landlords or their agents to demand more than one year's rent in advance from a new tenant, and limits advance payments from sitting tenants. Despite this, demands for two or more years' rent have remained a widespread concern.

Rhodes-Vivour's pitch

At the same debate, Gbadebo Rhodes-Vivour, the governorship candidate of the opposition ADC, said APC governments in Lagos had focused on policies that favoured the elite for more than 20 years, while residents of poorer and rural communities did not feel the benefit of the state's high revenue. He promised more than 100,000 housing units, a safer and cleaner state, and technology-driven measures to end flooding.

Background

Hamzat is seeking to succeed Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu in the 2027 governorship election. Former governor Akinwunmi Ambode recently pledged to mobilise support for him. Housing costs are one of the biggest concerns for Lagos residents.

Okowa says he left Atiku for Tinubu because he has 'seen the light'

8 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former Delta governor Ifeanyi Okowa, Atiku Abubakar's running mate in 2023, told an APC rally in Port Harcourt that he switched to backing President Tinubu because he has seen the light, crediting the government's fiscal reforms.

Read more

Former Delta State governor Ifeanyi Okowa has explained why he abandoned former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar to support President Bola Tinubu ahead of the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at an All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Okowa reportedly said he had 'seen the light'. He argued that Tinubu inherited a country with foundations weakened by decades of mismanagement, and that the current administration was making tough but necessary decisions to fix them. He credited the government's fiscal reforms with increasing the revenue shared with states and local governments, saying the results could be seen in better project delivery and salary payments. He also compared Tinubu's record as Lagos governor with his performance as President, saying the same transformation was now happening across the country.

Background

Okowa was Atiku's vice-presidential running mate on the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) ticket in the 2023 election, which Tinubu won. He later left the PDP for the APC, following his successor in Delta, Governor Sheriff Oborevwori. Atiku is now the candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), although a Federal High Court ruling last week voided his nomination, a decision the party has appealed. At the same rally, Tinubu questioned how Atiku built a university while he was vice-president, prompting a sharp reply from Atiku's campaign.

What happens next

Atiku's campaign had not publicly responded to Okowa's remarks at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Amaechi warns court ruling against ADC could trigger unrest if mishandled

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

ADC vice-presidential candidate Rotimi Amaechi has warned that the court judgment removing the party's leadership and voiding Atiku Abubakar's nomination could lead to serious unrest, even civil war, if the dispute is not properly managed.

Read more

Rotimi Amaechi, the vice-presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has warned that the court ruling against the party's leadership could cause serious unrest if it is not handled carefully.

What he said

In comments reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Amaechi said the judgment that nullified the party's leadership and the process that produced Atiku Abubakar as presidential candidate could lead to civil war if the dispute is not properly managed. He did not set out in detail how he believed the crisis should be resolved.

The ruling

On Thursday, Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja ruled that the national working committee led by former Senate President David Mark was invalid. The judgment also nullified the emergence of former Interior Minister Rauf Aregbesola as national secretary, and decisions taken by the Mark-led leadership, including the process that produced Atiku as candidate. The court directed INEC not to recognise decisions taken at the party's national executive committee meeting of 27 July 2025. The suit was filed by Nafiu Gombe, a former deputy national chairman of the party.

Background

The ADC has appealed the judgment and asked INEC not to act on it until the appeal is heard. Atiku has vowed to stay in the race and accused the government of using the courts against the opposition. Amaechi, a former Rivers governor and transport minister, this week also said former President Muhammadu Buhari had promised in writing to back him in 2022.

What happens next

Attention is now on the Court of Appeal and on how INEC responds before the presidential election on 16 January 2027.
