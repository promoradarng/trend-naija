INEC under fire over alleged AI-generated images in voter register as concerns grow over Nigeria's democracy

27 Oct 2026, 14:19 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the credibility of Nigeria's Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) following allegations that AI-generated images were found in the preliminary voter register, raising serious questions about the integrity of the country's electoral system.

The claims surfaced after INEC uploaded the preliminary register online, with users sharing screenshots of entries from several polling units in Edo State whose photographs they say look artificially generated. Some users have also alleged duplicate entries with the same photograph under different voter numbers. Economist and politician Pat Utomi has publicly asked INEC to explain the images. INEC had not publicly responded to the specific allegations at the time of writing.

The allegations have sparked concerns about the effectiveness of INEC's verification procedures, transparency and accountability, particularly as Nigeria prepares for future elections.

For an institution entrusted with protecting the voting rights of millions of Nigerians, the possibility of AI-generated photographs appearing in an official electoral database is a matter that deserves urgent investigation.

If confirmed, such irregularities could expose serious weaknesses in the processes used to register voters, authenticate their identities and maintain accurate electoral records.

How can Nigerians trust an electoral system if the authenticity of the photographs and identities contained in its register cannot be guaranteed?

INEC has a constitutional responsibility to conduct credible elections and maintain a reliable register of eligible voters. Any failure to detect manipulated images or questionable registrations could further weaken public confidence in an institution that has faced criticism over previous electoral exercises.

The growing sophistication of artificial intelligence also presents new challenges for electoral authorities worldwide. Images that appear genuine can now be generated or manipulated using readily available technology, making strong identity verification procedures increasingly important.

However, the existence of suspicious images alone would not establish electoral fraud. A proper investigation would need to determine whether the photographs were genuinely AI-generated, how they entered the register and whether they were connected to valid voter records.

Nigerians deserve clear answers from INEC. The commission should establish the facts, explain its verification procedures and disclose what safeguards are in place to prevent fraudulent registrations and manipulation of electoral information.

Where wrongdoing is established, those responsible should be identified and held accountable under the law.

Nigeria cannot afford another election overshadowed by doubts about the credibility of the electoral process. Democracy depends on public confidence that every legitimate vote will count and that electoral institutions can protect the integrity of the process.

With the 2027 general elections approaching, INEC must demonstrate that its systems are secure, its records are reliable and its operations are open to independent scrutiny.

The credibility of Nigeria's democracy is too important to be left to assumptions, political assurances or unanswered allegations.

The question Nigerians are asking is simple: if INEC cannot guarantee the authenticity of the identities in its electoral register, how can citizens be confident that it will deliver free, fair and credible elections in 2027?

Utomi says an Obi government would aim for ₦500 to the dollar

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

NDC chieftain Pat Utomi has reportedly said Peter Obi's economic plan would aim to strengthen the naira to ₦500 per dollar, from about ₦1,350 today. He claimed this would sharply cut food and fuel prices, but the party has not said how long it would take.

Read more

Political economist Pat Utomi, a leading figure in the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has reportedly said that a Peter Obi government would work towards an exchange rate of ₦500 to the dollar.

What he said

In a post on X reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, Utomi said the target would be part of Obi's economic agenda. He argued that the naira could be strengthened from its current rate of about ₦1,350 to ₦500 per dollar through investment in security, electricity and roads, together with market reforms. He also projected large falls in the prices of everyday goods, claiming for example that a 50kg bag of imported rice could drop from about ₦55,000 to ₦27,500 and a loaf of sliced bread from ₦1,750 to ₦800.

The questions

The NDC has not said how long such an improvement would take, or what monetary and fiscal steps would be used to achieve and sustain it. Reaching ₦500 would require the naira to gain about 63 per cent against the dollar. The naira traded at about ₦1,347 on the official market and around ₦1,385 on the parallel market at the end of last week. No independent economists or government officials had responded to the target at the time of the report.

Background

The exchange rate has been one of the biggest economic issues since the naira was floated in 2023, when it weakened sharply. The Tinubu government says its reforms have stabilised the currency, while the opposition argues that the weaker naira has driven up living costs. Utomi, who once ran for president himself, is a long-time ally of Obi.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters welcomed the promise, while critics described it as unrealistic and questioned how it could be achieved without large reserves.

Okorocha calls Obi's one-term pledge a 'dangerous gamble' and backs Tinubu

30 Oct 2026, 06:44 GMT

Former Imo governor Rochas Okorocha has reportedly described Peter Obi's promise to serve only one four-year term as a dangerous gamble for the South East. Speaking on television, he urged the region to choose its alliances carefully and backed President Tinubu's re-election.

Read more

Former Imo State governor Rochas Okorocha has criticised Peter Obi's plan to serve a single four-year term if elected president, reportedly calling it a dangerous gamble.

What he said

Speaking on a Channels Television politics programme on Thursday, 29 October 2026, Okorocha is said to have questioned whether Obi's one-term model would work and urged the South East to be careful and strategic in choosing political alliances rather than backing what he described as risky options. He also backed President Bola Tinubu's re-election, arguing that the President had not acted against the interests of the Igbo people and was a more reliable choice for the region.

Background

Obi, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate, has promised to serve only one term, a pledge his supporters say shows he is not driven by personal ambition. Critics, including Works Minister David Umahi, have argued that the South East should aim for eight years in power like other regions. Okorocha's comments came on the same day a Federal High Court ordered INEC to recognise him as the APC candidate for the Imo West Senate seat in place of Governor Hope Uzodimma, and former APC chairman Abdullahi Ganduje visited him to congratulate him.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters dismissed the comments as politically motivated, while APC supporters shared the clip widely. The Obi campaign had not responded at the time of the report.

What happens next

The debate over whether the South East should back Obi or Tinubu is likely to grow as both camps campaign across the region.

Obi tells Ebonyi town hall Nigerians should not vote for leaders who stay at home while people suffer

29 Oct 2026, 20:44 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has reportedly told a town hall in Ebonyi State that voters should reject candidates who do not know the communities they want to lead. He said leadership should come from the people's demand, not be imposed on them.

Read more

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), took his campaign to Ebonyi State on Thursday, 29 October 2026, where he reportedly told a town hall meeting that Nigerians should not vote for politicians who stay in their homes while ordinary people suffer.

What he said

According to reports, Obi said some of the people seeking the presidency could not find places such as Abakaliki, Ikwo and Nkalagu on a map, and did not understand the problems facing people in those communities. He argued that leaders must engage directly with citizens and listen to their needs, and said leadership should come from the demand of the people rather than being supplied to them by a few. The meeting was organised to hear residents' concerns first-hand.

Background

The Ebonyi visit came a day after Obi's town hall in Nasarawa State, where he said election riggers would be jailed under his government, and on the same day Oyo governor Seyi Makinde told a meeting in Enugu that his team had been warned against campaigning in the South East because it is seen as Obi's stronghold. Obi, a former Anambra governor, won most of the South East in 2023 as the Labour Party candidate and is now running with Rabiu Kwankwaso on the NDC ticket.

How people reacted

Supporters shared clips from the meeting widely online. Some commenters claimed that tricycle operators were restricted around the venue, but these claims have not been confirmed. The APC and other parties have not responded to his latest remarks.

What happens next

Obi is expected to continue his tour of the South East and North Central in the coming days as campaigning for the January 2027 elections intensifies.

Ganduje visits Okorocha to congratulate him after court ruling on Imo West ticket

29 Oct 2026, 20:44 GMT

Former APC national chairman Abdullahi Ganduje has reportedly visited Rochas Okorocha to congratulate him after a Federal High Court in Abuja ordered INEC to recognise Okorocha, not Governor Hope Uzodimma, as the party's Imo West senatorial candidate. Ganduje is said to have described the ruling as a reason to celebrate.

Read more

The fight over the APC ticket for the Imo West Senatorial District has drawn in a major party figure, with former APC national chairman Abdullahi Ganduje reportedly visiting former Imo governor Rochas Okorocha to congratulate him on his court victory.

What happened

According to reports on Thursday, 29 October 2026, Ganduje, a former governor of Kano State, visited Okorocha to celebrate the judgment. He is said to have told Okorocha he was excited when he heard about the ruling and described it as a reason for celebration. No direct statement from Okorocha about the visit has been reported.

The court ruling

A Federal High Court sitting in Abuja ruled that Governor Hope Uzodimma should stop presenting himself as the APC candidate for Imo West and directed the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to publish Okorocha's name instead. Okorocha had gone to court claiming he won the party's primary on 18 May with about 150,780 votes to Uzodimma's 102,158, while the returning officer had declared Uzodimma the winner with 230,464 votes. The case also raises the question of whether a sitting governor, whose term runs until January 2028, can contest a Senate seat in 2027.

Background

Okorocha governed Imo from 2011 to 2019 and has had a long-running rivalry with Uzodimma, who succeeded him after a Supreme Court judgment in 2020. Uzodimma has been campaigning for President Tinubu's re-election and has rejected the Rainbow Coalition in the state.

What happens next

Uzodimma has not publicly responded to the visit, and it is not yet clear whether he will appeal. The ruling is expected to deepen divisions within the Imo APC ahead of the 2027 elections.
