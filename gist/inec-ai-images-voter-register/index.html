INEC under fire over alleged AI-generated images in voter register as concerns grow over Nigeria's democracy

27 Oct 2026, 14:19 GMT

Fresh concerns have emerged over the credibility of Nigeria's Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) following allegations that AI-generated images were found in the preliminary voter register, raising serious questions about the integrity of the country's electoral system.

The claims surfaced after INEC uploaded the preliminary register online, with users sharing screenshots of entries from several polling units in Edo State whose photographs they say look artificially generated. Some users have also alleged duplicate entries with the same photograph under different voter numbers. Economist and politician Pat Utomi has publicly asked INEC to explain the images. INEC had not publicly responded to the specific allegations at the time of writing.

The allegations have sparked concerns about the effectiveness of INEC's verification procedures, transparency and accountability, particularly as Nigeria prepares for future elections.

For an institution entrusted with protecting the voting rights of millions of Nigerians, the possibility of AI-generated photographs appearing in an official electoral database is a matter that deserves urgent investigation.

If confirmed, such irregularities could expose serious weaknesses in the processes used to register voters, authenticate their identities and maintain accurate electoral records.

How can Nigerians trust an electoral system if the authenticity of the photographs and identities contained in its register cannot be guaranteed?

INEC has a constitutional responsibility to conduct credible elections and maintain a reliable register of eligible voters. Any failure to detect manipulated images or questionable registrations could further weaken public confidence in an institution that has faced criticism over previous electoral exercises.

The growing sophistication of artificial intelligence also presents new challenges for electoral authorities worldwide. Images that appear genuine can now be generated or manipulated using readily available technology, making strong identity verification procedures increasingly important.

However, the existence of suspicious images alone would not establish electoral fraud. A proper investigation would need to determine whether the photographs were genuinely AI-generated, how they entered the register and whether they were connected to valid voter records.

Nigerians deserve clear answers from INEC. The commission should establish the facts, explain its verification procedures and disclose what safeguards are in place to prevent fraudulent registrations and manipulation of electoral information.

Where wrongdoing is established, those responsible should be identified and held accountable under the law.

Nigeria cannot afford another election overshadowed by doubts about the credibility of the electoral process. Democracy depends on public confidence that every legitimate vote will count and that electoral institutions can protect the integrity of the process.

With the 2027 general elections approaching, INEC must demonstrate that its systems are secure, its records are reliable and its operations are open to independent scrutiny.

The credibility of Nigeria's democracy is too important to be left to assumptions, political assurances or unanswered allegations.

The question Nigerians are asking is simple: if INEC cannot guarantee the authenticity of the identities in its electoral register, how can citizens be confident that it will deliver free, fair and credible elections in 2027?

The video

A Facebook video reportedly showing Ozonna Soludo, the governor's son, dancing to and promoting a song associated with the NDC has sparked reactions online. In online discussions, the NDC has been linked to Peter Obi and Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso.

Father and son in different camps?

What makes the situation interesting is the apparent political contrast between father and son.

Former Labour Party chairman Abure rules out backing Obi, open to Tinubu or Atiku

2 Nov 2026, 09:44 GMT

Julius Abure, the former national chairman of the Labour Party, has reportedly said he will not support Peter Obi in 2027 but could back either President Tinubu or Atiku Abubakar. Obi's camp says it has moved on.

Read more

Julius Abure, who led the Labour Party when Peter Obi was its presidential candidate in 2023, has said he will not support Obi in the 2027 election.

What he said

In a newspaper interview reported on Monday, 2 November 2026, Abure reportedly said he was open to working with any of the major candidates but was very sure he would not back Obi, who is now the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate. He said he could support either President Bola Tinubu or Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, depending on the outcome of a pending Supreme Court case on the Labour Party's leadership, whether a candidate's agenda matches his views, and how acceptable the candidate is to voters. He said that if the court has not ruled by the end of November, he will consult his supporters.

His criticism of Obi

Abure argued that Obi had squandered the goodwill and structures that helped him win more than six million votes in 2023, and predicted he would do worse in 2027. He also questioned Obi's leadership, accusing him and Abia Governor Alex Otti of undermining the party.

The other side

Obi's allies have previously accused Abure of breaking an agreement to hold an inclusive party convention. The NDC and Obi's camp dismissed his latest comments, saying they have moved on and are focused on the election.

Background

The Labour Party has been split by a long leadership dispute since the 2023 election, which eventually led Obi to leave and join the NDC.

Seun Okinbaloye says his 2027 rigging warning named no party or candidate

2 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Channels TV anchor Seun Okinbaloye has clarified his widely shared warning about rigging in the 2027 elections, saying it did not accuse any party, candidate or sponsor. Reactions remain divided, with some praising his courage and others accusing him of backtracking.

Read more

Popular Channels Television anchor Seun Okinbaloye has responded to the storm over his recent comments on election rigging ahead of 2027.

What he said

Speaking on the channel on Sunday, 1 November 2026, Okinbaloye said his earlier remarks had been widely misread. He said his warning had named no political party, accused no candidate and identified no sponsor. Critics had interpreted his original comments as a claim that rigging of the 2027 elections had already begun.

Background

Okinbaloye's original warning went viral last week, coming amid wider concerns about the integrity of the voter register after INEC admitted that some of its registration officers had entered AI-generated photos. Opposition candidates, including Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi, have called for audits and promised to prosecute anyone involved in rigging. INEC says the register is still being cleaned up and will be certified in December.

How people reacted

Opinion was divided. Some viewers praised the anchor for speaking out and for clarifying his position, saying he was simply doing his job as a journalist. Others accused him of damage control and asked why he had not named those he believed were planning to rig. Several users said INEC itself should identify and prosecute staff involved in irregularities in the register.

Why it matters

Okinbaloye presents one of Nigeria's most-watched political programmes, and his comments carry weight with voters and politicians alike. The debate shows how sensitive questions about election credibility have become as campaigning intensifies.

Yahaya Bello vows to spend two weeks in Kano campaigning for Tinubu

1 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former Kogi governor Yahaya Bello has reportedly pledged to move to Kano for two weeks to campaign for President Tinubu and Governor Abba Yusuf. He urged non-indigenous communities in the state to deliver more votes than in 2023.

Read more

Former Kogi State Governor Yahaya Bello has promised to personally campaign in Kano for President Bola Tinubu's re-election.

What he said

Speaking at a political mobilisation and empowerment programme for Yoruba and other non-indigenous communities in Kano, organised by the foundation of Rep. Leke Abejide and reported on Sunday, 1 November 2026, Bello said he would move to Kano for two weeks to campaign for the President and for Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf. He urged non-indigenous communities in the state to beat the nearly 500,000 votes they reportedly gave Tinubu in 2023, and said Abejide's network could deliver at least two million votes. He also pointed to work on the Kano–Abuja highway as evidence of the President's commitment to the North.

Support for communities

Abejide, who represents Yagba Federal Constituency in Kogi State and holds a Yoruba community title in Kano, announced ₦500 million in interest-free loans for women's cooperatives, along with support for widows. He said programmes for young people and men are being planned.

Background

Kano has the second-highest number of registered voters in Nigeria after Lagos, and the APC is investing heavily there after Governor Yusuf moved from the NNPP to the APC. The state is also the home of Rabiu Kwankwaso, Peter Obi's running mate on the NDC ticket. Bello, who governed Kogi from 2016 to 2024, is facing a money-laundering case brought by the EFCC, which he denies. Deputy Senate President Barau Jibrin also predicted a Tinubu victory at an APC event in Kano on Sunday.

What happens next

The campaign in Kano is expected to intensify as the APC and NDC compete for the state's large vote.
