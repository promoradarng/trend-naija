Nigeria's external debt rises to $145 billion, World Bank report says

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Nigeria's external debt reached about $145.0 billion in 2025, according to the World Bank's International Debt Report 2026.

The figures

According to reports on Friday, 6 November 2026, the total was 6.6 per cent higher than the year before and about 74 per cent higher than the $83.2 billion recorded in 2015. Public and publicly guaranteed debt made up about $62.4 billion of the total, private debt without government guarantees about $54.5 billion, and short-term debt about $28.1 billion. Multilateral lenders were the biggest creditors of the government, at about $31.4 billion, including about $18.5 billion owed to the World Bank's International Development Association. China remained Nigeria's largest single-country lender, owed about $5.7 billion.

Burden of repayments

Some measures of the debt burden improved: external debt fell to 212 per cent of export earnings and to 51 per cent of gross national income. But debt service took a bigger share of income, rising to 19 per cent of exports and 5 per cent of gross national income.

The global picture

The World Bank said debt service payments by low- and middle-income countries reached $1.4 trillion in 2025, the fifth yearly rise in a row, driven largely by payments to private lenders such as bondholders. It noted that loans from official lenders carried an average interest rate of 1.9 per cent, compared with 9.6 per cent from private creditors.

Why it matters

The figures come as President Bola Tinubu seeks Senate approval for new external borrowing of about $7.7 billion, a request opposition figures, including Atiku Abubakar, have urged lawmakers to reject. The government says borrowing is needed to fund infrastructure and that its reforms are improving the economy.

Suspected bandits have reportedly abducted six residents in the Millennium City area of Chikun Local Government Area, Kaduna State.

What happened

The attackers stormed two houses at around midnight on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, shooting and breaking in before taking nine people away. Residents said they began hearing gunshots at about 11:30pm.

Three of those abducted, including an elderly woman and a mother with her toddler, were later released, leaving six people in captivity.

Residents' fears

A man whose wife is among those still held said he feared for her safety because she has difficulty walking, and appealed for help to rescue her.

Background

Kaduna has seen several kidnapping incidents in recent weeks, including a foiled attempt at a school in Zangon Kataf and the abduction of Boys' and Girls' Brigade members in Kachia/Kajuru, most of whom troops rescued.

CBN raises weekly dollar limit for BDCs from $150,000 to $1m

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has reportedly raised the weekly dollar purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators to $1 million to ease festive pressure on the naira. Banks have begun implementing the change.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has reportedly raised the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change (BDC) operators from $150,000 to $1 million, in a move aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the foreign exchange market.

How it will work

Sources said commercial banks had started contacting BDC operators to set up arrangements for the new limit. Banks will act only as intermediaries between BDCs and the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market, using a purchase tracking platform.

Background

The change is a major relaxation of restrictions the CBN introduced in February. It comes as the naira has weakened against the dollar in recent days and the country's external reserves have fallen. The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate at 23%.
