Infantino says Nigeria is not ready to host a World Cup yet

23 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

FIFA President Gianni Infantino has reportedly said Nigeria cannot yet host the World Cup because of gaps in stadiums, airports and transport. He added that the country has the building blocks to get there.

Read more

FIFA President Gianni Infantino has reportedly said Nigeria is not currently ready to host the FIFA World Cup.

What he said

Speaking during his visit to Nigeria, Infantino was asked whether the country could stage a tournament of that size. He is said to have pointed to serious shortfalls in stadiums, airports and transport networks as the main obstacles, and explained the kind of infrastructure any host nation must have in place.

Hope for the future

However, he reportedly did not rule out Nigeria's long-term chances, saying the country has the building blocks needed to eventually meet the standard. He also described the national training facilities he visited in Abuja and Warri as impressive.

Background

South Africa remains the only sub-Saharan African country to have hosted the World Cup, in 2010, while Morocco will co-host the 2030 edition with Spain and Portugal.

How fans reacted

His comments drew mixed reactions from Nigerian football fans online.

CAF adopts Kiswahili as its fifth official language

23 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

African football's governing body has added Kiswahili to its official languages alongside English, French, Arabic and Portuguese. The decision was taken at CAF's General Assembly in Abuja.

Read more

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) has adopted Kiswahili as its fifth official language, alongside English, French, Arabic and Portuguese.

The decision

The change was approved at CAF's 48th Ordinary General Assembly, held in Abuja on Saturday, 21 November 2026. CAF described it as a historic decision that reflects its commitment to Africa's linguistic and cultural diversity, and said it would strengthen the place of African languages in football administration.

Who pushed for it

The proposal was championed by the Tanzania Football Federation, with backing from the football bodies of Kenya and Uganda. CAF President Patrice Motsepe congratulated the three East African countries.

About Kiswahili

Kiswahili is spoken by more than 200 million people, mainly in Tanzania, Kenya and Uganda, and is also widely used in other parts of East, Central and Southern Africa.

CAS to rule on Tuesday on who won AFCON 2025, Senegal or Morocco

23 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Court of Arbitration for Sport will announce its decision on the disputed 2025 Africa Cup of Nations final on Tuesday, 24 November. Senegal won the match on the pitch, but CAF later handed the title to Morocco.

Read more

The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) will deliver its long-awaited ruling on the disputed 2025 Africa Cup of Nations final between Senegal and Morocco on Tuesday, 24 November 2026.

The announcement

CAS confirmed on Monday that all the parties had been notified of the decision date. The ruling is expected on Tuesday afternoon and will settle which country is officially recognised as champion. The court reminded the public that only information released through its own channels should be treated as official.

How the dispute started

The final was played in Rabat on 18 January 2026. Senegal beat hosts Morocco 1-0 after extra time, with Pape Gueye scoring the winner. But the match was marred by chaotic scenes when Senegal's players briefly walked off the pitch in protest at a late penalty awarded to Morocco. When play resumed after about 15 minutes, Brahim Diaz's attempted Panenka penalty was saved by Senegal goalkeeper Edouard Mendy.

Title stripped

On 17 March, CAF's Appeal Board overturned the result, ruling that Senegal had forfeited the match by leaving the pitch, and awarded the title to Morocco. The Senegalese Football Federation rejected the decision and took the case to CAS, naming CAF and the Royal Moroccan Football Federation as respondents.

What happens next

Tuesday's decision is final at the sporting level and will either restore Senegal's title or confirm Morocco as champions.

Mauricio Pochettino's time in charge of Manchester United got off to a painful start on Sunday, 22 November 2026, as Liverpool beat his side 4-0 at Anfield.

How the game went

United's plans fell apart early when defender Lenny Yoro was shown a red card in the eighth minute, leaving them with ten men for more than 80 minutes. Liverpool quickly took advantage. Dominik Szoboszlai opened the scoring in the 10th minute, and Florian Wirtz doubled the lead three minutes later.

Liverpool kept their two-goal lead until half-time and added two more after the break through Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz.

Where it leaves the clubs

The result left United in 19th place, inside the Premier League relegation zone, while Liverpool moved into the top four.

Pochettino's reaction

Speaking after the match, Pochettino reportedly admitted that the early red card shaped the game, but urged his players to turn their frustration into better performances in the coming matches.

Background

Pochettino took over at Old Trafford only recently. The club has been linked with several players ahead of the January window, including Super Eagles defender Calvin Bassey.
