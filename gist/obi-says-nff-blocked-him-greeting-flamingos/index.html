Obi says NFF officials stopped him from greeting world champion Flamingos

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has accused officials of the Nigeria Football Federation (NFF) of stopping him from congratulating the Flamingos after their World Cup triumph.

What Obi said

Obi said he was at the airport in Abuja at about 7am on Monday, 9 November 2026, to catch a flight to Enugu, when he saw the Flamingos, who had just arrived with the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup trophy. He said he asked his protocol officer to find out if he could go over and welcome the players, but was told that NFF officials had refused, reportedly warning that anyone who allowed him near the team would lose their job.

'Not the first time'

Obi said this was not the first time he had been prevented from supporting a national team. He recalled travelling to Uyo in March 2025 to watch a World Cup qualifier against Zimbabwe, and said that although he was warmly received before the match, he was not allowed the same access afterwards.

Background

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final in Rabat on Saturday to become the first African team to win the tournament, and were welcomed back to Nigeria on Monday. President Bola Tinubu and the First Lady have both congratulated the team.

Presidency responds

The Presidency accused Obi of trying to politicise national sports teams. In a post on X on Monday, the President's special assistant on social media, Dada Olusegun, said the national teams, including the Under-17 girls, represent the whole country and not any party or individual's ambitions, and that protocol rules around national teams are designed to protect young athletes from political exploitation, commercial distractions and media pressure. The NFF itself had not publicly commented.

  • Obi says he saw the Flamingos at Abuja airport.
  • He says NFF officials refused his request to greet them.
  • The NFF has not responded.

DSS says it found no evidence it planned to disrupt ADC campaign in Sokoto, asks Atiku to retract or provide proof

18 Nov 2026, 07:57 GMT

The Department of State Services says its internal investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off in Sokoto. It has asked Atiku Abubakar to retract or clarify his allegation, or provide evidence.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) says its investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential campaign in Sokoto.

How it started

Earlier this month, former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar accused the leadership of the DSS of recruiting protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto.

The allegation drew wide attention, and the DSS responded by ordering an investigation into the claim.

The DSS findings

The agency now says its investigation found nothing to support the allegation that it planned to recruit protesters or disrupt the ADC campaign.

It has asked Atiku to either retract or clarify his claim, or to provide evidence that could support further investigation.

Not the final word

It is important to note that this is the DSS's own preliminary finding. It was not an independent investigation, which means the agency effectively investigated an allegation made against itself.

Atiku has not issued a fresh public response to the DSS findings.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections approaching, allegations that security agencies are being used against opposition parties are likely to keep coming up. Public trust depends on such claims being properly examined, whether they turn out to be true or not.

Should Atiku withdraw his allegation, provide evidence, or should an independent body investigate the claim?

Corps members of the Oyo State Batch 25C have reportedly received their long-delayed final allowance, after social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM), whose real name is Martins Vincent Otse, publicly called out the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC).

What happened

Nigerian stock market loses ₦2.27 trillion in one day

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Investors lost about ₦2.27 trillion on the Nigerian Exchange on Tuesday as the All-Share Index fell 1.38%. Trading volume and deals also dropped.

Read more

The Nigerian stock market closed sharply lower on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, as sell-offs in major stocks wiped about ₦2.27 trillion off market value in a single trading session.

The numbers

Data from the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) showed the All-Share Index fell by 3,491.13 points, or 1.38%, to close at 249,318.65, down from 252,809.78 in the previous session. Total market capitalisation dropped by the same percentage, from ₦164.15 trillion to ₦161.88 trillion.

Trading activity

Activity also slowed. Investors traded 564.04 million shares, down 16.26% from 673.6 million in the previous session, while the number of deals fell by 18.91% to 30,992.

Bright spots

The losses were partly cushioned by buying interest in consumer goods and insurance stocks, with Cadbury Nigeria among the top gainers.

MTN launches MTN One TV streaming service with Nigerian original series

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

MTN has introduced MTN One TV, an ad-supported streaming platform to compete with Netflix and others. Its first original is a Nigerian series, 100 Ways to Stay Single in Lagos.

Read more

Telecommunications giant MTN has launched MTN One TV, an advertising-supported streaming service aimed at African audiences, as it takes on global platforms such as Netflix, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video and Apple TV.

The platform

The service is designed to give viewers access to local entertainment and original productions, and is expected to eventually include free-to-air television channels.

The first original

MTN has unveiled 100 Ways to Stay Single in Lagos, a Nigerian-produced series featuring actors from Nigeria, Ghana and South Africa. It is the platform's first original series.

Why it matters

The move signals MTN's ambition to become more than a mobile network provider, as competition for viewers in Africa's growing streaming market increases.
