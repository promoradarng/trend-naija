Obi says NFF officials stopped him from greeting world champion Flamingos

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has accused officials of the Nigeria Football Federation (NFF) of stopping him from congratulating the Flamingos after their World Cup triumph.

What Obi said

Obi said he was at the airport in Abuja at about 7am on Monday, 9 November 2026, to catch a flight to Enugu, when he saw the Flamingos, who had just arrived with the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup trophy. He said he asked his protocol officer to find out if he could go over and welcome the players, but was told that NFF officials had refused, reportedly warning that anyone who allowed him near the team would lose their job.

'Not the first time'

Obi said this was not the first time he had been prevented from supporting a national team. He recalled travelling to Uyo in March 2025 to watch a World Cup qualifier against Zimbabwe, and said that although he was warmly received before the match, he was not allowed the same access afterwards.

Background

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final in Rabat on Saturday to become the first African team to win the tournament, and were welcomed back to Nigeria on Monday. President Bola Tinubu and the First Lady have both congratulated the team.

Presidency responds

The Presidency accused Obi of trying to politicise national sports teams. In a post on X on Monday, the President's special assistant on social media, Dada Olusegun, said the national teams, including the Under-17 girls, represent the whole country and not any party or individual's ambitions, and that protocol rules around national teams are designed to protect young athletes from political exploitation, commercial distractions and media pressure. The NFF itself had not publicly commented.

  • Obi says he saw the Flamingos at Abuja airport.
  • He says NFF officials refused his request to greet them.
  • The NFF has not responded.

Adamu Garba denies endorsing Peter Obi for 2027, says he has quit partisan politics

19 Nov 2026, 17:04 GMT

Former APC presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has dismissed reports that he endorsed Peter Obi for the 2027 presidential election, saying he has withdrawn from partisan politics to focus on his businesses and Africa's digital economy.

Former All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has denied reports that he endorsed Peter Obi ahead of Nigeria's 2027 presidential election.

"The claim is false"

In a public statement dated 18 November 2026, Garba described the claim circulating online as false.

He said he has withdrawn from partisan politics and is now focusing on his business ventures and on Africa's digital economy.

His new focus

Garba named Crowwe, Tablars and Justitia SI among the projects currently receiving his attention.

He also urged the public to rely only on his official channels for accurate information about his activities.

Where did the story come from?

The origin of the endorsement claim remains unclear. No post, interview or public statement showing Garba endorsing Obi has been found.

What is clear is that Garba has publicly disputed the claim.

A familiar pattern

With the 2027 elections approaching, false claims about endorsements are spreading quickly on social media. It is the second time this week that a story about backing for Obi has been corrected, after Obi himself clarified that his Washington visit was not an endorsement from the US Congress.

Many feared dead as boat carrying over 300 passengers capsizes in Bayelsa

20 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

A boat reportedly carrying more than 300 passengers to a burial ceremony has capsized in Southern Ijaw, Bayelsa State. Police say marine units have been deployed for rescue, but casualty figures are not yet known.

Read more

A boat said to be carrying more than 300 passengers has capsized in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of Bayelsa State, with many people feared dead or missing.

What happened

The boat was travelling from Ayama community to Ukubie for a burial ceremony when it capsized at about noon on Friday, 20 November 2026, shortly after passing Oweikorogha community.

The exact number of passengers on board and the number of casualties had not been established at the time of the reports, but residents fear several people may have lost their lives.

Rescue efforts

The Bayelsa State Police Command spokesperson, DSP Musa Muhammad, confirmed the incident and said the Commissioner of Police had deployed the Marine Police Unit and the local divisional police officer for rescue operations. He said further details on casualties and missing persons would be released.

A former state chairman of the Maritime Workers Union also confirmed the incident.

What happens next

Rescue and recovery efforts are continuing, and official casualty figures are expected once they are confirmed.

Ex-Super Eagles captain Segun Odegbami survives attack at his Abeokuta home

20 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Former Super Eagles captain Segun Odegbami says he fought off three armed men who broke into his home in Abeokuta in the early hours of Friday. Police say he has been treated and discharged from hospital.

Read more

Former Super Eagles captain and football administrator Segun Odegbami has survived an attack by three armed men who broke into his home in Abeokuta, Ogun State, in the early hours of Friday, 20 November 2026.

What happened

The attackers reportedly overpowered his domestic staff and driver before forcing their way into his bedroom at about 2:30am. The 74-year-old was injured in the attack.

Odegbami described the incident as an attempt on his life. He said he fought back with help from his driver and house help, and the attackers eventually fled. He said it was the closest he had ever come to death.

Police response

The Ogun State Police Command confirmed the attack and said the former international had been treated in hospital and discharged.

Who he is

Odegbami is a former captain of the Super Eagles and one of Nigeria's best-known football figures.

Oyedepo says church leadership should not be handed to children like a family business

20 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

A video of Bishop David Oyedepo saying church succession should be a spiritual process, not a family arrangement, has gone viral amid the Fountain of Life leadership crisis.

Read more

A video of Bishop David Oyedepo speaking about church succession has gone viral, drawing attention amid the leadership dispute at The Fountain of Life Church involving Pastor Jimmy Odukoya.

What he said

In the clip, posted on Instagram on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, the founder of Living Faith Church Worldwide (Winners' Chapel) said planning for who comes next is one of the most important responsibilities of church leadership.

He said leadership transition in ministry is a spiritual process and not a family arrangement, warning against simply handing churches over to children. He pointed to Joshua succeeding Moses in the Bible as the model.

Why it is trending

The video spread widely as Nigerians debated the Fountain of Life crisis, where trustees say the late founder Taiwo Odukoya wanted another pastor to succeed him, while his son Jimmy insists his removal as senior pastor is invalid.
