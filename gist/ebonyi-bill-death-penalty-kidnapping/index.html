Ebonyi governor sends bill to make kidnapping punishable by death

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

Ebonyi State governor Francis Nwifuru says he has sent a bill to the state House of Assembly that would make kidnapping punishable by death by hanging, with no other option.

Where he said it

Nwifuru made the announcement over the weekend at the funeral of HRH Eze Francis Igwe, the traditional ruler of Ndufu-Alike autonomous community in Ikwo Local Government Area. The monarch, the father of a former deputy governor of the state, Eric Igwe, was abducted on Sunday, 1 March 2026, while travelling to church, and was reportedly killed by his captors the next day.

What he said

The governor said the bill was meant to deter anyone planning a kidnapping, whether for money or for political reasons. He said he would not delay in carrying out any court judgment against those arrested over the monarch's killing, and that the murder must be the last of its kind in Ikwo and across the state. He also expressed concern about what he described as a moral decline among young people and urged them to turn away from crime.

Tributes

Former governors Sam Egwu and Martin Elechi and former Senate President Anyim Pius Anyim paid tribute to the late monarch, describing him as a peacemaker, educationist and good administrator who brought projects such as the Folk Technical College to his community. His eldest son said his father's last wish was for Governor Nwifuru to win a second term.

Background

Several Nigerian states already have laws that allow the death penalty for kidnapping, but executions are rare because governors must sign death warrants. Human rights groups oppose the death penalty, while supporters argue it is needed to deter a crime that has spread across the country.

What happens next

The bill must be passed by the House of Assembly and signed by the governor before it becomes law.

  • The bill would make kidnapping punishable by death by hanging.
  • He announced it at the funeral of a monarch killed by kidnappers.
  • The Assembly must pass the bill before it becomes law.

Woman confronts herder after cattle destroy her farm crops in Nasarawa State

10 Nov 2026, 00:44 GMT

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle strayed onto her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months growing. The incident has reopened the question of who pays when a farmer's livelihood is wiped out.

Read more

A woman in Nasarawa State has confronted a herder after his cattle entered her farmland and destroyed crops she had spent months cultivating.

Months of work gone

For someone who depends on farming to survive, watching cattle destroy months of hard work can be heartbreaking.

Imagine spending your money on seeds, preparing the land, planting and waiting patiently for harvest, only to wake up one day and find your crops destroyed. Worse still, many farmers borrow money to cultivate their land, hoping to repay the loan after harvest.

An old source of tension

Incidents like this remain one of the main causes of tension between farmers and herders in parts of Nigeria, and some have escalated into violence in the past.

The question is: who compensates the woman for her losses? Who takes responsibility for the destroyed crops?

Protection for everyone

Farmers deserve protection, just as herders have a right to carry out their lawful activities. But nobody should be allowed to destroy another person's livelihood without consequences.

Clear rules on grazing, quick ways to settle disputes and fair compensation could help prevent confrontations like this from turning into something worse.

Osun government offices shut as NLC begins three-day strike over council funds

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Government offices and public schools in Osogbo were shut on Monday as workers in Osun began a three-day strike in solidarity with local government employees over withheld statutory allocations to the state's councils.

Read more

Government offices in Osun State were shut on Monday, 9 November 2026, as workers began a three-day strike over withheld allocations to local governments.

What happened

The gates of the state government secretariat and the House of Assembly in Osogbo were locked, with no workers or business activity at either, according to reports. Local government secretariats in Olorunda and Osogbo were also closed, and pupils in public schools in Osogbo were sent home after teachers joined the strike.

Why workers are striking

In a statement on Sunday, the state chapter of the Nigeria Labour Congress (NLC), led by its chairman, Christopher Arapasopo, directed all affiliated unions to join the strike in solidarity with the Nigeria Union of Local Government Employees (NULGE). The NLC said the action followed the expiry of a seven-day ultimatum to the federal and Osun state governments over the continued withholding of statutory allocations to local councils in the state, which it said remained unresolved.

Background

Osun's local governments have been at the centre of a long legal and political dispute over who controls the councils, which has led to federal allocations being withheld. Osun is governed by Ademola Adeleke, who has backed President Bola Tinubu's re-election.

Naira up about 6% against the dollar in a year as reserves reach $55bn

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The naira has strengthened by about 6.3% against the US dollar over the past year, trading at about ₦1,347 on 6 November compared with ₦1,437 a year earlier, while Nigeria's foreign reserves have risen to about $55.1 billion.

Read more

The naira has gained ground against the US dollar over the past year, according to official exchange rate data.

The figures

The naira traded at about ₦1,346.79 to the dollar on 6 November 2026, compared with ₦1,436.74 on the same date in 2025, a gain of about ₦90, or 6.26 per cent, according to reports on Monday, 9 November. Nigeria's external reserves have risen to about $55.13 billion.

Why it matters

A stronger naira could reduce the cost of imported raw materials, machinery, medicines and other goods priced in foreign currency. However, how quickly that feeds through to lower prices for consumers depends on businesses passing on the savings.

Background

The naira lost much of its value after the government unified the exchange rate in 2023, when the official rate was about ₦461 to the dollar. The Central Bank of Nigeria has since taken steps to stabilise the foreign exchange market and improve dollar supply. The naira's performance has become a campaign issue: President Bola Tinubu said last week that it would have become worthless without his reforms, while the campaign of APM candidate Seyi Makinde said the President could not claim credit for a recovery after policies that weakened it.
