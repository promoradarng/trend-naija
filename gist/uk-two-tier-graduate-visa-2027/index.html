UK splits post-study work visa into two tiers from 2027

1 Nov 2026, 11:44 GMT

Nigerian students planning to study in the United Kingdom will face new rules on how long they can stay and work after graduation, under changes announced in the UK government's 2026 Budget.

What is changing

From 1 January 2027, the Graduate visa will reportedly be split into two tiers. Most bachelor's and master's graduates will be allowed 18 months to stay and work, down from two years. Graduates of universities with strong immigration compliance records and good graduate job outcomes will keep two years. Doctoral graduates will keep their existing three years. Applications submitted before 1 January 2027 will be decided under the current rules.

Unanswered questions

The UK government has not yet published the list of qualifying universities or the exact criteria that will be used. The change partly reverses an earlier plan to cut the visa to 18 months for everyone.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Nigeria was the third-largest source of sponsored study visas to the UK in the year to June 2026, with more than 26,000 main applicants, behind only China and India. More than 20,000 Nigerians were granted permission on the Graduate route in the same period. Prospective students are being advised to check a university's immigration compliance record and graduate employment outcomes, as well as fees and reputation.

Background

The Graduate visa cannot be extended, so graduates who want to stay longer must switch to another route, such as the Skilled Worker visa. The UK has tightened immigration rules in recent years, including restrictions on bringing family members on student visas.

  • Most graduates will get 18 months instead of two years from January 2027.
  • Graduates of high-performing universities keep two years.
  • The list of qualifying universities has not been published.

US Congress to hold hearing on Nigeria's protection of Christians and the Middle Belt

10 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

A US House subcommittee chaired by Congressman Chris Smith will hold a hearing on Thursday, 12 November, to assess Nigeria's efforts to protect Christians and curb violence in the Middle Belt, with State Department officials due to testify.

Read more

A United States congressional committee will hold a hearing this week to review Nigeria's record on protecting Christians and tackling violence in the Middle Belt.

The hearing

The House Foreign Affairs Africa Subcommittee will hold the session on Thursday, 12 November 2026, according to a notice from the office of its chairman, Republican Congressman Chris Smith of New Jersey, issued on Monday. The hearing is titled 'One Year Later: Assessing Nigeria's Efforts to Protect Christians and Secure the Middle Belt'.

What will be discussed

The hearing will examine religious violence by extremist groups, including Boko Haram, and how effectively the Nigerian government has responded to attacks on vulnerable communities. It will also consider what measures the United States could press for to make sure perpetrators are held accountable and to prevent future attacks on Christians and non-radical Muslims. The first panel will hear from two senior State Department officials, Frank Garcia, assistant secretary of state for African affairs, and Riley Barnes, assistant secretary of state for democracy, human rights and labour, followed by outside experts.

Background

The title refers to developments around a year ago, when the US designated Nigeria a 'Country of Particular Concern' over religious freedom. The Nigerian government has rejected claims of a targeted genocide against Christians, saying insecurity affects people of all faiths, and has pointed to military operations against armed groups. Attacks have continued in states such as Plateau and Benue, including a deadly attack in Barkin Ladi at the weekend.

Republican congressman files articles of impeachment against Pete Hegseth over Iran war

10 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Republican Congressman Thomas Massie has introduced articles of impeachment against US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, accusing him of waging war against Iran without congressional authorisation, as criticism of the conflict grows after the midterms.

Read more

Articles of impeachment have been introduced in the US House of Representatives against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

What happened

Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky brought the resolution on Monday, 9 November 2026, as Congress returned for a 'lame-duck' session after the midterm elections. Massie accused Hegseth of high crimes and misdemeanours and abuse of power, alleging that he violated the War Powers Resolution by carrying out military operations against Iran and other countries without a declaration of war or specific authorisation from Congress.

Background

The United States has been in a conflict with Iran since late February, with US forces carrying out strikes on Iranian targets as recently as last week. President Donald Trump has insisted the US is not at war, but has acknowledged that the conflict contributed to his party's heavy losses in the midterms, partly by pushing up petrol prices.

What happens next

Impeachment would require a majority vote in the House, followed by a trial in the Senate.

Actress Toukie Smith, mother of Robert De Niro's twins, dies at 74

10 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Toukie Smith, known for the TV sitcom '227' and 'Miami Vice', has died in New York at 74 after a long battle with multiple sclerosis, her twins Airyn and Julian said.

Read more

American actress and model Toukie Smith has died at the age of 74.

What happened

Her twins, Airyn and Julian, whom she shared with her former partner, actor Robert De Niro, said she died in New York on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, after a long and courageous battle with multiple sclerosis. The news was reported on Tuesday, 10 November.

Her career

Smith was best known for playing Eva Rawley on the NBC sitcom '227', which she joined in 1989, first as a guest star. She also appeared in 'Miami Vice' and worked as a model, including in campaigns for Yves Saint Laurent. Born Dorris Smith in 1952, she took her stage name from a nickname given to her by her grandmother.

Background

Smith and De Niro began dating in 1988, and their twins were born via a surrogate. The couple's on-off relationship ended in 1996.

White House briefly locked down after car crashes into barrier near Lafayette Park

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The White House was briefly locked down after a driver crashed a car into a security barrier at Lafayette Park and clashed with Secret Service agents and National Guard troops. Two people were arrested; President Trump was inside.

Read more

The White House was briefly placed on lockdown on Monday, 9 November 2026, after a car crashed into a security barrier near the complex.

What happened

Shortly before 10am, a silver sedan mounted the pavement and hit a sign at the northern end of Lafayette Park, directly opposite the White House, according to reports. Witnesses said the driver got out and became involved in a physical confrontation with Secret Service agents and National Guard troops. Two people were arrested. The press area was locked down, reporters were ordered indoors, and roads around the park were closed. President Donald Trump was in the White House at the time. Officials said the driver was combative but was quickly arrested.

Background

The incident came days after the Secret Service arrested an armed man near the White House who allegedly said voices had told him to kill the President. Officials have said the White House has seen a rise in security incidents, many linked to mental health problems. No motive for Monday's crash was given, and there were no reports of serious injuries.
