Governor Peter Mbah claims he started the 'APC Ka Mma' campaign song

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Enugu State governor Peter Mbah has sparked reactions after claiming credit for a popular campaign song.

What he said

In comments shared on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Mbah reportedly said he was the first person to start singing 'APC Ka Mma', an Igbo phrase meaning 'APC is better'. He added that he was not sure other political parties had been given copyright permission to sing it. The song has become popular at campaign events, and supporters of other parties have adapted it, including a recent viral version in which a Catholic priest led a congregation in singing that Peter Obi's party is better.

Background

Mbah was elected governor of Enugu State in 2023 on the platform of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) and later joined the All Progressives Congress (APC). He is one of the South East governors who have declared support for President Bola Tinubu's re-election, and Enugu has hosted major APC rallies. The South East is expected to be a key battleground between Tinubu and Obi, a former Anambra governor.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the comment, saying a campaign song should not be used to restrict political expression and that the governor should focus on economic and security problems. Others saw it as a joke and said the song's popularity showed the APC was gaining ground in the region.

INEC holds nationwide mock accreditation in 17,656 polling units

28 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

INEC is conducting a mock voter accreditation and result transmission exercise across 17,656 polling units in all 36 states and the FCT on Saturday. It is testing its upgraded BVAS machines and security arrangements.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) is conducting a nationwide mock voter accreditation and result transmission exercise on Saturday, 28 November 2026.

The exercise

The test is taking place at 17,656 polling units across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory. It is designed to test INEC's upgraded Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS), the result transmission process and election security arrangements under simulated election-day conditions. Sample ballot papers are being used.

Why it matters

The exercise comes ahead of the presidential and National Assembly elections on 16 January 2027. INEC has faced criticism in past elections over delays in uploading results, and this week the commission named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence.

Lagos adjusts

Lagos State cancelled its monthly environmental sanitation exercise, which had been scheduled for the same morning, so that residents could take part.

Presidential election turnout has fallen from 69% to 27% since 2003

28 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has dropped from a Fourth Republic high of about 69% in 2003 to under 27% in 2023, according to figures compiled by a statistics platform. The decline has continued for five straight elections.

Read more

Voter turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has fallen sharply over the past two decades, according to figures shared by a statistics platform this week.

The numbers

Measured as total votes cast as a share of registered voters, turnout reached a Fourth Republic high of 69.08% in 2003. It then fell to 57.49% in 2007 and 53.68% in 2011, and continued to decline in each election after that, reaching just 26.71% in 2023. That is a drop of more than 42 percentage points in 20 years.

Why it matters

The figures come as parties and INEC step up efforts to get Nigerians to collect their Permanent Voter Cards and vote in the 16 January 2027 elections. INEC is also running a nationwide mock accreditation exercise on Saturday to test its systems.

Background

Concerns about insecurity, logistics and confidence in the electoral process have often been cited as reasons for low turnout. INEC this week named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence.
