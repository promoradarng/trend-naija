Nigerian man reportedly hacked to death in South Africa as xenophobic attacks continue

23 Oct 2026, 19:53 BST

A man believed to be a Nigerian has reportedly been killed by a mob in Durban, South Africa, as attacks on foreigners in the country go on. Police in KwaZulu-Natal say a group of about 50 people was marching along Dr Pixley kaSeme Street in the city centre on Thursday, 22 October, when two men broke away, approached a man standing by the roadside and attacked him. He was declared dead at the scene. Police have not named the victim, describing him only as a foreign national in his late 30s or early 40s. A source told SaharaReporters that he was Nigerian and that he was hacked to death, but this has not been independently confirmed. Videos circulating online show a crowd armed with sticks and other weapons chasing a man in a red top and blue jeans through an open street in Durban, knocking him down and beating him repeatedly. Two security officers appear to try to step in but pull back as the crowd grows. It is not yet clear whether the videos show the same attack police are investigating. Police have opened a murder case and say they are following several leads to identify and arrest the suspects. No arrests have been announced. The killing comes amid a wave of anti-immigrant protests and violence that spread across South Africa this year, with groups demanding that foreigners leave the country. Nigeria's ambassador-designate, Femi Fani-Kayode, said in September that at least 12 Nigerians had been killed in South Africa in 2026, and 112 since 2022, with no convictions. Nigeria's Foreign Affairs Ministry says more than 2,000 Nigerians have returned home on evacuation flights since June, and has urged those still in South Africa to stay vigilant, avoid affected areas and keep in touch with the High Commission and consulate.

  • Victim not yet named; Nigerian nationality not officially confirmed
  • At least 12 Nigerians killed in South Africa this year, says envoy

"8 out of every 10 obituaries in the Southeast are men!" Woman raises alarm at Imo funeral over pressure on Igbo men

26 Oct 2026, 05:02 GMT

A woman reportedly lost her cool at a funeral in Imo State, saying eight out of every ten obituaries she sees in the Southeast are of men. Her figure has not been independently verified, but her outburst has sparked a wider conversation about the pressures many men face in silence.

Read more

A woman reportedly lost her cool during a funeral ceremony in Imo State as she raised serious concerns about what she believes is an alarming number of men dying prematurely across Ala Igbo.

According to her, eight out of every ten obituaries she comes across in the Southeast are those of men. Although this figure has not been independently verified, her emotional outburst has raised an important conversation about the struggles many men face in silence.

She argued that financial responsibilities, marital problems, emotional stress, family expectations and the constant pressure to provide are gradually wearing many men down.

In her view, some men are carrying burdens that nobody seems interested in discussing. They are expected to pay the bills, provide for their wives and children, support extended family members and remain strong regardless of what they are going through.

She also accused some wives of making matters worse through constant domestic conflict, excessive financial demands and a lack of emotional support.

However, it would be unfair to blame women generally for men's deaths without credible evidence. Health conditions, economic hardship, lifestyle factors and access to medical care must also be considered.

But there is a serious question here that deserves attention.

When a man is struggling financially, who checks on his mental health? When he is emotionally exhausted, who listens to him? When he can no longer meet everyone's expectations, does his family support him or make him feel like a failure?

In many homes, men are expected to be strong even when everything around them is falling apart.

Marriage should be a place of peace and mutual support, not another source of unbearable pressure for either partner.

If this woman's observation reflects a wider problem, then Igbo communities need to examine the health, welfare and life expectancy of their men more seriously.
