Cars damaged as suspected thugs reportedly attack APC rally in Katsina

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

An All Progressives Congress (APC) campaign rally in Malumfashi Local Government Area of Katsina State was reportedly disrupted on Wednesday, 28 October 2026, when a group of suspected thugs attacked party members and their vehicles.

What happened

According to reports, APC supporters had gathered to campaign for the party's candidates ahead of the 2027 general elections when the attackers arrived. They were said to be chanting a Hausa phrase meaning 'we do not agree' as they threw stones and other objects at the crowd. Several cars were damaged, with windscreens and windows smashed, and the attack reportedly caused panic among motorists and passengers in the area. Several people are said to have suffered minor injuries, although no exact number has been given.

Official response

The Katsina State Police Command had not commented on the incident at the time of the report, and no arrests have been announced. The APC has also not issued a public statement on who it believes was responsible.

Background

Katsina, the home state of the late former President Muhammadu Buhari, is an APC stronghold, but the 2027 contest in the North West is expected to be keenly fought, with the ADC, NDC and other parties also campaigning hard for votes. Campaigns for the 2027 elections formally began this month, and security agencies have repeatedly warned parties against using thugs. Similar clashes at rallies have been reported in other states in recent weeks.

What happens next

Observers are calling on the police to investigate and on all parties to restrain their supporters as campaigning intensifies across the North.

Thugs attack Peter Obi's convoy in Calabar, one arrested

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Suspected thugs attacked NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi's convoy in Calabar on Wednesday, throwing stones and bottles. Police say one suspect has been arrested. Obi blamed APC supporters, a claim the party has not answered.

Read more

The convoy of Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate Peter Obi was attacked by suspected political thugs in Calabar, Cross River State, on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

What happened

According to reports, the attack happened around the Watt Market area as Obi's convoy moved through the city towards a hotel where the party had scheduled a presidential town hall meeting. Obi was in Calabar with his wife, Margaret, and his running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso. Attackers threw stones, bottles and other objects at vehicles and supporters who had lined the route. Several supporters were reportedly injured and vehicles were damaged. An aide to Obi, Esther Umoh, said one supporter was hit by a stray bullet.

Police response

The Cross River State Police Command confirmed the incident and said a 27-year-old suspect had been arrested. Police said investigations were continuing to identify those behind the attack.

What Obi said

Speaking after the incident, Obi alleged that APC supporters threw stones at his convoy, forcing people to run for safety. He said such attacks happen because Nigerians tolerate them, and vowed that it would not be allowed in 2027. The APC had not responded to the claim at the time of reporting.

Background

Cross River is governed by the APC. Attacks on campaign events have increased as the January 2027 elections approach. Earlier this week, ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar accused the DSS of plotting to disrupt his Sokoto rally, and APC supporters were attacked ahead of a rally in Katsina. Civil society groups and religious leaders have urged all parties to restrain their supporters.

Obasanjo's son Olujuwon joins pro-Tinubu movement, backs President for 2027

4 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Olujuwon Obasanjo, a son of former President Olusegun Obasanjo, has reportedly joined the PBAT Door-to-Door Movement as Special Adviser on Political Affairs and pledged to support President Tinubu's re-election. His father has been a fierce critic of the government.

Read more

A son of former President Olusegun Obasanjo, Olujuwon Obasanjo, has thrown his support behind President Bola Tinubu's bid for a second term.

What happened

According to reports on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Olujuwon joined the PBAT Door-to-Door Movement as Special Adviser on Political Affairs during a visit to its headquarters in Abuja, where he was received by the National Coordinator, Sunday Asuku, and shown around the facility. He described the appointment as an honour, praised the movement's community outreach and said he had followed its work for some time.

What he said

Olujuwon reportedly said the President had started on a good note and urged Nigerians, especially young people, to support the continuation of the government's reforms. He said the country had made progress in several areas and pledged to contribute to the President's re-election.

Background

The move stands out because his father has been one of the government's harshest critics. Last week, the former President described the government's borrowing as 'absolute nonsense'. The PBAT movement, whose grand patron is former Niger Delta militant leader Government Ekpemupolo (Tompolo), recently launched its Ogun State chapter and donated 26 campaign vehicles. Members of political families supporting different sides is common in Nigerian politics.

How people reacted

APC supporters welcomed the news, while some critics said it would not change the former President's views or influence voters in Ogun State, where both Obasanjo and the President's allies are active.

Court sacks Nasarawa ADC governorship candidate; Rivers APC candidate's case adjourned

4 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has nullified the nomination of retired Major General Nuhu Angbazo as the ADC's Nasarawa governorship candidate and ordered a fresh primary. In Port Harcourt, a suit seeking to disqualify Rivers APC candidate Kingsley Chinda was adjourned to 10 December.

Read more

Two court cases involving governorship candidates for the 2027 elections made news on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

Nasarawa ADC candidate sacked

A Federal High Court in Abuja reportedly nullified the nomination of retired Major General Nuhu Angbazo as the governorship candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) in Nasarawa State. Justice Mohammed Umar ruled that the primary election that produced him was inconclusive, and ordered the party to hold a fresh primary in Karu Local Government Area within 14 days. The court also barred INEC from recognising Angbazo as the party's candidate. The case was filed by former Minister of Environment Mohammed Abdullahi, who took part in the primary along with Angbazo and Senator Mohammed Onawo. Angbazo can appeal the judgment.

Rivers APC case adjourned

In Port Harcourt, a Federal High Court fixed 10 December for hearing in a suit seeking to disqualify Kingsley Chinda, the All Progressives Congress (APC) governorship candidate in Rivers State. The plaintiff, Samuel Amatonjie, argues that Chinda did not properly leave the PDP for the APC under the Constitution, and has asked the court to compel INEC, the Speaker of the House of Representatives and the Clerk of the National Assembly to produce documents on his defection. Chinda has not commented publicly on the case.

Background

The 2027 party primaries have produced many disputes, and courts have already ordered changes to several candidacies, including the Imo West senatorial ticket. INEC recently removed some candidates from its list, citing a Supreme Court ruling on party membership registers.

CAN disowns cleric claiming to be its general secretary, says it backs no candidate

4 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Christian Association of Nigeria says Archbishop Chivir Chianson is not its general secretary and cannot speak for it, after he linked the body to church mobilisation for a candidate. CAN president Daniel Okoh warned the public to disregard such claims.

Read more

The Christian Association of Nigeria (CAN) has distanced itself from a cleric who presented himself as its general secretary and tried to link the body to support for a candidate in the 2027 elections.

What CAN said

In a statement signed by its president, Archbishop Daniel Okoh, and reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, CAN said Archbishop Chivir Chianson does not hold any position in the association and has no authority to speak or act on its behalf. It said he had tried to identify himself as CAN's general secretary and to associate the body with what he described as a church mobilisation in support of a political candidate. Okoh warned the public and the media to disregard any claim that Chianson or his group represents CAN.

Neutrality

The statement was widely read as reaffirming CAN's position that it does not endorse any candidate in the race between President Bola Tinubu of the APC, Atiku Abubakar of the ADC and Peter Obi of the NDC.

Background

Religious leaders and groups have become increasingly involved in the 2027 campaign. A bishops' conference has reportedly endorsed Tinubu for a second term, while a video of a Catholic priest leading his congregation in a song for Obi and Kwankwaso went viral this week. Religion has long been a sensitive issue in Nigerian elections, especially after the APC's Muslim-Muslim ticket in 2023.

Why it matters

CAN is the umbrella body for most Christian denominations in Nigeria, and its position can influence many voters.
