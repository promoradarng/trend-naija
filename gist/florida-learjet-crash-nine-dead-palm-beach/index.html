Nine killed as Bahamas-bound private jet plunges into Florida roadside

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

A private jet carrying nine people crashed beside a road in Palm Beach County, Florida, on Sunday, 1 November 2026, killing everyone on board in a fireball that was caught on video by passing motorists.

What happened

US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the Learjet 60 went down at about 12.50pm local time and described the crash as a horrific tragedy. The aircraft had taken off from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was flying to the Bahamas. Florida governor Ron DeSantis said there were no immediate reports of anyone on the ground being hurt, and described the crash as cataclysmic, saying the plane looked like a missile coming down and left a large crater by the roadside. Reports said the site was a few miles from President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago estate.

The final minutes

According to air traffic control recordings and flight tracking data, the jet was cruising at about 35,000 feet when the pilots reported light ice, moderate turbulence and rain, and asked to climb a further 2,000 feet. Shortly afterwards the plane went into a steep dive. The pilot declared a mayday, reporting that the aircraft was descending at 10,000 and then 20,000 feet per minute in an overspeed condition, before saying the jet was going to crash. Tracking data showed the rate of descent at one point exceeded 32,000 feet per minute.

The investigation

The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation, supported by a team from the Federal Aviation Administration. The airport confirmed it was a private flight but did not say who was on board. Records list the plane's owner as a company registered in Delaware. Heavy rain had hit the region on Sunday, and a flood watch was in place until Monday afternoon.

Co-pilot identified

On Monday, relatives identified the co-pilot as 20-year-old Nathalia Gadelha, who grew up in Palm Beach County. She earned her private pilot's licence at 17, in October 2023, before she could even drive, and had trained at a flight school with a branch in Pompano Beach. She had said she dreamed of becoming a commercial airline pilot. The other victims have not yet been publicly named.

Victims named

On Monday, relatives identified the passengers as a 44-year-old technology entrepreneur, his wife and their two children, aged two and four, together with two close friends of the family and the children's nanny. The two pilots, an experienced aviator and his 20-year-old co-pilot, also died. In a statement, the families thanked first responders and the Palm Beach County community and said their hearts were also with the pilots' families.

What happens next

Investigators are expected to examine the wreckage, the weather and the aircraft's maintenance history.

  • All nine people on board the Learjet 60 were killed.
  • The pilot reported ice and turbulence before a steep dive.
  • The co-pilot was a 20-year-old from Palm Beach County.

Canada resumes Express Entry draws, invites 1,999 for permanent residence

24 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Canada has resumed Express Entry draws, inviting 1,999 provincial nominees to apply for permanent residence on 23 November. It was the first draw under new ranking rules that reward Canadian work experience and high-wage job offers.

Read more

Canada has resumed its Express Entry draws after a short pause, issuing 1,999 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) for permanent residence to provincial nominees on 23 November 2026.

The draw

According to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada, candidates needed a minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score of 673 to be invited, and must have created their profile before 31 July 2026.

New rules

It was the first draw since major changes to the CRS took effect on 3 November 2026. Candidates can now earn extra points through a new Labour Market Integration Factor, which rewards Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation.

Why it matters

Express Entry is one of Canada's main routes to permanent residence for skilled workers.

Iran reportedly gives singer Parastoo Ahmadi 74 lashes for performing without hijab

24 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Iranian authorities have reportedly flogged singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her team over an online concert she performed without a headscarf. Rights groups say they also received two-year bans on artistic work.

Read more

Iranian authorities have reportedly administered 74 lashes to the singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her production team over a concert she performed without wearing the mandatory Islamic headscarf.

What happened

According to a human rights lawyer and monitoring groups, the punishment was carried out on Monday, 23 November 2026, at the prosecutor's office in the city of Qom.

The concert

The case stems from a virtual concert Ahmadi recorded in December 2024 at a historic caravanserai in Qom and streamed on YouTube. In the video, which was widely viewed, she performed traditional Persian music with male musicians while wearing a sleeveless dress with her hair uncovered, defying rules that ban women from singing solo in public and appearing without a hijab.

The sentence

A court found Ahmadi and four male musicians and crew members guilty of offending public decency by publishing what it called immoral content online. As well as the lashes, each of the five was reportedly given a two-year ban on artistic activity.

US adds 24 more Nigerians to 'worst of the worst' deportation list

The US Department of Homeland Security has reportedly added 24 Nigerians to its list of foreign nationals arrested for serious crimes, bringing the number of Nigerians on it to 157. The allegations range from fraud to drug offences.

Read more

The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has reportedly added 24 Nigerians to its deportation list, bringing the total number of Nigerian nationals named to 157.

The list

DHS updated its 'Worst of the Worst' initiative on Monday, 23 November 2026. The list identifies foreign nationals arrested by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) for what it describes as serious crimes, and now includes more than 45,000 people.

The allegations

The people listed face allegations ranging from drug and weapons offences and violent crimes to fraud and the misuse of public benefits. Many of the Nigerians named were arrested over alleged wire fraud, mail fraud or impersonation in states including Texas, Virginia, New Jersey, Georgia and Indiana.

Background

Being named on the list does not mean a person has been convicted.
