Nine killed as Bahamas-bound private jet plunges into Florida roadside

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

A private jet carrying nine people crashed beside a road in Palm Beach County, Florida, on Sunday, 1 November 2026, killing everyone on board in a fireball that was caught on video by passing motorists.

What happened

US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the Learjet 60 went down at about 12.50pm local time and described the crash as a horrific tragedy. The aircraft had taken off from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was flying to the Bahamas. Florida governor Ron DeSantis said there were no immediate reports of anyone on the ground being hurt, and described the crash as cataclysmic, saying the plane looked like a missile coming down and left a large crater by the roadside. Reports said the site was a few miles from President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago estate.

The final minutes

According to air traffic control recordings and flight tracking data, the jet was cruising at about 35,000 feet when the pilots reported light ice, moderate turbulence and rain, and asked to climb a further 2,000 feet. Shortly afterwards the plane went into a steep dive. The pilot declared a mayday, reporting that the aircraft was descending at 10,000 and then 20,000 feet per minute in an overspeed condition, before saying the jet was going to crash. Tracking data showed the rate of descent at one point exceeded 32,000 feet per minute.

The investigation

The National Transportation Safety Board is leading the investigation, supported by a team from the Federal Aviation Administration. The airport confirmed it was a private flight but did not say who was on board. Records list the plane's owner as a company registered in Delaware. Heavy rain had hit the region on Sunday, and a flood watch was in place until Monday afternoon.

Co-pilot identified

On Monday, relatives identified the co-pilot as 20-year-old Nathalia Gadelha, who grew up in Palm Beach County. She earned her private pilot's licence at 17, in October 2023, before she could even drive, and had trained at a flight school with a branch in Pompano Beach. She had said she dreamed of becoming a commercial airline pilot. The other victims have not yet been publicly named.

Victims named

On Monday, relatives identified the passengers as a 44-year-old technology entrepreneur, his wife and their two children, aged two and four, together with two close friends of the family and the children's nanny. The two pilots, an experienced aviator and his 20-year-old co-pilot, also died. In a statement, the families thanked first responders and the Palm Beach County community and said their hearts were also with the pilots' families.

What happens next

Investigators are expected to examine the wreckage, the weather and the aircraft's maintenance history.

  • The pilot reported ice and turbulence before a steep dive.
  • The co-pilot was a 20-year-old from Palm Beach County.

Zambia and Congo pull out of Miss Universe over US travel rules; Nigeria's queen arrives

15 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Two African countries have withdrawn from the 75th Miss Universe pageant because their contestants could not get permission to travel to Puerto Rico, a US territory.

Who is affected

According to reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Zambia and the Republic of the Congo have pulled out, while representatives from Zimbabwe and South Sudan were still awaiting decisions ahead of the final on 24 November. Miss Universe Zambia said its representative, a 25-year-old registered nurse who won the national title in October, could not secure the required travel authorisation, and blamed US travel restrictions rather than any failing by her or the organisers. The Republic of the Congo had been due to send its first ever Miss Universe contestant.

Nigeria's contestant

Nigeria is also affected by partial US entry restrictions, but its representative, Danielle Adewusi, arrived in San Juan on 9 November and has joined the preliminary activities.

Background

Because Puerto Rico is a US territory, contestants travelling there must meet US immigration requirements.

His death

Tracy died on 12 November 2026, according to reports. No official cause of death has been made public. His grandson shared an emotional tribute online.

Tracy built his career in sales, marketing, real estate and consulting before founding Brian Tracy International. When Congo turned on Lumumba, Egypt did not abandon his family: how Nasser sent a colonel to save his children

14 Nov 2026, 14:31 GMT

As Patrice Lumumba's government collapsed and his life came under threat, Egypt's President Gamal Abdel Nasser ordered a secret mission led by Colonel Saad El-Din El-Shazly to get Lumumba's children out of Congo. They grew up in Cairo under Egypt's protection.

Read more

It is one of the lesser-known chapters of Africa's liberation story, and a remarkable act of solidarity between two African nations.

A leader in danger

Patrice Lumumba, Congo's first Prime Minister after independence from Belgium in 1960, was overthrown within months and placed under house arrest. In January 1961 he was handed over to his enemies and assassinated, one of the most shocking political killings in African history.

As Congo descended into chaos, Lumumba's family was in grave danger, and his wife Pauline and their children had nowhere safe to turn.

Nasser's secret mission

Egypt did not look away. On the direct orders of President Gamal Abdel Nasser, Colonel Saad El-Din El-Shazly, who later became one of Egypt's most celebrated military commanders, took on the task of getting Lumumba's children safely out of the country.

El-Shazly knew Congo well. He had commanded the Egyptian battalion sent there in 1960 as part of the international force during the independence crisis, at a time when Egypt firmly backed Lumumba.

The operation was carried out in complete secrecy. Lumumba's children, including his eldest son François, were smuggled out of Congo and flown to Cairo, where they arrived safely while their father was still under arrest.

A new home in Cairo

After Lumumba's death, Pauline joined the children in Egypt. Nasser offered the family protection and support, and the Egyptian government helped with their housing, schooling and other needs.

Lumumba's children grew up in Egypt, which became not just a place of refuge but a home that embraced their father's cause as a symbol of African liberation.

Pauline later spent time in Europe before returning to Congo in the late 1960s, after Lumumba began to be honoured as a national hero. She settled in Kinshasa.

Africa looking out for its own

The story is a reminder that Egypt was not just a witness to Africa's independence struggles, but an active partner in them. When Lumumba's family was in danger, an African nation stepped in to protect them.
