Court voids ADC governorship nomination in Nasarawa, orders fresh primary in Karu

6 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has set aside the nomination of retired Major General Nuhu Angbazo as the African Democratic Congress (ADC) governorship candidate in Nasarawa State.

The judgment

In a ruling reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Justice Mohammed Umar held that the party's governorship primary was inconclusive because results from Karu Local Government Area were not included. The judge said shutting out party members in Karu meant the exercise could not validly produce a candidate. The case was brought by a former Minister of Environment, Mohammed Abdullahi, who took part in the primary alongside Angbazo and Senator Mohammed Onawo, who represents Nasarawa South.

Orders

The court ordered the ADC to hold a fresh primary in Karu, restrained Angbazo from presenting himself as the party's candidate, and barred the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) from recognising him as such. The plaintiff's lawyer welcomed the ruling and said the party must now complete the primary properly before any candidate can be recognised.

Background

The ruling adds to the ADC's legal troubles. This week, another Federal High Court judgment removed the party's David Mark-led national leadership and voided Atiku Abubakar's presidential nomination, a ruling the party has said it will challenge. Nasarawa is governed by the APC's Abdullahi Sule, who is completing his second term. Angbazo has not publicly said whether he will appeal.

What happens next

Unless the ruling is overturned on appeal, the ADC will have to return to Karu to finish the primary before it can field a governorship candidate in the state.

  • The court said the primary left out Karu LGA.
  • INEC was barred from recognising Angbazo.
  • The ADC must hold a fresh primary in Karu.

"Political immaturity": Why attack Peter Obi when you are not contesting against him? Rep Ogene questions Umahi, Soludo

8 Nov 2026, 04:33 GMT

Rep Afam Victor Ogene has accused Works Minister David Umahi and Anambra Governor Charles Soludo of "political immaturity" for repeatedly attacking Peter Obi, pointing out that South-West leaders are not attacking Governor Seyi Makinde over his own presidential ambition.

Read more

A member of the House of Representatives, Hon. Afam Victor Ogene, has questioned what he sees as growing political hostility towards Peter Obi, the NDC presidential candidate and Labour Party candidate in 2023, particularly from prominent politicians in the South-East.

A tale of two regions

Ogene drew attention to what he described as a striking difference between the political atmosphere in the South-West and the South-East ahead of the 2027 general elections.

According to him, governors and ministers from the South-West are not publicly attacking Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde over his perceived presidential ambition, even though he could challenge President Bola Tinubu in 2027.

In the South-East, however, he questioned why Minister of Works David Umahi and Anambra State Governor Charles Soludo keep directing criticism at Obi, even though neither of them is contesting the presidency against him.

"Southwest governors and ministers are not attacking Seyi Makinde for contesting against Tinubu, but Umahi and Soludo keep attacking Peter Obi even when none of them is contesting. That's political immaturity," Ogene said.

Rivalry or genuine criticism?

His remarks raise a wider question about the nature of political rivalry in Nigeria, particularly in the South-East, where disagreements among prominent figures often attract public attention.

Why should politicians who are not directly competing against Obi appear more concerned about his political future than those who may eventually face him at the ballot box?

For Ogene, the issue goes beyond ordinary disagreement. His criticism suggests some leaders may be letting personal rivalry and party differences overshadow mutual respect.

The comparison with the South-West is particularly interesting. Despite belonging to different parties and having different presidential interests, leaders in that region have often managed their disagreements while keeping working relationships.

Obi is not above scrutiny

Every politician has the right to criticise another, and Obi, as an active national figure, is equally open to public scrutiny. Umahi has recently questioned whether Obi is better than him or Pius Anyim, while Soludo has backed President Tinubu. Ogene's argument raises the question of whether such criticism is based on genuine policy differences or unnecessary rivalry.

Political disagreements are unavoidable in a democracy, but how leaders handle them says a great deal about their political maturity.

"I established my university before becoming Vice-President": Atiku fires back at Tinubu

8 Nov 2026, 03:13 GMT

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has responded to President Bola Tinubu's questions about how he built a university while in government, saying his journey into education began in 1993 with ABTI Academy, six years before he took office, and that his business record is open to scrutiny.

Read more

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has responded to President Bola Tinubu over questions about his investment in education, insisting that the foundation of his educational institution was laid long before he became Vice-President.

Atiku's response

Atiku, who served as Vice-President from 1999 to 2007, said the suggestion that he established a university with resources acquired while in public office was misleading.

According to him, his journey into education began in 1993, when he established ABTI Academy, six years before he took office as Vice-President under former President Olusegun Obasanjo.

He made the clarification after remarks attributed to President Tinubu questioning how he was able to set up a university while serving in government.

Why the dates matter

Atiku maintained that his investment in education was part of his business activities before he entered the country's second-highest office. By the time he became Vice-President in May 1999, he says, he had already begun building what would eventually grow into a major private institution.

That institution later became the American University of Nigeria (AUN) in Yola, Adamawa State. The university itself was established in the mid-2000s, during his time as Vice-President, which is why questions about its funding and expansion continue to come up.

Atiku also insisted that his business activities and investments are open to public scrutiny, and that anyone genuinely interested in the source and history of his wealth can examine his record.

A growing rivalry

The exchange adds another chapter to the rivalry between President Tinubu and the former Vice-President, two prominent figures whose differences have shaped several national debates.

Beyond the personal disagreement, the controversy raises a question Nigerians have long asked of their leaders: how did those who held public office acquire their wealth, and can they give a clear account of their investments?

The fact that an educational project began before someone took office does not, on its own, settle every question about its funding or later expansion. Those questions would require documentary evidence and a proper look at the institution's financial history.

The road to 2027

As activities ahead of the 2027 general election gather pace, exchanges like this between leading political figures are likely to attract even more attention.

For now, Atiku has made his position clear: his educational investment began in 1993, well before he became Vice-President, and he is prepared to have his business record examined.

Crowd erupts in 'Obi' chants as Deputy Speaker Ben Kalu campaigns for Tinubu at Item Okpi festival in Aba

7 Nov 2026, 20:53 GMT

As Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu urged the crowd at the Item Okpi cultural festival in Aba to back President Tinubu, sections of the audience reportedly broke into loud chants of "Obi! Obi! Obi!", even as he declared, "On your mandate we shall stand."

Read more

What was meant to be a moment of political mobilisation for President Bola Ahmed Tinubu reportedly took an unexpected turn at the Item Okpi cultural festival in Aba, Abia State, when members of the crowd began chanting the name of Peter Obi.

"Obi! Obi! Obi!"

The Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives, Benjamin Kalu, was addressing a large gathering at the cultural event when he turned to national politics and began appealing to the people to support President Tinubu.

The response from sections of the audience was not what he appeared to be hoping for. As Kalu spoke in support of the President, loud chants of "Obi! Obi! Obi!" reportedly rose from the crowd.

Even when the Deputy Speaker repeated the familiar declaration, "On your mandate we shall stand," the chants continued, creating an unusual scene in which the politician's message and the crowd's response seemed to be moving in opposite directions.

Why it matters

The moment has drawn attention because of Abia State's political significance and the continuing debate about the popularity of Nigeria's major political figures ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Peter Obi, who contested the 2023 presidential election under the Labour Party and is now the NDC presidential candidate, continues to command a strong following in sections of the electorate, particularly in the South-East.

For Kalu, an influential APC politician from Abia State, the incident highlights the challenge of promoting the ruling party's candidate in a region where political loyalties remain divided.

A cultural festival turned political

Chants from a crowd at a cultural gathering are not a scientific measure of how a state or region will vote. But public reactions like this can still offer a glimpse into the political conversations happening among ordinary Nigerians.

What makes this incident stand out is the setting. The gathering was mainly a cultural celebration, yet it became a scene of competing political expressions once the Deputy Speaker raised the subject of presidential support.

Tinubu asks how Atiku built a university while he was vice-president

7 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

At an APC rally in Port Harcourt, President Tinubu said he was cleaning up a mess created by people complaining about hunger, naming Atiku Abubakar, and questioned how the ADC candidate built a university while in office.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has launched one of his sharpest attacks yet on Atiku Abubakar, presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC).

What he said

Speaking at an All Progressives Congress (APC) rally in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Tinubu dismissed critics who point to economic hardship under his government, saying he was cleaning up the mess left by those who came before him and that Atiku was one of them. He also questioned how Atiku had built a university while he was vice-president, a remark widely seen as raising questions about how the former vice-president acquired his wealth while in public office.

Background

Atiku served as vice-president from 1999 to 2007 under Olusegun Obasanjo. He founded the American University of Nigeria in Yola, Adamawa State, and has long said his wealth comes from legitimate private business interests. He has repeatedly blamed Tinubu's policies, including the way fuel subsidy removal was handled, for rising prices. Tinubu's Rivers visit follows his trip to Akwa Ibom on Friday, where he said the naira would have become worthless without his reforms. FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, whose Rainbow Coalition backs the President, is a key ally in Rivers, and Governor Siminalayi Fubara has also declared support for Tinubu.

Atiku replies

Later on Saturday, Atiku hit back in a statement issued by his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu. He said his investment in education began long before he entered public office, when he laid the foundation for ABTI Academy in 1993, which later grew into the American University of Nigeria. In the same statement he referred to a 1993 US court forfeiture involving funds in an account linked to Tinubu, a matter the President's supporters have long said was a civil case that did not involve any criminal conviction. Atiku also said Tinubu's policies had pushed millions of Nigerians into extreme poverty, and rejected the President's claim that the economy is being rescued.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027, and campaigns by all the major parties are expected to intensify in the coming weeks.
