Kenyan pastor sentenced to 200 years in prison over Shakahola starvation tragedy that claimed hundreds of lives

7 Nov 2026, 05:40 GMT

Kenyan preacher Paul Nthenge Mackenzie has reportedly been sentenced to 200 years in prison over the deaths of 191 members of his Good News International Church, including 190 children, in one of the most disturbing religious tragedies in Kenya's history.

The sentence

The Mombasa High Court handed down the sentence on Friday, 6 November 2026, following its ruling on 19 October in which Mackenzie and 28 other defendants were found guilty. The prosecution had earlier asked the court to impose the death penalty.

What happened in Shakahola

According to the prosecution, between 2021 and 2023 Mackenzie gathered his followers on a 325-hectare property in the Shakahola Forest, near the coastal town of Malindi in south-eastern Kenya.

There, he convinced them that starving themselves to death would allow them to meet Jesus.

Imagine the level of trust these families must have placed in their religious leader. Parents, children and entire households were drawn into a belief system that eventually turned deadly.

During the trial, the defence argued that the children had taken part in the fasting voluntarily. The judge rejected that argument.

How the tragedy came to light

The case first drew international attention in April 2023, when authorities discovered the first bodies buried in the forest.

What first looked like an isolated discovery soon turned into something far more horrifying. As investigators kept searching, more graves were uncovered. Eventually, more than 400 bodies were exhumed, revealing the enormous scale of the disaster.

More than 200 witnesses testified during the proceedings. Mackenzie had been in custody since 14 April 2023, when he surrendered to authorities after the first victims were found.

The children who died

Kenya, a country where religion plays an important part in everyday life, was deeply shaken by the discoveries.

For many, the most painful part of the story is the number of children who died. These were young lives that depended on adults for protection, yet they became victims of circumstances they could neither understand nor control.

A warning about blind obedience

The case is a troubling reminder of what can happen when people give up their ability to question religious authority, and it raises serious questions about religious manipulation and the protection of vulnerable people.

Faith is deeply personal, and millions find comfort, hope and direction in religion. But when a religious leader begins demanding actions that threaten human life, the consequences can be devastating.

Anthropic raises $81.3bn in IPO ahead of Nasdaq debut

19 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

AI company Anthropic has reportedly raised $81.3 billion in its initial public offering, and $107.3 billion including a separate private sale. The deal values the company at about $1.5 trillion.

Read more

Artificial intelligence company Anthropic has reportedly raised $81.3 billion through its initial public offering, ahead of its stock market debut.

The numbers

The company, which makes the Claude chatbot, sold 980 million shares at $83 each, above its earlier range of $70 to $78. Including a separate $26 billion private share sale, its combined fundraising reached $107.3 billion, valuing it at about $1.5 trillion.

Its shares were scheduled to begin trading on the Nasdaq exchange in New York on Thursday, 19 November 2026, under the ticker ANT.

A record?

The combined fundraising is more than the $86 billion raised when SpaceX went public in June, although the IPO alone was below that figure.

Why it matters

The listing is being watched as a test of investor confidence in AI after a difficult fortnight for the industry. Anthropic makes much of its money selling AI services to businesses.

Thousands march in Haiti to praise El Salvador's President Bukele as his troops take on gangs

18 Nov 2026, 14:49 GMT

Thousands of Haitians took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other cities on Tuesday to praise El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his soldiers, who are leading a tough new offensive against the gangs that have terrorised Haiti for years.

Read more

Thousands of people took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other parts of Haiti on Tuesday, praising El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his troops as they take on the country's powerful gangs.

"Viv Bukele"

Photos from the marches show huge crowds filling the streets, waving Haitian flags and holding up posters of the Salvadoran president. Many carried banners reading "Viv Bukele", Haitian Creole for "Long live Bukele".

Why Haitians are cheering

For years, armed gangs have controlled large parts of Port-au-Prince and beyond, killing, kidnapping and forcing hundreds of thousands of people from their homes.

Salvadoran troops have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an international mission, and recently launched their first major offensive against the gangs, with reports of heavy fighting and deaths.

Many marchers said Bukele is doing what the United Nations and the United States failed to do for years, while ordinary Haitians continued to suffer.

The Bukele model

Bukele became popular at home by cracking down hard on El Salvador's gangs, sharply reducing violence in a country once known as one of the most dangerous in the world. Many Haitians hope his forces can bring the same results.

His methods have also faced criticism from human rights groups, who say the crackdown in El Salvador involved mass arrests and abuses. But for Haitians who have lived in fear for years, many say any hope of relief is welcome.

Over to you

Can Bukele's tough approach finally free Haiti from the grip of gangs, and could other troubled countries learn from it?

VeryDarkMan accuses Davido of hypocrisy over photo with Seyi Tinubu

19 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

VeryDarkMan has called out Davido over a photo with Seyi Tinubu in which both appeared to raise their hands in a gesture he read as the APC's "4+4" slogan. He said it contradicts the singer's past stance against the APC.

Read more

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has reportedly called out Afrobeats star Davido after a photo of the singer with Seyi Tinubu, son of President Bola Tinubu, circulated online.

The photo

The picture showed Davido and Seyi Tinubu raising their hands in a gesture VDM interpreted as the APC's "4+4" slogan, which supporters use to call for a second term for President Tinubu in 2027.

What VDM said

VDM said the photo was significant given Davido's status and his previous statements that he would never support the All Progressives Congress (APC). He accused the singer of contradicting himself and questioned why he would pose in a picture that could be read as a political statement.

He also said that his past claims always end up being proved right, dismissing what he called propaganda against him.

Background

Davido had not responded to VDM's comments at the time of the reports.

Tems opens up about living with ADHD

19 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Grammy-winning singer Tems has spoken about how ADHD affects her relationships, saying she finds it hard to keep in touch with people and sometimes switches topics mid-conversation.

Read more

Nigerian singer Temilade Openiyi, known as Tems, has opened up about living with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and how it affects her relationships with family and friends.

What she said

In a radio interview in Lagos, the singer said keeping in touch with people is something she finds difficult, even though she has many supportive people around her.

She explained that ADHD sometimes makes her switch topics suddenly during conversations, and that she often forgets to reach out to people. She admitted she has not always been consistent in communicating with people who matter to her, but said she is trying.

Background

Tems leads the Nigerian nominees for the 2027 Grammy Awards with four nominations.
