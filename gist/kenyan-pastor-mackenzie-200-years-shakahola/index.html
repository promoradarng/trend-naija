Kenyan pastor sentenced to 200 years in prison over Shakahola starvation tragedy that claimed hundreds of lives

7 Nov 2026, 05:40 GMT

Kenyan preacher Paul Nthenge Mackenzie has reportedly been sentenced to 200 years in prison over the deaths of 191 members of his Good News International Church, including 190 children, in one of the most disturbing religious tragedies in Kenya's history.

The sentence

The Mombasa High Court handed down the sentence on Friday, 6 November 2026, following its ruling on 19 October in which Mackenzie and 28 other defendants were found guilty. The prosecution had earlier asked the court to impose the death penalty.

What happened in Shakahola

According to the prosecution, between 2021 and 2023 Mackenzie gathered his followers on a 325-hectare property in the Shakahola Forest, near the coastal town of Malindi in south-eastern Kenya.

There, he convinced them that starving themselves to death would allow them to meet Jesus.

Imagine the level of trust these families must have placed in their religious leader. Parents, children and entire households were drawn into a belief system that eventually turned deadly.

During the trial, the defence argued that the children had taken part in the fasting voluntarily. The judge rejected that argument.

How the tragedy came to light

The case first drew international attention in April 2023, when authorities discovered the first bodies buried in the forest.

What first looked like an isolated discovery soon turned into something far more horrifying. As investigators kept searching, more graves were uncovered. Eventually, more than 400 bodies were exhumed, revealing the enormous scale of the disaster.

More than 200 witnesses testified during the proceedings. Mackenzie had been in custody since 14 April 2023, when he surrendered to authorities after the first victims were found.

The children who died

Kenya, a country where religion plays an important part in everyday life, was deeply shaken by the discoveries.

For many, the most painful part of the story is the number of children who died. These were young lives that depended on adults for protection, yet they became victims of circumstances they could neither understand nor control.

A warning about blind obedience

The case is a troubling reminder of what can happen when people give up their ability to question religious authority, and it raises serious questions about religious manipulation and the protection of vulnerable people.

Faith is deeply personal, and millions find comfort, hope and direction in religion. But when a religious leader begins demanding actions that threaten human life, the consequences can be devastating.

US corrections officer accused of inviting another man home, then calling him an intruder and firing gun when boyfriend returned

20 Nov 2026, 09:20 GMT

Lasacia Gore, a 34-year-old corrections officer in Michigan, has been charged with six felonies. Prosecutors say she invited another man to her home, then reported him as an intruder and fired a gun when her boyfriend came back unexpectedly. She has pleaded not guilty.

Read more

A 34-year-old woman in Michigan, United States, has been charged with six felonies after police say she invited another man to her home while her boyfriend was away, then reported him as an intruder when her partner returned unexpectedly.

The 7am phone call

According to prosecutors, Lasacia Gore, a corrections officer with Wayne County, called police at around 7am on Monday, claiming an unknown man had broken into her home in Eastpointe and was fighting with her boyfriend.

But investigators say they found that she had invited the man over herself, and had even left the door unlocked for him.

What police say happened

According to police, things turned violent when her boyfriend came home unexpectedly, found the visitor in the house and confronted him, leading to a fight.

Eastpointe Police Chief Corey Haines said Gore fired a gun while both men were wrestling on the kitchen floor. The bullet missed them and struck the wall above them.

"You told me to leave the door open"

Macomb County Prosecutor Pete Lucido said the visitor's account contradicted hers. "She's making a call saying she doesn't know him and this and that, and he says, 'What are you talking about? You're the one who told me to leave the door open and I'm coming in!'" he said.

"It's crazy. This could have been very tragic and somebody could've died," Lucido added.

Chief Haines also expressed concern that a corrections officer was allegedly involved in making a false police report and firing a weapon.

The charges

Gore faces six felony charges, including assault with a dangerous weapon, discharging a firearm at a building, filing a false felony report, lying to investigators and two firearm-related counts.

She pleaded not guilty at her arraignment on Wednesday. Her bond was set at $30,000, with a 10 per cent option. If released, she must stay at home under GPS monitoring, surrender her weapons and avoid contact with the complainant.

She is due back in court on 2 December for a probable cause conference. The charges are allegations and have not been proven in court.

UN top court finds Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority during the 2017 military crackdown. It is the first time the court has found a state responsible for genocide.

Read more

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the United Nations' highest court, has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority during a military crackdown in 2017.

The ruling

In a landmark judgment on Thursday, 19 November 2026, the court found Myanmar responsible for genocide in northern Rakhine State. The decision was reached by a 9-6 majority and marks the first time in the court's 80-year history that it has found a state responsible for committing genocide.

The court said genocidal intent was the only reasonable conclusion that could be drawn from the evidence before it.

The case

The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019, accusing Myanmar of violating the 1948 Genocide Convention. The 2017 crackdown killed thousands of Rohingya and forced more than 700,000 to flee the country.

Myanmar's position

Myanmar had denied the accusations, saying its forces were responding to attacks by armed groups.

Hackers leak data of JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard customers

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Hackers have published the email addresses and phone numbers of 16,000 wealthy JPMorgan Chase customers after targeting third-party vendors. It is the second major cyber incident hitting the financial sector in a week.

Read more

Hackers have stolen and published personal information belonging to thousands of customers linked to JPMorgan Chase, Mastercard and Visa, in the second major cyber incident to hit the financial industry in a week.

What was taken

JPMorgan Chase said the email addresses and phone numbers of about 16,000 of its wealthiest customers were published by a ransomware gang. The data appears to relate to the bank's invitation-only Reserve credit card, which usually requires customers to have more than $10 million in assets with the bank.

How it happened

The bank said the hackers targeted third-party vendors that provide services to it. A Visa spokesperson said a service provider in the Asia-Pacific region was targeted, but that none of Visa's own systems were involved.

What the bank is doing

JPMorgan said it would contact affected customers directly and offer support, including free credit monitoring.

Wider trend

Earlier in the week, malicious code was planted on websites belonging to about 178 organisations after hackers exploited a flaw in a third-party web tool.

Anthropic raises $81.3bn in IPO ahead of Nasdaq debut

19 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

AI company Anthropic has reportedly raised $81.3 billion in its initial public offering, and $107.3 billion including a separate private sale. The deal values the company at about $1.5 trillion.

Read more

Artificial intelligence company Anthropic has reportedly raised $81.3 billion through its initial public offering, ahead of its stock market debut.

The numbers

The company, which makes the Claude chatbot, sold 980 million shares at $83 each, above its earlier range of $70 to $78. Including a separate $26 billion private share sale, its combined fundraising reached $107.3 billion, valuing it at about $1.5 trillion.

Its shares were scheduled to begin trading on the Nasdaq exchange in New York on Thursday, 19 November 2026, under the ticker ANT.

A record?

The combined fundraising is more than the $86 billion raised when SpaceX went public in June, although the IPO alone was below that figure.

Why it matters

The listing is being watched as a test of investor confidence in AI after a difficult fortnight for the industry. Anthropic makes much of its money selling AI services to businesses.
