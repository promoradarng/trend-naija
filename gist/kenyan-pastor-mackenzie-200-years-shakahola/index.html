Kenyan pastor sentenced to 200 years in prison over Shakahola starvation tragedy that claimed hundreds of lives

7 Nov 2026, 05:40 GMT

Kenyan preacher Paul Nthenge Mackenzie has reportedly been sentenced to 200 years in prison over the deaths of 191 members of his Good News International Church, including 190 children, in one of the most disturbing religious tragedies in Kenya's history.

The sentence

The Mombasa High Court handed down the sentence on Friday, 6 November 2026, following its ruling on 19 October in which Mackenzie and 28 other defendants were found guilty. The prosecution had earlier asked the court to impose the death penalty.

What happened in Shakahola

According to the prosecution, between 2021 and 2023 Mackenzie gathered his followers on a 325-hectare property in the Shakahola Forest, near the coastal town of Malindi in south-eastern Kenya.

There, he convinced them that starving themselves to death would allow them to meet Jesus.

Imagine the level of trust these families must have placed in their religious leader. Parents, children and entire households were drawn into a belief system that eventually turned deadly.

During the trial, the defence argued that the children had taken part in the fasting voluntarily. The judge rejected that argument.

How the tragedy came to light

The case first drew international attention in April 2023, when authorities discovered the first bodies buried in the forest.

What first looked like an isolated discovery soon turned into something far more horrifying. As investigators kept searching, more graves were uncovered. Eventually, more than 400 bodies were exhumed, revealing the enormous scale of the disaster.

More than 200 witnesses testified during the proceedings. Mackenzie had been in custody since 14 April 2023, when he surrendered to authorities after the first victims were found.

The children who died

Kenya, a country where religion plays an important part in everyday life, was deeply shaken by the discoveries.

For many, the most painful part of the story is the number of children who died. These were young lives that depended on adults for protection, yet they became victims of circumstances they could neither understand nor control.

A warning about blind obedience

The case is a troubling reminder of what can happen when people give up their ability to question religious authority, and it raises serious questions about religious manipulation and the protection of vulnerable people.

Faith is deeply personal, and millions find comfort, hope and direction in religion. But when a religious leader begins demanding actions that threaten human life, the consequences can be devastating.

Over to you

How could such a tragedy continue for so long before authorities uncovered what was happening in the forest? And how far should people go in obeying religious leaders when their teachings put lives in danger?

MTN reopens talks to buy South Africa's Telkom

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

MTN Group has confirmed early talks to buy rival Telkom, four years after an earlier attempt collapsed. Telkom shares jumped 18% on the news.

Read more

MTN Group, Africa's largest mobile operator by subscribers, has reportedly reopened talks to acquire South African rival Telkom.

What happened

The two companies confirmed preliminary discussions on Monday, 16 November 2026, four years after an earlier round of negotiations ended without a deal. MTN said it was considering a cash offer, possibly with an option for Telkom shareholders to take MTN shares instead, but stressed the talks were at an early stage and might not lead to an agreement.

Market reaction

Telkom's shares rose about 18% to their highest level in more than seven years, while MTN's shares fell 1.7%. Telkom's enterprise value was put at about 38 billion rand, or around $2.2 billion, while MTN's market value stood at about 432 billion rand.

Why it matters

A deal would add Telkom's mobile customers to MTN's South African business, which could push MTN ahead of Vodacom as the biggest mobile operator in South Africa by subscribers.

Pope Leo hit in the eye by rosary thrown from crowd in Peru

17 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Pope Leo XIV was reportedly struck in the eye by a rosary thrown from the crowd as he rode through Lima in the Popemobile. He was on the last day of a 12-day South America tour.

Read more

Pope Leo XIV was reportedly hit in the eye by a rosary thrown by a worshipper as he toured Lima, Peru, on Monday, 16 November 2026.

What happened

The Pope was riding through the city in the Popemobile when the rosary, thrown from the crowd, struck him. Video of the moment shows him holding and rubbing his eye. There was no report of serious injury, and he continued to a farewell Mass at Las Palmas air base, which was said to have drawn around one million people.

A special visit

The trip marked the final day of a 12-day tour of South America, six days of which he spent in Peru. The 71-year-old has deep ties to the country. Born in Chicago, he arrived in Peru in 1985 as a 29-year-old Augustinian missionary and spent many years there, including a decade in the northern city of Chiclayo, where he was known as Padre Roberto.

He is the first American pope and the first to hold Peruvian citizenship. Tens of thousands of Peruvians lined the streets on Monday, many carrying banners in his honour.

Robert Pattinson reportedly named the new James Bond

16 Nov 2026, 17:45 GMT

Robert Pattinson has reportedly been confirmed as the next James Bond, ending years of speculation over who would follow Daniel Craig. He is said to star in the upcoming 007 film directed by Denis Villeneuve.

Read more

British actor Robert Pattinson has reportedly been named the next James Bond, ending years of guessing over who would take over the role of the famous secret agent.

The announcement

The franchise is said to have confirmed the casting on Monday, 16 November 2026, with a short video showing the 40-year-old actor in a black tuxedo, pointing a gun. Pattinson is best known for the Twilight films and for currently playing Bruce Wayne in DC's Batman films.

The race for the role

Several actors, including Callum Turner, Nicholas Hoult, Jack Lowden and Josh O'Connor, were reportedly screen-tested before the casting was announced for the new film, which will be directed by Denis Villeneuve. In a recent magazine interview, Villeneuve spoke about the pressure of taking on the franchise and said casting was still in progress at the time.

Background

Daniel Craig stepped down as 007 after five films over 15 years, ending with No Time to Die in October 2021. He was the sixth actor to play the character, after Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton and Pierce Brosnan. If confirmed, Pattinson would become the seventh actor to play Bond in the official film series.

What happens next

No release date for the new Bond film was given in the reports.

Fountain of Life Church removes Jimmy Odukoya as senior pastor

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The board of trustees of The Fountain of Life Church in Lagos has reportedly removed Jimmy Odukoya as senior pastor with immediate effect. It said his appointment did not reflect the succession wishes of his late father and founder, Taiwo Odukoya.

Read more

The Fountain of Life Church in Ilupeju, Lagos, has reportedly removed Jimmy Odukoya as its senior pastor, with the board of trustees saying his appointment conflicted with the succession plans left by the church's late founder, Taiwo Odukoya.

What the board said

In a statement on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the board is said to have explained that it reviewed the church's constitution alongside recordings of the founder's wishes made before his death in 2023. It concluded that Jimmy's tenure did not reflect those wishes and said he was no longer recognised as senior pastor, effective immediately.

The board reportedly stressed that the decision was not a personal judgement against him but a governance matter, describing it as necessary to uphold the church's constitution and preserve the succession arrangements set up by the founder. It said the decision was not taken lightly.

Background

Jimmy Odukoya, an actor and pastor, took over leadership of the church after his father's death. Earlier this month, on 1 November, he remarried and presented his new wife to the congregation, in a ceremony at which Pastor Tunde Bakare officiated the blessing.

How people reacted

The news quickly became a talking point online, with many people revisiting a cryptic post he shared days before the announcement, and a video of the late founder that is said to show the role he intended for his son.

What happens next

There was no public response from Jimmy Odukoya in the reports, and the board did not say who would lead the church.
