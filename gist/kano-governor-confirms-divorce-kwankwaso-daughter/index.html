Kano Governor Abba Yusuf confirms divorce from Kwankwaso's daughter, says he will marry again

16 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Kano State Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf has reportedly confirmed that his marriage to Zainab Musa Kwankwaso, the daughter of former Kano governor and NDC leader Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso, has ended.

What the governor said

Yusuf is said to have spoken about the matter on Monday, 16 November 2026, while addressing pensioners at an event at the Government House in Kano. Speaking in Hausa and in a light-hearted tone, he compared going from two wives to one to a demotion in military rank, from corporal to constable.

He reportedly confirmed that he had agreed to the divorce and that the papers for the dissolution had been issued. He also told the gathering that he still plans to take another wife, and asked residents to pray that he finds a suitable partner. He said he was at peace with the decision.

Background

The confirmation came two days after reports that Zainab Kwankwaso had filed a divorce petition at an Upper Area Court in Gudu, Abuja. She was said to be the governor's second wife.

The separation has drawn wide attention because of the long political relationship between Yusuf and Kwankwaso, who was his political mentor.

Court adjourns case

On Wednesday, 18 November, an Upper Area Court in Gudu, Abuja, adjourned the divorce case to 15 December 2026 to allow both sides to file and exchange documents. The governor was represented by Kano's Commissioner for Information, Sanusi Bature, who told the court he had not yet been able to fully brief the governor on the case.

Dalung's criticism

Former sports minister Solomon Dalung has criticised the governor over his public remarks on the divorce. He said a man does not need to humiliate the mother of his children to show that a marriage is over.

Reports say the governor told a gathering that the papers had been granted and joked that an advert would be placed for anyone wishing to fill the vacant position.

The political backdrop

Yusuf won the Kano governorship on the NNPP platform with Kwankwaso's backing, but later defected to the APC. The political fallout between the two men, which began about a year ago, is said to have strained the marriage.

  • Yusuf says the dissolution papers have been issued.
  • Solomon Dalung says he should not have humiliated his wife publicly.
  • Yusuf left Kwankwaso's camp and joined the APC.

Kwankwaso accuses Kano Governor Abba Yusuf of using EFCC to target him

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

NDC vice-presidential candidate Rabiu Kwankwaso has accused his former protégé, Kano Governor Abba Yusuf, of trying to bring him down through anti-corruption agencies ahead of 2027. He claimed the governor asked the EFCC to act against him.

Read more

Rabiu Kwankwaso, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) vice-presidential candidate and former governor of Kano State, has publicly accused Kano Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf of plotting against him through anti-corruption agencies ahead of the 2027 elections.

The accusation

In a statement on his X account on Friday, 27 November 2026, Kwankwaso claimed that the governor, who served under him for decades as an aide and commissioner, had formally asked the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) to act against him. He alleged that when that effort failed, the governor turned to a little-known group to stage protests at the EFCC's office to put pressure on the agency.

Kwankwaso said the governor wanted to damage his reputation.

Background

The two men, once close allies, have fallen out since Yusuf defected from the NNPP to the APC in January 2026. In recent days the governor claimed that Kwankwaso once sent him to promise President Tinubu they would rejoin the APC, a claim the Kwankwasiyya Movement has rejected. Yusuf has also spoken publicly about the end of his marriage to Kwankwaso's niece.

The other side

No response from Governor Yusuf or the EFCC to Kwankwaso's claims was included in the reports.

Netflix removes Tyson Fury schizophrenia claim from documentary

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Netflix has removed references to schizophrenia from its Tyson Fury documentary after it emerged the boxer has not been medically diagnosed with the condition. The claim had come from his wife, Paris.

Read more

Netflix has removed references to schizophrenia from its upcoming documentary about boxer Tyson Fury, after it emerged that he has not been medically diagnosed with the condition.

What changed

In the original version of the 90-minute film, Fury's wife, Paris, said doctors had regarded him as a mild schizophrenic in the year after he beat Wladimir Klitschko in November 2015. Those references have now been taken out. The documentary premieres on Netflix on Friday, 27 November 2026.

Boxing board concerns

The British Boxing Board of Control, which is sanctioning Fury's fight with Anthony Joshua on 11 December, had raised concerns about his mental health after the original statements. Its rules bar boxers from fighting if they have certain psychological disorders.

Background

Fury struggled with depression, drug and alcohol problems after the Klitschko win and did not return to the ring for nearly three years. He has spoken openly about his mental health, including bipolar disorder. An earlier story on this site said he had been diagnosed with schizophrenia; that report has been corrected.

Fashola says no one should die for rigging elections

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Former Lagos Governor Babatunde Fashola has condemned the 'rig and die' slogan, saying death is not the penalty for electoral offences. He backed tougher prison terms instead.

Read more

Former Lagos State Governor Babatunde Fashola has condemned the 'rig and die' slogan ahead of the 2027 elections, insisting that nobody should lose their life over electoral offences.

What he said

In a television interview that aired on Friday, 27 November 2026, Fashola, a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, said the punishment for rigging under the Electoral Act is not death. He added that even if it were, which he would not support, there must be a process, including a charge, a trial and a chance to defend oneself, before a life is taken.

He nevertheless said electoral manipulation should attract serious consequences, suggesting tougher prison sentences without the option of a fine for offences that undermine democracy, and urged politicians to campaign rather than rig.

Background

The slogan has sparked a national debate. INEC has warned it could incite mob attacks at polling units, while singer 2Baba said this week he saw nothing wrong with it and backed severe punishment for riggers. Fashola's comments drew mixed reactions online.
