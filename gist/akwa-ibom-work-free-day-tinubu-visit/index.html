Akwa Ibom declares Friday work-free day for Tinubu's visit

5 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Akwa Ibom State governor Umo Eno has declared Friday, 6 November 2026, a work-free day in the state ahead of a visit by President Bola Tinubu.

The visit

The governor made the announcement after inspecting projects lined up for commissioning during the visit, including the International Terminal Building at Victor Attah International Airport in Uyo, according to reports. He described the visit as timely and significant for the state's development and urged residents to come out and welcome the President. A rally in support of Tinubu's 2027 re-election bid is also planned.

Background

Eno was elected governor in 2023 on the PDP platform and later joined the APC. He is one of several governors who have defected to the ruling party and endorsed the President's re-election. The visit comes shortly after Tinubu returned from Saudi Arabia, where he performed Umrah. Uyo will also host the Super Eagles' AFCON qualifier against Tanzania on 13 November.

Reactions

Supporters welcomed the visit and the new projects.

Two-state trip

Presidential spokesman Bayo Onanuga said the President will leave Abuja on Friday for a two-day visit to Akwa Ibom and Rivers states. After commissioning projects in Uyo, he will attend an APC rally in Port Harcourt, his third campaign outing since the start of his re-election drive.

Security measures

Akwa Ibom police commissioner Baba Azare banned the use of drones, fireworks and other devices that could cause panic during the visit, and said access would be restricted on major roads including Airport Road, Iron Road and Ring Road III leading to the Godswill Akpabio Stadium. He urged other parties to reschedule events planned for Friday. Projects due to be inaugurated include the new terminal at Victor Attah International Airport, an Aviation Village, the Ring Road III extension and a resort park in Uyo.

The visit

On Friday, the President inaugurated several projects in Uyo, including the new terminal building at Victor Attah International Airport. He described the trip as a homecoming and praised Governor Eno, saying the airport's international status was a result of the governor's commitment. Vice-President Kashim Shettima then addressed an APC campaign rally in the city, where he criticised NDC candidate Peter Obi.

What happens next

Security is expected to be tight in Uyo during the visit.

  • Friday is a work-free day in Akwa Ibom.
  • Tinubu will commission projects including a new airport terminal.
  • A pro-Tinubu rally is planned.

Atiku accuses Tinubu's camp of using AI to fake rally crowds, says Potiskum crowd was real

16 Nov 2026, 17:45 GMT

Atiku Abubakar has reportedly accused the presidency of using artificial intelligence to create images of large crowds at its events. He said the crowd that welcomed him in Potiskum, Yobe State, was genuine.

Read more

Former Vice President and ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has reportedly accused President Bola Tinubu's administration of using artificial intelligence to produce images of crowds at political events, while insisting the turnout at his own rally in Potiskum, Yobe State, was real.

What Atiku said

Atiku is said to have contrasted the welcome he received in Potiskum with what he described as doctored images released by the presidency, and mocked the quality of those images. He described the reception from the time his team entered Potiskum from Azare in Bauchi State as breathtaking, pointing to the size of the crowd and the chants from residents.

He said his campaign, which he calls the Affordable Life campaign, did not need artificial crowds, and that chants of "Tinubu Must Go" at his rallies reflected a real demand for change.

Praise for Yobe governor

Atiku reportedly thanked the Yobe State governor for allowing a fair environment for his campaign, saying open political competition strengthens democracy.

His promise

He pledged to act quickly on the cost of living if elected at the presidential election scheduled for 16 January 2027.

The other side

The reports did not include a response from the presidency or the APC campaign to the AI claim.

Osun government demotes two traditional rulers; one monarch claims APC sympathy was the reason

16 Nov 2026, 17:45 GMT

Governor Ademola Adeleke has reportedly approved the downgrading of two Osun traditional stools from Part II to Part III chieftaincies. One of the affected monarchs says no reason was given and alleges the move was political.

Read more

The Osun State Government has reportedly stripped two traditional rulers of their Part II chieftaincy status, reducing their stools to minor chieftaincies in a decision approved by Governor Ademola Adeleke.

What the government did

A letter from the state's Ministry of Local Government and Chieftaincy Affairs is said to show that the governor approved the de-recognition of the Alagunbelewo of Agunbelewo, Oba Maruf Lawal, and the Olu of Irepodun, Oba Adewale Adeniyi Sikiru, under the state's Chiefs Law. Both stools now revert to Part III, which means the rights, privileges and allowances attached to their former status have been withdrawn. The government reportedly said the approval would be published in the state gazette.

The monarch's response

Oba Lawal reportedly said the affected rulers received letters without any official reason and questioned whether the decision had been ratified by the State Executive Council as the law requires. He alleged that the action may have been linked to perceived sympathy for the All Progressives Congress (APC).

He further claimed that the rulers had been expected to show loyalty to the governor's party in return for support they received. These claims have not been independently confirmed.

What happens next

No response from the Osun government to the monarchs' allegations was included in the reports. The affected rulers say they want answers on the reasons for their demotion.

Pastor Ibiyeomie accuses fellow preachers of staying silent on Tinubu government

16 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Pastor David Ibiyeomie has accused many Nigerian preachers of keeping quiet about the Tinubu administration out of tribal loyalty and financial ties, contrasting it with how vocal they were under Goodluck Jonathan.

Read more

Pastor David Ibiyeomie has criticised fellow preachers over what he described as their silence on the Tinubu administration.

What he said

In a sermon shared on Instagram on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Ibiyeomie argued that many clerics had gone quiet on pressing national issues, including poverty, the killing of Nigerians in South Africa and what he described as anti-Christian laws, because of tribal loyalty and financial compromise. He contrasted the outspokenness of many preachers during former President Goodluck Jonathan's government with their silence now.

No money from government

The pastor said he had never accepted money from any government official and challenged anyone to prove otherwise, arguing that financial dependence on politicians is what silences preachers.

Background

Ibiyeomie, based in Port Harcourt, recently dedicated a 120,000-seat cathedral attended by Bishop David Oyedepo, who has also warned politicians over insecurity. The sermon sparked significant debate online.

Goodluck Jonathan calls for review of Nigeria's democracy and institutions

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a broad review of Nigeria's democratic structures, how politicians are recruited and the state of public institutions, saying the country owes a debt to its founding nationalists.

Read more

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a wide-ranging review of Nigeria's democratic structures, political recruitment and public institutions.

What he said

Chairing a colloquium at the Nigerian Institute of International Affairs in Lagos to mark the 162nd birth anniversary of nationalist Herbert Macaulay, reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Jonathan said the country's founding nationalists did not merely fight to end colonial rule, but imagined a sovereign, united and prosperous Nigeria where every citizen would enjoy equal rights, dignity and opportunity. He said independence was also meant to bring economic freedom, industrialisation, quality education, healthcare and social justice.

Honesty about failures

While acknowledging progress in some areas, Jonathan said true patriotism requires honesty about the country's failures. He pointed to leadership failures and weak institutions, and urged a review of how the country's democracy works and how leaders emerge.

Background

Jonathan, who governed from 2010 to 2015, is widely remembered for conceding defeat to Muhammadu Buhari in the 2015 election, a first for a sitting Nigerian president. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
