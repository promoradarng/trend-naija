Akwa Ibom declares Friday work-free day for Tinubu's visit

5 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Akwa Ibom State governor Umo Eno has declared Friday, 6 November 2026, a work-free day in the state ahead of a visit by President Bola Tinubu.

The visit

The governor made the announcement after inspecting projects lined up for commissioning during the visit, including the International Terminal Building at Victor Attah International Airport in Uyo, according to reports. He described the visit as timely and significant for the state's development and urged residents to come out and welcome the President. A rally in support of Tinubu's 2027 re-election bid is also planned.

Background

Eno was elected governor in 2023 on the PDP platform and later joined the APC. He is one of several governors who have defected to the ruling party and endorsed the President's re-election. The visit comes shortly after Tinubu returned from Saudi Arabia, where he performed Umrah. Uyo will also host the Super Eagles' AFCON qualifier against Tanzania on 13 November.

Reactions

Supporters welcomed the visit and the new projects.

Two-state trip

Presidential spokesman Bayo Onanuga said the President will leave Abuja on Friday for a two-day visit to Akwa Ibom and Rivers states. After commissioning projects in Uyo, he will attend an APC rally in Port Harcourt, his third campaign outing since the start of his re-election drive.

Security measures

Akwa Ibom police commissioner Baba Azare banned the use of drones, fireworks and other devices that could cause panic during the visit, and said access would be restricted on major roads including Airport Road, Iron Road and Ring Road III leading to the Godswill Akpabio Stadium. He urged other parties to reschedule events planned for Friday. Projects due to be inaugurated include the new terminal at Victor Attah International Airport, an Aviation Village, the Ring Road III extension and a resort park in Uyo.

The visit

On Friday, the President inaugurated several projects in Uyo, including the new terminal building at Victor Attah International Airport. He described the trip as a homecoming and praised Governor Eno, saying the airport's international status was a result of the governor's commitment. Vice-President Kashim Shettima then addressed an APC campaign rally in the city, where he criticised NDC candidate Peter Obi.

What happens next

Security is expected to be tight in Uyo during the visit.

  • Friday is a work-free day in Akwa Ibom.
  • Tinubu will commission projects including a new airport terminal.
  • A pro-Tinubu rally is planned.

"Pharaoh, let my people go!": Cubana Chief Priest replies Umahi, accuses him of giving Tinubu fake promises on South-East votes

9 Nov 2026, 11:14 GMT

Cubana Chief Priest has fired back at Works Minister David Umahi, nicknaming him "Pharaoh" and accusing him of making empty promises to President Tinubu about South-East votes. He also claimed Umahi is preparing his son to become Ebonyi governor, and mocked him with his own "money na water" catchphrase.

Socialite and businessman Cubana Chief Priest has replied Minister of Works David Umahi, a day after the minister mocked his famous "money na water" catchphrase and asked how many people he has given scholarships.

"Pharaoh, let my people go"

Borrowing from the Bible story of Moses and Pharaoh, Cubana Chief Priest gave the minister a new nickname, and accused him of making promises to President Bola Tinubu that he cannot deliver.

"Pharaoh, let my people go. Stop giving Tinubu fake promises on votes. What project are you using to convince South-East people to vote for him?" he said.

The governorship claim

He went further, claiming that Umahi is preparing his son to become governor of Ebonyi State after the current governor, Francis Nwifuru.

Speaking partly in Pidgin, he said, in effect: "You're busy preparing your son to be governor after Nwifuru. God won't let that happen. You want to use Ebonyi State to settle your child. Let the young man come and meet us on the street so we can teach him how to make 'money na water'."

This is Cubana Chief Priest's claim. Umahi has not publicly declared any plan for his son to contest the Ebonyi governorship, and he has not yet responded to this latest attack.

How it started

The exchange began weeks ago when Cubana Chief Priest described Umahi as the second-worst minister from the South-East. Umahi hit back by telling him he was "becoming very noisy", comparing him unfavourably with Dangote and Otedola, and asking, "How many people have you given scholarships?"

Now the socialite has turned the argument back to politics, questioning what Umahi has done to win South-East votes for the President.

Over to you

Who is winning this war of words: the minister or the socialite? And is Cubana Chief Priest right to question what the South-East has to show for supporting Tinubu?

Nigeria seeks $500m World Bank loan for infrastructure financing platform

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The federal government has approached the World Bank for a $500 million loan to set up a platform to attract private investment into infrastructure, part of a wider $3.85 billion programme called INFRAGROW.

Read more

The federal government has asked the World Bank for a $500 million loan to set up a platform to attract private investment into Nigerian infrastructure.

The loan

According to a World Bank project document updated on 4 November 2026 and reported on Monday, 9 November, the facility is part of a broader $3.85 billion programme called the Nigeria Infrastructure Financing and De-Risking Platform for Growth and Jobs, or INFRAGROW. The Federal Ministry of Finance is listed as the borrower and the Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority (NSIA) as the implementing agency. The aim is to reduce the risks that discourage private investors from funding infrastructure projects.

Why the bank says it is needed

The World Bank estimates that Nigeria has an infrastructure financing gap of about $100 billion a year, which holds back growth, competitiveness and job creation. It says the problem is not only a lack of money but also weak project preparation and difficulty turning proposals into commercially viable investments.

Background

The request comes as President Bola Tinubu also seeks Senate approval for about $7.7 billion in new external borrowing, and as government borrowing becomes a major campaign issue. NDC candidate Peter Obi has challenged the President to account for loans, while the Presidency says claims that it has borrowed ₦120 trillion are distorted. The World Bank reported last week that Nigeria's total external debt rose to about $145 billion in 2025.

Presidency rejects claim that Tinubu borrowed ₦120 trillion in three years

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

The Presidency says viral claims that President Tinubu added ₦120 trillion to Nigeria's debt in three years are misleading, arguing that they ignore old Central Bank overdrafts added to the books in 2023 and the effect of unifying the exchange rate.

Read more

The Presidency has rejected viral social media claims that President Bola Tinubu's government has added ₦120 trillion to Nigeria's debt in its first three years.

What the Presidency said

In a statement on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the President's special assistant on social media, Dada Olusegun, described the figures as a distorted picture of the country's public finances. The claim came from a widely shared graphic saying that 16 previous Nigerian leaders borrowed a total of ₦46 trillion over 63 years, compared with ₦120 trillion under Tinubu.

The Presidency's argument

Olusegun said the ₦46 trillion figure, from late 2022, left out ₦22.7 trillion in Central Bank of Nigeria 'Ways and Means' overdrafts to the previous government, which were only formally added to the official public debt in May 2023. He also said earlier valuations of foreign debt used an official exchange rate of about ₦448 to the dollar, which understated the true naira value of those debts; when the exchange rate was unified, the naira value of existing foreign debt rose sharply even without new borrowing.

Background

The rebuttal came after NDC candidate Peter Obi challenged Tinubu to account for subsidy savings and more than $100 billion he said the government had borrowed. The World Bank reported last week that Nigeria's total external debt, including private debt, rose to about $145 billion in 2025. Tinubu is also seeking Senate approval for about $7.7 billion in new external loans, which opposition figures have urged lawmakers to reject.

Why it matters

Debt and the cost of living are expected to be major issues in the 16 January 2027 presidential election.
