NAPTIP warns traffickers are luring victims at gyms and with fake jobs

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons (NAPTIP) has warned Nigerians about new tactics used by human traffickers to recruit victims.

What NAPTIP said

Speaking at a joint briefing by security and intelligence agencies in Abuja on Thursday, 5 November 2026, NAPTIP spokesman Adekoye Vincent said traffickers are increasingly exploiting social relationships, especially with women. He said they approach people at gyms, recreational centres and other social venues, first presenting themselves as service providers offering help with domestic needs, shopping or sourcing goods. Once they have built trust and familiarity, they lure victims with fake job offers and other promises.

Advice to the public

The agency urged Nigerians, particularly young women, to be cautious about strangers offering help or opportunities that seem too good to be true, to verify job offers, and to report suspicious approaches to the authorities.

Background

Nigeria is a major source, transit and destination country for human trafficking, with victims taken to other African countries, Europe and the Middle East for forced labour and sexual exploitation. This week, police in Mali rescued Nigerian women from a trafficking ring. NAPTIP has long warned that traffickers use fake job offers abroad, and more recently social media, to recruit victims.

Why it matters

Officials say awareness is one of the best defences against traffickers, who constantly change their methods.

  • Traffickers are approaching women at gyms and social venues.
  • They pose as helpful service providers before offering fake jobs.
  • NAPTIP urged people to verify offers and report suspicions.

FG signs Port Harcourt airport concession deal, unions say no jobs will be lost

14 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

The federal government has signed a concession agreement for Port Harcourt International Airport with PH Airport Limited. Aviation unions backed the deal after assurances that no worker would lose their job.

The federal government has signed a concession agreement for the Port Harcourt International Airport with a private operator.

The deal

The agreement with PH Airport Limited was signed at the office of the Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, according to reports on Saturday, 14 November 2026. The concessionaire was represented by its group managing director, Eyo Asuquo. The heads of the Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority and the Federal Airports Authority of Nigeria, ministry officials and aviation union leaders attended the signing. The company reportedly plans to recruit more workers.

Unions back the deal

Ben Nnabue, president of the National Union of Air Transport Employees, said organised labour supported the agreement after receiving assurances that no aviation worker would lose their job because of the concession, adding that protecting jobs remained the unions' top priority.

Background

The government has been seeking private investors to run and upgrade major airports.

Copyright Commission takes legal action against NetNaija, MovieBox over piracy

14 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigerian Copyright Commission says it has begun legal action against operators of platforms including NetNaija and MovieBox over alleged copyright violations, warning that sites distributing protected content without permission could be shut down.

Read more

The Nigerian Copyright Commission (NCC) says it has started legal action against the operators of several online platforms, including NetNaija and MovieBox, over alleged copyright infringement.

What the commission said

Speaking on Thursday, 12 November 2026, at a copyright sensitisation programme at the University of Ibadan, Barrister Kunle Olatunji, deputy director of the commission's Regulatory and Compliance Department, said the action was part of efforts to protect authors, publishers, filmmakers and other creators whose work is used without permission. He said the Copyright Act 2022 had widened the commission's powers to tackle online piracy, allowing it to seek the closure of entire websites rather than just the removal of individual pirated items.

Why it matters

Olatunji said the law protects the rights of creative professionals whose works are exploited without their consent.

Background

The allegations against the platforms have not been tested in court, and their operators had not publicly responded at the time of writing.

Sweden returns 39 Benin Bronzes to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Sweden has handed back 39 Benin artefacts taken during the British invasion of Benin in 1897, at a ceremony in Lagos. Six of the objects will stay in Sweden on a 10-year loan.

Read more

Sweden has returned 39 Benin Bronzes and other artefacts to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted from the Benin Kingdom.

The handover

Representatives of Sweden's National Museums of World Culture formally handed over the objects at a ceremony in Lagos on Thursday, 12 November 2026. They will be received by Nigeria's National Commission for Museums and Monuments. The collection includes cultural objects known as Emwin Arre. Six of the 39 artefacts will remain in Sweden under a 10-year loan arrangement.

Why it matters

The objects were among thousands taken by British forces who attacked and burned Benin City in 1897 before bringing the kingdom under British rule. Michael Barrett, curator for Africa at Sweden's National Museums of World Culture, described the return as an important step in addressing colonial-era looting of cultural property.

Background

Nigeria has been campaigning for decades for the return of the Benin Bronzes, which are held in museums and private collections around the world. The Netherlands returned 119 objects in 2025, and Germany handed over 20 Benin Bronzes in 2022. Many more remain abroad.
