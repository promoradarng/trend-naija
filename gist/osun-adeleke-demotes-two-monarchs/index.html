Osun government demotes two traditional rulers; one monarch claims APC sympathy was the reason

16 Nov 2026, 17:45 GMT

The Osun State Government has reportedly stripped two traditional rulers of their Part II chieftaincy status, reducing their stools to minor chieftaincies in a decision approved by Governor Ademola Adeleke.

What the government did

A letter from the state's Ministry of Local Government and Chieftaincy Affairs is said to show that the governor approved the de-recognition of the Alagunbelewo of Agunbelewo, Oba Maruf Lawal, and the Olu of Irepodun, Oba Adewale Adeniyi Sikiru, under the state's Chiefs Law. Both stools now revert to Part III, which means the rights, privileges and allowances attached to their former status have been withdrawn. The government reportedly said the approval would be published in the state gazette.

The monarch's response

Oba Lawal reportedly said the affected rulers received letters without any official reason and questioned whether the decision had been ratified by the State Executive Council as the law requires. He alleged that the action may have been linked to perceived sympathy for the All Progressives Congress (APC).

He further claimed that the rulers had been expected to show loyalty to the governor's party in return for support they received. These claims have not been independently confirmed.

What happens next

No response from the Osun government to the monarchs' allegations was included in the reports. The affected rulers say they want answers on the reasons for their demotion.

  • The two stools were moved from Part II to Part III.
  • One monarch says no reason was given.
  • He alleges the move was linked to APC sympathy.

Tinubu's praise singer Rarara mobbed at Abuja National Mosque

21 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Hausa praise singer Dauda Kahutu Rarara, a well-known supporter of President Tinubu, was attacked by an angry crowd at the Abuja National Mosque during Friday prayers. Security operatives struggled to hold the crowd back.

Read more

Popular Hausa praise singer Dauda Kahutu, widely known as Rarara, was attacked by an angry crowd at the Abuja National Mosque during Friday prayers on 20 November 2026.

What happened

Rarara, a prominent supporter of President Bola Tinubu, was entering the mosque with plain-clothed and uniformed security personnel when a group of people rushed at him. Security operatives tried to hold the crowd back but could not fully contain them. At one point, the singer removed his cap, which carried Tinubu's insignia.

A video of the incident has spread widely online.

Context

The incident came a week after President Tinubu's convoy was pelted with stones after Friday prayers at a mosque in Garki, Abuja, an attack for which police arrested 78 people before the Presidency directed their release.

Reactions

Paul Ibe, media adviser to ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar, condemned the violence on X while also making a political point about the incident.

Yahaya Bello says he will beat Natasha Akpoti-Uduaghan 'overwhelmingly' in Kogi Central

Former Kogi Governor Yahaya Bello, who plans to contest the Kogi Central senatorial seat in 2027, has claimed his political reach far exceeds that of the sitting senator, Natasha Akpoti-Uduaghan.

Read more

Former Kogi State Governor Yahaya Bello has said he will win the Kogi Central senatorial seat in the 2027 elections overwhelmingly, describing the incumbent, Senator Natasha Akpoti-Uduaghan, as politically junior to him.

What he said

In an interview published on Friday, 20 November 2026, Bello reportedly said his political influence in Kogi State is far greater than that of any other politician. Peter Obi says Nigerians will 'feel the difference from day one' if he and Kwankwaso win

20 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, speaking in Anambra alongside former Kano governor Rabiu Kwankwaso, has promised to fight corruption and use public money for the public good.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has told supporters in Anambra State that Nigerians will feel a difference from the very first day if he and Rabiu Kwankwaso win the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking in Anambra alongside the former Kano governor, Obi reportedly said their administration would prioritise accountability, transparency and the responsible management of public funds.

He said the two of them would fight corruption and make sure public money is used for the public good, adding that Nigerians would know from day one that a new team was in charge.

Defend your votes

At an NDC town hall and rally at All Saints Anglican Cathedral in Onitsha on Friday, Obi also urged Nigerians to turn out in large numbers on 16 January 2027, stay at polling units until results are counted and transmitted, and guard against rigging. He said Nigerians should accept the result of any genuinely free and fair contest.

The context

Obi's comments came a day after Anambra Governor Charles Soludo hosted an APC rally in Awka for President Bola Tinubu and said the election was already decided. ADC chieftain Dele Momodu responded that Obi would win Anambra comfortably.

The race

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027, with Tinubu (APC), Atiku Abubakar (ADC), Obi (NDC) and other candidates in the race.
