Kwara APC governorship rally in Offa reportedly turns violent over unpaid mobilisation funds

16 Nov 2026, 11:21 GMT

A governorship campaign rally organised by the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Offa, Kwara State, has reportedly descended into chaos following claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised.

What reportedly happened

According to reports, the rally drew a large crowd of supporters to the streets of Offa. But tension rose when some of those who had turned up complained that they had not received the money they were allegedly promised for attending.

The situation reportedly turned violent as angry participants confronted organisers, disrupting the event.

The full details, including whether anyone was injured and how much money was involved, have not been independently confirmed, and the party has not publicly commented on the incident.

The business of crowds

The incident shines a light on a practice many Nigerians already know about: paying people, often called "mobilisation", to attend political rallies.

Across parties, campaign organisers frequently provide transport, food, branded caps and T-shirts, and sometimes cash, to fill venues and create the impression of massive support. When those promises are not kept, frustration can quickly boil over.

It also raises a deeper question. If people have to be paid to attend, how much do the crowds at political rallies really tell us about genuine support?

Peter Obi corrects claims that his Washington visit was an endorsement from the US Congress

18 Nov 2026, 16:43 GMT

Peter Obi says his recent trip to Washington involved talks on Nigeria's development, citizens' welfare and democracy, and that he neither sought nor announced any endorsement, after social media posts claimed the US Congress had backed him.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has corrected claims that his recent visit to Washington amounted to an endorsement from the United States Congress.

What Obi said

According to Obi, the trip involved discussions about Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and the state of democracy. He said he neither sought nor announced any endorsement.

He said he met serving and former US lawmakers and other stakeholders during the visit.

How the confusion started

The confusion followed a viral video in which retired US Army Colonel Albert Short praised Obi.

Some social media posts presented those personal remarks as if they were official backing from the US Congress, and the claim spread quickly online.

What the facts show

There is no public evidence that the US Congress as an institution has endorsed Obi's 2027 presidential bid. Congress does not endorse candidates in other countries' elections.

A meeting with politicians, or kind words from an individual, is not the same as an endorsement from their institution.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections drawing closer, claims about foreign support for candidates are likely to keep spreading on social media. Obi's correction is a reminder for supporters on all sides to check claims before sharing them.

DSS says it found no evidence it planned to disrupt ADC campaign in Sokoto, asks Atiku to retract or provide proof

18 Nov 2026, 07:57 GMT

The Department of State Services says its internal investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off in Sokoto. It has asked Atiku Abubakar to retract or clarify his allegation, or provide evidence.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) says its investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential campaign in Sokoto.

How it started

Earlier this month, former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar accused the leadership of the DSS of recruiting protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto.

The allegation drew wide attention, and the DSS responded by ordering an investigation into the claim.

The DSS findings

The agency now says its investigation found nothing to support the allegation that it planned to recruit protesters or disrupt the ADC campaign.

It has asked Atiku to either retract or clarify his claim, or to provide evidence that could support further investigation.

Not the final word

It is important to note that this is the DSS's own preliminary finding. It was not an independent investigation, which means the agency effectively investigated an allegation made against itself.

Atiku has not issued a fresh public response to the DSS findings.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections approaching, allegations that security agencies are being used against opposition parties are likely to keep coming up. Public trust depends on such claims being properly examined, whether they turn out to be true or not.

US praises Nigerian military for rescuing 125 kidnapping victims

18 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The United States has commended the Nigerian military for rescuing 125 kidnapping victims this month and dismantling terrorist camps. The statement drew mixed reactions from Nigerians online.

Read more

The United States government has praised the Nigerian military for rescuing 125 kidnapping victims this month, describing the operations as a significant step towards ending violence in the country.

What the US said

In a statement shared on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, by the US State Department's Bureau of African Affairs, Washington commended recent operations that led to the rescue of the victims, the dismantling of terrorist camps, the recovery of weapons and the clearing of explosive devices.

Growing partnership

The statement comes days after Defence Minister Christopher Musa said the US was providing Nigeria with satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help track down militant leaders ahead of the 2027 elections.

How Nigerians reacted

Many Nigerians on social media welcomed the commendation and praised the military's efforts, while some others were sceptical about the statement and the wider security situation.

Nine dead, 46 injured as overloaded truck crashes in Cross River

18 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

At least nine people died and 46 were injured when a truck carrying 85 people crashed near the Yahe Bridge in Cross River State. The FRSC blamed excessive speed.

Read more

At least nine people have died and 46 others were injured after a truck crashed near the Yahe Bridge area of Cross River State on Wednesday, 18 November 2026.

What happened

The Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) unit commander in Yahe, Pascal Anigbo, said the crash involved a DAF truck carrying 85 people as well as goods including palm oil, refrigerators and motorbikes.

He said the driver was speeding and lost control while trying to avoid a head-on collision with another truck coming from the opposite direction, causing the vehicle to veer off the road into the bush.

Casualties

According to the FRSC, 46 people were injured and taken to hospital, while 30 others escaped unhurt. Three bodies were taken to the mortuary after being confirmed dead by a doctor, while six others were collected by their families after being identified.
