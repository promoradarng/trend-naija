US congressman introduces bill to stop lobbyists working for Nigeria

31 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

A United States congressman has introduced legislation aimed at stopping American lobbying firms from working for the Nigerian government.

What the bill does

Representative Barry Moore introduced H.R. 10939, which would bar people and firms in the United States from accepting payment to lobby on Nigeria's behalf for as long as Nigeria is designated a Country of Particular Concern (CPC) for religious freedom violations. Firms that take such payments during that period could face sanctions. The bill was reported on Saturday, 31 October 2026, and has been referred to the House Committee on Foreign Affairs.

Why Moore introduced it

Moore reportedly accused the Nigerian government of paying expensive lobbyists in Washington to play down the persecution of Christians in the country. The allegations of persecution are disputed by the Nigerian government, which has said that insecurity affects people of all faiths and is driven by terrorism, banditry and communal conflicts.

Background

The United States designated Nigeria a Country of Particular Concern, a label for countries accused of severe violations of religious freedom. The designation has strained relations between the two countries, and the Nigerian government has hired lobbying firms in Washington to present its position. The issue gained new attention this week after a Christian woman was convicted in Borno State over converting an adult, a case criticised by Christian groups.

What happens next

The bill must pass through committee and both houses of Congress before it can become law. The Nigerian government had not responded publicly at the time of the report.

  • The bill would bar paid lobbying for Nigeria while it is a CPC.
  • Moore accuses Nigeria of paying lobbyists to counter criticism.
  • The bill has been referred to the House Foreign Affairs Committee.

Trump posthumously pardons baseball legend Pete Rose

20 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

US President Donald Trump has granted a posthumous pardon to baseball great Pete Rose, who pleaded guilty to tax offences in 1990 and was banned from the sport for betting on games.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has granted a posthumous pardon to baseball legend Pete Rose, two years after his death.

The pardon

Trump announced the pardon on Truth Social on Thursday, 19 November 2026, calling Rose a true baseball superstar and legend and praising his record of 4,256 career hits.

His conviction

Rose, who died in 2024 aged 83, pleaded guilty in 1990 to two counts of filing false tax returns, admitting he failed to report $354,968 over four years. He was also given a lifetime ban from Major League Baseball after an investigation found he bet on games.

Reinstatement

Rose, who played 24 seasons, was posthumously reinstated by MLB last year, making him eligible for the Baseball Hall of Fame. He remains the sport's all-time leader in career hits.

US corrections officer accused of inviting another man home, then calling him an intruder and firing gun when boyfriend returned

20 Nov 2026, 09:20 GMT

Lasacia Gore, a 34-year-old corrections officer in Michigan, has been charged with six felonies. Prosecutors say she invited another man to her home, then reported him as an intruder and fired a gun when her boyfriend came back unexpectedly. She has pleaded not guilty.

Read more

A 34-year-old woman in Michigan, United States, has been charged with six felonies after police say she invited another man to her home while her boyfriend was away, then reported him as an intruder when her partner returned unexpectedly.

The 7am phone call

According to prosecutors, Lasacia Gore, a corrections officer with Wayne County, called police at around 7am on Monday, claiming an unknown man had broken into her home in Eastpointe and was fighting with her boyfriend.

But investigators say they found that she had invited the man over herself, and had even left the door unlocked for him.

What police say happened

According to police, things turned violent when her boyfriend came home unexpectedly, found the visitor in the house and confronted him, leading to a fight.

Eastpointe Police Chief Corey Haines said Gore fired a gun while both men were wrestling on the kitchen floor. The bullet missed them and struck the wall above them.

"You told me to leave the door open"

Macomb County Prosecutor Pete Lucido said the visitor's account contradicted hers. "She's making a call saying she doesn't know him and this and that, and he says, 'What are you talking about? You're the one who told me to leave the door open and I'm coming in!'" he said.

"It's crazy. This could have been very tragic and somebody could've died," Lucido added.

Chief Haines also expressed concern that a corrections officer was allegedly involved in making a false police report and firing a weapon.

The charges

Gore faces six felony charges, including assault with a dangerous weapon, discharging a firearm at a building, filing a false felony report, lying to investigators and two firearm-related counts.

She pleaded not guilty at her arraignment on Wednesday. Her bond was set at $30,000, with a 10 per cent option. If released, she must stay at home under GPS monitoring, surrender her weapons and avoid contact with the complainant.

She is due back in court on 2 December for a probable cause conference. The charges are allegations and have not been proven in court.

UN top court finds Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority during the 2017 military crackdown. It is the first time the court has found a state responsible for genocide.

Read more

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the United Nations' highest court, has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya Muslim minority during a military crackdown in 2017.

The ruling

In a landmark judgment on Thursday, 19 November 2026, the court found Myanmar responsible for genocide in northern Rakhine State. The decision was reached by a 9-6 majority and marks the first time in the court's 80-year history that it has found a state responsible for committing genocide.

The court said genocidal intent was the only reasonable conclusion that could be drawn from the evidence before it.

The case

The case was brought by The Gambia in 2019, accusing Myanmar of violating the 1948 Genocide Convention. The 2017 crackdown killed thousands of Rohingya and forced more than 700,000 to flee the country.

Myanmar's position

Myanmar had denied the accusations, saying its forces were responding to attacks by armed groups.

Tinubu endorses APC governorship candidates in Oyo, Ogun and Lagos at Ibadan rally, promises better power

21 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

At an APC rally at Mapo Hall in Ibadan, President Tinubu raised the hands of the party's governorship candidates for Oyo, Ogun and Lagos. He also guaranteed improved electricity and promised a factory in Ibadan.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has publicly endorsed the All Progressives Congress (APC) governorship candidates in Oyo, Ogun and Lagos States at a campaign rally at Mapo Hall in Ibadan on Saturday, 21 November 2026.

The endorsements

Telling the crowd he would show them who to vote for as governor, Tinubu raised the hands of Senator Sharafadeen Alli, the APC candidate in Oyo; Senator Solomon Adeola, known as Yayi, the party's candidate in Ogun; and Lagos Deputy Governor Obafemi Hamzat, the APC flagbearer in Lagos. He also raised the hand of Remi Oseni, the party's senatorial candidate for Oyo South.

Promise on power

During the visit, the President also assured Nigerians of better electricity supply, saying the Minister of Power and his team were working hard. He added that the government would build a factory in Ibadan.

Context

The rally is part of the APC's campaign ahead of the 16 January 2027 elections.

The other side

Reactions from the opposition parties to the rally were not included in the reports.
