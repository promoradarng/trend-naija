CBN sets ₦15bn capital rule for dollar-backed stablecoins in new crypto rules

8 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has introduced tougher rules for cryptocurrency and stablecoin businesses, including minimum capital requirements of up to ₦15 billion.

The new requirements

Under the regulations, reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026, issuers of naira-backed stablecoins must have at least ₦10 billion in paid-up capital, while operators offering tokens backed by foreign currencies, such as dollar-pegged stablecoins, will need ₦15 billion. Other virtual asset service providers, including digital wallet, payment, transfer and custody businesses, must have at least ₦1 billion. The CBN said it may demand more capital from larger or riskier operators.

Licensing and foreign firms

The rules make it illegal for individuals or companies to issue, exchange, distribute or facilitate regulated virtual assets in Nigeria without CBN approval. Foreign operators targeting Nigerian customers must incorporate and obtain licences locally, regardless of approvals elsewhere. Companies already licensed by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) will face extra capital requirements if they offer services under the CBN's oversight, while digital assets classed as securities will remain under SEC supervision. The rules also set standards for reserve management and consumer protection.

Deadlines and fines

The regulations, released on Friday, give existing operators 30 days to register with the CBN, 60 days to begin submitting supervisory reports and 180 days to complete licence applications. Operators can continue at their current scale during the transition, but those who miss the deadline must suspend operations until licensed. Firms operating without approval face fines starting at ₦100 million.

Background

Nigeria has one of the world's largest communities of cryptocurrency users. The CBN banned banks from dealing with crypto firms in 2021 before lifting the restriction in 2023, and the SEC has since moved to register and regulate crypto exchanges. Stablecoins, which are designed to keep a steady value linked to a currency such as the dollar, are widely used by Nigerians for savings and payments.

  • Dollar-backed stablecoin issuers need ₦15 billion in capital.
  • Naira-backed issuers need ₦10 billion.
  • Foreign operators must be licensed locally.

NAFDAC shuts Abuja's Zuba International Market over fake products worth ₦300m

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has shut down Zuba International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

The operation

The closure followed a joint enforcement operation with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services, which reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome products worth more than ₦300 million. The exercise covered more than 160 shops in the market.

Why now

Naira gains ₦19 to ₦1,378 per dollar after CBN raises BDC limit

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The naira strengthened by ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar on the official market on Thursday, after the CBN raised the weekly dollar sales limit for bureaux de change. It had lost over ₦43 in the previous two weeks.

Read more

The naira recovered strongly against the US dollar on the official foreign exchange market on Thursday, 26 November 2026.

The rebound

The currency gained ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar, up from ₦1,397 on Wednesday. The recovery reversed some of the losses it suffered between 13 and 25 November, when it fell by ₦43.50 amid rising demand for dollars.

Why

The gain followed the Central Bank of Nigeria's decision to raise the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators from $150,000 to $1 million, aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the market.

Background

The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 23%.

Dangote cuts cooking gas price to ₦1,000 per kg, but retail prices stay high

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its cooking gas gantry price to ₦1,000 per kg, with its marketers selling at ₦1,100. Consumers, however, are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kg at retail outlets.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery has reportedly reduced the wholesale price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), commonly known as cooking gas, to ₦1,000 per kilogram.

The new prices

Marketers linked to the refinery have lowered their selling price to ₦1,100 per kilogram, according to a market survey.

The retail gap

Despite the cuts, consumers are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kilogram at retail outlets, raising concerns about the gap between wholesale and retail prices. Earlier this week, cooking gas was reported to be selling for up to ₦2,000 per kg in parts of Abuja.

Background

The refinery has also cut its petrol and diesel prices in recent weeks, most recently reducing diesel by ₦125 to ₦1,595 per litre.
