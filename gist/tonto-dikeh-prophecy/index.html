Funke Akindele says 'Everybody Loves Jenifa' is her most expensive film

31 Oct 2026, 20:42 GMT

Funke Akindele has reportedly named 'Everybody Loves Jenifa' as the most expensive film she has produced, citing travel to Ghana for the cast and crew and the cost of building her own sets. She spoke in a CNN interview about her filmmaking career.

Read more

Nollywood's top-grossing filmmaker Funke Akindele has revealed which of her films cost the most to make.

What she said

In an interview with CNN journalist Larry Madowo, reported on Saturday, 31 October 2026, Funke Akindele reportedly named 'Everybody Loves Jenifa' as the most expensive film she has produced. She said the costs came from flying the entire cast and crew to Ghana, filming some scenes at a high-end resort, and building sets back in Nigeria. She explained that she prefers to build her own sets rather than shoot in other people's homes. She did not give a figure for the budget.

Box office success

Akindele said the film performed well both in cinemas and on streaming platforms. 'Everybody Loves Jenifa', released in December 2024, became one of the highest-grossing Nollywood films of all time, continuing her run of record-breaking releases that includes 'Battle on Buka Street' and 'A Tribe Called Judah'.

Background

Akindele is best known for the Jenifa character, which began in 2008 and grew into a television series and several films. She is currently promoting her next film, 'The Four', for which she recently announced a partnership with Facebook. Fellow actress Toyin Abraham this week shared an old TikTok collaboration, which fans read as a response to Funke's announcement.

How fans reacted

Fans praised her ambition and investment in quality productions, saying her success has raised the standard for Nollywood films.

Joke Silva thanks supporters as 40-day mourning for Olu Jacobs ends

31 Oct 2026, 17:44 GMT

Veteran actress Joke Silva has thanked Nigerians for their prayers, visits and support as the family marks the end of the 40-day mourning period for her husband, the late screen legend Olu Jacobs.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actress Joke Silva has issued a heartfelt message of thanks as her family marks the end of the 40-day mourning period for her late husband, Olu Jacobs.

What she wrote

In an Instagram post on Saturday, 31 October 2026, accompanied by a photo of herself and her husband, Joke Silva wrote on behalf of the Jacobs family to thank everyone who had supported them. She thanked those who sent prayers, messages and calls, those who visited in person, and those who gave gifts or contributed towards the funeral. She said the 40 days had come and gone, and that the family was humbled and deeply grateful. She signed the message as Ajoke Silva, on behalf of the Jacobs family.

Who was Olu Jacobs

Olu Jacobs was one of the most respected actors in Nigerian film history, with a career spanning stage, television and cinema in Nigeria and the United Kingdom. He appeared in many Nollywood classics and international productions and was widely regarded as a father figure in the industry. He and Joke Silva, herself an acclaimed actress and co-founder of the Lufodo Academy of Performing Arts, were one of Nollywood's most admired couples.

How fans reacted

Fans and colleagues responded with messages of love and prayers for the family, praising Joke Silva for her strength and dignity during the period of mourning.

Toyin Abraham's throwback TikTok post sparks talk of rivalry with Funke Akindele

31 Oct 2026, 09:42 GMT

Toyin Abraham has shared an old TikTok collaboration from her film 'OverSabi Aunty' and said she looks forward to more ahead of her December release 'Bestie'. Fans linked the post to Funke Akindele's new Facebook partnership for 'The Four', reviving talk of a rivalry.

Read more

Nollywood actress and filmmaker Toyin Abraham has once again found herself at the centre of talk about a rivalry with Funke Akindele, after a social media post that many fans read as a response to her colleague.

What she posted

On Friday, 30 October 2026, Toyin Abraham shared on Instagram a throwback clip of a TikTok collaboration she did while promoting her earlier film, 'OverSabi Aunty'. In the caption, she said she was looking forward to more collaborations ahead of her new film, 'Bestie', which is due in cinemas in December 2026.

Why fans linked it to Funke

The post came just days after Funke Akindele announced a partnership with Facebook for her new film, 'The Four'. Many fans interpreted Toyin's post as a subtle reminder that she had worked with a major platform first, although she did not mention Funke, and her intention has not been confirmed.

Background

The two actresses are among the most successful filmmakers in Nollywood, and fans have long compared their box office results. Toyin has also faced accusations of copying Funke's ideas, which she has denied. In the past she has spoken warmly about Funke, congratulating her and calling her the winner of their rivalry after one of Funke's films crossed ₦1 billion at the box office.

How people reacted

Reactions were divided. Critics questioned the timing of the post and accused her of seeking attention, while her supporters said she had every right to talk about her own past work. Funke Akindele has not commented.

Social media clout without financial growth is a wasted opportunity: what Daniel Regha can learn from VDM's RATEL

1 Nov 2026, 09:07 GMT

Daniel Regha has questioned VeryDarkMan's move from activism into business with his RATEL brand. But VDM's shift raises a bigger question for every Nigerian influencer: what are you building with all that attention?

Read more

Daniel Regha has weighed in on VeryDarkMan's growing RATEL business, writing on X that VDM had gone "from activist, to NGO business, to CEO" and "from criticizing business owners to joining the same businesses to make profit."

His post drew mixed reactions. One user replied that anyone who has not yet understood VDM is to be pitied, while another asked what VDM is supposed to do with the money he makes from social media: give it all away and own nothing because he is an activist?

Influence without a business

Sometimes one wonders whether Daniel Regha sees the business opportunities sitting right in front of him. For someone who commands so much attention on X, sparks conversations almost daily and has built a considerable following, you would expect him to have turned that influence into something financially rewarding by now.

Perhaps the fear of what people will say is holding him back.

You are only a tenant

Many influencers forget that they do not own these platforms. They are simply tenants. Facebook, X, TikTok and Instagram can suspend an account, restrict its reach or change their policies overnight, and years of gathering followers could suddenly amount to very little.

What VDM seems to understand

VeryDarkMan appears to understand something many influencers have yet to figure out: popularity has an expiry date. The same people cheering you today may move on to another personality tomorrow.

Instead of depending entirely on public attention, he is gradually converting his influence into a business. He is introducing products, building a customer base and creating something that could keep generating income long after the social media excitement fades. That is smart thinking.

People will always talk

Imagine spending years building an audience of hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of people, only to have nothing financially sustainable to show for it.

If you have the audience, build a business. Sell products, offer services, create something valuable and earn legitimate money from the influence you have worked hard to establish. If you sell something, people will criticise you. If you remain broke, those same people will still have something to say. The people whose opinions you fear will not come together to pay your rent, feed your family or settle your bills.

The real question

The advice to Daniel Regha and other social media personalities is simple: stop treating online popularity as a lifetime achievement, and find a way to turn that attention into lasting value. VDM may have his critics, but his decision to venture into business deserves recognition.

Because when the followers stop cheering and the cameras stop rolling, the real question will be: what did you build with all that influence?

What do you think? Should influencers concentrate on entertaining their followers, or should they use their popularity to build businesses?
