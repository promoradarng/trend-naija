Atiku says Tinubu has broken his promise of constant electricity

1 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The presidential campaign of Atiku Abubakar has accused President Bola Tinubu of breaking a key campaign promise on electricity.

What Atiku's campaign said

In a statement issued on Sunday, 1 November 2026, by Phrank Shaibu, communications director of the ADC presidential campaign, the campaign recalled that Tinubu promised during his 2022 campaign that Nigerians should not vote for him again if he failed to provide constant electricity in four years. The statement accused the ruling APC of hiding the failure behind announcements of new laws, bonds and committees, saying that settling old debts owed to power companies is not the same as building new generation capacity. It argued that signing a law or raising a bond does not produce electricity.

Tariffs and taxes

The campaign also questioned what would happen to electricity bills when subsidies end in 2027, although officials have said there will be no immediate tariff rise. It criticised a planned 5 per cent surcharge on some fuels under the Nigeria Tax Act, which the government says is not yet active. Atiku pledged to work with states and regulators to replace the current band system with a framework based on how much electricity customers actually receive.

The government's position

The Federal Government says it is fixing the power sector step by step. This week the Federal Executive Council approved procurement for new East and West super grids to raise transmission capacity to 19,000MW within two years, and the Minister of Power set a long-term target of 70,000MW by 2060. The government has also paid part of the debts owed to gas suppliers and power companies. The APC had not responded to Atiku's latest statement at the time of the report.

Why it matters

Electricity supply is one of the issues that matter most to voters, and all the main candidates have made promises on power.

  • Atiku says Tinubu has not delivered the constant power he promised in 2022.
  • He says paying debts and passing laws is not the same as generating power.
  • He promises to replace the band tariff system.

Yoruba Muslim clerics' league says it will not endorse any 2027 candidate

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland says it will not back any politician or party in the 2027 elections, insisting its role is to pray for Nigeria and guide the people, not to campaign.

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland has declared that it will not endorse any politician or party ahead of the 2027 elections.

What the clerics said

At a gathering in Ibadan on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the chairman of the league's board of trustees, Sheikh Muhammad Habibullah Adam-Abdullahi El-Ilory, said the league had neither the capacity nor the intention to name a preferred candidate for any office. He said Islamic clerics are not politicians and their duty does not include campaigning for any individual or party. League president Sheikh Jamiu Kewulere said imams must stay focused on religious guidance.

Background

The statement came after Oyo governor and APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde made a donation to the league. The lecturers' union ASUU also said this week that it would not endorse any candidate. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Tinubu and Aregbesola have met three times since their rift, says APC chieftain

15 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Professor Sola Adeyeye, an APC campaign director in the South-West, says President Tinubu and former Osun governor Rauf Aregbesola have met at least three times since their political falling-out, and that he has tried to reconcile them.

President Bola Tinubu and his former close ally Rauf Aregbesola have met at least three times since their political falling-out, according to a senior figure in the ruling party.

What he said

Professor Sola Adeyeye, director of electioneering planning for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign in the South-West, said in an interview reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, that he knew the two men had met about three times since the rift began. He described them as former very close friends, said the hurt is deeper when people who care about each other feel betrayed, and suggested time could heal the relationship. He said he had personally tried to broker a reconciliation but did not give details.

Background

Aregbesola, a former Osun governor and minister, was one of Tinubu's closest political allies for decades before they fell out. He is now national secretary of the Mark-led faction of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), whose leadership was voided by a Federal High Court this month, a ruling the party has appealed. Aregbesola has not commented on Adeyeye's claim.

Atiku and Makinde camps reportedly hold alliance talks ahead of 2027

15 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Representatives of ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar and APM candidate Seyi Makinde have reportedly held a series of meetings, with sources in both camps saying an alliance before the 16 January election looks increasingly likely.

Read more

Representatives of former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde have reportedly been meeting to discuss a possible alliance ahead of the 2027 presidential election.

The talks

According to reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, sources from both camps say the prospect of an alliance is looking increasingly likely. An ADC leader in the South-West, speaking anonymously, described the discussions as fresh, purposeful and promising, and suggested an announcement would come before the 16 January election. The talks are said to be separate from wider opposition efforts to agree on a single candidate. Neither Atiku nor Makinde has publicly confirmed any deal.

Background

Atiku is the candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), while Makinde is running on the platform of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM). Makinde hosted an opposition summit in Ibadan in April to discuss a joint candidate, but no agreement emerged, with Atiku, Makinde and Peter Obi each pressing ahead with their own campaigns. Makinde said this week that talks with both Atiku and Obi remained open. ADC running mate Rotimi Amaechi also told a rally in Bauchi that the APM would win one South-West state.

ASUU warns officials against using union's name to endorse 2027 candidates

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Academic Staff Union of Universities says it will sanction any official who uses the union's name to back a candidate or party in 2027, stressing that it remains apolitical though members are free to vote.

Read more

The Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) has warned its members and officials not to use the union's name to endorse political candidates or parties ahead of the 2027 elections.

What ASUU said

Speaking at the union's 2026 Heroes' Day celebration in Abuja, reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, ASUU National President Professor Christopher Piwuna said individual lecturers keep their constitutional right to vote and take part in politics, but must not present their personal choices as the union's position. He said no member had permission to use ASUU's name to support any candidate or party, that the union remained apolitical, and that any official found compromising that stance by associating with parties or politicians would face disciplinary action.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
