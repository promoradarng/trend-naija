Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

Atiku responds

In a statement issued from Birnin Kebbi on Thursday through his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku condemned the ruling and described it as a deliberate attempt to derail his campaign after the large turnout at his Sokoto launch. His campaign pointed to an earlier Federal High Court ruling that had dismissed a similar challenge to the David Mark-led leadership, and said the judgment would be challenged at the Court of Appeal. It said the campaign would continue, moving on to Kebbi State.

Party and government react

The ADC's national publicity secretary, Bolaji Abdullahi, described the judgment as legally indefensible and politically motivated, and said its timing, straight after the Sokoto rally, raised serious concerns. The party cited a ruling by another Federal High Court judge on 2 July 2026 that affirmed the David Mark-led leadership, and warned the APC against using the courts to eliminate competition. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo welcomed the judgment, saying justice may be slow but is always sure, and rejected claims that it threatens democracy.

What the judge ruled

According to further reports, Justice Lifu held that Mark's emergence as ADC chairman violated the party's constitution, the Electoral Act and the 1999 Constitution. The court also struck down the appointment of former Interior Minister Rauf Aregbesola as national secretary, along with other national officers and candidates the party had presented for 2027. Nafiu Bala Gombe, who filed the suit and leads a rival faction, praised the court for handling a politically sensitive case without outside influence.

Atiku vows to stay in the race

On Friday, 6 November, Atiku declared that he would not step back from the 2027 presidential contest. He accused the Tinubu administration of using political pressure and court cases to weaken opposition parties, and alleged that there were attempts to compromise the voter register with AI-generated photographs and false identities, warning that Nigeria risked drifting towards a one-party system. He also criticised the government's handling of fuel subsidy removal, the fall of the naira and rising food prices. Separately, Shehu Sani, the APC senatorial candidate in Kaduna, advised the ADC to concentrate on appealing the ruling, saying the party was paying the price for taking over an existing, troubled party instead of registering a new one.

INEC disowns fake poster

Also on Friday, INEC said a poster circulating on social media, which claimed the commission had reaffirmed the David Mark-led leadership of the ADC, was fake and did not come from it. The commission condemned the unauthorised use of its logo and of a photograph of National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, and urged the public to rely only on its official channels.

ADC files appeal

Later on Friday, the ADC said it had filed an appeal at the Court of Appeal against the judgment, together with an application for a stay of execution to halt any action based on it while the appeal is heard. The party said INEC was formally notified of both filings on 6 November. In a statement by Phrank Shaibu, the party urged the commission not to take any action on the judgment until the stay application is decided, and asked its members and supporters to remain calm.

Keyamo: ADC will be on the ballot

On Sunday, 8 November, Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo said on X that the ADC as a party would take part in the 2027 elections, arguing that the judgment affected only the faction led by David Mark, under which Atiku emerged as candidate, and not the party itself. He questioned why critics described the ruling as an attack on democracy, saying candidates who won primaries under the party's original leadership also had rights. Amaechi, Atiku's running mate, has warned that the ruling could cause unrest if it is not properly managed.

INEC waits for judgment

INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna said on Sunday that the commission would not act on the ruling until it had obtained and reviewed a certified true copy of the judgment, as it could not rely on media reports. He said the matter would be discussed at the commission's meeting next week, and that INEC would obey any court order arising from an appeal.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign.

Makinde visits families of Jos Palm Sunday attack victims

9 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde has visited Angwan Rukuba in Jos North, Plateau State, to console families of the 33 people killed in an attack on 29 March, telling residents not to give up on Nigeria.

Read more

Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde, presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), has visited Angwan Rukuba in Jos North, Plateau State, to console families of victims of a deadly attack earlier this year.

The visit

During the visit on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Makinde met residents at the home of the community head and described the killings as a national tragedy rather than a local matter. He urged the grieving community not to give up on Nigeria and assured them they were not alone. He also visited the family of Promise Gideon Ayuba, one of the victims, whose mother received him and prayed for him.

The attack

Armed men on motorcycles attacked the Gari Ya Waye area of the community on the evening of 29 March 2026, Palm Sunday, killing 33 people. The victim's mother, Favour Ayuba, drew national attention after a video showed her placing a cutlass in her son's coffin as a demand for justice. She was also among relatives who met former Archbishop of Canterbury Justin Welby when he visited Plateau in September.

Background

Plateau State has suffered repeated deadly attacks in recent years, with communities in Jos and surrounding areas affected. Makinde is campaigning across the country ahead of the 16 January 2027 presidential election, and his campaign this week also criticised President Bola Tinubu's claims about the naira.

Makinde campaign says Tinubu cannot claim credit for naira recovery

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The campaign of APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde has accused President Tinubu of seeking praise for the naira's recent stability after policies that sharply weakened it, saying small exchange rate gains do little for Nigerians facing high prices.

Read more

The presidential campaign of Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde has criticised President Bola Tinubu's claims about the naira.

What the campaign said

In a statement reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, Richard Nnanna Ihediwa, director of strategic communications for the Makinde–Daura campaign, accused the President of trying to take credit for the naira's recent stability after policies that first caused a drastic fall in its value. He said the President could not preside over the destruction of the currency's purchasing power and then ask for applause. The campaign pointed out that the official exchange rate was about ₦461 to the dollar when Tinubu took office in May 2023, before the removal of the fuel subsidy and the unification of the exchange rate, and said marginal improvements offer little relief to people facing high inflation.

Background

The statement responds to Tinubu's remarks in Uyo last week, where he said the naira would have become worthless without his reforms and put the current rate at about ₦1,300 to the dollar. Makinde is the candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), with former DSS chief Lawal Musa Daura as his running mate, and recently launched his 'Reset Agenda' manifesto. The government has said the old official rate understated the true value of the dollar and that unification was needed.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Hamzat vows to enforce one-year rent limit as Lagos governorship candidates debate

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

Lagos APC governorship candidate Obafemi Hamzat says the state will enforce the legal limit on advance rent to one year, warning landlords against demanding two or three years upfront. He spoke at a televised governorship debate.

Read more

Lagos State Deputy Governor Obafemi Hamzat, the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate for governor, says the state government will enforce the legal limit on advance rent payments.

What he said

Speaking at a governorship debate organised by a coalition of media organisations on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Hamzat said the one-year cap on advance rent is already contained in the Lagos State Real Estate Registration Agency law passed in 2025, and that the focus now is making sure it is followed. He warned landlords against demanding two or three years' rent upfront, and said estate agents are required to register with the state and appear on an official database, urging residents to check it before dealing with agents.

The law

The Lagos State Tenancy Law of 2011 already makes it unlawful for landlords or their agents to demand more than one year's rent in advance from a new tenant, and limits advance payments from sitting tenants. Despite this, demands for two or more years' rent have remained a widespread concern.

Rhodes-Vivour's pitch

At the same debate, Gbadebo Rhodes-Vivour, the governorship candidate of the opposition ADC, said APC governments in Lagos had focused on policies that favoured the elite for more than 20 years, while residents of poorer and rural communities did not feel the benefit of the state's high revenue. He promised more than 100,000 housing units, a safer and cleaner state, and technology-driven measures to end flooding.

Background

Hamzat is seeking to succeed Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu in the 2027 governorship election. Former governor Akinwunmi Ambode recently pledged to mobilise support for him. Housing costs are one of the biggest concerns for Lagos residents.

Okowa says he left Atiku for Tinubu because he has 'seen the light'

8 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former Delta governor Ifeanyi Okowa, Atiku Abubakar's running mate in 2023, told an APC rally in Port Harcourt that he switched to backing President Tinubu because he has seen the light, crediting the government's fiscal reforms.

Read more

Former Delta State governor Ifeanyi Okowa has explained why he abandoned former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar to support President Bola Tinubu ahead of the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at an All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Okowa reportedly said he had 'seen the light'. He argued that Tinubu inherited a country with foundations weakened by decades of mismanagement, and that the current administration was making tough but necessary decisions to fix them. He credited the government's fiscal reforms with increasing the revenue shared with states and local governments, saying the results could be seen in better project delivery and salary payments. He also compared Tinubu's record as Lagos governor with his performance as President, saying the same transformation was now happening across the country.

Background

Okowa was Atiku's vice-presidential running mate on the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) ticket in the 2023 election, which Tinubu won. He later left the PDP for the APC, following his successor in Delta, Governor Sheriff Oborevwori. Atiku is now the candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), although a Federal High Court ruling last week voided his nomination, a decision the party has appealed. At the same rally, Tinubu questioned how Atiku built a university while he was vice-president, prompting a sharp reply from Atiku's campaign.

What happens next

Atiku's campaign had not publicly responded to Okowa's remarks at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
