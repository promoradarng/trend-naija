Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

Atiku responds

In a statement issued from Birnin Kebbi on Thursday through his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku condemned the ruling and described it as a deliberate attempt to derail his campaign after the large turnout at his Sokoto launch. His campaign pointed to an earlier Federal High Court ruling that had dismissed a similar challenge to the David Mark-led leadership, and said the judgment would be challenged at the Court of Appeal. It said the campaign would continue, moving on to Kebbi State.

Party and government react

The ADC's national publicity secretary, Bolaji Abdullahi, described the judgment as legally indefensible and politically motivated, and said its timing, straight after the Sokoto rally, raised serious concerns. The party cited a ruling by another Federal High Court judge on 2 July 2026 that affirmed the David Mark-led leadership, and warned the APC against using the courts to eliminate competition. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo welcomed the judgment, saying justice may be slow but is always sure, and rejected claims that it threatens democracy.

What the judge ruled

According to further reports, Justice Lifu held that Mark's emergence as ADC chairman violated the party's constitution, the Electoral Act and the 1999 Constitution. The court also struck down the appointment of former Interior Minister Rauf Aregbesola as national secretary, along with other national officers and candidates the party had presented for 2027. Nafiu Bala Gombe, who filed the suit and leads a rival faction, praised the court for handling a politically sensitive case without outside influence.

Atiku vows to stay in the race

On Friday, 6 November, Atiku declared that he would not step back from the 2027 presidential contest. He accused the Tinubu administration of using political pressure and court cases to weaken opposition parties, and alleged that there were attempts to compromise the voter register with AI-generated photographs and false identities, warning that Nigeria risked drifting towards a one-party system. He also criticised the government's handling of fuel subsidy removal, the fall of the naira and rising food prices. Separately, Shehu Sani, the APC senatorial candidate in Kaduna, advised the ADC to concentrate on appealing the ruling, saying the party was paying the price for taking over an existing, troubled party instead of registering a new one.

INEC disowns fake poster

Also on Friday, INEC said a poster circulating on social media, which claimed the commission had reaffirmed the David Mark-led leadership of the ADC, was fake and did not come from it. The commission condemned the unauthorised use of its logo and of a photograph of National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, and urged the public to rely only on its official channels.

ADC files appeal

Later on Friday, the ADC said it had filed an appeal at the Court of Appeal against the judgment, together with an application for a stay of execution to halt any action based on it while the appeal is heard. The party said INEC was formally notified of both filings on 6 November. In a statement by Phrank Shaibu, the party urged the commission not to take any action on the judgment until the stay application is decided, and asked its members and supporters to remain calm.

Keyamo: ADC will be on the ballot

On Sunday, 8 November, Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo said on X that the ADC as a party would take part in the 2027 elections, arguing that the judgment affected only the faction led by David Mark, under which Atiku emerged as candidate, and not the party itself. He questioned why critics described the ruling as an attack on democracy, saying candidates who won primaries under the party's original leadership also had rights. Amaechi, Atiku's running mate, has warned that the ruling could cause unrest if it is not properly managed.

INEC waits for judgment

INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna said on Sunday that the commission would not act on the ruling until it had obtained and reviewed a certified true copy of the judgment, as it could not rely on media reports. He said the matter would be discussed at the commission's meeting next week, and that INEC would obey any court order arising from an appeal.

ADC demands copy of judgment

On Monday, 9 November, the ADC called on the Federal High Court to release the judgment, saying paid applications for certified copies had gone unanswered although the judge said in open court that copies were ready. The party had earlier raised objections to Justice Lifu hearing the case, questioning his impartiality.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign.

INEC removes over 1.6 million invalid registrants from voter register

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

INEC says it has removed 1,608,660 invalid registrants and 876 dead voters from the register after a public review of the preliminary list. The cleaned-up register will be published on 16 December.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has removed 1,608,660 invalid registrants from Nigeria's voter register.

What INEC announced

The commission said on Monday, 9 November 2026, that the removals followed complaints, objections and claims submitted by the public after it displayed the preliminary voter register in October. It also deleted the records of 876 voters confirmed to have died, after verifying claims submitted during the review. INEC said the display of the preliminary register was meant to allow citizens and civil society groups to flag errors, duplicates and other irregularities.

What happens next

INEC said it will publish the final, cleaned-up register on 16 December 2026, as required by the Electoral Act. The commission expects more than 103 million registered voters for the 2027 elections.

Background

The figure is in line with the roughly 1.5 million questionable records INEC said earlier it had identified. A former INEC director, Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, questioned last week how so many invalid entries could have entered the register despite biometric safeguards, and called on the commission to explain how they were detected. Opposition parties have also raised concerns about alleged AI-generated photographs in the register.

Why it matters

A credible voter register is key to public confidence in the 16 January 2027 presidential election.

Chief Justice swears in 375 judges for 2027 election tribunals

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Chief Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun has sworn in 375 judges to serve on election petition tribunals for 2027, warning them to resist political pressure and cautioning politicians and lawyers against abusing ex parte applications.

Read more

The Chief Justice of Nigeria, Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, has sworn in 375 judges who will serve on the election petition tribunals for the 2027 general elections.

The ceremony

The swearing-in took place on Monday, 9 November 2026, at the Body of Benchers auditorium in Abuja, alongside the launch of the second edition of the Judicial Electoral Manual and a training workshop for judicial officers.

The CJN's warning

Justice Kekere-Ekun charged the judges to uphold judicial independence, resist political pressure and deliver justice without fear or favour, in line with the Constitution, judicial ethics and the rule of law. She also warned political parties, lawyers, the media and the public against behaviour aimed at undermining the judicial process, and specifically cautioned against the abuse of ex parte applications, which are orders granted without hearing the other side.

Background

Election petition tribunals hear challenges to the results of governorship, National Assembly and state assembly elections. Courts have played a major role in recent Nigerian elections, and pre-election cases are already shaping the 2027 contest, including a Federal High Court ruling that voided Atiku Abubakar's ADC nomination, which the party has appealed, and a ruling on the Imo West Senate ticket. Concerns about conflicting court orders, especially ex parte orders, have been raised repeatedly in recent years.

Victor Umeh: Any Igbo person against Peter Obi is an enemy of the Igbo

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Senator Victor Umeh of the NDC has said any Igbo person who campaigns against Peter Obi's presidential bid is an enemy of the Igbo people, and urged the South-East to unite behind him. He also cautioned the Anambra government.

Read more

Senator Victor Umeh, who represents Anambra Central, has warned that any Igbo person working against Peter Obi's presidential ambition is an enemy of the Igbo people.

What he said

In a video that circulated on Monday, 9 November 2026, Umeh, a member of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), described Obi as the conscience of the Igbo people in the 2027 election and urged South-East residents to unite behind his bid. He cautioned political leaders in the region, particularly the Anambra State Government, against actions that could undermine Obi's campaign.

Background

Obi, a former Anambra governor, is the NDC's presidential candidate, with Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate. While he remains popular in the South-East, several of the region's governors have declared support for President Bola Tinubu. Anambra Governor Charles Soludo's APGA last week adopted Tinubu as its candidate and promised him half of the state's votes, while Ebonyi Governor Francis Nwifuru on Monday promised Tinubu 80 per cent of Ebonyi's votes. The NDC in Anambra has set a target of 98 per cent of the state's votes for Obi.

Reactions

The Anambra government had not publicly responded at the time of writing.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Viral image of Peter Obi in hospital after gym injury is AI-generated, checks show

9 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

A viral image claiming NDC candidate Peter Obi was admitted to hospital after a treadmill injury has been found to be AI-generated, according to a fact-check using several detection tools. Obi's camp had earlier denied the report.

Read more

A viral image claiming that Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), was in hospital after a gym injury has been found to be fake.

The claim

The image was posted on X on Sunday, 1 November 2026, with a caption claiming Obi had injured his neck on a treadmill and been admitted to hospital. By Monday, 9 November, the post had been viewed more than 67,000 times, and similar posts had spread on other platforms.

The fact-check

According to a fact-check published on Monday, a reverse image search linked the picture to similar claims on other platforms, suggesting a wider effort to spread it. Several AI-detection tools found a high probability that the image was AI-generated, and a further check indicated it had been created with an AI image tool.

Background

Obi's campaign had earlier denied reports that he broke his neck in a gym accident, and the former Anambra governor has continued to campaign publicly, including at airports and rallies in recent days. Opposition parties have raised concerns about alleged AI-generated images in the voter register.

Why it matters

Fake images and videos of candidates are expected to be a growing problem in the 2027 campaign.
