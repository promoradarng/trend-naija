Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

Atiku responds

In a statement issued from Birnin Kebbi on Thursday through his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku condemned the ruling and described it as a deliberate attempt to derail his campaign after the large turnout at his Sokoto launch. His campaign pointed to an earlier Federal High Court ruling that had dismissed a similar challenge to the David Mark-led leadership, and said the judgment would be challenged at the Court of Appeal. It said the campaign would continue, moving on to Kebbi State.

Party and government react

The ADC's national publicity secretary, Bolaji Abdullahi, described the judgment as legally indefensible and politically motivated, and said its timing, straight after the Sokoto rally, raised serious concerns. The party cited a ruling by another Federal High Court judge on 2 July 2026 that affirmed the David Mark-led leadership, and warned the APC against using the courts to eliminate competition. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo welcomed the judgment, saying justice may be slow but is always sure, and rejected claims that it threatens democracy.

What the judge ruled

According to further reports, Justice Lifu held that Mark's emergence as ADC chairman violated the party's constitution, the Electoral Act and the 1999 Constitution. The court also struck down the appointment of former Interior Minister Rauf Aregbesola as national secretary, along with other national officers and candidates the party had presented for 2027. Nafiu Bala Gombe, who filed the suit and leads a rival faction, praised the court for handling a politically sensitive case without outside influence.

Atiku vows to stay in the race

On Friday, 6 November, Atiku declared that he would not step back from the 2027 presidential contest. He accused the Tinubu administration of using political pressure and court cases to weaken opposition parties, and alleged that there were attempts to compromise the voter register with AI-generated photographs and false identities, warning that Nigeria risked drifting towards a one-party system. He also criticised the government's handling of fuel subsidy removal, the fall of the naira and rising food prices. Separately, Shehu Sani, the APC senatorial candidate in Kaduna, advised the ADC to concentrate on appealing the ruling, saying the party was paying the price for taking over an existing, troubled party instead of registering a new one.

INEC disowns fake poster

Also on Friday, INEC said a poster circulating on social media, which claimed the commission had reaffirmed the David Mark-led leadership of the ADC, was fake and did not come from it. The commission condemned the unauthorised use of its logo and of a photograph of National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, and urged the public to rely only on its official channels.

ADC files appeal

Later on Friday, the ADC said it had filed an appeal at the Court of Appeal against the judgment, together with an application for a stay of execution to halt any action based on it while the appeal is heard. The party said INEC was formally notified of both filings on 6 November. In a statement by Phrank Shaibu, the party urged the commission not to take any action on the judgment until the stay application is decided, and asked its members and supporters to remain calm.

Keyamo: ADC will be on the ballot

On Sunday, 8 November, Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo said on X that the ADC as a party would take part in the 2027 elections, arguing that the judgment affected only the faction led by David Mark, under which Atiku emerged as candidate, and not the party itself. He questioned why critics described the ruling as an attack on democracy, saying candidates who won primaries under the party's original leadership also had rights. Amaechi, Atiku's running mate, has warned that the ruling could cause unrest if it is not properly managed.

INEC waits for judgment

INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna said on Sunday that the commission would not act on the ruling until it had obtained and reviewed a certified true copy of the judgment, as it could not rely on media reports. He said the matter would be discussed at the commission's meeting next week, and that INEC would obey any court order arising from an appeal.

ADC demands copy of judgment

On Monday, 9 November, the ADC called on the Federal High Court to release the judgment, saying paid applications for certified copies had gone unanswered although the judge said in open court that copies were ready. The party had earlier raised objections to Justice Lifu hearing the case, questioning his impartiality.

ADC accuses chief judge of bias

On 10 November, the ADC campaign accused Chief Judge of the Federal High Court, Justice John Tsoho, of bias over the transfer of the case from Justice Emeka Nwite, who had already begun hearing it, to Justice Lifu, a move the party said it had formally opposed as forum shopping. The party urged the APC to face Atiku at the polls rather than in court. The court had not responded to the claims.

Court rejected resignation letter

Details of the judgment published on Wednesday, 11 November, show that Justice Peter Lifu rejected a resignation letter the Mark-led faction had presented as evidence that the plaintiff, former deputy national chairman Nafiu Bala, had left his post. After comparing signatures, the judge found that the letter did not carry Bala's authentic signature. Bala had always insisted the letter was forged.

Protest in Abuja

On Wednesday, Atiku's supporters gathered outside the Federal High Court in Abuja to demand the release of a certified true copy of the judgment, which the party needs for its appeal.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign.

  • That includes Atiku's nomination as presidential candidate.
  • An appeal is widely expected.

Crowd chants 'Obi' as Deputy Speaker Kalu campaigns for Tinubu at Abia festival

12 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

A crowd at a cultural festival in Bende, Abia State, reportedly drowned out Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu with chants of 'Obi, Obi' as he tried to rally support for President Tinubu's re-election.

Read more

Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives Benjamin Kalu was reportedly met with chants of Peter Obi's name as he campaigned for President Bola Tinubu at a festival in Abia State.

What happened

Kalu, an All Progressives Congress (APC) lawmaker who represents Bende Federal Constituency, was addressing the Item Okpi Festival, an annual cultural celebration in Bende Local Government Area, on Saturday. According to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the audience responded with loud and sustained chants of Obi's name rather than applause. When a woman on stage began a pro-Obi song, Kalu reportedly asked her to switch to a pro-Tinubu song, but the chanting continued. The Deputy Speaker was said to have remarked in Igbo that the crowd did not seem to want a university the President plans to build for them, and suggested some of those chanting were not from Item.

Background

Abia is governed by Alex Otti of the Labour Party, a former ally of Obi who has said he supports Tinubu as the sitting president while acknowledging that his party has its own candidate. Obi, now the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), remains popular in the South-East, where the APC is working hard to win support ahead of the 16 January 2027 election. Kalu has not publicly commented on the video.

NDC deputy national spokesman Abdulmumin Abdulsalami quits party

12 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Abdulmumin Abdulsalami, deputy national publicity secretary of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, has resigned from the party, saying a few powerful officials sidelined him. He says he is stepping back from politics.

Read more

The deputy national publicity secretary of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Abdulmumin Abdulsalami, has resigned from the party.

His reasons

In a resignation letter addressed to NDC national chairman Senator Moses Cleopas and reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Abdulsalami said he was a foundation member who played a central role in the party's early growth, but that his efforts had clashed with the interests of a few powerful officials. He said the decision was about his peace of mind, citing what he described as repeated injustices against him. He declined to name the officials he blamed.

What next

Abdulsalami said he was stepping away from partisan politics entirely. A party source quoted in reports claimed he had not been paid a salary or given a working budget since the party was formed, and that the NDC's national leader, Senator Seriake Dickson, had persuaded him to stay on several occasions since October.

Background

Abdulsalami was previously the NDC's interim chairman in Kogi State. The NDC is the party of presidential candidate Peter Obi and his running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso. His exit comes a day after Obi's former spokesman Yunusa Tanko took a senior role in the SDP's presidential campaign. The party had not publicly responded to his resignation at the time of writing.

EU raises alarm over election violence and hate speech ahead of 2027 polls

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The European Union's ambassador to Nigeria, Gautier Mignot, has warned that rising election-related violence, hate speech and intimidation threaten democratic progress and could discourage Nigerians from voting in 2027.

Read more

The European Union has expressed concern about rising election-related violence, hate speech and political intimidation in Nigeria ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What the EU said

Speaking at the opening of the 2026 Civil Society and Human Rights Defenders Forum in Abuja, reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the EU Ambassador to Nigeria and ECOWAS, Gautier Mignot, warned that such incidents erode democratic gains and discourage citizens from taking full part in elections. He said free civic and political space allows civil society groups and citizens to monitor how elections are run, track campaign promises and hold leaders to account, and helps prevent authorities from manipulating the rules.

Support for institutions

Mignot said open public debate, independent media, peaceful assembly and an active civil society are vital to credible elections. He said the EU would continue to support Nigerian institutions, including the National Human Rights Commission, to help create a free and fair electoral environment, while stressing that it would not interfere in domestic politics.

Background

The warning follows a string of violent incidents linked to campaigns in several states. Peter Obi's convoy was attacked in Calabar, NDC supporters' vehicles were destroyed at a rally in Delta, and APC supporters were attacked ahead of a rally in Katsina. The Inspector-General of Police has also warned officers against being used by politicians. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

IGP Disu warns officers: 'Do not be used' by politicians in 2027 elections

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Inspector-General of Police Tunji Disu has warned officers that anyone who colludes with politicians, facilitates thuggery or interferes with voters will be disciplined, and has ordered senior officers to stay in their commands until the elections are over.

Read more

The Inspector-General of Police, Tunji Disu, has warned police officers against allowing politicians to use them to undermine the 2027 elections.

What he said

Speaking on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, at a monthly meeting with senior officers in Abuja, Disu said the police would remain neutral and give equal protection to all candidates, parties, supporters and voters. He said he would not tolerate any officer using police authority to advance the interests of a party, candidate or sponsor, and that officers found colluding with politicians, aiding thuggery or interfering with voters' rights would be disciplined. He told officers that the force is not a tool for any politician and urged them not to allow themselves to be used.

Officers to stay at posts

The IGP directed commissioners of police and other senior officers to remain in their commands throughout the electoral process.

Background

The warning comes amid concern over political violence ahead of the elections and a recent controversy over a video showing police officers escorting businessman Blord, which the police said was unlawful. On the same day, the police said they had raided so-called phone farms used to spread election misinformation. The European Union has also raised concerns about violence and hate speech before the polls.
