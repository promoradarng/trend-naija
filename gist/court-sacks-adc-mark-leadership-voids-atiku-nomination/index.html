Court sacks ADC's David Mark-led leadership, voids Atiku's presidential nomination

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has removed the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and voided its decisions, including the nomination of former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as the party's 2027 presidential candidate, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The judgment

Justice Peter Lifu reportedly delivered the judgment in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former national deputy chairman of the ADC for the North-East. The court ruled against the caretaker committee led by former Senate President David Mark and set aside all decisions it took on behalf of the party, which would include the primary that produced Atiku as candidate. Full details of the court's reasoning and any orders to INEC were still emerging.

Timing

The ruling came less than 24 hours after Atiku formally launched his presidential campaign at a rally in Sokoto, which his campaign said drew a large crowd.

Background

The ADC became the platform of a coalition of opposition figures in 2025, with David Mark as interim national chairman. Its leadership has faced legal challenges from former officials who say they were wrongly pushed aside. Several court cases over party primaries are ongoing across all major parties, and courts have recently sacked candidates in Nasarawa and ordered changes elsewhere.

Atiku responds

In a statement issued from Birnin Kebbi on Thursday through his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku condemned the ruling and described it as a deliberate attempt to derail his campaign after the large turnout at his Sokoto launch. His campaign pointed to an earlier Federal High Court ruling that had dismissed a similar challenge to the David Mark-led leadership, and said the judgment would be challenged at the Court of Appeal. It said the campaign would continue, moving on to Kebbi State.

Party and government react

The ADC's national publicity secretary, Bolaji Abdullahi, described the judgment as legally indefensible and politically motivated, and said its timing, straight after the Sokoto rally, raised serious concerns. The party cited a ruling by another Federal High Court judge on 2 July 2026 that affirmed the David Mark-led leadership, and warned the APC against using the courts to eliminate competition. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo welcomed the judgment, saying justice may be slow but is always sure, and rejected claims that it threatens democracy.

What the judge ruled

According to further reports, Justice Lifu held that Mark's emergence as ADC chairman violated the party's constitution, the Electoral Act and the 1999 Constitution. The court also struck down the appointment of former Interior Minister Rauf Aregbesola as national secretary, along with other national officers and candidates the party had presented for 2027. Nafiu Bala Gombe, who filed the suit and leads a rival faction, praised the court for handling a politically sensitive case without outside influence.

Atiku vows to stay in the race

On Friday, 6 November, Atiku declared that he would not step back from the 2027 presidential contest. He accused the Tinubu administration of using political pressure and court cases to weaken opposition parties, and alleged that there were attempts to compromise the voter register with AI-generated photographs and false identities, warning that Nigeria risked drifting towards a one-party system. He also criticised the government's handling of fuel subsidy removal, the fall of the naira and rising food prices. Separately, Shehu Sani, the APC senatorial candidate in Kaduna, advised the ADC to concentrate on appealing the ruling, saying the party was paying the price for taking over an existing, troubled party instead of registering a new one.

INEC disowns fake poster

Also on Friday, INEC said a poster circulating on social media, which claimed the commission had reaffirmed the David Mark-led leadership of the ADC, was fake and did not come from it. The commission condemned the unauthorised use of its logo and of a photograph of National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, and urged the public to rely only on its official channels.

ADC files appeal

Later on Friday, the ADC said it had filed an appeal at the Court of Appeal against the judgment, together with an application for a stay of execution to halt any action based on it while the appeal is heard. The party said INEC was formally notified of both filings on 6 November. In a statement by Phrank Shaibu, the party urged the commission not to take any action on the judgment until the stay application is decided, and asked its members and supporters to remain calm.

Keyamo: ADC will be on the ballot

On Sunday, 8 November, Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo said on X that the ADC as a party would take part in the 2027 elections, arguing that the judgment affected only the faction led by David Mark, under which Atiku emerged as candidate, and not the party itself. He questioned why critics described the ruling as an attack on democracy, saying candidates who won primaries under the party's original leadership also had rights. Amaechi, Atiku's running mate, has warned that the ruling could cause unrest if it is not properly managed.

INEC waits for judgment

INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna said on Sunday that the commission would not act on the ruling until it had obtained and reviewed a certified true copy of the judgment, as it could not rely on media reports. He said the matter would be discussed at the commission's meeting next week, and that INEC would obey any court order arising from an appeal.

ADC demands copy of judgment

On Monday, 9 November, the ADC called on the Federal High Court to release the judgment, saying paid applications for certified copies had gone unanswered although the judge said in open court that copies were ready. The party had earlier raised objections to Justice Lifu hearing the case, questioning his impartiality.

ADC accuses chief judge of bias

On 10 November, the ADC campaign accused Chief Judge of the Federal High Court, Justice John Tsoho, of bias over the transfer of the case from Justice Emeka Nwite, who had already begun hearing it, to Justice Lifu, a move the party said it had formally opposed as forum shopping. The party urged the APC to face Atiku at the polls rather than in court. The court had not responded to the claims.

Court rejected resignation letter

Details of the judgment published on Wednesday, 11 November, show that Justice Peter Lifu rejected a resignation letter the Mark-led faction had presented as evidence that the plaintiff, former deputy national chairman Nafiu Bala, had left his post. After comparing signatures, the judge found that the letter did not carry Bala's authentic signature. Bala had always insisted the letter was forged.

Protest in Abuja

On Wednesday, Atiku's supporters gathered outside the Federal High Court in Abuja to demand the release of a certified true copy of the judgment, which the party needs for its appeal.

What happens next

The ADC and Atiku are widely expected to appeal. Until the appeal is decided, it is not clear how the ruling will affect his candidacy or the party's campaign.

  • The court voided all decisions of the Mark-led caretaker committee.
  • That includes Atiku's nomination as presidential candidate.
  • An appeal is widely expected.

DSS says it found no evidence it planned to disrupt ADC campaign in Sokoto, asks Atiku to retract or provide proof

18 Nov 2026, 07:57 GMT

The Department of State Services says its internal investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off in Sokoto. It has asked Atiku Abubakar to retract or clarify his allegation, or provide evidence.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) says its investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential campaign in Sokoto.

How it started

Earlier this month, former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar accused the leadership of the DSS of recruiting protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto.

The allegation drew wide attention, and the DSS responded by ordering an investigation into the claim.

The DSS findings

The agency now says its investigation found nothing to support the allegation that it planned to recruit protesters or disrupt the ADC campaign.

It has asked Atiku to either retract or clarify his claim, or to provide evidence that could support further investigation.

Not the final word

It is important to note that this is the DSS's own preliminary finding. It was not an independent investigation, which means the agency effectively investigated an allegation made against itself.

Atiku has not issued a fresh public response to the DSS findings.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections approaching, allegations that security agencies are being used against opposition parties are likely to keep coming up. Public trust depends on such claims being properly examined, whether they turn out to be true or not.

CBN and NNPC sign new agreement to tighten oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue

18 Nov 2026, 14:54 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement covering foreign-exchange inflows, remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing and more. It promises a more structured system, but the full agreement has not been released and no revenue targets have been set.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement aimed at tightening oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue.

What the agreement covers

The agreement covers foreign-exchange inflows, the remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing, digital payments, regulatory compliance and cybersecurity.

Officials say it will replace the old practice of dealing with issues one transaction at a time with a more structured system, including joint committees and performance monitoring.

Why it matters

Oil remains Nigeria's biggest source of foreign exchange and a major part of government revenue. For years, questions have been raised about how much oil money actually reaches government accounts, and how quickly.

Stronger coordination between the central bank and the national oil company could, in theory, improve transparency, boost foreign-exchange supply and help stabilise the naira.

Promises versus results

It sounds promising, but Nigerians will need to see measurable results. The full agreement has not been made public, and no target has been given for additional revenue or foreign-exchange inflows.

Without published targets and regular public reporting, it will be difficult for citizens to judge whether the new arrangement is working.

The real test

The real test is simple: will more oil money reach government accounts, and will Nigerians be able to see it?

State police bill moves to Tinubu's table after all 36 state assemblies approve it

18 Nov 2026, 14:51 GMT

The Senate has authorised its Clerk to send the constitutional amendment creating state police to President Bola Tinubu for assent, after all 36 state Houses of Assembly approved it. But state police will not start immediately: a separate National Policing Bill is still needed.

Read more

Nigeria has moved a step closer to state police. The Senate has authorised its Clerk to transmit the constitutional amendment establishing state police to President Bola Tinubu for his assent.

All 36 states on board

All 36 state Houses of Assembly have approved the proposal, completing the state-legislature stage of the constitutional amendment process.

The bill was originally sent to the National Assembly by President Tinubu himself, as part of efforts to tackle the country's worsening insecurity, so his assent is widely expected.

Not an overnight change

However, state police will not begin operating immediately once the President signs the amendment.

According to the Senate, a separate National Policing Bill is still needed to settle the key details: how state police will be funded, how officers will be recruited, who will command them and who will oversee them.

Why it matters

For years, many Nigerians, especially in states hit hard by banditry, kidnapping and communal violence, have argued that a single, centrally controlled police force is too stretched to protect every community.

Supporters say state police will bring security closer to the people, with officers who know the local terrain, language and culture.

Critics worry that governors could use state police against political opponents, journalists and critics, particularly at election time, and that poorer states may struggle to fund them properly.

The real test

Nigeria is now closer to decentralised policing, but the most important safeguards, the rules that will decide whether state police protect citizens or serve politicians, are still to be written.

Thousands march in Haiti to praise El Salvador's President Bukele as his troops take on gangs

18 Nov 2026, 14:49 GMT

Thousands of Haitians took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other cities on Tuesday to praise El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his soldiers, who are leading a tough new offensive against the gangs that have terrorised Haiti for years.

Read more

Thousands of people took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other parts of Haiti on Tuesday, praising El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his troops as they take on the country's powerful gangs.

"Viv Bukele"

Photos from the marches show huge crowds filling the streets, waving Haitian flags and holding up posters of the Salvadoran president. Many carried banners reading "Viv Bukele", Haitian Creole for "Long live Bukele".

Why Haitians are cheering

For years, armed gangs have controlled large parts of Port-au-Prince and beyond, killing, kidnapping and forcing hundreds of thousands of people from their homes.

Salvadoran troops have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an international mission, and recently launched their first major offensive against the gangs, with reports of heavy fighting and deaths.

Many marchers said Bukele is doing what the United Nations and the United States failed to do for years, while ordinary Haitians continued to suffer.

The Bukele model

Bukele became popular at home by cracking down hard on El Salvador's gangs, sharply reducing violence in a country once known as one of the most dangerous in the world. Many Haitians hope his forces can bring the same results.

His methods have also faced criticism from human rights groups, who say the crackdown in El Salvador involved mass arrests and abuses. But for Haitians who have lived in fear for years, many say any hope of relief is welcome.
