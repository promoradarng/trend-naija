Bayern Munich coach Vincent Kompany discharged from hospital after collapse

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Bayern Munich head coach Vincent Kompany was discharged from hospital on Wednesday, 28 October 2026, days after he collapsed at the club's training ground. The club said he will now rest at home before returning to work.

What happened

Kompany collapsed on Friday while watching a second-team training session. Bayern later said the cause was meningeal irritation, an inflammation of the membranes around the brain. Earlier that week he had skipped media duties before the Champions League match against Arsenal because of a viral infection, although he was in the dugout for the 2-2 draw.

What the club said

Sporting director Max Eberl described the discharge as the best possible news for everyone at the club. He said Kompany would rest and take medication and expressed confidence that the coach would return at full strength. Assistant coach Aaron Danks confirmed that Kompany would only return after receiving medical clearance and said there was no fixed timescale. In his absence, the coaching staff led the team to a 6-1 win over Magdeburg in the German Cup.

Background

Kompany, a former Manchester City captain and Belgium international, took charge of Bayern in 2024 after spells as manager of Anderlecht and Burnley. He led the club back to the Bundesliga title in his first season and remains popular with players and fans.

How fans reacted

Messages of support poured in from around football after news of the collapse broke, with former teammates and rival clubs wishing him a full recovery. Fans welcomed the update on Wednesday but urged the club not to rush him back.

FIFA says hackers stole personal and business information in cyberattack

FIFA has confirmed it was the victim of a cyberattack in which criminals gained access to a limited set of personal and business information. It is working with law enforcement.

World football's governing body FIFA has confirmed that it was hit by a cyberattack in which hackers gained unauthorised access to personal and business information.

What FIFA said

In a statement on Thursday, 26 November 2026, FIFA said it had been the victim of a criminal cyberattack that gave access to a limited set of personal and business information. It said it was working with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement, to investigate.

The organisation strongly condemned the unlawful taking and publication of the information, particularly personal data, describing it as a serious violation of privacy that could have significant consequences for the people affected.

Unanswered questions

FIFA did not say when the attack happened, what kind of information was accessed or how many people were affected.

Presidential election turnout has fallen from 69% to 27% since 2003

28 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has dropped from a Fourth Republic high of about 69% in 2003 to under 27% in 2023, according to figures compiled by a statistics platform. The decline has continued for five straight elections.

Read more

Voter turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has fallen sharply over the past two decades, according to figures shared by a statistics platform this week.

The numbers

Measured as total votes cast as a share of registered voters, turnout reached a Fourth Republic high of 69.08% in 2003. It then fell to 57.49% in 2007 and 53.68% in 2011, and continued to decline in each election after that, reaching just 26.71% in 2023. That is a drop of more than 42 percentage points in 20 years.

Why it matters

The figures come as parties and INEC step up efforts to get Nigerians to collect their Permanent Voter Cards and vote in the 16 January 2027 elections. INEC is also running a nationwide mock accreditation exercise on Saturday to test its systems.

Background

Concerns about insecurity, logistics and confidence in the electoral process have often been cited as reasons for low turnout. INEC this week named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence.
