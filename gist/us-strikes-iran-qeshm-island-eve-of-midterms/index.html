US reportedly carries out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island on eve of midterms

3 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The United States military has reportedly carried out limited strikes on Qeshm Island, a strategic Iranian island near the Strait of Hormuz, on Monday, 2 November 2026, the eve of the US midterm elections.

What happened

According to US officials quoted in reports, the operation was a defensive response after Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) fired surface-to-air missiles at US assets. Iranian state media confirmed several explosions on the island. Officials reportedly said it was one of a series of low-profile strikes carried out in recent weeks, kept quiet to avoid signalling a return to full-scale fighting. Separately, the IRGC claimed it had intercepted and captured two US drones south of Qeshm.

Why Qeshm matters

Qeshm is the largest island in the Persian Gulf and is said to hold Iranian anti-ship missiles, sea mines and drones in underground tunnels. Its position near the Strait of Hormuz, through which a large share of the world's oil passes, makes it militarily important.

Diplomacy continues

The strikes came as Qatar leads indirect talks between the two sides. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, described the talks as active, and on Monday Iran said it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if the US met its conditions. US Vice President JD Vance reportedly said there had been progress, but warned that Iran remains unpredictable and that President Donald Trump would keep both military and diplomatic options open.

Why it matters

Oil prices rose slightly after reports of the strikes, with Brent crude trading at about $105 a barrel. Higher oil prices affect Nigeria's revenue and fuel costs around the world. The conflict between the US and Iran began on 28 February.

US grounds all departing flights nationwide after telecom outage

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The US Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop on all departing flights on Friday, citing safety. More than 2,000 flights were cancelled and over 10,000 delayed, and officials blamed a telecommunications outage.

Read more

The United States briefly halted all departing flights across the country on Friday, 13 November 2026, causing widespread disruption.

What happened

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a sudden nationwide ground stop at 3.54pm Eastern time, citing extraordinary circumstances and safety, and said all departing traffic should remain on the ground until further notice. It said inbound international flights would be handled case by case. At the time, more than 4,000 flights bound for US destinations were in the air, according to flight-tracking data.

The impact

Within hours, more than 2,000 flights had been cancelled and more than 10,000 delayed, according to flight-tracking figures, and lawmakers demanded an explanation.

The cause

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication so far of anything malicious, and that the problem appeared to be a telecommunications outage. He said President Donald Trump had been briefed.

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Iran launched drones at a US military installation in Bahrain on Friday in what it called retaliation for an American strike, its first attack on a US base in the Gulf in over two months. US forces said both drones were intercepted with no damage.

Iran has targeted a United States military facility in Bahrain, its first attempt to strike a US base in a Gulf state in more than two months.

What happened

US Central Command said American air defences intercepted and destroyed two Iranian attack drones launched towards Bahrain early on Friday, 13 November 2026, and reported no casualties or damage. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed through state-linked media that they had destroyed a US early-warning radar on the island, home to the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, in response to an alleged attack on a telecommunications tower in the southern Iranian town of Sirik. The US rejected that claim.

Escalation

The strike marks a sharp escalation after a pause since early September, during which Iran had focused on disrupting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Hopes of a deal between Washington and Tehran appear to be fading, with President Donald Trump reportedly telling Vice-President JD Vance that the US does not need a deal.

Why it matters

The US also recently withdrew from the FIFA U-17 World Cup in Qatar, citing a travel advisory linked to the risk of conflict in the region.

US revokes more than 6,000 visas in crackdown on 'birth tourism'

13 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The US State Department says its birth tourism task force has revoked more than 6,000 visas held by foreigners who travelled to give birth in the United States or helped others to do so. It did not say which nationalities were affected.

Read more

The United States has revoked more than 6,000 visas in a crackdown on so-called birth tourism.

What was announced

In a statement on X on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the State Department said its Birth Tourism Prevention Task Force had cancelled the visas of foreign nationals found to have taken part in or facilitated the practice. It said the crackdown targets not only people who travelled to give birth but also those who helped arrange such trips, and described the practice as illicit. The department did not release a breakdown of the nationalities affected.

What is birth tourism

Birth tourism refers to travelling to the United States specifically to give birth, so that the child acquires US citizenship under the birthright citizenship provision of the 14th Amendment to the US Constitution.

Why it matters to Nigerians

Giving birth in the US has been popular among some Nigerians who can afford it, and the US has previously warned that visitor visas may be refused to applicants suspected of travelling to give birth.
