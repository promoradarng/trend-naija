"Your approach was inappropriate": South African presidency fires back at Fani-Kayode over livestream complaint

14 Nov 2026, 09:27 GMT

The South African presidency has criticised Nigeria's High Commissioner to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, over his public complaint that his speech was cut off during the ceremony where he presented his letters of credence to President Cyril Ramaphosa.

Fani-Kayode's complaint

Fani-Kayode had raised concerns about the incident, suggesting that he was interrupted while addressing the gathering during the official diplomatic ceremony.

South Africa responds

The South African presidency has rejected his account. Responding to the controversy, President Ramaphosa's spokesperson, Vincent Magwenya, accused Fani-Kayode of misrepresenting the situation and of wrongly dragging the South African President into a matter that did not require his involvement.

Magwenya also described the Nigerian diplomat's handling of the issue as inappropriate.

A routine ceremony turned row

The disagreement has drawn attention to what should have been a routine diplomatic ceremony between two African countries.

Diplomatic engagements are usually guided by established protocols, and disagreements over procedure are generally expected to be handled through official channels rather than in public.

For a newly appointed high commissioner, the public exchange has raised questions about diplomatic conduct, and about how misunderstandings between representatives of friendly nations should be resolved.

African Action Congress (AAC) presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore has made a surprise visit to the Ogbo Ogwu medicine market in Onitsha, Anambra State, where he says traders gave him a warm welcome.

The visit

Sowore shared details of the visit on X on Saturday, 21 November 2026. He said he walked across the First Niger Bridge and arrived at the market without any prior announcement.

He described the reception as spontaneous and genuine, insisting that no crowds had been hired or organised for his arrival, and promised to return.

Bridge complaint

Context

Tinubu's praise singer Rarara mobbed at Abuja National Mosque

21 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Hausa praise singer Dauda Kahutu Rarara, a well-known supporter of President Tinubu, was attacked by an angry crowd at the Abuja National Mosque during Friday prayers. Security operatives struggled to hold the crowd back.

Read more

Popular Hausa praise singer Dauda Kahutu, widely known as Rarara, was attacked by an angry crowd at the Abuja National Mosque during Friday prayers on 20 November 2026.

What happened

Rarara, a prominent supporter of President Bola Tinubu, was entering the mosque with plain-clothed and uniformed security personnel when a group of people rushed at him. Security operatives tried to hold the crowd back but could not fully contain them. At one point, the singer removed his cap, which carried Tinubu's insignia.

A video of the incident has spread widely online.

Context

The incident came a week after President Tinubu's convoy was pelted with stones after Friday prayers at a mosque in Garki, Abuja, an attack for which police arrested 78 people before the Presidency directed their release.

Reactions

Paul Ibe, media adviser to ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar, condemned the violence on X while also making a political point about the incident.

APC reaction

Former Imo East APC senatorial aspirant Belusochukwu Enwere condemned the attack, describing it as part of a growing pattern of hostility towards supporters of the ruling party and a threat to democratic stability ahead of 2027.
