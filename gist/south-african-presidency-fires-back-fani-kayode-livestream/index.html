"Your approach was inappropriate": South African presidency fires back at Fani-Kayode over livestream complaint

14 Nov 2026, 09:27 GMT

The South African presidency has criticised Nigeria's High Commissioner to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, over his public complaint that his speech was cut off during the ceremony where he presented his letters of credence to President Cyril Ramaphosa.

Fani-Kayode's complaint

Fani-Kayode had raised concerns about the incident, suggesting that he was interrupted while addressing the gathering during the official diplomatic ceremony.

South Africa responds

The South African presidency has rejected his account. Responding to the controversy, President Ramaphosa's spokesperson, Vincent Magwenya, accused Fani-Kayode of misrepresenting the situation and of wrongly dragging the South African President into a matter that did not require his involvement.

Magwenya also described the Nigerian diplomat's handling of the issue as inappropriate.

A routine ceremony turned row

The disagreement has drawn attention to what should have been a routine diplomatic ceremony between two African countries.

Diplomatic engagements are usually guided by established protocols, and disagreements over procedure are generally expected to be handled through official channels rather than in public.

For a newly appointed high commissioner, the public exchange has raised questions about diplomatic conduct, and about how misunderstandings between representatives of friendly nations should be resolved.

Former Kaduna State Governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly told a Federal High Court in Abuja that he never intercepted the telephone conversations of National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu, and never asked anyone to do so for him.

In court

El-Rufai opened his defence on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, before Justice Joyce Abdulmalik, after his no-case submission was dismissed in October. Led in evidence by his lawyer, he told the court that he had never owned equipment capable of intercepting calls and had no technical knowledge of how such interception works.

He also denied having any access to the NSA's communication networks or personal devices.

How the case began

The case arises from remarks El-Rufai made in a television interview on 13 February 2026. He denies the charges.

What happens next

The trial continues as El-Rufai presents his defence.

'Political rhetoric'

El-Rufai told the court that his 'tapped' remark was political rhetoric. The court later adjourned the trial to February to allow his defence to continue.

At least 30 killed as fuel tanker overturns and bursts into flames in Cairo

26 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

At least 30 people died and 15 were injured when a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday. The blaze engulfed 27 vehicles.

Read more

At least 30 people have died and 15 others were injured after a fuel tanker overturned and burst into flames on a busy stretch of the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday, 25 November 2026.

What happened

According to Egypt's Ministry of Health and Population, the fire broke out above Tunnel 10 in the densely populated Al-Warraq area of Imbaba, a suburb of Greater Cairo. Preliminary findings suggest the privately operated tanker swerved, hit a concrete road divider and toppled over, causing an explosion that spread to 27 nearby vehicles.

The response

Authorities sent 39 ambulances to the scene, taking survivors to three hospitals in the area. Firefighters brought the blaze under control, and heavy machinery was brought in to clear the burnt-out vehicles.

Condolences

Egyptian President Abdel-Fattah el-Sisi and Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly sent condolences to the families of the victims.
