NDC vice-chairman and ward leaders in Kano's Bichi reportedly defect to APC

16 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) in Bichi Local Government Area of Kano State has reportedly lost several officials to the All Progressives Congress (APC).

Who moved

The group was said to be led by the NDC vice-chairman in Bichi, Haladu Sa'adu, and included the party's local treasurer, the chairmen of five wards with their secretaries, and women and youth leaders, as well as some leaders of the Kwankwasiyya movement. They announced the move on Monday, 16 November 2026, during a visit to the office of Abubakar Kabir Abubakar Bichi, the APC lawmaker representing Bichi Federal Constituency and chairman of the House Committee on Appropriations.

Their reasons

Speaking for the group, the Danzabuwa ward chairman reportedly praised the lawmaker's projects in the area, saying his scholarship programme had paid for his own postgraduate studies. He also complained of poor coordination and a lack of respect for ordinary members within the NDC in Kano.

The APC welcome

The lawmaker welcomed the defectors and said their arrival would strengthen the APC at the grassroots in Bichi and Kano North.

The other side

No response from the NDC leadership in Kano was included in the reports. Bichi is the same area where Rabiu Kwankwaso's convoy was reportedly attacked last week.

State police bill moves to Tinubu's table after all 36 state assemblies approve it

18 Nov 2026, 14:51 GMT

The Senate has authorised its Clerk to send the constitutional amendment creating state police to President Bola Tinubu for assent, after all 36 state Houses of Assembly approved it. But state police will not start immediately: a separate National Policing Bill is still needed.

Read more

Nigeria has moved a step closer to state police. The Senate has authorised its Clerk to transmit the constitutional amendment establishing state police to President Bola Tinubu for his assent.

All 36 states on board

All 36 state Houses of Assembly have approved the proposal, completing the state-legislature stage of the constitutional amendment process.

The bill was originally sent to the National Assembly by President Tinubu himself, as part of efforts to tackle the country's worsening insecurity, so his assent is widely expected.

Not an overnight change

However, state police will not begin operating immediately once the President signs the amendment.

According to the Senate, a separate National Policing Bill is still needed to settle the key details: how state police will be funded, how officers will be recruited, who will command them and who will oversee them.

Why it matters

For years, many Nigerians, especially in states hit hard by banditry, kidnapping and communal violence, have argued that a single, centrally controlled police force is too stretched to protect every community.

Supporters say state police will bring security closer to the people, with officers who know the local terrain, language and culture.

Critics worry that governors could use state police against political opponents, journalists and critics, particularly at election time, and that poorer states may struggle to fund them properly.

The real test

Nigeria is now closer to decentralised policing, but the most important safeguards, the rules that will decide whether state police protect citizens or serve politicians, are still to be written.

Over to you

Should governors control state police directly, or should an independent body supervise them?

Peter Obi says his US trip was not to seek American endorsement

18 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has dismissed reports that he went to Washington to seek the backing of US lawmakers for 2027. He said his meetings focused on Nigeria's democracy and development.

Read more

Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate Peter Obi has dismissed reports that his recent trip to Washington, DC, was aimed at winning the backing of United States lawmakers for his 2027 bid.

What Obi said

In a statement on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, the former Anambra governor said media interpretations of his meetings with US lawmakers and policy figures had been inaccurate. He said he did not ask for an endorsement and did not present the meetings as formal support from the US Congress or any individual legislator.

He explained that the purpose of the visit was to exchange views with congressional leaders and other American stakeholders on Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and, above all, the strengthening of its democracy. He said the talks focused on democratic institutions, accountability and making sure elections are free, fair and credible.

His advice to others

Obi encouraged other Nigerian politicians to hold similar international discussions on governance, and urged that public debate on Nigeria's democratic future should not be reduced to claims of foreign endorsement.

Background

Claims that the US Congress had endorsed Obi circulated online after the trip, and a US lobbyist also commented on the matter.
