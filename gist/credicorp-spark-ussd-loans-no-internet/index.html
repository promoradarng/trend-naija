Federal government's CREDICORP unveils SPARK loans for people without smartphones

1 Nov 2026, 11:44 GMT

Nigerians without smartphones or internet access may soon be able to borrow money through a new government-backed scheme.

What was unveiled

The Nigerian Consumer Credit Corporation (CREDICORP) launched SPARK at a 'CREDICORP for All' event in Aba, Abia State, attended by more than 1,000 residents, according to reports on Sunday, 1 November 2026. The product works on USSD, the basic mobile service available on simple feature phones, allowing eligible users to start credit transactions without a data connection.

Who it is for

SPARK is aimed at first-time borrowers, low-income earners and others who are poorly served by traditional banks. CREDICORP says it will also help users build formal credit histories by borrowing and repaying responsibly. Loan sizes, interest rates and eligibility rules were not disclosed, and the USSD code has not yet been published. CREDICORP expects the product to be widely available before the end of 2026.

Who spoke

CREDICORP's chief executive, Uzoma Nwagba, spoke at the event, along with a representative of Abia Governor Alex Otti and an official of the Ministry of Finance Incorporated.

Background

CREDICORP was set up in 2024 to expand access to consumer credit, so that Nigerians can buy goods such as phones, solar systems and household items and pay over time. Many Nigerians rely on online loan apps, some of which have been criticised for high interest rates and harsh debt-recovery methods.

A warning

Officials urged people to wait for official announcements and to avoid unverified platforms claiming to register people for SPARK loans.

  • SPARK works through USSD on basic phones.
  • It targets first-time and low-income borrowers.
  • Loan sizes and interest rates have not been announced.

Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Residents of Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo are facing worsening power cuts. Power Minister Joseph Tegbe has reportedly blamed gas shortages, maintenance work and low water levels at hydro plants, saying there has been no national grid collapse.

Read more

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.

Bandits demand over ₦100 million for abducted Niger Islamiyya students, teacher says

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The teacher of the Islamiyya students abducted from his farm in Rafi, Niger State, says the kidnappers have demanded more than ₦100 million for their release. He said families have had little official help and called for military action.

Read more

The kidnappers of a group of Islamiyya students abducted in Niger State have demanded more than ₦100 million for their release, according to their teacher.

What the teacher said

Malam Aminu Bako, whose students were taken while helping him harvest maize on his farm in Rafi Local Government Area, said on Thursday, 5 November 2026, that the bandits had contacted village leaders with the ransom demand. He said 15 children were abducted, and called for urgent military action to rescue them. According to him, only the acting chairman of the local government and the divisional police officer had visited since the abduction, and families were relying on local vigilantes to track the kidnappers.

Where the children may be

Bako said information gathered by vigilantes suggests the children are still in Niger State, held in an isolated building guarded by heavily armed men, possibly without enough food. He said he was under intense pressure from worried parents.

Background

The students were abducted on 31 October from a farm in Tungan-Bako village near Pandogari. Police at the time said 14 students were taken and that a joint security operation had been launched. Rafi and surrounding areas have suffered repeated attacks by armed groups, and kidnapping for ransom remains widespread in north-west and north-central Nigeria. Paying ransom is illegal in Nigeria, although families often feel they have no choice.

What happens next

Families and community leaders are calling on the federal and state governments to step up efforts to rescue the children.

Sego declares end of feud with MC Oluomo after KWAM 1 steps in

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Lagos NURTW chairman Mustapha Sego has announced that his dispute with national president MC Oluomo is over, after Fuji star KWAM 1 reportedly brokered peace. The announcement came days after a court ordered Sego's arrest in a separate case.

Read more

The Lagos State chairman of the National Union of Road Transport Workers (NURTW), Mustapha Adekunle, known as Sego, has declared an end to his dispute with the union's national president, Musiliu Akinsanya, popularly known as MC Oluomo.

What happened

In a video from a Lagos NURTW meeting on Thursday, 5 November 2026, Sego reportedly told members that the disagreement had ended. The announcement followed an intervention by Fuji music star Wasiu Ayinde Marshal, known as KWAM 1, who is said to have brokered peace between the two transport union leaders.

Background

The NURTW is one of the most powerful unions in Lagos, controlling motor parks across the state, and its leaders are influential in local politics. MC Oluomo was previously chairman of the Lagos State Parks Management Committee before becoming national president of the union. Details of the dispute between him and Sego were not made public. Earlier this week, the National Industrial Court in Lagos ordered the arrest of Sego and the union's Lagos secretary for failing to appear in a contempt case linked to a dispute with tricycle operators. MC Oluomo also made headlines this week after saying he was taking adult English lessons.

Why it matters

Peace between the two men is seen as important for stability in the union and in motor parks across Lagos, especially ahead of the 2027 elections.

NAPTIP warns traffickers are luring victims at gyms and with fake jobs

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The anti-trafficking agency NAPTIP has warned that human traffickers are approaching women at gyms and social venues, posing as helpful service providers and offering fake jobs to win their trust.

Read more

The National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons (NAPTIP) has warned Nigerians about new tactics used by human traffickers to recruit victims.

What NAPTIP said

Speaking at a joint briefing by security and intelligence agencies in Abuja on Thursday, 5 November 2026, NAPTIP spokesman Adekoye Vincent said traffickers are increasingly exploiting social relationships, especially with women. He said they approach people at gyms, recreational centres and other social venues, first presenting themselves as service providers offering help with domestic needs, shopping or sourcing goods. Once they have built trust and familiarity, they lure victims with fake job offers and other promises.

Advice to the public

The agency urged Nigerians, particularly young women, to be cautious about strangers offering help or opportunities that seem too good to be true, to verify job offers, and to report suspicious approaches to the authorities.

Background

Nigeria is a major source, transit and destination country for human trafficking, with victims taken to other African countries, Europe and the Middle East for forced labour and sexual exploitation. This week, police in Mali rescued Nigerian women from a trafficking ring. NAPTIP has long warned that traffickers use fake job offers abroad, and more recently social media, to recruit victims.

Why it matters

Officials say awareness is one of the best defences against traffickers, who constantly change their methods.
