Federal government's CREDICORP unveils SPARK loans for people without smartphones

1 Nov 2026, 11:44 GMT

Nigerians without smartphones or internet access may soon be able to borrow money through a new government-backed scheme.

What was unveiled

The Nigerian Consumer Credit Corporation (CREDICORP) launched SPARK at a 'CREDICORP for All' event in Aba, Abia State, attended by more than 1,000 residents, according to reports on Sunday, 1 November 2026. The product works on USSD, the basic mobile service available on simple feature phones, allowing eligible users to start credit transactions without a data connection.

Who it is for

SPARK is aimed at first-time borrowers, low-income earners and others who are poorly served by traditional banks. CREDICORP says it will also help users build formal credit histories by borrowing and repaying responsibly. Loan sizes, interest rates and eligibility rules were not disclosed, and the USSD code has not yet been published. CREDICORP expects the product to be widely available before the end of 2026.

Who spoke

CREDICORP's chief executive, Uzoma Nwagba, spoke at the event, along with a representative of Abia Governor Alex Otti and an official of the Ministry of Finance Incorporated.

Background

CREDICORP was set up in 2024 to expand access to consumer credit, so that Nigerians can buy goods such as phones, solar systems and household items and pay over time. Many Nigerians rely on online loan apps, some of which have been criticised for high interest rates and harsh debt-recovery methods.

A warning

Officials urged people to wait for official announcements and to avoid unverified platforms claiming to register people for SPARK loans.

  • SPARK works through USSD on basic phones.
  • It targets first-time and low-income borrowers.
  • Loan sizes and interest rates have not been announced.

CBN raises weekly dollar limit for BDCs from $150,000 to $1m

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has reportedly raised the weekly dollar purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators to $1 million to ease festive pressure on the naira. Banks have begun implementing the change.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has reportedly raised the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change (BDC) operators from $150,000 to $1 million, in a move aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the foreign exchange market.

How it will work

Sources said commercial banks had started contacting BDC operators to set up arrangements for the new limit. Banks will act only as intermediaries between BDCs and the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market, using a purchase tracking platform.

Background

The change is a major relaxation of restrictions the CBN introduced in February. It comes as the naira has weakened against the dollar in recent days and the country's external reserves have fallen. The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate at 23%.

NNPC says $2.04bn in crude oil proceeds is not missing

25 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

NNPC has dismissed reports that $2.04 billion in crude oil proceeds linked to its former trading arm Duke Oil is missing. It says the figure is the gross value of 29 cargoes and that all transactions were properly accounted for.

Read more

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC) has dismissed reports that $2.04 billion in crude oil proceeds involving its trading subsidiary, formerly known as Duke Oil, is missing.

What NNPC said

In a statement on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, NNPC's chief corporate communications officer said the $2.04 billion represented the gross value of 29 crude oil cargoes, not missing or unpaid revenue. The statement was titled 'Duke Oil cargoes are fully accounted for'.

The company maintained that all transactions under the Direct Sale Direct Purchase (DSDP) programme were properly documented. It said the apparent gaps in dollar receipts were due to the way the programme's settlements were structured.

Background

Reports suggesting the money could not be traced had sparked concern online.
