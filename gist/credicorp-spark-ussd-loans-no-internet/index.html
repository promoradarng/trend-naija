Federal government's CREDICORP unveils SPARK loans for people without smartphones

1 Nov 2026, 11:44 GMT

Nigerians without smartphones or internet access may soon be able to borrow money through a new government-backed scheme.

What was unveiled

The Nigerian Consumer Credit Corporation (CREDICORP) launched SPARK at a 'CREDICORP for All' event in Aba, Abia State, attended by more than 1,000 residents, according to reports on Sunday, 1 November 2026. The product works on USSD, the basic mobile service available on simple feature phones, allowing eligible users to start credit transactions without a data connection.

Who it is for

SPARK is aimed at first-time borrowers, low-income earners and others who are poorly served by traditional banks. CREDICORP says it will also help users build formal credit histories by borrowing and repaying responsibly. Loan sizes, interest rates and eligibility rules were not disclosed, and the USSD code has not yet been published. CREDICORP expects the product to be widely available before the end of 2026.

Who spoke

CREDICORP's chief executive, Uzoma Nwagba, spoke at the event, along with a representative of Abia Governor Alex Otti and an official of the Ministry of Finance Incorporated.

Background

CREDICORP was set up in 2024 to expand access to consumer credit, so that Nigerians can buy goods such as phones, solar systems and household items and pay over time. Many Nigerians rely on online loan apps, some of which have been criticised for high interest rates and harsh debt-recovery methods.

A warning

Officials urged people to wait for official announcements and to avoid unverified platforms claiming to register people for SPARK loans.

  • SPARK works through USSD on basic phones.
  • It targets first-time and low-income borrowers.
  • Loan sizes and interest rates have not been announced.

Petrol depot prices fall to about ₦1,210 per litre in Lagos, diesel also drops

13 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Average depot prices of petrol in Lagos have reportedly fallen by ₦29 to about ₦1,210 per litre, with diesel also cheaper, raising hopes of further cuts at filling stations.

Read more

Depot prices of petrol and diesel have fallen further in Lagos, according to market data.

The new prices

Figures published on Thursday, 12 November 2026, show that the average depot price of petrol dropped by ₦29 per litre, from ₦1,239 to ₦1,210, a decline of about 2.3 per cent. Some depots were reportedly selling at around ₦1,200. The average price of diesel also fell, to about ₦1,688 per litre.

Why it matters

Depot prices are what marketers pay before selling to motorists, so lower wholesale prices could lead to cheaper petrol at filling stations in the coming days. The cuts came despite a rebound in global crude oil prices linked to renewed tension between the United States and Iran.

Background

Petrol prices have been easing in recent days. Dangote Refinery reportedly cut its loading price to ₦1,250 per litre after switching to a pricing system linked to global petrol prices, and MRS filling stations in Abuja reduced their pump price to ₦1,399 per litre.

TRCN uncovers 250 fake teaching certificates in one state's recruitment

13 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria says it found 250 fake teaching certificates during a state's recruitment of about 1,000 teachers, and is now issuing certificates with QR codes for instant checks.

Read more

The Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria (TRCN) says it uncovered 250 fake teaching certificates during a single recruitment exercise.

What was found

TRCN Registrar Dr Ronke Soyombo disclosed this on Thursday, 12 November 2026, while monitoring the council's Professional Qualifying Examination in Abuja. She said the discovery was made when an unnamed state was recruiting about 1,000 teachers.

New safeguards

Soyombo said the council now issues certificates with QR codes that allow employers to verify them instantly on its portal before hiring. She said the council had also tightened exam security by giving candidates with different qualifications different, randomised questions, so that people sitting next to each other cannot copy.

Background

The TRCN regulates the teaching profession and requires teachers to pass its qualifying examination before they can be registered and licensed.

Senior civil servants demand ₦300,000 minimum wage and state police

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Association of Senior Civil Servants of Nigeria has called for the minimum wage to be raised from ₦70,000 to ₦300,000, prompt payment of gratuities and the creation of state police.

Read more

Nigeria's senior civil servants have called for a new national minimum wage of ₦300,000.

The demands

At a National Executive Council meeting of the Association of Senior Civil Servants of Nigeria (ASCSN) in Abuja, reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, union president Shehu Mohammed said the current ₦70,000 minimum wage had steadily lost value amid economic pressures and could no longer meet workers' basic needs. The union also demanded prompt payment of outstanding gratuities and the creation of state police, saying insecurity continues to put workers and their families at risk.

Collaboration call

Senior lawyer Femi Falana addressed delegates under the theme 'From Confrontation to Collaboration', arguing that lasting progress between labour and government depends on how the authorities treat workers.

Background

The ₦70,000 minimum wage was signed into law in 2024 after months of negotiations and strike threats by organised labour. The federal government had not responded to the new demand at the time of writing.

Four UNN students die after off-campus party in Nsukka, six in hospital

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka has confirmed that four students died after a party held to celebrate a friend's final exams, after allegedly consuming a mix of alcohol and drugs. Six others are in hospital, and the university has declared three days of mourning.

Read more

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka (UNN) has confirmed that four of its students have died after an off-campus party in Nsukka, Enugu State.

What happened

Speaking to journalists on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Vice-Chancellor Professor Simon Ortuanya said 10 students were involved in the incident at the party on Wednesday, 11 November, which had been organised to celebrate a friend finishing his final-year examinations. He said the four who died allegedly consumed substances believed to be a mixture of alcohol and drugs. The other six are receiving treatment in different hospitals. Earlier reports had put the death toll as high as seven.

University responds

The Vice-Chancellor described the deaths as one too many and declared three days of mourning in honour of the students. He also corrected reports that all the students were from the Faculty of Pharmaceutical Sciences, saying they came from different departments. The exact substances involved have not been officially identified, and it is not clear whether the police have opened an investigation.

Police investigate

The Enugu State Police Command said on Thursday evening that it had opened an investigation into the deaths. It said the four victims, two men and two women, fell ill after allegedly drinking alcoholic beverages at a gathering at a hostel in Nsukka and were confirmed dead on arrival at hospital. Police said the drinks had been recovered from the scene and the bodies taken to a mortuary for autopsy.

Background

Drug and alcohol misuse among young people has become a growing public health concern in Nigeria.
