Tinubu takes swipe at Peter Obi in Akwa Ibom, says he confronted the Atlantic Ocean while Obi ran away from erosion in Anambra

6 Nov 2026, 16:47 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has taken a swipe at former Anambra State Governor Peter Obi, comparing their records on tackling environmental challenges.

What Tinubu said

Speaking in Akwa Ibom State, Tinubu accused Obi of failing to confront the serious erosion problems affecting Anambra State during his time as governor, and pointed out that Obi is now running for president under the NDC.

"Peter Obi is in one party they call NDC where he's running for president. He ran away from erosion in Anambra. He couldn't confront erosion. I, Bola Ahmed Tinubu, confronted the Atlantic Ocean. I tamed it for you," Tinubu said.

The President appeared to be referring to efforts to reclaim and protect land along the Lagos coastline, particularly the Eko Atlantic project, which began during his time as Lagos State governor.

Can the two really be compared?

Anambra has struggled with severe gully erosion for decades, with communities losing farmland, roads and homes. Tackling it requires substantial funding, engineering work and cooperation between different levels of government.

The Eko Atlantic project, on the other hand, involved major private investment and extensive coastal engineering to reclaim land from the Atlantic Ocean.

Both involve serious environmental challenges, but their circumstances, funding arrangements and engineering requirements are quite different.

The road to 2027

As the 2027 presidential election draws closer, exchanges like this are likely to become more common, with leaders publicly questioning one another's records in office.

Nigerians may also want to look closely at the actual results of these projects and the roles played by the leaders involved.

Kwara APC governorship rally in Offa reportedly turns violent over unpaid mobilisation funds

16 Nov 2026, 11:21 GMT

An APC governorship rally in Offa, Kwara State, reportedly descended into chaos after claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised. The incident has revived debate about paying crowds to attend political events.

Read more

A governorship campaign rally organised by the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Offa, Kwara State, has reportedly descended into chaos following claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised.

What reportedly happened

According to reports, the rally drew a large crowd of supporters to the streets of Offa. But tension rose when some of those who had turned up complained that they had not received the money they were allegedly promised for attending.

The situation reportedly turned violent as angry participants confronted organisers, disrupting the event.

The full details, including whether anyone was injured and how much money was involved, have not been independently confirmed, and the party has not publicly commented on the incident.

The business of crowds

The incident shines a light on a practice many Nigerians already know about: paying people, often called "mobilisation", to attend political rallies.

Across parties, campaign organisers frequently provide transport, food, branded caps and T-shirts, and sometimes cash, to fill venues and create the impression of massive support. When those promises are not kept, frustration can quickly boil over.

It also raises a deeper question. If people have to be paid to attend, how much do the crowds at political rallies really tell us about genuine support?

Should people be paid to attend political rallies, or should campaigns rely only on supporters who come out of conviction?

Super Eagles held 0-0 by Tanzania, AFCON 2027 hopes take a hit

17 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nigeria were held to a goalless draw by Tanzania in Zanzibar in their AFCON 2027 qualifier, missing the chance to move to nine points. Tolu Arokodare hit the post in the first half, and coach Eric Chelle admitted afterwards that the team is in trouble.

Read more

Nigeria's Super Eagles were held to a 0-0 draw by Tanzania at the Amaan Stadium in Zanzibar on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, a result that reportedly dents their hopes in 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying Group L.

The match

Nigeria went into the game knowing a win would take them to nine points and put pressure on the group leaders, Guinea-Bissau. Despite having long spells of possession, they could not break down a well-organised Tanzanian side.

Their best chance came in the 33rd minute, when Moses Usor set up Tolu Arokodare, whose close-range shot hit the post. Calvin Bassey fired over five minutes later, while Tanzania also struck the woodwork in the 42nd minute. Coach Eric Chelle made changes just after the hour, but the breakthrough never came.

After the game

Chelle reportedly admitted after the match that the team is now in trouble in the qualifying race. Nigeria had won the first leg 3-1 in Uyo, but the draw means they missed the chance to move above Guinea-Bissau.

Background

The game came a day after Chelle clashed with a journalist over his tactics at the pre-match press conference. Nigeria were without injured forwards Ademola Lookman and Victor Osimhen.

Nigeria and South Africa hold talks after Fani-Kayode diplomatic row

17 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nigeria and South Africa have held talks in Pretoria over tensions following a public dispute involving Nigeria's ambassador, Femi Fani-Kayode. South Africa had summoned the envoy over his conduct after he presented his credentials.

Read more

Nigeria and South Africa have held talks to ease tensions in their relationship, days after a public dispute involving Nigeria's ambassador to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, and officials of the South African government.

The summons

South Africa's Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) summoned the envoy over his conduct following the presentation of his credentials. The row followed Fani-Kayode's complaint about being left out of a presidential livestream, which drew a response from the South African presidency.

The meeting

Fani-Kayode shared the outcome of the meeting on his X account, posting a clip of himself at DIRCO's head office in Pretoria. He said both sides raised concerns about the state of relations and the events that sparked the controversy.

He said the two countries look forward to building on the meeting and strengthening their long-standing bilateral ties.

Senate Chief Whip Mohammed Tahir Monguno reportedly escaped a Boko Haram attack during a campaign tour of Kukawa, Borno State. Troops are said to have moved quickly to repel the attackers.

Senate Chief Whip Mohammed Tahir Monguno, who represents Borno North, has reportedly escaped a Boko Haram attack during a 2027 campaign tour of Kukawa Local Government Area of Borno State on Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

What happened

Monguno is said to have travelled to Kukawa with former governor and senator Maina Maaji Lawan and other APC figures to campaign when the insurgents tried to overrun the area. Troops of Operation Hadin Kai, acting on intelligence, reportedly moved quickly to repel the attackers.

The campaign tour

The APC entourage had earlier gathered in Monguno town on Sunday, where the senator joined Governor Babagana Zulum for the launch of the party's campaign in the area. The governor returned to Maiduguri by helicopter on Monday, while Monguno and others continued to Kukawa.
