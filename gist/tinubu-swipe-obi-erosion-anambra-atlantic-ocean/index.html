Tinubu takes swipe at Peter Obi in Akwa Ibom, says he confronted the Atlantic Ocean while Obi ran away from erosion in Anambra

6 Nov 2026, 16:47 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has taken a swipe at former Anambra State Governor Peter Obi, comparing their records on tackling environmental challenges.

What Tinubu said

Speaking in Akwa Ibom State, Tinubu accused Obi of failing to confront the serious erosion problems affecting Anambra State during his time as governor, and pointed out that Obi is now running for president under the NDC.

"Peter Obi is in one party they call NDC where he's running for president. He ran away from erosion in Anambra. He couldn't confront erosion. I, Bola Ahmed Tinubu, confronted the Atlantic Ocean. I tamed it for you," Tinubu said.

The President appeared to be referring to efforts to reclaim and protect land along the Lagos coastline, particularly the Eko Atlantic project, which began during his time as Lagos State governor.

Can the two really be compared?

Anambra has struggled with severe gully erosion for decades, with communities losing farmland, roads and homes. Tackling it requires substantial funding, engineering work and cooperation between different levels of government.

The Eko Atlantic project, on the other hand, involved major private investment and extensive coastal engineering to reclaim land from the Atlantic Ocean.

Both involve serious environmental challenges, but their circumstances, funding arrangements and engineering requirements are quite different.

The road to 2027

As the 2027 presidential election draws closer, exchanges like this are likely to become more common, with leaders publicly questioning one another's records in office.

Nigerians may also want to look closely at the actual results of these projects and the roles played by the leaders involved.

OK Movement withdraws support from Obi and the NDC

27 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

The OK Movement, named after Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso, has reportedly pulled its campaign support from the NDC. Its director-general said the group had been told Obi and the party had chosen to cut ties with it.

Read more

The OK Movement, a support group named after the initials of NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi and his running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso, has reportedly withdrawn its campaign and mobilisation support from the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC).

The announcement

In a statement on Friday, 27 November 2026, the movement's national director-general, John Ughulu, said the group had been informed that Obi and the NDC had decided to cut ties with it. He directed the movement's ward, local government and state structures to stop all involvement in NDC activities immediately.

What next

Ughulu said the group had begun discussions with other political parties and presidential aspirants.

Background

The development comes days before Obi is due to attend an opposition summit in Abuja with Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde.

The other side

No response from Obi or the NDC was included in the reports.

Fashola says Tinubu stands out because he has kept his allies for 16 years

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Former Lagos Governor Babatunde Fashola has argued that President Tinubu differs from Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi because he has kept the loyalty of his political allies for about 16 years. He said even former opponents now work with him.

Read more

Former Lagos State Governor Babatunde Fashola has made a case for President Bola Tinubu's re-election, arguing that he stands apart from his main rivals by keeping his political allies for about 16 years.

The comparison

Speaking in a television interview on Friday, 27 November 2026, Fashola noted that Tinubu, Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi all began their political rise as state governors, although Atiku was elected governor but did not serve. He framed his argument as a job reference question, saying that Tinubu's long-time allies have stayed with him.

He also said that even some of the president's fiercest former opponents now work with him.

Background

Fashola, who succeeded Tinubu as Lagos governor and later served as a federal minister, also condemned the 'rig and die' slogan in the same interview. Atiku and Obi are expected to sign an opposition cooperation pact at a summit in Abuja on Monday.

The other side

No response from the Atiku or Obi campaigns was included in the reports.

Obi urges mothers to pray for Nigeria and guard their votes

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has appealed to Nigerian mothers to pray for the country and to stay behind after voting to make sure their votes are counted. He spoke at an ECWA women's fellowship in Abuja.

Read more

Peter Obi, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate, has appealed to Nigerian mothers to pray for the country and to protect their votes at the 2027 elections.

What he said

Speaking at an ECWA Women Fellowship event in Abuja, Obi expressed concern about rising poverty, unemployment and insecurity. He said Nigeria now has the highest number of people living in poverty in the world, that millions of young people are unemployed, and that mothers are suffering because of insecurity.

He urged the women, after voting for the candidate of their choice on election day, to stay behind at polling units to make sure their votes are counted.

A warm welcome

Videos from the convention showed women rushing excitedly to greet him.

Background

Obi is expected to attend an opposition summit in Abuja on Monday, where he, Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde are due to sign a cooperation pact.

Kwankwaso accuses Kano Governor Abba Yusuf of using EFCC to target him

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

NDC vice-presidential candidate Rabiu Kwankwaso has accused his former protégé, Kano Governor Abba Yusuf, of trying to bring him down through anti-corruption agencies ahead of 2027. He claimed the governor asked the EFCC to act against him.

Read more

Rabiu Kwankwaso, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) vice-presidential candidate and former governor of Kano State, has publicly accused Kano Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf of plotting against him through anti-corruption agencies ahead of the 2027 elections.

The accusation

In a statement on his X account on Friday, 27 November 2026, Kwankwaso claimed that the governor, who served under him for decades as an aide and commissioner, had formally asked the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) to act against him. He alleged that when that effort failed, the governor turned to a little-known group to stage protests at the EFCC's office to put pressure on the agency.

Kwankwaso said the governor wanted to damage his reputation.

Background

The two men, once close allies, have fallen out since Yusuf defected from the NNPP to the APC in January 2026. In recent days the governor claimed that Kwankwaso once sent him to promise President Tinubu they would rejoin the APC, a claim the Kwankwasiyya Movement has rejected. Yusuf has also spoken publicly about the end of his marriage to Kwankwaso's niece.

The other side

No response from Governor Yusuf or the EFCC to Kwankwaso's claims was included in the reports.

Kano government responds

The Kano State Government has rejected Kwankwaso's claims. In a statement, the Commissioner for Information said the governor had no involvement in Kwankwaso's dealings with the EFCC, which it said predate the current administration, and accused him of trying to win public sympathy.
