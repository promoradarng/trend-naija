Tinubu takes swipe at Peter Obi in Akwa Ibom, says he confronted the Atlantic Ocean while Obi ran away from erosion in Anambra

6 Nov 2026, 16:47 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has taken a swipe at former Anambra State Governor Peter Obi, comparing their records on tackling environmental challenges.

What Tinubu said

Speaking in Akwa Ibom State, Tinubu accused Obi of failing to confront the serious erosion problems affecting Anambra State during his time as governor, and pointed out that Obi is now running for president under the NDC.

"Peter Obi is in one party they call NDC where he's running for president. He ran away from erosion in Anambra. He couldn't confront erosion. I, Bola Ahmed Tinubu, confronted the Atlantic Ocean. I tamed it for you," Tinubu said.

The President appeared to be referring to efforts to reclaim and protect land along the Lagos coastline, particularly the Eko Atlantic project, which began during his time as Lagos State governor.

Can the two really be compared?

Anambra has struggled with severe gully erosion for decades, with communities losing farmland, roads and homes. Tackling it requires substantial funding, engineering work and cooperation between different levels of government.

The Eko Atlantic project, on the other hand, involved major private investment and extensive coastal engineering to reclaim land from the Atlantic Ocean.

Both involve serious environmental challenges, but their circumstances, funding arrangements and engineering requirements are quite different.

The road to 2027

As the 2027 presidential election draws closer, exchanges like this are likely to become more common, with leaders publicly questioning one another's records in office.

Nigerians may also want to look closely at the actual results of these projects and the roles played by the leaders involved.

INEC says it will obey final court ruling on Atiku, begins moving election materials

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

INEC chairman Joash Amupitan says the commission will abide by the final court decision on Atiku Abubakar's candidacy. The commission has also started moving non-sensitive election materials to states and says PVC production is complete.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) says it will be bound by the final court decision on whether Atiku Abubakar can contest the 2027 presidential election.

What INEC said about Atiku

Speaking in London on Thursday, 12 November 2026, where he was briefing an audience at Chatham House on election preparations, INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan said the commission would follow whatever final ruling the courts give in the dispute over Atiku's nomination. A Federal High Court in Abuja on 5 November nullified the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and reportedly voided the primary that produced Atiku as its candidate. The ADC has appealed, and its lawyer has argued that the judgment contained no express order invalidating Atiku's candidacy.

Materials on the move

Separately, INEC National Commissioner Professor Rhoda Gumus said on Thursday that the commission had begun moving non-sensitive election materials to some geopolitical zones. Speaking while receiving a delegation from Kenya's electoral commission, she said INEC had completed nine of the 14 activities on its election timetable, finished producing Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs) and sent the first two batches to most states. She urged registered voters to collect their cards and said staff training was under way.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027. Amupitan recently completed a tour of the six geopolitical zones to assess readiness. Separately, a Federal High Court in Kaduna on Thursday nullified an ADC primary in the state, adding to the party's legal troubles.

Obi pledges 10,000MW of electricity in four years at Lagos town hall

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has promised to generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000 megawatts of electricity within four years if elected, and to declare emergencies on security, education and power. He spoke at the Lagos town hall that was attacked by thugs earlier in the day.

Read more

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has promised to deliver at least 10,000 megawatts of electricity within four years if he wins the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at the party's town hall meeting in Oregun, Lagos, on Thursday, 12 November 2026, which went ahead despite an attack on the venue by suspected thugs that morning, Obi said unreliable electricity was one of the biggest obstacles to economic growth and industrialisation. He said his government would declare an immediate state of emergency on security, education and electricity, and would generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000MW. He said Nigeria currently generates and distributes roughly 4,000MW, and compared the country's power supply unfavourably with South Africa's.

Background

Power supply has long been one of Nigeria's most persistent problems. President Bola Tinubu's government has begun a ₦30bn programme to distribute free prepaid meters. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Atiku blames Tinubu as data shows 638 killed in October and 64,000 fleeing to Niger

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has demanded that President Tinubu take personal responsibility for insecurity, citing tracker data showing 638 people killed in October and UN figures showing about 64,000 people fleeing northern Nigeria into Niger.

Read more

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has called on President Bola Tinubu to take personal responsibility for worsening insecurity in Nigeria.

The figures

In a statement issued on Thursday, 12 November 2026, by Phrank Shaibu, director of strategic communication for his campaign, Atiku cited a security tracker run by the Centre for Democracy and Development, which recorded 638 people killed in insecurity incidents in October, up from 396 in September. The North-East accounted for 409 of the deaths. He also pointed to United Nations figures showing that nearly 64,000 people have fled violence in northern Nigeria and crossed into Niger.

His demand

The statement said the President should accept responsibility for the security situation rather than blame others.

Background

Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has made insecurity and the economy key themes of his campaign. This week the DSS intercepted a large weapons consignment in Sokoto State. The Presidency had not responded to the statement at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Makinde gives four reasons he is challenging Tinubu, whom he backed in 2023

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Oyo governor Seyi Makinde, the APM presidential candidate, says he is running against President Tinubu because economic gains have not reached ordinary Nigerians, the government keeps blaming inherited problems, alliances serve politics rather than the nation, and reforms lacked cushions.

Read more

Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde has explained why he is running against President Bola Tinubu in the 2027 presidential election, three years after supporting him.

His reasons

In a newsletter published on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Makinde, the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), said his decision was not about personal rivalry or a desire to live in Aso Rock. He said he backed Tinubu in 2023 because he believed the President's experience and relationships could unite the country. He listed four concerns: that improved economic figures have not translated into better lives for ordinary Nigerians; that the government keeps blaming problems it inherited; that political alliances appear to serve political rather than national interests, while opposition parties have been weakened; and that fuel subsidy removal and currency reforms came without enough protection for citizens.

What he would have done

Makinde said he would have used the transition period to coordinate with governors through the National Economic Council and prepare support measures before removing the subsidy. He pointed to Oyo's response, saying his government deployed extra buses, froze fares and introduced half-price travel for students and the elderly within 11 days, and later delivered food relief to 200,000 households. He also noted that Tinubu had opposed an abrupt subsidy removal under President Goodluck Jonathan in 2012.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027. The President has defended his reforms, saying ending the subsidy freed money for infrastructure and other investments. His campaign had not responded to the newsletter at the time of writing.
