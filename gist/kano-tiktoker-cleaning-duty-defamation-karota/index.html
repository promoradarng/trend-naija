Kano court orders TikToker to clean traffic agency offices for two weeks over defamation

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A magistrate court in Kano has handed an unusual sentence to a TikTok user over videos that ridiculed officials of the state's traffic agency.

The sentence

According to the Kano State Road Traffic Agency (KAROTA), the court found that the TikTok user published videos containing statements that damaged the image of its personnel and exposed them to public ridicule. Magistrate Halima Wali ordered him to clean the agency's headquarters on Club Road, Kano, for two weeks and to carry out any other task the agency considers appropriate. He was also ordered to write a formal letter of apology to KAROTA's management and promise not to repeat the conduct. The agency's spokesman, Abubakar Ibrahim Sharada, confirmed the sentence.

Background

Non-custodial sentences such as community service are increasingly used by Nigerian courts for minor offences, partly to reduce overcrowding in prisons. Content creators have faced a rising number of cases over online posts in recent years, often under defamation or cybercrime laws. Rights groups have warned that such laws can be used to silence critics, while authorities say people must be held responsible for false or damaging content.

How people reacted

Many social media users found the sentence amusing and said it was a fair alternative to jail, while others said it showed the risks content creators face when they criticise officials.

Ebonyi women parade man in wheelbarrow over alleged repeated beating of his wife

7 Nov 2026, 18:58 GMT

Women in Ishiagu, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly paraded a man around the community in a wheelbarrow over claims that he repeatedly beat his wife. The unusual punishment has sparked debate about domestic violence and community justice.

Read more

Women in Ishiagu community, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly taken an unusual step to show their anger over domestic violence, publicly parading a man accused of repeatedly beating his wife.

Pushed around in a wheelbarrow

According to reports circulating online, the women placed the man in a wheelbarrow and pushed him around the community, apparently determined to make an example of him over his alleged treatment of his wife.

Photos show a large group of women surrounding the man, who sits in the wheelbarrow in a yellow T-shirt as several women push and guide it along a dirt path, with one carrying a calabash.

"Enough is enough"

The incident has drawn attention because of the unusual punishment the women chose. They appeared to have reached a point where they could no longer tolerate the alleged abuse.

In many communities, domestic violence is still treated as a private family matter, and victims are sometimes encouraged to endure abuse for the sake of their marriage. The women of Ishiagu reportedly decided that enough was enough.

Justice or humiliation?

Their action raises an important question about how communities should respond when a woman is repeatedly subjected to violence in her own home.

Many people may applaud the women for standing up against domestic abuse. Others may question whether publicly humiliating an accused person, who has not been tried, is the right approach.

The full circumstances of the incident, including the man's side of the story and whether the authorities have stepped in, remain unclear.

Trump admits immigration crackdown was 'too tough' after midterm defeat

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

President Donald Trump has said his administration's immigration crackdown was too tough and hurt Republicans in the midterm elections, particularly the deportation of people with stable jobs whose only offence was breaking immigration rules.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has acknowledged that his administration's immigration crackdown went too far, in a notable change of tone after his party's heavy losses in the midterm elections.

What he said

In an interview broadcast by the CBS programme '60 Minutes' on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Trump defended his record on border security and on removing dangerous criminals, but said he thought the crackdown had been too tough. He said the administration had suffered politically by deporting people who had stable jobs and had only committed immigration violations, and that this had hurt Republican candidates at the ballot box.

The election

Exit polls showed immigration was the third most important issue for voters, after the economy and the state of democracy. In traditionally conservative states such as Texas, a shift among Latino voters towards the Democrats contributed to their gains in Congress, where they are projected to control both the House of Representatives and the Senate.

Background

Mass deportations became a defining policy of Trump's second term, drawing protests and legal challenges. Only days ago he promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of the deportation programme, to White House chief of staff, so it is unclear how far policy will change in practice. Groups of Nigerians have been among those deported, including six who arrived in Lagos last week.

Nigeria seeks $500m World Bank loan for infrastructure financing platform

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The federal government has approached the World Bank for a $500 million loan to set up a platform to attract private investment into infrastructure, part of a wider $3.85 billion programme called INFRAGROW.

Read more

The federal government has asked the World Bank for a $500 million loan to set up a platform to attract private investment into Nigerian infrastructure.

The loan

According to a World Bank project document updated on 4 November 2026 and reported on Monday, 9 November, the facility is part of a broader $3.85 billion programme called the Nigeria Infrastructure Financing and De-Risking Platform for Growth and Jobs, or INFRAGROW. The Federal Ministry of Finance is listed as the borrower and the Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority (NSIA) as the implementing agency. The aim is to reduce the risks that discourage private investors from funding infrastructure projects.

Why the bank says it is needed

The World Bank estimates that Nigeria has an infrastructure financing gap of about $100 billion a year, which holds back growth, competitiveness and job creation. It says the problem is not only a lack of money but also weak project preparation and difficulty turning proposals into commercially viable investments.

Background

The request comes as President Bola Tinubu also seeks Senate approval for about $7.7 billion in new external borrowing, and as government borrowing becomes a major campaign issue. NDC candidate Peter Obi has challenged the President to account for loans, while the Presidency says claims that it has borrowed ₦120 trillion are distorted. The World Bank reported last week that Nigeria's total external debt rose to about $145 billion in 2025.

Makinde campaign says Tinubu cannot claim credit for naira recovery

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The campaign of APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde has accused President Tinubu of seeking praise for the naira's recent stability after policies that sharply weakened it, saying small exchange rate gains do little for Nigerians facing high prices.

Read more

The presidential campaign of Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde has criticised President Bola Tinubu's claims about the naira.

What the campaign said

In a statement reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, Richard Nnanna Ihediwa, director of strategic communications for the Makinde–Daura campaign, accused the President of trying to take credit for the naira's recent stability after policies that first caused a drastic fall in its value. He said the President could not preside over the destruction of the currency's purchasing power and then ask for applause. The campaign pointed out that the official exchange rate was about ₦461 to the dollar when Tinubu took office in May 2023, before the removal of the fuel subsidy and the unification of the exchange rate, and said marginal improvements offer little relief to people facing high inflation.

Background

The statement responds to Tinubu's remarks in Uyo last week, where he said the naira would have become worthless without his reforms and put the current rate at about ₦1,300 to the dollar. Makinde is the candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), with former DSS chief Lawal Musa Daura as his running mate, and recently launched his 'Reset Agenda' manifesto. The government has said the old official rate understated the true value of the dollar and that unification was needed.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
