Spain's transport minister resigns after deadly train crashes

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Spain's Transport Minister Oscar Puente has announced his resignation after two major train crashes in less than a year, including an October collision south of Madrid that killed 69 people. He said staying on would be inconsistent with political dignity.

Read more

Spain's Transport Minister, Oscar Puente, has announced that he will resign following two serious train crashes in less than a year.

What he said

Puente told lawmakers on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, that a transport minister in a country like Spain could not stay in office after two accidents of such severity, regardless of whether he was directly responsible. He described the decision as a matter of political dignity, consistency and empathy, and said he had decided to step down within minutes of the latest crash but stayed on to manage the aftermath and introduce safety measures. He did not say exactly when he would leave office, and confirmed he would not stand in Spain's early general election on 29 November.

The latest crash

The resignation came six days after a crash on 28 October near Urda, south of Madrid, in which a high-speed train run by the state operator Renfe hit the back of a stationary train run by a private operator on the Madrid–Andalusia line. The crash reportedly killed 69 people and injured more than 200. Authorities have blamed a combination of human and computer failures, and Puente said a red signal should have stopped the train.

Background

Spain has one of Europe's largest high-speed rail networks. The two crashes have caused public anger over rail safety.

What happens next

Investigations into both crashes are continuing.

UK court rejects Ibori's appeal to cut £101.5m confiscation order

5 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Court of Appeal in London has dismissed former Delta governor James Ibori's bid to reduce a £101.5 million confiscation order made after his 2012 conviction for money laundering. Ibori says he is consulting his lawyers.

Read more

Britain's Court of Appeal has dismissed a challenge by former Delta State governor James Ibori to reduce a £101.5 million confiscation order against him, according to reports.

The ruling

The court rejected the appeal on Tuesday, 3 November 2026. Ibori had sought to reduce the amount by about £4.3 million. The order was issued by Southwark Crown Court in July 2023, directing him to pay or face an eight-year prison term in default. The appeal had delayed enforcement, which can now proceed unless he takes the case to the UK Supreme Court.

Ibori's response

Ibori reportedly said he was consulting his lawyers on his next steps and expressed frustration at how long the proceedings had taken.

Background

Ibori governed Delta State from 1999 to 2007. He was arrested in Dubai and extradited to Britain in 2011. In 2012 he pleaded guilty in a London court to laundering tens of millions of dollars of public funds through British banks and properties, and was sentenced to 13 years in prison. He was released in late 2016 after serving about half of the sentence and returned to Nigeria in 2017. The confiscation proceedings followed investigations by Nigerian and British anti-corruption authorities, and Nigeria has pushed for the recovered funds to be returned.

Why it matters

The case is one of the most significant examples of international cooperation to recover stolen public funds from Nigeria.

Former Osun governor Oyinlola says his endorsement of Tinubu 'has expired'

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Former Osun governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has reportedly withdrawn his 2025 endorsement of President Tinubu for 2027, saying it was a political move to resolve Osun's local government crisis that did not work.

Read more

Former Osun State governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has said his endorsement of President Bola Tinubu for the 2027 election is no longer valid.

What he said

Asked in a television interview, reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026, whether he still stood by the endorsement, Oyinlola reportedly replied that it had expired. He explained that he and other Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) stakeholders in Osun endorsed the President on 20 July 2025 as a strategic move, because of problems in the state that they believed only the President could solve, including missing local government funds and a dispute over who should occupy the local council offices. He said the endorsement was meant to help resolve those issues, but the solution had not come.

Background

Oyinlola governed Osun State from 2003 to 2010. Osun has been at the centre of a long-running dispute over control of its local governments, involving the PDP-led state government of Governor Ademola Adeleke and APC-backed council officials, with court rulings and disagreements over the release of council funds. The PDP itself is divided, with some members backing Tinubu, others joining opposition coalitions, and a court battle over its national leadership.

Why it matters

The withdrawal is a setback for the President's campaign in Osun, a swing state in the South West. The APC says it remains confident of winning the state, while opposition parties hope to benefit from discontent there.

What happens next

Oyinlola did not say which candidate, if any, he would now support.

Their relationship drew strong reactions online because IVD's late wife, Bimbo, had died in a fire months earlier, and Blessing CEO had first met him as his relationship counsellor. In recent weeks Blessing CEO has been mourning the death of her nine-year-old son, who died while she was in custody, and this week she issued a tearful public apology to people she had hurt with her words. How people reacted

Many fans welcomed the news and wished her peace and healing, while others said she should focus on her children and her recovery.
