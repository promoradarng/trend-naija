Crowd erupts in 'Obi' chants as Deputy Speaker Ben Kalu campaigns for Tinubu at Item Okpi festival in Aba

7 Nov 2026, 20:53 GMT

What was meant to be a moment of political mobilisation for President Bola Ahmed Tinubu reportedly took an unexpected turn at the Item Okpi cultural festival in Aba, Abia State, when members of the crowd began chanting the name of Peter Obi.

"Obi! Obi! Obi!"

The Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives, Benjamin Kalu, was addressing a large gathering at the cultural event when he turned to national politics and began appealing to the people to support President Tinubu.

The response from sections of the audience was not what he appeared to be hoping for. As Kalu spoke in support of the President, loud chants of "Obi! Obi! Obi!" reportedly rose from the crowd.

Even when the Deputy Speaker repeated the familiar declaration, "On your mandate we shall stand," the chants continued, creating an unusual scene in which the politician's message and the crowd's response seemed to be moving in opposite directions.

Why it matters

The moment has drawn attention because of Abia State's political significance and the continuing debate about the popularity of Nigeria's major political figures ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Peter Obi, who contested the 2023 presidential election under the Labour Party and is now the NDC presidential candidate, continues to command a strong following in sections of the electorate, particularly in the South-East.

For Kalu, an influential APC politician from Abia State, the incident highlights the challenge of promoting the ruling party's candidate in a region where political loyalties remain divided.

A cultural festival turned political

Chants from a crowd at a cultural gathering are not a scientific measure of how a state or region will vote. But public reactions like this can still offer a glimpse into the political conversations happening among ordinary Nigerians.

What makes this incident stand out is the setting. The gathering was mainly a cultural celebration, yet it became a scene of competing political expressions once the Deputy Speaker raised the subject of presidential support.

Over to you

Were the chants simply a spontaneous reaction from enthusiastic Obi supporters, or a sign of wider political sentiment in the area? One thing is clear: Ben Kalu was talking about Tinubu, but some voices in the crowd wanted to talk about Obi.

Lalong, Dariye and other Plateau leaders endorse Tinubu for 2027

13 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A coalition of Plateau State political leaders, including former governors Simon Lalong and Joshua Dariye, has endorsed President Tinubu and other APC candidates for 2027 at a meeting in Jos.

Read more

Prominent political figures from Plateau State have endorsed President Bola Tinubu's re-election bid for 2027.

The meeting

The endorsement was made by voice vote at a meeting in Jos organised by Arewa for Asiwaju (A4A), a pro-Tinubu group, according to a statement released on Friday, 13 November 2026. Those present included former governors, ministers, senators, members of the House of Representatives, commissioners and council chairmen, who also adopted other All Progressives Congress (APC) candidates in the state.

What the leaders said

Former governor Simon Lalong, who chaired the meeting as deputy chairman of A4A's board of trustees, said those present believed the President was committed to Nigeria's development and that keeping him in office would speed up economic growth. Former governor Joshua Dariye credited the Tinubu administration with meaningful contributions to Plateau and the country.

Background

Plateau is a key battleground in the North Central. Peter Obi won the state in the 2023 presidential election. A Middle Belt youth group that backed Obi in 2023 also recently switched its support to Tinubu. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

ECOWAS has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Akufo-Addo to head its election observation mission for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections on 16 January 2027.

Read more

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to lead its election observation mission to Nigeria.

The appointment

The appointment was announced in a statement from Akufo-Addo's office on Thursday, 12 November 2026. He will oversee the deployment of ECOWAS observers for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections, scheduled for 16 January 2027. The mission will monitor the electoral process and report on how the polls are conducted.

Who is Akufo-Addo

Akufo-Addo served two terms as Ghana's president, from 2017 to January 2025.

Background

International attention on Nigeria's election is growing. The European Union has raised concerns about violence and hate speech, and a senior US State Department official told Congress this week that the vote could spur violence. INEC says preparations are on track, with non-sensitive materials already being moved to the states.

A senior US State Department official has told a congressional hearing that Nigeria's presidential election in January could spur violence, as a US lawmaker urged Washington to pay close attention to the vote.

Read more

The United States says it will keep a close watch on Nigeria's 2027 presidential election, with a senior official warning that it could trigger violence.

What was said

Frank Garcia, the Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs, made the remarks on Thursday, 12 November 2026, in prepared testimony before the House Foreign Affairs Subcommittee on Africa during a hearing on violence in Nigeria. He said he was realistic about the challenges ahead and that the January presidential election could spur violence. He also pointed to an overstretched security system, limited reach of the courts in the Middle Belt and a lack of resources as obstacles to tackling insecurity.

Congress weighs in

Congressman Riley Moore used the hearing to call for close American attention to how the election unfolds.

Makinde says talks with Atiku and Obi are still open ahead of 2027

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Oyo governor and APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde says discussions with Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi are ongoing and could lead to different political arrangements before the election, but that any choice of partner must be based on evidence and public opinion.

Read more

Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde, the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), says he is still open to working with Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi before the 2027 elections.

What he said

In a television interview aired on Friday, 13 November 2026, Makinde said discussions with both men were still active and that no option had been ruled out. He said the talks could lead to a range of political arrangements before the election, and that any decision would be guided by the goal of changing the country's direction. Asked whether he preferred Atiku or Obi as a partner, he said the choice should be based on evidence and public opinion rather than sentiment. He has also said he is willing to work with any Nigerian except President Bola Tinubu.

Background

Atiku is the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and Obi of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC). Efforts to agree a single opposition candidate have repeatedly stalled, with each insisting on running. Earlier this week, Makinde gave four reasons for challenging Tinubu, whom he supported in 2023, and PRP candidate Donald Duke said he was willing to step down for a consensus candidate. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
