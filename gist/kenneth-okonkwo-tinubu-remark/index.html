Kenneth Okonkwo apologises to God over Tinubu presidency in TV interview

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Nollywood actor turned politician Kenneth Okonkwo has once again attacked President Bola Tinubu's administration, this time with a remark that quickly went viral.

What he said

Speaking on News Central TV on Wednesday, 28 October 2026, Okonkwo said he did not know what Nigerians had done to God for Tinubu to become president, and followed it with an apology to God on behalf of the country. A clip of the moment was shared widely on Instagram and X on Wednesday evening.

Background

Okonkwo, best known for his role in the 1992 classic Living in Bondage, is a lawyer and one of Nollywood's most politically active stars. He supported Tinubu's APC before switching to back Peter Obi and the Labour Party in 2023, when he served as a spokesman for Obi's campaign. He has frequently criticised the government's economic policies, including the removal of the fuel subsidy and the rising cost of living.

How people reacted

Reactions were sharply divided along political lines. Opposition supporters shared the clip as a summary of their frustration with the economy, while APC supporters reminded him of his past support for the ruling party and accused him of changing sides. The APC has not formally responded to his comments.

Why it matters

With campaigns for the January 2027 elections now in full swing, celebrities on all sides are becoming more vocal, and their comments are being used by both camps to rally supporters online.

  • Okonkwo made the remark on News Central TV.
  • He has been a frequent critic of the government's economic policies.
  • APC supporters reminded him of his earlier support for the party.

"You're becoming very noisy, 'money na water'!": Umahi fires back at Cubana Chief Priest, questions his wealth and contributions to society

8 Nov 2026, 17:03 GMT

Works Minister David Umahi has hit back at Cubana Chief Priest, weeks after the celebrity businessman called him the second-worst minister from the South-East. Umahi mocked his famous "money na water" catchphrase and asked how many people he has given scholarships.

Read more

"You're becoming very noisy. Money na water. How much do you have? Have you ever seen Dangote saying, 'I have money,' or Otedola saying that he's rich? Money speaks for itself. It's not by saying money na water. What have you done? How many people have you given scholarships?"

Those were the strong words of Nigeria's Minister of Works, David Umahi, as he fired back at Cubana Chief Priest, weeks after the celebrity businessman described him as the second-worst minister from the South-East.

"Money speaks for itself"

The criticism clearly did not go down well with the former Ebonyi State governor, who has now turned the conversation towards wealth, public conduct and what rich people are doing to improve the lives of others.

Umahi questioned the constant public display of wealth, particularly the expression "money na water" (meaning money flows like water), which Cubana Chief Priest is widely known for.

He pointed to two of Nigeria's richest businessmen, Aliko Dangote and Femi Otedola, and asked whether Nigerians regularly hear them announcing how much money they have. For Umahi, genuine wealth should speak through achievements, investments and contributions to society, not constant declarations on social media.

The scholarship question

The line that stands out most is his question: "How many people have you given scholarships?"

That question takes the argument beyond who has more money. It raises a different issue entirely: what impact are wealthy Nigerians making in the lives of ordinary people?

Two different conversations

Cubana Chief Priest has every right to criticise a public officeholder, and Umahi, as a serving minister, is equally entitled to respond. But criticism of a minister's performance is ultimately best answered with evidence of work done, projects completed and results delivered.

That is what makes this exchange interesting. Umahi is questioning Cubana Chief Priest's display of wealth, while the original criticism was about Umahi's performance as Minister of Works. These are two different conversations, and Nigerians will have their opinions on both.

"Buhari promised me APC presidential ticket in 2022, I regret listening to him": Rotimi Amaechi

8 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

Former Rivers governor and ex-Transport Minister Rotimi Amaechi has reportedly said the late President Muhammadu Buhari promised him the APC presidential ticket in 2022, a promise he now regrets believing. Amaechi went on to lose the primary to Bola Tinubu.

Read more

Former Rivers State Governor and ex-Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi has reportedly expressed regret over his decision to trust former President Muhammadu Buhari during the race for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential ticket in 2022.

The promise

According to the statement attributed to Amaechi, Buhari had promised him the party's presidential ticket, a promise he now regrets believing.

The claim raises fresh questions about the political arrangements and behind-the-scenes discussions that took place before the APC primary that eventually produced Bola Ahmed Tinubu as the party's candidate.

Buhari died in July 2025, so he is no longer able to respond to Amaechi's account.

How the 2022 race played out

Amaechi was among the prominent politicians who contested the ticket in 2022. Despite being a serving minister in Buhari's government and a close associate of the President, he lost the primary to Tinubu.

As the sitting President and leader of the ruling party at the time, Buhari's influence was considerable, although the final decision rested with the party's delegates.

Loyalty without reward

For Amaechi, the disappointment appears to go beyond losing an election. His comments suggest he believed he had received an assurance from someone whose word carried enormous political weight.

That points to one of the oldest lessons in Nigerian politics: loyalty does not always guarantee reward.

Amaechi served under Buhari for nearly seven years as Minister of Transportation, and he was one of the key figures who helped build the coalition that brought the APC to power in 2015. Yet when his chance came to seek the presidency, things did not turn out as he had apparently expected.

The questions that remain

Did Buhari actually make such a commitment, and if he did, what happened between that promise and the primary?

Was Amaechi politically outplayed, or did he place too much confidence in a promise that was never guaranteed?

Whatever the answer, his regret offers another glimpse into the complicated world of Nigerian party politics, where today's allies can have very different interests when power is at stake.

Amaechi says Buhari promised in writing to back him at 2022 APC primary

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

ADC vice-presidential candidate Rotimi Amaechi says former President Buhari assured him in writing that he would name him as his preferred successor in 2022, and that relying on the promise was his biggest mistake. He lost the APC ticket to Tinubu.

Read more

Rotimi Amaechi, the vice-presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) for 2027, says former President Muhammadu Buhari promised to back him as his preferred candidate at the 2022 All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential primary.

What he said

Speaking in a radio interview in Lagos on Friday, 6 November 2026, Amaechi said Buhari had given him the assurance in writing. Because of it, he said, he held back from campaigning, lobbying delegates or making political deals before the primary, which he now considers his biggest mistake. He said his refusal to promise positions such as the vice-presidency and the post of Secretary to the Government of the Federation to governors cost him support.

Claims about religion

Amaechi also accused President Bola Tinubu's camp of appealing to religious identity to win Northern delegates, alleging that a delegation of Muslim politicians from the South-West urged governors in the North-West to back Tinubu on religious grounds. He said he would never use religion to win an election. Tinubu's camp had not responded to the claims at the time of writing.

Background

Tinubu won the June 2022 APC primary at Eagle Square in Abuja with 1,271 votes to Amaechi's 316, and went on to win the 2023 presidential election. Amaechi, a former Rivers governor, directed Buhari's successful 2015 campaign and served as his Minister of Transportation. Buhari, who died in 2025, did not publicly endorse any aspirant at the primary. Amaechi is now Atiku Abubakar's running mate, though a recent court judgment voided Atiku's nomination, a ruling the ADC has appealed.

House in Kano State catches fire, blaze linked to solar panel installation

9 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

A house in Kano State has caught fire, with the blaze linked to its solar power installation. Photos show flames bursting through the roof beside the solar panels as neighbours try to help. The exact cause has not yet been confirmed.

Read more

A house in Kano State has caught fire, with the incident being linked to its solar power installation.

Flames on the roof

Photos from the scene show thick smoke rising from the roof of a two-storey building, with flames breaking through beside a row of solar panels. A man can be seen climbing up to an upper-floor balcony as a crowd of neighbours gathers below, some filming on their phones and others watching in shock.

A growing safety concern

The incident raises serious concerns about the safety of solar installations, especially as more Nigerians turn to solar energy because of unreliable electricity supply.

Poor wiring, faulty batteries, overloaded inverters and improper installation can all create fire hazards. Experts often advise homeowners to use qualified installers, buy certified equipment and have their systems checked regularly.

Cause not yet confirmed

The exact cause of this particular fire has not yet been independently confirmed.
