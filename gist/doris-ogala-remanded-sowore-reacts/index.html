Doris Ogala kneels to apologise in court as judge sets 6 November for bail ruling

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Nollywood actress Doris Ogala, who is the African Action Congress (AAC) candidate for governor of Abia State in 2027, has been remanded in a correctional centre in Lagos after appearing in court on Wednesday, 28 October 2026. Her application for bail is listed for hearing on Friday, 30 October.

What happened

Ogala was arraigned before a Federal High Court in Lagos on three counts linked to a complaint by the cleric Pastor Chris Okafor. The charges relate to the alleged publication of photographs, the alleged spreading of false electronic messages and alleged threats against the pastor. She has not been convicted of anything, and the allegations are yet to be tested at trial.

News of the remand first spread on Wednesday afternoon, when comedian Deeone shared it online. Reports differ on which Lagos facility she was taken to. Before she was led away, Ogala posted a short video from the courtroom in which she described the case as a political move aimed at stopping her governorship bid. Clips of the moment she was taken out of court were shared widely on Thursday.

Background

The dispute between Ogala and Okafor has played out on social media for months. It reportedly began after the pastor announced plans to marry another woman, which led Ogala to criticise him publicly. She has since spoken several times about their past relationship. In another video from court, she was also heard telling the judge that her former partner was using the law against her.

How people reacted

Human rights activist and politician Omoyele Sowore condemned the remand in a Facebook post, calling it an abuse of power. He accused the police of repeatedly using the Cybercrimes Act to shield powerful people, argued that what he called a private quarrel should not have become a criminal case, and urged the courts to stay impartial. He also said her rights as a candidate must be protected. Online, fans were divided, with some backing the actress and others saying she should answer the charges in court.

Friday's hearing

At the bail hearing on Friday, 30 October, Ogala knelt before Justice Akintayo Aluko of the Federal High Court in Lagos and apologised for disrupting the previous sitting. The judge reserved his ruling on her bail application until Friday, 6 November, and she was returned to the correctional centre. The charges against her reportedly include cyberstalking, cyberbullying and attempted extortion, all of which are yet to be tested at trial. Pastor Chris Okafor shared a cryptic post on social media the same day, without commenting directly on the case.

What happens next

Ogala will remain in custody at least until the bail ruling on 6 November. Her party has not said how the case will affect her campaign in Abia.

OK Movement withdraws support from Obi and the NDC

27 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

The OK Movement, named after Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso, has reportedly pulled its campaign support from the NDC. Its director-general said the group had been told Obi and the party had chosen to cut ties with it.

Read more

The OK Movement, a support group named after the initials of NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi and his running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso, has reportedly withdrawn its campaign and mobilisation support from the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC).

The announcement

In a statement on Friday, 27 November 2026, the movement's national director-general, John Ughulu, said the group had been informed that Obi and the NDC had decided to cut ties with it. He directed the movement's ward, local government and state structures to stop all involvement in NDC activities immediately.

What next

Ughulu said the group had begun discussions with other political parties and presidential aspirants.

Background

The development comes days before Obi is due to attend an opposition summit in Abuja with Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde.

The other side

No response from Obi or the NDC was included in the reports.
