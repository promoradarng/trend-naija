From NYSC in Sokoto to billionaire: how Tony Elumelu got his first banking job with a 2:2

14 Nov 2026, 19:37 GMT

This is Nigerian billionaire Tony Elumelu with a friend during his National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) days in Sokoto, around 1985.

Two young men and their dreams

Look at the photograph and you see two young men enjoying their youth, probably talking about life, their dreams and what the future might hold.

At the time, Elumelu was just another young Nigerian graduate trying to find his feet. He had ambitions, like many young people, but there was no guarantee of where life would take him.

The job he was not 'qualified' for

Years later, after earning his master's degree, he came across a job advertisement from Allstates Trust Bank. The bank wanted applicants with at least a Second Class Upper degree, popularly known as a 2:1.

Elumelu had graduated with a 2:2.

For many people, that would have been reason enough to forget the job and move on. The bank had clearly stated its requirements, and he did not meet them.

But he decided to try anyway. He applied, explaining that although his result fell below the requirement, he was intelligent, hardworking and ambitious, and ready to prove himself if given the chance.

He got the job.

The turning point

That single decision became an important turning point in his career. He worked his way up through banking, took on bigger and bigger responsibilities, and eventually became one of the most influential businessmen and investors in Africa.

Today, he is a billionaire and respected entrepreneur whose investments have created opportunities for thousands of people across the continent.

The lesson

Look at that old NYSC photograph again. The young man in Sokoto could hardly have imagined what was waiting for him.

Your result may not be the best. You may have started life without connections, money or anyone powerful to open doors for you. But life has a way of surprising people who refuse to give up on themselves.

Imagine if Elumelu had seen the 2:1 requirement and simply walked away. That opportunity might have passed him by.

Sometimes the biggest mistake you can make is rejecting yourself before anyone else has had the chance to consider you. Apply for that job. Pursue that opportunity. You may not always succeed, but you will never know what could have happened if you never tried.

Over to you

How many opportunities have people missed simply because they believed they were not qualified enough?

  • Elumelu served his NYSC in Sokoto around 1985.
  • He applied for a job requiring a 2:1 despite having a 2:2.
  • He got the job at Allstates Trust Bank.

NAFDAC has shut Zuba International Market in Abuja after a joint operation with police and the DSS reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome goods worth more than ₦300 million. Over 160 shops were inspected.

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has shut down Zuba International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

The operation

The closure followed a joint enforcement operation with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services, which reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome products worth more than ₦300 million. The exercise covered more than 160 shops in the market.

Why now

Naira gains ₦19 to ₦1,378 per dollar after CBN raises BDC limit

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The naira strengthened by ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar on the official market on Thursday, after the CBN raised the weekly dollar sales limit for bureaux de change. It had lost over ₦43 in the previous two weeks.

Read more

The naira recovered strongly against the US dollar on the official foreign exchange market on Thursday, 26 November 2026.

The rebound

The currency gained ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar, up from ₦1,397 on Wednesday. The recovery reversed some of the losses it suffered between 13 and 25 November, when it fell by ₦43.50 amid rising demand for dollars.

Why

The gain followed the Central Bank of Nigeria's decision to raise the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators from $150,000 to $1 million, aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the market.

Background

The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 23%.

Dangote cuts cooking gas price to ₦1,000 per kg, but retail prices stay high

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its cooking gas gantry price to ₦1,000 per kg, with its marketers selling at ₦1,100. Consumers, however, are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kg at retail outlets.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery has reportedly reduced the wholesale price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), commonly known as cooking gas, to ₦1,000 per kilogram.

The new prices

Marketers linked to the refinery have lowered their selling price to ₦1,100 per kilogram, according to a market survey.

The retail gap

Despite the cuts, consumers are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kilogram at retail outlets, raising concerns about the gap between wholesale and retail prices. Earlier this week, cooking gas was reported to be selling for up to ₦2,000 per kg in parts of Abuja.

Background

The refinery has also cut its petrol and diesel prices in recent weeks, most recently reducing diesel by ₦125 to ₦1,595 per litre.
