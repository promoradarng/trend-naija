From NYSC in Sokoto to billionaire: how Tony Elumelu got his first banking job with a 2:2

14 Nov 2026, 19:37 GMT

This is Nigerian billionaire Tony Elumelu with a friend during his National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) days in Sokoto, around 1985.

Two young men and their dreams

Look at the photograph and you see two young men enjoying their youth, probably talking about life, their dreams and what the future might hold.

At the time, Elumelu was just another young Nigerian graduate trying to find his feet. He had ambitions, like many young people, but there was no guarantee of where life would take him.

The job he was not 'qualified' for

Years later, after earning his master's degree, he came across a job advertisement from Allstates Trust Bank. The bank wanted applicants with at least a Second Class Upper degree, popularly known as a 2:1.

Elumelu had graduated with a 2:2.

For many people, that would have been reason enough to forget the job and move on. The bank had clearly stated its requirements, and he did not meet them.

But he decided to try anyway. He applied, explaining that although his result fell below the requirement, he was intelligent, hardworking and ambitious, and ready to prove himself if given the chance.

He got the job.

The turning point

That single decision became an important turning point in his career. He worked his way up through banking, took on bigger and bigger responsibilities, and eventually became one of the most influential businessmen and investors in Africa.

Today, he is a billionaire and respected entrepreneur whose investments have created opportunities for thousands of people across the continent.

The lesson

Look at that old NYSC photograph again. The young man in Sokoto could hardly have imagined what was waiting for him.

Your result may not be the best. You may have started life without connections, money or anyone powerful to open doors for you. But life has a way of surprising people who refuse to give up on themselves.

Imagine if Elumelu had seen the 2:1 requirement and simply walked away. That opportunity might have passed him by.

Sometimes the biggest mistake you can make is rejecting yourself before anyone else has had the chance to consider you. Apply for that job. Pursue that opportunity. You may not always succeed, but you will never know what could have happened if you never tried.

Over to you

How many opportunities have people missed simply because they believed they were not qualified enough?

  • He got the job at Allstates Trust Bank.

Nigerian stock market loses ₦2.27 trillion in one day

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Investors lost about ₦2.27 trillion on the Nigerian Exchange on Tuesday as the All-Share Index fell 1.38%. Trading volume and deals also dropped.

Read more

The Nigerian stock market closed sharply lower on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, as sell-offs in major stocks wiped about ₦2.27 trillion off market value in a single trading session.

The numbers

Data from the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) showed the All-Share Index fell by 3,491.13 points, or 1.38%, to close at 249,318.65, down from 252,809.78 in the previous session. Total market capitalisation dropped by the same percentage, from ₦164.15 trillion to ₦161.88 trillion.

Trading activity

Activity also slowed. Investors traded 564.04 million shares, down 16.26% from 673.6 million in the previous session, while the number of deals fell by 18.91% to 30,992.

Bright spots

The losses were partly cushioned by buying interest in consumer goods and insurance stocks, with Cadbury Nigeria among the top gainers.

NCC gives MTN, Airtel and others until 31 December to use idle spectrum or lose it

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigerian Communications Commission has told telecom operators to prove they are using their allocated spectrum by 31 December 2026 or risk losing it. It wants better network quality for subscribers.

Read more

The Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) has given telecommunications operators, including MTN and Airtel, until 31 December 2026 to show they are effectively using their allocated spectrum or risk having it taken back.

What spectrum is

Spectrum refers to the radio frequencies operators use to carry calls, internet data and other wireless services. Because it is a limited national resource, the regulator wants to make sure allocations are supporting real network deployment.

Why the NCC is acting

The directive is aimed at ensuring valuable frequencies are not left idle while many Nigerians complain of poor network quality, congestion and limited broadband coverage.

What operators must do

Under the directive, operators must show that their assigned frequencies are being actively used through investment in network infrastructure and improved services. Unused spectrum could be reclaimed and reassigned.
