From NYSC in Sokoto to billionaire: how Tony Elumelu got his first banking job with a 2:2

14 Nov 2026, 19:37 GMT

This is Nigerian billionaire Tony Elumelu with a friend during his National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) days in Sokoto, around 1985.

Two young men and their dreams

Look at the photograph and you see two young men enjoying their youth, probably talking about life, their dreams and what the future might hold.

At the time, Elumelu was just another young Nigerian graduate trying to find his feet. He had ambitions, like many young people, but there was no guarantee of where life would take him.

The job he was not 'qualified' for

Years later, after earning his master's degree, he came across a job advertisement from Allstates Trust Bank. The bank wanted applicants with at least a Second Class Upper degree, popularly known as a 2:1.

Elumelu had graduated with a 2:2.

For many people, that would have been reason enough to forget the job and move on. The bank had clearly stated its requirements, and he did not meet them.

But he decided to try anyway. He applied, explaining that although his result fell below the requirement, he was intelligent, hardworking and ambitious, and ready to prove himself if given the chance.

He got the job.

The turning point

That single decision became an important turning point in his career. He worked his way up through banking, took on bigger and bigger responsibilities, and eventually became one of the most influential businessmen and investors in Africa.

Today, he is a billionaire and respected entrepreneur whose investments have created opportunities for thousands of people across the continent.

The lesson

Look at that old NYSC photograph again. The young man in Sokoto could hardly have imagined what was waiting for him.

Your result may not be the best. You may have started life without connections, money or anyone powerful to open doors for you. But life has a way of surprising people who refuse to give up on themselves.

Imagine if Elumelu had seen the 2:1 requirement and simply walked away. That opportunity might have passed him by.

Sometimes the biggest mistake you can make is rejecting yourself before anyone else has had the chance to consider you. Apply for that job. Pursue that opportunity. You may not always succeed, but you will never know what could have happened if you never tried.

Over to you

How many opportunities have people missed simply because they believed they were not qualified enough?

  • He applied for a job requiring a 2:1 despite having a 2:2.
  • He got the job at Allstates Trust Bank.

At least 30 feared dead, about 300 missing as boat carrying mourners capsizes in Bayelsa

20 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

At least 30 people are feared dead and about 300 missing after an overloaded boat carrying mourners to a burial capsized near Oweikorogha in Southern Ijaw, Bayelsa State. Police marine units are leading rescue efforts.

Read more

A boat said to be carrying more than 300 passengers has capsized in Southern Ijaw Local Government Area of Bayelsa State, with many people feared dead or missing.

What happened

The boat was travelling from Ayama community to Ukubie for a burial ceremony when it capsized at about noon on Friday, 20 November 2026, shortly after passing Oweikorogha community.

The exact number of passengers on board and the number of casualties had not been established at the time of the reports, but residents fear several people may have lost their lives.

Rescue efforts

The Bayelsa State Police Command spokesperson, DSP Musa Muhammad, confirmed the incident and said the Commissioner of Police had deployed the Marine Police Unit and the local divisional police officer for rescue operations. He said further details on casualties and missing persons would be released.

A former state chairman of the Maritime Workers Union also confirmed the incident.

Death toll rises

Later reports put the number feared dead at at least 30, with around 300 others unaccounted for. Witnesses described the boat as heavily overloaded, and a community source said some people had raised concerns as passengers boarded.

What happens next

Rescue and recovery efforts are continuing, and official casualty figures are expected once they are confirmed.

A video of Bishop David Oyedepo saying church succession should be a spiritual process, not a family arrangement, has gone viral amid the Fountain of Life leadership crisis.

Read more

A video of Bishop David Oyedepo speaking about church succession has gone viral, drawing attention amid the leadership dispute at The Fountain of Life Church involving Pastor Jimmy Odukoya.

What he said

In the clip, posted on Instagram on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, the founder of Living Faith Church Worldwide (Winners' Chapel) said planning for who comes next is one of the most important responsibilities of church leadership.

He said leadership transition in ministry is a spiritual process and not a family arrangement, warning against simply handing churches over to children. He pointed to Joshua succeeding Moses in the Bible as the model.

Why it is trending

The video spread widely as Nigerians debated the Fountain of Life crisis, where trustees say the late founder Taiwo Odukoya wanted another pastor to succeed him, while his son Jimmy insists his removal as senior pastor is invalid.
