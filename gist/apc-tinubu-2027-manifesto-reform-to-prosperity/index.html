APC manifesto promises 50,000 police recruits, 200,000 homes and 10,000MW of power

10 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

The All Progressives Congress (APC) has released President Bola Tinubu's manifesto for the 2027 presidential election, setting out his plans for a second term.

The document

The 109-page manifesto, titled 'From Reform to Prosperity Agenda 2027–2031', was due to be formally unveiled at the Shehu Musa Yar'Adua Centre in Abuja on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. It covers security, the economy, housing, health and education, and says a second term would focus on turning the economic foundations of the first term into concrete gains for Nigerians.

Security

The manifesto pledges to train and deploy 50,000 new police officers and work towards one officer for every 400 citizens, recruit 28,000 more military personnel, and complete four new Army divisions in Makurdi, Ilorin, Jalingo and Benin City. It proposes that the civil defence corps take over static guard duties from the police, the rollout of body cameras and forensic facilities, wider electronic surveillance of borders, and the deployment of forest guards, agro-rangers and mining marshals.

Economy, housing and power

It targets annual economic growth of 6 per cent by 2031, halving youth unemployment, and extending credit to half of economically active Nigerians. It promises 200,000 affordable homes under a mass housing scheme and aims to raise available power generation towards 10,000 megawatts.

Health and education

The plan aims to raise the share of functional primary health centres from 53 to 90 per cent, extend health insurance to 40 per cent of Nigerians, cut out-of-pocket health spending from 71 per cent to below 50 per cent, recruit 20,000 more teachers, halve the number of out-of-school children and expand the student loan scheme.

Background

Tinubu faces 17 other presidential candidates, including Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, Peter Obi of the NDC and Seyi Makinde of the APM, in the election on 16 January 2027. Makinde launched his 'Reset Agenda' last week. Opposition candidates have criticised the government's record on the economy and insecurity, while the President says his reforms saved the economy from collapse.

  • It targets 6% annual growth by 2031.

Peter Obi corrects claims that his Washington visit was an endorsement from the US Congress

18 Nov 2026, 16:43 GMT

Peter Obi says his recent trip to Washington involved talks on Nigeria's development, citizens' welfare and democracy, and that he neither sought nor announced any endorsement, after social media posts claimed the US Congress had backed him.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has corrected claims that his recent visit to Washington amounted to an endorsement from the United States Congress.

What Obi said

According to Obi, the trip involved discussions about Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and the state of democracy. He said he neither sought nor announced any endorsement.

He said he met serving and former US lawmakers and other stakeholders during the visit.

How the confusion started

The confusion followed a viral video in which retired US Army Colonel Albert Short praised Obi.

Some social media posts presented those personal remarks as if they were official backing from the US Congress, and the claim spread quickly online.

What the facts show

There is no public evidence that the US Congress as an institution has endorsed Obi's 2027 presidential bid. Congress does not endorse candidates in other countries' elections.

A meeting with politicians, or kind words from an individual, is not the same as an endorsement from their institution.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections drawing closer, claims about foreign support for candidates are likely to keep spreading on social media. Obi's correction is a reminder for supporters on all sides to check claims before sharing them.

DSS says it found no evidence it planned to disrupt ADC campaign in Sokoto, asks Atiku to retract or provide proof

18 Nov 2026, 07:57 GMT

The Department of State Services says its internal investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off in Sokoto. It has asked Atiku Abubakar to retract or clarify his allegation, or provide evidence.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) says its investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential campaign in Sokoto.

How it started

Earlier this month, former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar accused the leadership of the DSS of recruiting protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto.

The allegation drew wide attention, and the DSS responded by ordering an investigation into the claim.

The DSS findings

The agency now says its investigation found nothing to support the allegation that it planned to recruit protesters or disrupt the ADC campaign.

It has asked Atiku to either retract or clarify his claim, or to provide evidence that could support further investigation.

Not the final word

It is important to note that this is the DSS's own preliminary finding. It was not an independent investigation, which means the agency effectively investigated an allegation made against itself.

Atiku has not issued a fresh public response to the DSS findings.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections approaching, allegations that security agencies are being used against opposition parties are likely to keep coming up. Public trust depends on such claims being properly examined, whether they turn out to be true or not.

FIFA appoints normalisation committee to run NFF, names Amokachi and Enakhena

19 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

FIFA has named lawyer Sampson Ebomhe to chair a normalisation committee for the Nigeria Football Federation ahead of its 2027 elections. Daniel Amokachi, Godwin Enakhena, Amina Yerima and Joe Abah are also members.

Read more

FIFA has set up a normalisation committee to run the Nigeria Football Federation (NFF), appointing lawyer Sampson Ebomhe as chairman with immediate effect.

The members

The world governing body named four other members: sports journalist and administrator Godwin Enakhena, former Super Eagles captain Daniel Amokachi, Amina Yerima and Dr Joe Abah, former Director-General of the Bureau of Public Service Reforms.

FIFA's Chief Member Associations Officer, Elkhan Mammadov, confirmed the appointments in a letter, noting that all members are still subject to an eligibility check by the FIFA Review Committee before they are formally confirmed.

Background

Amokachi, nicknamed The Bull, played at two World Cups for Nigeria and is one of the country's best-known football figures.
