APC chairman says Tinubu's dancing and busy campaign schedule show he is fit

10 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

The national chairman of the All Progressives Congress (APC), Nentawe Yilwatda, has defended President Bola Tinubu's physical fitness for a second term.

What he said

Speaking on a television politics programme on Monday, 9 November 2026, Yilwatda pointed to the President's busy weekend in Akwa Ibom and Rivers states as evidence of his health. He said he was there in person when Tinubu danced to local music in Akwa Ibom, and that the President attended a dinner and town hall meeting late on Friday before travelling to Rivers the next morning. He argued that someone who was not fit could not manage 48 hours of non-stop engagements and travel.

Background

Critics have raised questions about the President's health since before the 2023 election, and Obi has previously criticised the time Tinubu spends abroad. His supporters say he is fully fit and point to his campaign tour, which this week also includes Ebonyi and Jigawa states.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso visit Yelewata community in Benue State, offer support to vulnerable children

11 Nov 2026, 19:53 GMT

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have visited Yelewata in Benue State, where Obi laid a wreath at the memorial to victims of the community's deadly 2025 attack. The two leaders donated ₦10 million for vulnerable children and promised a motorised borehole.

Read more

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have visited Yelewata community in Benue State, bringing some hope to residents who have endured years of hardship and neglect.

A moment of remembrance

Yelewata was the scene of a deadly attack in June 2025 that left many residents dead and shocked the nation. During the visit, Obi knelt to lay a wreath at the memorial bearing the names of the victims, as a large crowd looked on.

Donations and promises

The two political leaders promised to provide a motorised borehole to help address the community's water needs. They also donated ₦10 million to support vulnerable children, while Professor Shija contributed another ₦2 million towards the education of orphans.

The people speak

Beyond the donations, the people of Yelewata had their own stories to tell. They spoke about years of neglect, the difficulties they face every day and the lack of meaningful government support.

Imagine living for years without some of the basic things that should make life easier, while those elected to serve appear distant from your suffering. For residents, the visit was a chance to make their voices heard and draw attention to their situation.

Action, not just visits

Promises must be followed by action. The community needs lasting solutions, not just occasional visits.

Leadership should be about compassion, listening to the people and doing something practical to improve their lives.

Police say they raided 'phone farms' used to sway online debate ahead of 2027 polls

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

The police say they have raided so-called phone farms allegedly used to manipulate online discussion before the 2027 elections, and have arrested a suspect accused of spreading false reports about a South-South candidate.

Read more

The Nigeria Police Force says it has raided facilities allegedly used to manipulate online conversations ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What the police said

Speaking on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, at a webinar on AI, misinformation and the 2027 elections, Assistant Commissioner of Police Bashir Abdullahi, who represented the Inspector-General of Police, said officers had carried out targeted raids on so-called phone farms after receiving information about a coordinated effort to interfere with the polls. He explained that a single operator can use hundreds of phones and automated accounts to push the same message, creating the impression that many people are independently discussing an issue. He said the raids had disrupted the operation.

Suspect arrested

Abdullahi said a suspect was in custody after officers traced allegedly false reports about a candidate from the South-South. He said the reports were allegedly meant to discredit the candidate and influence voters. The suspect has not been named or charged in court.

Free speech

He said the police would prosecute people found to have spread false information capable of undermining the elections, but stressed that enforcement would be guided by the law and that the force would not interfere with legitimate public debate. He also said the police had acquired tools that can detect deepfake videos, altered photos and cloned voices.

Background

At the same event, INEC said its own staff in about 10 states had uploaded AI-generated photos to the voters' register. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

INEC says its staff in about 10 states uploaded AI photos to voters' register

11 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

INEC says it has identified its own officials in about 10 states who uploaded AI-generated photographs to the voters' register. The images have been removed and the commission says the staff involved will face disciplinary action.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) says it has identified officials in about 10 states who uploaded photographs generated by artificial intelligence to Nigeria's voters' register.

What INEC said

Speaking on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, at a webinar on AI, misinformation and the 2027 elections, INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, who chairs the commission's Information and Voter Education Committee, said a large number of AI-generated pictures had been posted onto the register and had since been taken down. He said the commission had the names of the staff involved across about 10 states and would take disciplinary action against them.

Limits on what INEC can do

Haruna said it could be difficult for the commission to stop members of the public from digitally altering their own photographs before submitting them, but that INEC could act directly where its own staff were responsible.

Background

The issue of AI-generated images on the register had already drawn public attention, raising concerns about the accuracy of the voter roll ahead of the general elections. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Uzodimma insists he will keep Imo West Senate ticket despite court ruling

11 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Imo governor Hope Uzodimma says he will not give up the APC ticket for Imo West senatorial district, despite a Federal High Court ruling that named his predecessor Rochas Okorocha as the party's candidate.

Read more

Imo State governor Hope Uzodimma has vowed to continue his bid for the Imo West Senate seat, despite a court judgment that removed him as the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate.

What he said

Speaking after Mass at the Government House chapel in Owerri, reported on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, Uzodimma dismissed suggestions that he would surrender the ticket, telling supporters that anyone who says someone else will take it from him is lying. He urged residents to campaign for APC candidates in state and federal races, naming the party's candidates for the Okigwe and Owerri zones, and continued to present himself as the party's candidate for Orlu, the Imo West zone.

The court ruling

A Federal High Court recently ruled that former governor Rochas Okorocha, not Uzodimma, is the APC's rightful candidate for Imo West. Okorocha, who represented the district in the Senate from 2019 to 2023, was congratulated on the ruling by former APC chairman Abdullahi Ganduje.

Background

Uzodimma, who is serving his second term as governor and cannot run again, is a key ally of President Bola Tinubu in the South-East. FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has denied having any role in the judgment.
