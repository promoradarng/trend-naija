Nigerian-American ex-police officer Sinmi Asomuyide jailed 40 years for sexually assaulting 14-year-old girl he was sent to find

14 Nov 2026, 13:26 GMT

He was sent to help bring a missing 14-year-old girl home safely. Instead, he became the person who harmed her.

Sinmi Asomuyide, a 33-year-old Nigerian-American former police officer in Kokomo, Indiana, has been sentenced to 40 years in a US federal prison for sexually assaulting the teenager. He will also serve three years of supervised release after his prison term.

How it happened

On 30 June 2024, the girl's father called the emergency line to report her missing as a runaway. Asomuyide, then on patrol with the Kokomo Police Department, was one of the officers who found her at the home of another underage girl.

While driving her to a youth centre, he switched off his body camera, took her phone and used it to call his own private number. He told her he would contact her later to return an item he had found on her.

Later that evening, after his shift, he went to where she was staying, picked her up while still in uniform, drove her to an empty warehouse car park and sexually assaulted her, according to court documents and GPS data from his car.

The evidence that exposed him

Asomuyide denied everything. When state police questioned him about a week later, he claimed he had never given the girl his number and that she had contacted him on a work phone, even though Kokomo officers are not issued work phones.

Within hours of the assault, he had also searched online for how to delete the messaging account he used to contact her.

But the evidence caught up with him. Investigators examining his patrol car found a stain on the centre console that matched his DNA.

In June 2026, a federal jury found him guilty after a five-day trial of violating the girl's civil rights by sexually assaulting her while on duty, witness tampering by lying to investigators, and destroying records. The judge handed down the 40-year sentence this month.

An earlier warning sign

Even more disturbing, investigators found that this was not the first allegation against him.

Before joining the Kokomo police, Asomuyide worked with the Portsmouth Police Department in Virginia. He resigned there after an allegation that he had sexual intercourse with a 15-year-old girl while off duty. When he applied to Kokomo, he gave a false account of why he had left.

Sworn to protect

The case has shocked many, especially Nigerians at home and abroad. A police officer is trusted to protect the most vulnerable, and a missing child should be safest in the hands of the officer sent to find her.

It also raises hard questions about how a man with a previous allegation involving a minor was able to get another job in law enforcement.

Atiku denies quitting 2027 race, says those spreading the claim are working for Tinubu

22 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has reportedly dismissed claims that he has withdrawn from the 2027 election. His campaign said no group calling itself his supporters can speak for him, and accused rivals of tactics that help the ruling party.

Read more

Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has reportedly rejected claims that he has pulled out of the 2027 presidential race, describing them as a deliberate falsehood.

What his campaign said

In a statement issued on Sunday, 22 November 2026, by the director of strategic communication for the ADC Presidential Campaign Council, the campaign is said to have stressed that no group presenting itself as Atiku's supporters had the authority to announce a withdrawal on his behalf.

The statement reportedly accused those behind the claim of borrowing tactics it associated with the ruling APC, such as presenting so-called supporters from a rival camp. It said any opposition party spreading such stories was effectively helping President Bola Tinubu, and urged parties that want to defeat the APC to make their case directly to voters.

Still campaigning

Atiku's campaign said his Affordable Life campaign was still active and focused on the election.

Background

The denial comes as campaigns for the January 2027 presidential election gather pace, with Tinubu of the APC, Atiku of the ADC and Peter Obi of the NDC among the leading candidates. Speculation about possible withdrawals and alliances has been circulating online in recent days.

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has reportedly warned that rejecting President Bola Tinubu's re-election bid in 2027 would leave the government's economic reforms unfinished and could undo gains already made.

Where he spoke

Wike made the remarks on Sunday, 22 November 2026, at a thanksgiving and dedication service at St Peter's Anglican Church in Rumueprikom, his hometown in Obio/Akpor Local Government Area of Rivers State. The service formally dedicated the Rainbow Coalition Campaign Council ahead of the elections, and Wike said campaigning would begin on 23 November.

His argument

He reportedly described Tinubu as a leader who took unpopular but necessary decisions to stop the country from sliding into crisis. As an example, he pointed to the university system, saying the repeated cycle of lecturers' strikes over unpaid entitlements had largely been broken.

Background

Wike, a former PDP governor of Rivers State, is one of Tinubu's most vocal backers and leads a coalition of PDP members supporting the president. Earlier this month, he told his FCT campaign team not to let opposition parties reach 25 per cent of the vote in Abuja.
