Nigerian-American ex-police officer Sinmi Asomuyide jailed 40 years for sexually assaulting 14-year-old girl he was sent to find

14 Nov 2026, 13:26 GMT

He was sent to help bring a missing 14-year-old girl home safely. Instead, he became the person who harmed her.

Sinmi Asomuyide, a 33-year-old Nigerian-American former police officer in Kokomo, Indiana, has been sentenced to 40 years in a US federal prison for sexually assaulting the teenager. He will also serve three years of supervised release after his prison term.

How it happened

On 30 June 2024, the girl's father called the emergency line to report her missing as a runaway. Asomuyide, then on patrol with the Kokomo Police Department, was one of the officers who found her at the home of another underage girl.

While driving her to a youth centre, he switched off his body camera, took her phone and used it to call his own private number. He told her he would contact her later to return an item he had found on her.

Later that evening, after his shift, he went to where she was staying, picked her up while still in uniform, drove her to an empty warehouse car park and sexually assaulted her, according to court documents and GPS data from his car.

The evidence that exposed him

Asomuyide denied everything. When state police questioned him about a week later, he claimed he had never given the girl his number and that she had contacted him on a work phone, even though Kokomo officers are not issued work phones.

Within hours of the assault, he had also searched online for how to delete the messaging account he used to contact her.

But the evidence caught up with him. Investigators examining his patrol car found a stain on the centre console that matched his DNA.

In June 2026, a federal jury found him guilty after a five-day trial of violating the girl's civil rights by sexually assaulting her while on duty, witness tampering by lying to investigators, and destroying records. The judge handed down the 40-year sentence this month.

An earlier warning sign

Even more disturbing, investigators found that this was not the first allegation against him.

Before joining the Kokomo police, Asomuyide worked with the Portsmouth Police Department in Virginia. He resigned there after an allegation that he had sexual intercourse with a 15-year-old girl while off duty. When he applied to Kokomo, he gave a false account of why he had left.

Sworn to protect

The case has shocked many, especially Nigerians at home and abroad. A police officer is trusted to protect the most vulnerable, and a missing child should be safest in the hands of the officer sent to find her.

It also raises hard questions about how a man with a previous allegation involving a minor was able to get another job in law enforcement.

Zelensky says Ukrainian sea drones sank a Russian warship in Libya

23 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Ukraine says its naval drones sank a Russian corvette docked in Benghazi, Libya, and hit a Russian cargo ship off North Africa. President Zelensky released video of the operation on Monday.

Read more

Ukrainian naval drones have reportedly sunk a modern Russian warship docked in the eastern Libyan port of Benghazi and struck a Russian cargo ship off the North African coast, President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Monday, 23 November 2026.

The operation

In a statement on Telegram, released alongside video footage, Zelensky said Ukrainian forces used seven domestically made unmanned surface vessels to attack the corvette Merkury. The ship, commissioned in May 2023 and built with radar-evading features, caught fire and sank, and a combat helicopter on board was destroyed. Satellite images later reportedly showed the vessel capsized and mostly underwater beside the pier.

Zelensky described it as an exemplary operation in response to Russia's war and thanked Ukraine's Security Service and defence forces, saying the targets set had been achieved.

Why it matters

The strike marks a major expansion of Ukraine's attacks at sea, far beyond the Black Sea, where its drones have previously targeted Russian ships.

Russia's response

No response from Moscow was included in the initial reports.

Paolo Bulgari, jeweller to royals and film stars, dies at 89

22 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Paolo Bulgari, the longtime chairman of the Bvlgari luxury group, has died in Rome at the age of 89. His family called him the greatest jeweller of our times.

Read more

Italian jeweller Paolo Bulgari, who helped turn his family's Roman workshop into a global luxury brand, has died in Rome at the age of 89.

The announcement

His family announced his death on Saturday, 21 November 2026, describing him as an extraordinary entrepreneur who promoted Italian craftsmanship around the world. The cause of death was not disclosed. The Bvlgari brand also paid tribute to its former chairman.

His life and work

Born in Rome in 1937, Bulgari joined the family firm in 1960 and became known as one of the leading experts on precious gemstones. He became chairman in 1984. The house was founded in 1884 by his grandfather, a Greek silversmith who settled in Rome.

Under Paolo and his brothers, the brand became known for bold designs, colourful gems and yellow gold, opened shops in Paris, New York and Geneva, and expanded into watches, perfumes, leather goods and hotels.

Famous clients

During Rome's glamorous era of the 1950s and 1960s, the brand's flagship store became a favourite of royalty and film stars, including Sophia Loren and Audrey Hepburn.

CAS to rule on Tuesday on who won AFCON 2025, Senegal or Morocco

23 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Court of Arbitration for Sport will announce its decision on the disputed 2025 Africa Cup of Nations final on Tuesday, 24 November. Senegal won the match on the pitch, but CAF later handed the title to Morocco.

Read more

The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) will deliver its long-awaited ruling on the disputed 2025 Africa Cup of Nations final between Senegal and Morocco on Tuesday, 24 November 2026.

The announcement

CAS confirmed on Monday that all the parties had been notified of the decision date. The ruling is expected on Tuesday afternoon and will settle which country is officially recognised as champion. The court reminded the public that only information released through its own channels should be treated as official.

How the dispute started

The final was played in Rabat on 18 January 2026. Senegal beat hosts Morocco 1-0 after extra time, with Pape Gueye scoring the winner. But the match was marred by chaotic scenes when Senegal's players briefly walked off the pitch in protest at a late penalty awarded to Morocco. When play resumed after about 15 minutes, Brahim Diaz's attempted Panenka penalty was saved by Senegal goalkeeper Edouard Mendy.

Title stripped

On 17 March, CAF's Appeal Board overturned the result, ruling that Senegal had forfeited the match by leaving the pitch, and awarded the title to Morocco. The Senegalese Football Federation rejected the decision and took the case to CAS, naming CAF and the Royal Moroccan Football Federation as respondents.

What happens next

Tuesday's decision is final at the sporting level and will either restore Senegal's title or confirm Morocco as champions.
