Shettima says Peter Obi has run himself out of the 2027 race

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Vice-President Kashim Shettima has said that Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has ruined his own chances in the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at an All Progressives Congress (APC) campaign rally in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State, on Friday, 6 November 2026, Shettima reportedly said Obi had run himself out of the race through what he called lies and bogus statistics. He suggested that Obi had recently travelled abroad quietly before returning to Nigeria, and that he had thrown away the goodwill he once enjoyed among voters. The vice-president also mocked Obi's frequent comparisons between Nigeria and Bangladesh, saying a shirt made for one country cannot fit another, and dismissed Obi's claim that he won the 2023 presidential election.

Tinubu in Akwa Ibom

Before the rally, President Bola Tinubu inaugurated several projects built by Governor Umo Eno's administration, including the new terminal building at Victor Attah International Airport in Uyo. Tinubu described the visit as a homecoming, praised Eno as a visionary leader, and said the airport's international flight status was the result of the governor's commitment to the state. The President also took a swipe at Obi at the event over erosion in Anambra State.

Background

Obi, a former Anambra governor, came third in the 2023 presidential election and challenged the result in court, insisting he won. He is running again in 2027 with Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027. Obi's campaign had not publicly responded to Shettima's remarks at the time of writing.

Presidential election turnout has fallen from 69% to 27% since 2003

28 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has dropped from a Fourth Republic high of about 69% in 2003 to under 27% in 2023, according to figures compiled by a statistics platform. The decline has continued for five straight elections.

Read more

Voter turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has fallen sharply over the past two decades, according to figures shared by a statistics platform this week.

The numbers

Measured as total votes cast as a share of registered voters, turnout reached a Fourth Republic high of 69.08% in 2003. It then fell to 57.49% in 2007 and 53.68% in 2011, and continued to decline in each election after that, reaching just 26.71% in 2023. That is a drop of more than 42 percentage points in 20 years.

Why it matters

The figures come as parties and INEC step up efforts to get Nigerians to collect their Permanent Voter Cards and vote in the 16 January 2027 elections. INEC is also running a nationwide mock accreditation exercise on Saturday to test its systems.

Background

Concerns about insecurity, logistics and confidence in the electoral process have often been cited as reasons for low turnout. INEC this week named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence.

OK Movement withdraws support from Obi and the NDC

27 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

The OK Movement, named after Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso, has reportedly pulled its campaign support from the NDC. Its director-general said the group had been told Obi and the party had chosen to cut ties with it.

Read more

The OK Movement, a support group named after the initials of NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi and his running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso, has reportedly withdrawn its campaign and mobilisation support from the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC).

The announcement

In a statement on Friday, 27 November 2026, the movement's national director-general, John Ughulu, said the group had been informed that Obi and the NDC had decided to cut ties with it. He directed the movement's ward, local government and state structures to stop all involvement in NDC activities immediately.

What next

Ughulu said the group had begun discussions with other political parties and presidential aspirants.

Background

The development comes days before Obi is due to attend an opposition summit in Abuja with Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde.

The other side

No response from Obi or the NDC was included in the reports.

NDC responds

The NDC dismissed the withdrawal as a calculated distraction that would not slow its campaign. The director-general of its national campaign council said the move followed a decision already announced by Obi and the party, linked to a row over a parallel campaign structure.

Fashola says Tinubu stands out because he has kept his allies for 16 years

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Former Lagos Governor Babatunde Fashola has argued that President Tinubu differs from Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi because he has kept the loyalty of his political allies for about 16 years. He said even former opponents now work with him.

Read more

Former Lagos State Governor Babatunde Fashola has made a case for President Bola Tinubu's re-election, arguing that he stands apart from his main rivals by keeping his political allies for about 16 years.

The comparison

Speaking in a television interview on Friday, 27 November 2026, Fashola noted that Tinubu, Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi all began their political rise as state governors, although Atiku was elected governor but did not serve. He framed his argument as a job reference question, saying that Tinubu's long-time allies have stayed with him.

He also said that even some of the president's fiercest former opponents now work with him.

Background

Fashola, who succeeded Tinubu as Lagos governor and later served as a federal minister, also condemned the 'rig and die' slogan in the same interview. Atiku and Obi are expected to sign an opposition cooperation pact at a summit in Abuja on Monday.

The other side

No response from the Atiku or Obi campaigns was included in the reports.

Lagos in 2023

Fashola also admitted that the APC was caught off guard when Peter Obi won Lagos in the 2023 presidential election, saying internal surveys had not flagged the state as one to defend hard, and vowed that the party would not let it happen again.
