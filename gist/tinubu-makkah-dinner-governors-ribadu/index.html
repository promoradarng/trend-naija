Tinubu hosts governors, Ribadu and Gbajabiamila at dinner in Makkah

2 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has hosted senior government officials, state governors and business leaders at a dinner in Makkah, Saudi Arabia, during his Umrah pilgrimage.

Who attended

According to a statement by the President's adviser on information and strategy, Bayo Onanuga, on Sunday, 1 November 2026, those at the dinner included Yobe Governor Mai Mala Buni, Kaduna Governor Uba Sani, Jigawa Governor Mohammed Namadi, Sokoto Governor Ahmed Aliyu and Borno Governor Babagana Zulum. Also present were National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu, Chief of Staff Femi Gbajabiamila, Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate, political adviser Ibrahim Masari and Oando chief executive Wale Tinubu. Kwara Governor Abdulrahman AbdulRazaq, BUA chairman Abdul Samad Rabiu and Attorney-General Lateef Fagbemi also spoke at the event.

What the President said

Tinubu reportedly prayed for peace, unity and prosperity in Nigeria and urged Nigerians to see the country's ethnic, religious and cultural diversity as a strength. He said Nigeria's people and natural resources provide a foundation for an inclusive and prosperous nation, and asked God to turn the country's diversity into prosperity. Guests offered prayers for the President and the country.

Background

Tinubu travelled to Saudi Arabia on 29 October for the Umrah pilgrimage. The trip has been criticised by opposition figures, including Atiku Abubakar, who claimed it was a cover for travel to France, and activist Omoyele Sowore, who claimed it was for medical treatment. The Presidency has rejected both claims. It says the President is expected back in Nigeria this week.

How people reacted

Supporters shared photos of the dinner as evidence of the President's good health, while critics questioned the cost of taking a large delegation abroad.

Oyo commissioner and two other officials resign from Makinde government in one day

3 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Oyo's Commissioner for Budget and Economic Planning, a special adviser and an electoral commission member reportedly resigned on Monday. One said he was no longer comfortable with the working environment, adding to a wave of departures before 2027.

Read more

Three officials serving in Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde's administration resigned on Monday, 2 November 2026, according to reports, in the latest of a series of departures from the state government.

Who resigned

Those who left were reportedly the Commissioner for Budget and Economic Planning, Ayobami Ojo; the Special Adviser on Budget and Economic Planning, Adegbola Oyeleke; and Sunday Falana, a member of the Oyo State Independent Electoral Commission. Ojo, who is from Ago-Are in Atisbo Local Government Area, submitted his letter through the Secretary to the State Government, said his exit was with immediate effect and thanked the governor for the opportunity to serve, without giving a reason. Oyeleke reportedly said he was no longer comfortable with the working environment in the administration.

Background

Makinde is serving his second and final term as governor and is now the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) for 2027. Several officials have left his government in recent months as politicians reposition ahead of the elections, with some moving to rival parties. The governor has not publicly commented on the latest resignations.

Why it matters

The departures come as Makinde campaigns nationally, including in the South East, and as parties compete for control of Oyo State, where the governorship seat will be open in 2027.

Deposed Ipetumodu monarch sues Adeleke to stop appointment of successor

3 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Oba Joseph Oloyede, removed as Apetumodu of Ipetumodu after his conviction in the US for fraud, has reportedly asked an Osun High Court to stop Governor Ademola Adeleke from filling the stool. The case was adjourned to 21 January 2027.

Read more

The deposed Apetumodu of Ipetumodu in Osun State, Oba Joseph Oloyede, has gone to court to stop Governor Ademola Adeleke from appointing a new traditional ruler for the town.

The case

According to reports, Oloyede filed a motion at the Osun State High Court in Ile-Ife asking the court to restrain the governor and other officials from filling the stool until his main case is decided. In the application, dated 16 September 2026, he argued that he was not formally told of any wrongdoing before he was removed, and challenged the legality of his deposition. Members of a branch of the town's ruling house joined him as claimants. The defendants include the governor, the state Attorney-General, the Commissioner for Local Government and Chieftaincy Affairs, local chiefs and Ife North Local Government.

What happened in court

Proceedings stalled on Monday, 2 November, after some defendants said they had not received hearing notices. Justice Dapo Adeniji adjourned the case to 21 January 2027 and ordered that fresh notices be served.

Background

Oloyede was removed from the throne after he was convicted in the United States on charges related to tax fraud and money laundering. Governor Adeleke reportedly signed the order removing him in May 2026.

What happens next

The court will hear the case in January. Until then, it is not clear whether the state will go ahead with appointing a new monarch.

President Tinubu seen kissing the sacred Black Stone during Umrah in Makkah

2 Nov 2026, 20:39 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has been seen kissing the Black Stone, known as Al-Hajar Al-Aswad, while performing Umrah in Makkah, Saudi Arabia. Photos show him at the eastern corner of the Kaaba, surrounded by security officials.

Read more

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has been seen kissing the sacred Black Stone, known as Al-Hajar Al-Aswad, while performing Umrah in the holy city of Makkah, Saudi Arabia.

Photos from the visit show the President, dressed in white, leaning into the silver frame that holds the stone as Saudi security officers stand by. Another photo shows him with members of his delegation in the courtyard of the Grand Mosque.

About the Black Stone

The Black Stone sits at the eastern corner of the Kaaba, inside the Grand Mosque, and holds deep religious significance for Muslims around the world.

Kissing the stone is a practice associated with Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), and many pilgrims seek the chance to do so whenever they visit the holy site.

A spiritual journey

For Muslims, Umrah is a spiritual journey marked by prayer, worship and personal reflection.

The moment has also drawn attention to the religious side of Nigeria's leader as he performs one of Islam's important acts of worship.

National Assembly considering 74 extra seats reserved for women, says Senate leader

2 Nov 2026, 18:01 GMT

Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele says lawmakers are considering a constitutional amendment to add 37 women-only seats each to the Senate and House of Representatives. The arrangement would last 16 years before a review.

Read more

The National Assembly is considering a constitutional amendment that would create 74 new legislative seats reserved for women, Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele has said.

The proposal

Speaking on Monday, 2 November 2026, at a Law Week event organised by the Ekiti State branch of the International Federation of Women Lawyers, Bamidele reportedly said the plan would add 37 seats in the Senate and 37 in the House of Representatives, giving each state and the Federal Capital Territory one extra women-only seat in each chamber. He said the constitution review committee has also proposed three extra seats for women in each state house of assembly, and stressed that the new seats would be added to the existing ones rather than replacing any.

How long it would last

Bamidele, who is vice-chairman of the Senate committee reviewing the 1999 Constitution, said the arrangement would run for 16 years, or four election cycles, before a full review. He described this as a sunset clause meant to allow a more inclusive political culture to develop.

What happens next

Constitutional amendments need the approval of two-thirds of members of both chambers of the National Assembly and of at least 24 of the 36 state houses of assembly. Bamidele said the National Assembly was engaging state lawmakers to secure their support.

Background

Women hold only a small share of seats in Nigeria's National Assembly, one of the lowest rates in Africa. A similar bill on special seats for women failed in 2022, which led to protests by women's groups. Supporters say reserved seats would give women a stronger voice in law-making, while critics argue they would add to the cost of governance.
