Tinubu says naira would have been worthless without his reforms, rules out subsidy return

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has defended his removal of the fuel subsidy, saying the naira would have become worthless if his government had kept it.

What he said

Speaking at a state reception in his honour in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State, on Friday, 6 November 2026, after an APC rally at the Godswill Akpabio International Stadium, Tinubu said 27 of the 36 state governors could not pay salaries when he took office, let alone pensions and gratuities. He argued that his reforms had moved the country away from the risk of bankruptcy, and said that without them Nigerians would by now need wheelbarrows to carry their money. He put the exchange rate at about ₦1,300 to the US dollar and said Nigeria would never go back to the subsidy.

The visit

The reception followed a day in which the President inaugurated several projects in Akwa Ibom, including a new terminal at Victor Attah International Airport, and approved a request by Governor Umo Eno for the Ibom Deep Seaport. Vice-President Kashim Shettima used the same visit to criticise NDC candidate Peter Obi.

Background

Tinubu announced the end of the petrol subsidy in his inauguration speech in May 2023, and his government also unified the exchange rate. The reforms led to sharp rises in fuel and food prices. Opposition candidates, including Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi, have criticised the way the reforms were carried out, while the government says they were needed to save the economy and has introduced relief measures such as the ₦50,000 monthly grant to selected households.

Why it matters

The cost of living is expected to be one of the main issues in the January 2027 election.

  • Tinubu says Nigeria will never return to the subsidy.
  • He says 27 states could not pay salaries in 2023.
  • He spoke at a reception in Uyo.

Nigeria and South Africa hold talks after Fani-Kayode diplomatic row

17 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nigeria and South Africa have held talks in Pretoria over tensions following a public dispute involving Nigeria's ambassador, Femi Fani-Kayode. South Africa had summoned the envoy over his conduct after he presented his credentials.

Read more

Nigeria and South Africa have held talks to ease tensions in their relationship, days after a public dispute involving Nigeria's ambassador to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, and officials of the South African government.

The summons

South Africa's Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) summoned the envoy over his conduct following the presentation of his credentials. The row followed Fani-Kayode's complaint about being left out of a presidential livestream, which drew a response from the South African presidency.

The meeting

Fani-Kayode shared the outcome of the meeting on his X account, posting a clip of himself at DIRCO's head office in Pretoria. He said both sides raised concerns about the state of relations and the events that sparked the controversy.

He said the two countries look forward to building on the meeting and strengthening their long-standing bilateral ties.

Senate Chief Whip Mohammed Tahir Monguno, who represents Borno North, has reportedly escaped a Boko Haram attack during a 2027 campaign tour of Kukawa Local Government Area of Borno State on Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

What happened

Monguno is said to have travelled to Kukawa with former governor and senator Maina Maaji Lawan and other APC figures to campaign when the insurgents tried to overrun the area. Troops of Operation Hadin Kai, acting on intelligence, reportedly moved quickly to repel the attackers.

The campaign tour

The APC entourage had earlier gathered in Monguno town on Sunday, where the senator joined Governor Babagana Zulum for the launch of the party's campaign in the area. The governor returned to Maiduguri by helicopter on Monday, while Monguno and others continued to Kukawa.

The chairman of the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Gombe State, Mohammed Ndus, and the director-general of the party's 2027 campaign council, Sa'adu Hassan, have reportedly been injured in a road accident.

What happened

The two officials were travelling from Gombe to join Governor Muhammadu Inuwa Yahaya's convoy for a governorship campaign rally in Balanga North when the crash happened on Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

Ndus reportedly suffered serious injuries, while Hassan had minor ones. Both were taken to the Federal Teaching Hospital in Gombe for treatment.

Official response

The Federal Road Safety Corps sector commander in Gombe, Samson Kaura, confirmed receiving information about the crash, but said he was still working to establish the full circumstances.

ADC files 17-ground appeal against judgment that sacked its leadership

17 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The ADC has appealed against Justice Peter Lifu's 5 November judgment on its leadership, raising 17 grounds. It wants the Court of Appeal to stop INEC from acting on the ruling while the appeal is pending.

Read more

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has reportedly filed an appeal against the judgment delivered by Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court on 5 November 2026, which affected the party's leadership.

The appeal

The party's Director of Strategic Communication for its presidential campaign, Phrank Shaibu, said in a statement on Tuesday, 17 November, that the appeal raises 17 grounds covering the basis, findings and orders of the judgment. These include whether the trial court had the power to hear what the party considers an internal leadership matter.

The injunction request

The ADC is also asking the Court of Appeal to stop the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) from changing its leadership records or its candidates' details while the appeal is pending.

Background

The ADC is fielding former Vice President Atiku Abubakar as its presidential candidate for 2027. The party also filed a separate brief against an earlier ruling made in July.
