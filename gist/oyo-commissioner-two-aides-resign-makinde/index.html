Oyo commissioner and two other officials resign from Makinde government in one day

3 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Three officials serving in Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde's administration resigned on Monday, 2 November 2026, according to reports, in the latest of a series of departures from the state government.

Who resigned

Those who left were reportedly the Commissioner for Budget and Economic Planning, Ayobami Ojo; the Special Adviser on Budget and Economic Planning, Adegbola Oyeleke; and Sunday Falana, a member of the Oyo State Independent Electoral Commission. Ojo, who is from Ago-Are in Atisbo Local Government Area, submitted his letter through the Secretary to the State Government, said his exit was with immediate effect and thanked the governor for the opportunity to serve, without giving a reason. Oyeleke reportedly said he was no longer comfortable with the working environment in the administration.

Background

Makinde is serving his second and final term as governor and is now the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) for 2027. Several officials have left his government in recent months as politicians reposition ahead of the elections, with some moving to rival parties. The governor has not publicly commented on the latest resignations.

Why it matters

The departures come as Makinde campaigns nationally, including in the South East, and as parties compete for control of Oyo State, where the governorship seat will be open in 2027.

  • The budget commissioner, a special adviser and an OYSIEC member resigned.
  • One official cited the working environment.
  • Makinde has not commented.

Obi and NDC supporters recite old national anthem instead of new one at Lagos town hall

13 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

A video from the NDC town hall in Lagos shows Peter Obi and Lagos governorship candidate Funso Doherty leading supporters in reciting the old national anthem, 'Arise O Compatriots', instead of the new one adopted in 2024.

Read more

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has drawn attention after reportedly leading supporters in reciting Nigeria's former national anthem rather than the current one.

What happened

A video from the party's town hall meeting in Oregun, Lagos, on Thursday, 12 November 2026, which went viral afterwards, shows Obi and the NDC's Lagos governorship candidate, Funso Doherty, choosing not to recite the current anthem when attendees were asked to stand for it. Instead, they and the crowd recited the old anthem, 'Arise O Compatriots', together.

The anthem change

Nigeria replaced 'Arise O Compatriots', which had been in use since 1978, with its original post-independence anthem, 'Nigeria We Hail Thee', in May 2024. President Bola Tinubu signed the change into law after the bill passed quickly through the National Assembly. The speed of the change drew criticism at the time.

Response

Neither Obi's campaign nor the APC had commented on the video at the time of writing.

Background

The same town hall meeting was disrupted earlier in the day by suspected thugs, before it went ahead. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Petrol depot prices fall to about ₦1,210 per litre in Lagos, diesel also drops

13 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Average depot prices of petrol in Lagos have reportedly fallen by ₦29 to about ₦1,210 per litre, with diesel also cheaper, raising hopes of further cuts at filling stations.

Read more

Depot prices of petrol and diesel have fallen further in Lagos, according to market data.

The new prices

Figures published on Thursday, 12 November 2026, show that the average depot price of petrol dropped by ₦29 per litre, from ₦1,239 to ₦1,210, a decline of about 2.3 per cent. Some depots were reportedly selling at around ₦1,200. The average price of diesel also fell, to about ₦1,688 per litre.

Why it matters

Depot prices are what marketers pay before selling to motorists, so lower wholesale prices could lead to cheaper petrol at filling stations in the coming days. The cuts came despite a rebound in global crude oil prices linked to renewed tension between the United States and Iran.

Background

Petrol prices have been easing in recent days. Dangote Refinery reportedly cut its loading price to ₦1,250 per litre after switching to a pricing system linked to global petrol prices, and MRS filling stations in Abuja reduced their pump price to ₦1,399 per litre.
