"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum": Son of second wife accused of mistreating Mrs Anulika in Ukehe speaks out

10 Nov 2026, 10:13 GMT

The son of the second wife at the centre of the viral Ukehe controversy has spoken out, begging Nigerians to stop attacking his mother.

"Is it not enough already?"

"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum. Is it not enough already? That woman does not deserve all this hate," he said.

"Do you want her to die? If anything happens to my mum, you people won't like it. Please, this is enough."

How it started

His mother has faced a wave of criticism online since a video from his father's Ozo title-taking ceremony went viral. In the video, the second wife's children reportedly refused to spray money on the first wife, Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

Claims then spread online that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over being childless and had moved back to her father's home. Those claims have not been independently verified.

Since then, well-wishers have visited Mrs Anulika with gifts and money, and the Enugu governor's wife sent a representative with cash and promises of a house and a business.

The other side of the story

The son's plea is a reminder that there are always two sides to a family story, and that online anger can quickly turn into harassment.

Sympathy for Mrs Anulika is understandable. But insults and threats against another woman, whose side of the story has not been heard, help no one. Families caught up in viral moments are still real people.

The All Progressives Grand Alliance (APGA) has formally adopted President Bola Tinubu as its presidential candidate for the 2027 general elections.

The ceremony

Anambra State Governor and APGA leader Charles Soludo presided over a ceremony on Thursday, 19 November 2026, at which he presented the party's flag to Vice President Kashim Shettima, who received it on the President's behalf.

What it means

Tinubu is already the presidential candidate of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC). APGA's adoption means the party will back him rather than field its own candidate, marking a significant alliance between the party and the federal government.

Background

APGA controls Anambra State, the home state of NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi.

Anambra government says Peter Obi billboard was removed over unpaid signage fee

19 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Anambra State Government has confirmed it ordered the removal of a billboard featuring NDC candidate Peter Obi and Senator Victor Umeh. It said Umeh paid the fee but Obi did not.

Read more

The Anambra State Government has confirmed that it ordered the removal of a billboard showing Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC).

The government's explanation

The state government, led by Governor Charles Soludo, said the billboard was taken down because the required signage fee had not been paid for Obi's image.

The billboard belonged to Victor Umeh, the senator representing Anambra Central and the NDC candidate for the district, and featured photographs of both Umeh and Obi. According to the government, Umeh had paid the fee for his own image, but no fee had been paid for Obi's.

Context

The government's explanation came on the same day Governor Soludo's party, APGA, formally adopted President Bola Tinubu as its candidate for 2027. Obi is a former governor of Anambra State.

The other side

Reactions from Obi or the NDC to the government's explanation were not included in the reports.

ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has asked the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to make public all details of the artificial intelligence system it plans to use in the 2027 general elections.

The letter

According to his campaign's director of strategic communication, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku wrote directly to the INEC Chairman on behalf of the ADC and allied stakeholders. The statement was issued on Thursday, 19 November 2026.

He warned that Nigerians cannot be asked to trust a process they are not allowed to examine, and called for transparency about the technology, including its source code.

Background

The request follows INEC's announced plan to integrate AI and other intelligent systems into its election processes. AI has also become a flashpoint in the campaign, with the main parties accusing each other of using AI-generated images.
