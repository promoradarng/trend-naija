"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum": Son of second wife accused of mistreating Mrs Anulika in Ukehe speaks out

10 Nov 2026, 10:13 GMT

The son of the second wife at the centre of the viral Ukehe controversy has spoken out, begging Nigerians to stop attacking his mother.

"Is it not enough already?"

"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum. Is it not enough already? That woman does not deserve all this hate," he said.

"Do you want her to die? If anything happens to my mum, you people won't like it. Please, this is enough."

How it started

His mother has faced a wave of criticism online since a video from his father's Ozo title-taking ceremony went viral. In the video, the second wife's children reportedly refused to spray money on the first wife, Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

Claims then spread online that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over being childless and had moved back to her father's home. Those claims have not been independently verified.

Since then, well-wishers have visited Mrs Anulika with gifts and money, and the Enugu governor's wife sent a representative with cash and promises of a house and a business.

The other side of the story

The son's plea is a reminder that there are always two sides to a family story, and that online anger can quickly turn into harassment.

Sympathy for Mrs Anulika is understandable. But insults and threats against another woman, whose side of the story has not been heard, help no one. Families caught up in viral moments are still real people.

Delta man presumed dead after accident reportedly wakes up in mortuary

A man presumed dead after a motorcycle accident near Evwreni in Delta State reportedly woke up in a mortuary, sparking celebrations by residents in a video that has gone viral.

A man who was presumed dead after an accident in Delta State has reportedly woken up in a mortuary.

What happened

According to reports on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the man was involved in a motorcycle accident near Evwreni and was taken to a mortuary after being presumed dead. He is said to have later woken up there. A video shared online shows residents celebrating his survival. Voices in the video say that when he woke up, the first thing he asked for was a cigarette and a lighter.

Celebrations

Residents rubbed white powder on their faces and bodies, a traditional sign of celebration often used at a child's birth, and also applied it to the survivor. Others shared snacks with passers-by to mark the occasion, while some urged him to go for a proper medical check-up.

Background

It is not clear who declared the man dead or whether he was examined by a doctor before being taken to the mortuary. The authorities had not commented on the incident at the time of writing.

State police bill clears 32 state assemblies, heads for Tinubu's assent

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele says 32 state assemblies have approved the constitutional amendment to create state police, enough to send it to President Tinubu for assent. The remaining four states have until Tuesday to report.

Read more

The constitutional amendment to create state police in Nigeria has cleared a major hurdle, with 32 state Houses of Assembly approving it.

What the Senate said

Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele said in Abuja on Sunday, 15 November 2026, that 32 state assemblies had approved the State Police (Constitution Alteration) Bill, 2026, which is more than the two-thirds required under Section 9(2) of the 1999 Constitution. He gave the remaining four assemblies until Tuesday, 17 November, to submit their reports, but said the National Assembly now has a duty to send the bill to President Bola Tinubu for assent whether or not they meet the deadline.

What the bill would do

The amendment would replace Nigeria's single, centralised police force with a Federal Police Service and State Police Services, allowing states to set up their own forces.

Background

Calls for state police have grown stronger in recent years amid security threats including banditry and kidnapping. The senior civil servants' union this week also called for state police to be created.

UK now buys more refined fuel than crude oil from Nigeria, data shows

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

UK trade figures show Britain bought £1.02 billion of refined petroleum products from Nigeria in the year to June 2026, more than double the year before, overtaking crude oil imports, as Dangote Refinery expands exports.

Read more

Nigeria's petroleum trade with the United Kingdom has shifted sharply towards refined fuel, according to UK government figures.

The figures

The UK's trade and investment factsheet, released on 6 November 2026 and reported this weekend, shows Britain bought £1.02 billion worth of refined petroleum products from Nigeria in the 12 months to June 2026. That was more than double the figure a year earlier and made up 53.5 per cent of all UK goods imports from Nigeria. Over the same period, UK purchases of Nigerian crude oil fell by 56.2 per cent to £550.3 million, meaning refined fuel exports exceeded crude shipments by about £467 million. Nigeria recorded a petroleum trade surplus of about £245 million with Britain.

Why it matters

The change reflects the growth of the Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Lagos, which can process 650,000 barrels a day and has been expanding its exports.

Background

The government has also announced plans for a new NNPC refinery in Edo State.
