"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum": Son of second wife accused of mistreating Mrs Anulika in Ukehe speaks out

10 Nov 2026, 10:13 GMT

The son of the second wife at the centre of the viral Ukehe controversy has spoken out, begging Nigerians to stop attacking his mother.

"Is it not enough already?"

"Nigerians, please stop attacking my mum. Is it not enough already? That woman does not deserve all this hate," he said.

"Do you want her to die? If anything happens to my mum, you people won't like it. Please, this is enough."

How it started

His mother has faced a wave of criticism online since a video from his father's Ozo title-taking ceremony went viral. In the video, the second wife's children reportedly refused to spray money on the first wife, Mrs Gertrude Anulika Ngwu, despite repeated appeals from the master of ceremonies.

Claims then spread online that Mrs Anulika had endured years of mockery over being childless and had moved back to her father's home. Those claims have not been independently verified.

Since then, well-wishers have visited Mrs Anulika with gifts and money, and the Enugu governor's wife sent a representative with cash and promises of a house and a business.

The other side of the story

The son's plea is a reminder that there are always two sides to a family story, and that online anger can quickly turn into harassment.

Sympathy for Mrs Anulika is understandable. But insults and threats against another woman, whose side of the story has not been heard, help no one. Families caught up in viral moments are still real people.

A large inflatable balloon promoting President Bola Tinubu's 2027 re-election campaign has been spotted floating loose in the Lagos lagoon.

The video

A short clip shared on X on Friday, 13 November 2026, shows the oversized balloon, bearing Tinubu's image, the All Progressives Congress (APC) colours and campaign branding, drifting in the water beside the Third Mainland Bridge. US grounds all departing flights nationwide after telecom outage

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The US Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop on all departing flights on Friday, citing safety. More than 2,000 flights were cancelled and over 10,000 delayed, and officials blamed a telecommunications outage.

Read more

The United States briefly halted all departing flights across the country on Friday, 13 November 2026, causing widespread disruption.

What happened

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a sudden nationwide ground stop at 3.54pm Eastern time, citing extraordinary circumstances and safety, and said all departing traffic should remain on the ground until further notice. It said inbound international flights would be handled case by case. At the time, more than 4,000 flights bound for US destinations were in the air, according to flight-tracking data.

The impact

Within hours, more than 2,000 flights had been cancelled and more than 10,000 delayed, according to flight-tracking figures, and lawmakers demanded an explanation.

The cause

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication so far of anything malicious, and that the problem appeared to be a telecommunications outage. He said President Donald Trump had been briefed.

Iran fires drones at US base in Bahrain for first time in two months

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Iran launched drones at a US military installation in Bahrain on Friday in what it called retaliation for an American strike, its first attack on a US base in the Gulf in over two months. US forces said both drones were intercepted with no damage.

Read more

Iran has targeted a United States military facility in Bahrain, its first attempt to strike a US base in a Gulf state in more than two months.

What happened

US Central Command said American air defences intercepted and destroyed two Iranian attack drones launched towards Bahrain early on Friday, 13 November 2026, and reported no casualties or damage. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed through state-linked media that they had destroyed a US early-warning radar on the island, home to the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, in response to an alleged attack on a telecommunications tower in the southern Iranian town of Sirik. The US rejected that claim.

Escalation

The strike marks a sharp escalation after a pause since early September, during which Iran had focused on disrupting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Hopes of a deal between Washington and Tehran appear to be fading, with President Donald Trump reportedly telling Vice-President JD Vance that the US does not need a deal.

Why it matters

The US also recently withdrew from the FIFA U-17 World Cup in Qatar, citing a travel advisory linked to the risk of conflict in the region.

Lamido rules out Tinubu, backs Sandy Onor, warns divided opposition helps President

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Former Jigawa governor Sule Lamido says he will not support President Tinubu and has named Professor Sandy Onor as his choice for 2027, while warning that rivalry between Atiku and Obi is handing Tinubu an advantage.

Read more

Former Jigawa State governor Sule Lamido has declared that he will not back President Bola Tinubu's re-election and has named Professor Sandy Onor as his preferred presidential candidate.

His choice

Speaking in Dutse, Lamido reaffirmed his loyalty to the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) and named Onor outright as his candidate for the 16 January 2027 election, making a clear public break from the incumbent. Earlier this week, he denied a claim by Vice-President Kashim Shettima that he supported the APC.

Warning to the opposition

In a television interview on Friday, 13 November 2026, Lamido said the rivalry between Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi was working in Tinubu's favour. He pointed to the large number of serving and former governors in the APC and the failure of the two main opposition figures to cooperate, arguing that a joint ticket, with Atiku as president and Obi as running mate, would have posed a much stronger challenge. He also warned that whoever wins, Nigeria would lose if campaigns ignore national issues.

Background

Atiku is the ADC candidate and Obi the NDC candidate. Efforts to agree a single opposition candidate have repeatedly failed, though PRP candidate Donald Duke and APM candidate Seyi Makinde have both said they are open to collaboration.
