CAF scraps four-year plan, AFCON returns to every two years

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

The Africa Cup of Nations will once again be played every two years after the Confederation of African Football (CAF) reversed its earlier decision to move the tournament to a four-year cycle.

What happened

CAF's Executive Committee reportedly approved the change unanimously on Thursday, 29 October 2026, after months of discussion. CAF President Patrice Motsepe announced the decision in a statement on the body's website.

Why CAF changed course

Motsepe said the decision was taken to advance and protect the interests of African football, its 54 member associations and African players at clubs abroad. CAF had planned to introduce an Africa Nations League to fill the gap between tournaments, but it was reportedly concerned about how long the new competition would take to deliver the expected money and development benefits. Returning to a two-year AFCON was seen as the safer option in the short term.

What comes next

AFCON 2027 will be co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. Motsepe said he was confident the three countries would deliver a successful competition. He described AFCON as more than a football tournament, calling it a celebration of African cultures that brings together people on the continent and in the diaspora.

Background

AFCON has been held every two years for most of its history, which has sometimes caused friction with European clubs that lose key players in the middle of their seasons. The move to a four-year cycle had been welcomed by some clubs but criticised by many fans and smaller federations, who rely on tournament income.

How fans reacted

Many African football fans, including Nigerians, welcomed the decision, saying more frequent AFCONs give the Super Eagles and other national teams more chances to compete for the title.

CAS restores Senegal as AFCON 2025 champions, overturning CAF ruling

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Court of Arbitration for Sport has confirmed Senegal as winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. It overturned in full the CAF Appeal Board decision that had handed the title to Morocco.

Read more

Senegal are once again Africa's champions after the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) overturned the decision that had stripped them of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations title.

The verdict

CAS delivered its ruling on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, reversing in full the decision of CAF's Appeal Board, which had awarded the title to hosts Morocco. With the verdict, Senegal are officially recognised as the 2025 AFCON champions.

How it started

Senegal beat Morocco 1-0 after extra time in the final at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat on 18 January 2026, with Pape Gueye scoring the only goal. During the match, Senegal's players walked off the pitch for about 15 minutes in protest at a late penalty awarded to Morocco, which was later saved.

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation then took legal action, and on 17 March CAF's Appeal Board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match, awarding Morocco a 3-0 win and the trophy. The Senegalese Football Federation refused to accept the decision and appealed to CAS.

What it means

The ruling ends months of legal uncertainty over the continent's top football title. Before the verdict, CAF President Patrice Motsepe had said the body would accept whatever CAS decided.

Morocco to fight on

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation said it rejected the ruling and would challenge it through every legal channel available, citing what it described as procedural failures. Senegal star Sadio Mane welcomed the decision.

CAF responds

In a short statement, CAF acknowledged the ruling and pledged to act on it. The trophy, medals and prize money were never taken back from Senegal during the dispute and will remain with the team.

At least 30 killed as fuel tanker overturns and bursts into flames in Cairo

26 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

At least 30 people died and 15 were injured when a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday. The blaze engulfed 27 vehicles.

Read more

At least 30 people have died and 15 others were injured after a fuel tanker overturned and burst into flames on a busy stretch of the Greater Cairo Ring Road in Egypt on Wednesday, 25 November 2026.

What happened

According to Egypt's Ministry of Health and Population, the fire broke out above Tunnel 10 in the densely populated Al-Warraq area of Imbaba, a suburb of Greater Cairo. Preliminary findings suggest the privately operated tanker swerved, hit a concrete road divider and toppled over, causing an explosion that spread to 27 nearby vehicles.

The response

Authorities sent 39 ambulances to the scene, taking survivors to three hospitals in the area. Firefighters brought the blaze under control, and heavy machinery was brought in to clear the burnt-out vehicles.

Condolences

Egyptian President Abdel-Fattah el-Sisi and Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly sent condolences to the families of the victims.
