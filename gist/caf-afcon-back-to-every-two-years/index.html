CAF scraps four-year plan, AFCON returns to every two years

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

The Africa Cup of Nations will once again be played every two years after the Confederation of African Football (CAF) reversed its earlier decision to move the tournament to a four-year cycle.

What happened

CAF's Executive Committee reportedly approved the change unanimously on Thursday, 29 October 2026, after months of discussion. CAF President Patrice Motsepe announced the decision in a statement on the body's website.

Why CAF changed course

Motsepe said the decision was taken to advance and protect the interests of African football, its 54 member associations and African players at clubs abroad. CAF had planned to introduce an Africa Nations League to fill the gap between tournaments, but it was reportedly concerned about how long the new competition would take to deliver the expected money and development benefits. Returning to a two-year AFCON was seen as the safer option in the short term.

What comes next

AFCON 2027 will be co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. Motsepe said he was confident the three countries would deliver a successful competition. He described AFCON as more than a football tournament, calling it a celebration of African cultures that brings together people on the continent and in the diaspora.

Background

AFCON has been held every two years for most of its history, which has sometimes caused friction with European clubs that lose key players in the middle of their seasons. The move to a four-year cycle had been welcomed by some clubs but criticised by many fans and smaller federations, who rely on tournament income.

How fans reacted

Many African football fans, including Nigerians, welcomed the decision, saying more frequent AFCONs give the Super Eagles and other national teams more chances to compete for the title.

Marathon world champion Peres Jepchirchir banned for three years for doping

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Kenya's Peres Jepchirchir, the reigning marathon world champion and Tokyo Olympic gold medallist, has been banned for three years after testing positive for EPO. Two other Kenyan runners were also banned.

Read more

Reigning marathon world champion Peres Jepchirchir of Kenya has been banned for three years for doping, the Athletics Integrity Unit (AIU) announced on Thursday, 19 November 2026.

The ban

The 33-year-old, who also won Olympic gold at the Tokyo Games in 2021, was suspended after an out-of-competition sample showed traces of the banned blood booster EPO. Her ban runs from 20 October.

Others banned

Two other Kenyan runners were also sanctioned. Veronica Loleo, a half marathon silver medallist at this year's World Road Running Championships, received three years for the same offence, while Ronald Korir, fourth at the 2023 Berlin Marathon, was banned for six years over abnormalities in his biological passport.

A wider problem

They join more than 140 Kenyan athletes, including women's marathon world record-holder Ruth Chepngetich, who have been suspended or banned by the AIU since 2017. The AIU acknowledged that Kenyan authorities have made progress against doping but said these cases show there is still a long way to go.

The Coalition for Democracy, an alliance of opposition parties ahead of the 2027 elections, has reportedly postponed its second summit by one week.

The new date

The coalition's Central Leadership Committee announced on Friday, 20 November 2026, that the summit would now hold on Monday, 30 November, instead of Monday, 23 November.

Why it was moved

The committee said the request came from member parties whose presidential candidates and campaign teams are busy with intensive campaigns across the country. It said it was more important for every party, presidential candidate and national chairman to attend than to keep the original date.

Who is involved
