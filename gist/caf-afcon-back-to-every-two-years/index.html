CAF scraps four-year plan, AFCON returns to every two years

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

The Africa Cup of Nations will once again be played every two years after the Confederation of African Football (CAF) reversed its earlier decision to move the tournament to a four-year cycle.

What happened

CAF's Executive Committee reportedly approved the change unanimously on Thursday, 29 October 2026, after months of discussion. CAF President Patrice Motsepe announced the decision in a statement on the body's website.

Why CAF changed course

Motsepe said the decision was taken to advance and protect the interests of African football, its 54 member associations and African players at clubs abroad. CAF had planned to introduce an Africa Nations League to fill the gap between tournaments, but it was reportedly concerned about how long the new competition would take to deliver the expected money and development benefits. Returning to a two-year AFCON was seen as the safer option in the short term.

What comes next

AFCON 2027 will be co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. Motsepe said he was confident the three countries would deliver a successful competition. He described AFCON as more than a football tournament, calling it a celebration of African cultures that brings together people on the continent and in the diaspora.

Background

AFCON has been held every two years for most of its history, which has sometimes caused friction with European clubs that lose key players in the middle of their seasons. The move to a four-year cycle had been welcomed by some clubs but criticised by many fans and smaller federations, who rely on tournament income.

How fans reacted

Many African football fans, including Nigerians, welcomed the decision, saying more frequent AFCONs give the Super Eagles and other national teams more chances to compete for the title.

What is being reported

Martinez is said to be open to leaving Old Trafford, with Barcelona reportedly making enquiries. No formal offer has been reported, and any move is unlikely before the January transfer window at the earliest.

Samson Adamu becomes first Nigerian to be confirmed as CAF General Secretary

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

CAF has reportedly confirmed Samson Adamu, 40, as its substantive General Secretary, making him the first Nigerian to hold the post since the body was founded in 1957. He had served in an interim role since March.

Read more

Nigerian football administrator Samson Adamu has been confirmed as General Secretary of the Confederation of African Football (CAF), the first Nigerian to hold the position.

What happened

CAF's executive committee reportedly approved his appointment as substantive General Secretary, according to reports on Friday, 30 October 2026. Adamu had held the role on an interim basis since March 2026, after his predecessor, Veron Mosengo-Omba, resigned. The same executive committee meeting also voted to return the Africa Cup of Nations to a two-year cycle.

Who is Samson Adamu

The 40-year-old previously served as CAF's Director of Competitions, where he was involved in organising the Africa Cup of Nations and the CAF Champions League. He holds a FIFA Master's degree in sports management, law and humanities. He began his career in football administration by founding the Copa Lagos beach soccer tournament in the early 2010s.

What the job involves

As General Secretary, Adamu will run CAF's day-to-day administration, oversee legal matters and carry out decisions taken by the executive committee. His first big task will be preparations for AFCON 2027, which will be co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda.

How people reacted

Nigerian football fans and officials celebrated the appointment as a proud moment for the country, which has produced many top players but rarely held top administrative jobs in African football.

VeryDarkMan unveils RATEL Sensor Light, an affordable motion-activated light for Nigerian homes

31 Oct 2026, 14:32 GMT

VeryDarkMan has added a motion-activated sensor light to his growing RATEL brand, priced at ₦4,200 for the small size and ₦9,000 for the big size. The light switches on automatically when it detects movement, which could help households during power cuts and add a little security around the compound.

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) has unveiled another product under his growing RATEL brand, and this time it is something many Nigerian households may find useful, especially with the country's unpredictable electricity supply.

The new product is the RATEL Sensor Light, a motion-activated light that comes on automatically when it detects movement.

How much it costs

According to VDM, the sensor light comes in two sizes: the small size costs ₦4,200, while the big size costs ₦9,000.

Why it could be useful

Anyone living in Nigeria knows how frustrating the electricity situation can be. Imagine NEPA suddenly takes light at midnight and everywhere goes dark. You need to leave your bedroom, walk downstairs, check on your children or find your way outside to switch on the generator. Before you even find your torchlight, you have already knocked your leg against a chair.

This is where a sensor light could come in handy. It can be placed along staircases, corridors, bedroom entrances, wardrobes, storerooms and other dark corners of the house. Once it detects movement within its range, it switches on automatically, with no need to search for a wall switch in the dark.

A possible security benefit

The light could also help around entrances, gates and backyards. If somebody quietly approaches that area at night, the space around the sensor suddenly becomes bright.

Of course, a sensor light cannot replace proper home security, but it may help discourage unwanted visitors and make movement around a compound more noticeable.

VDM also explained that the product comes with different lighting colour options, including multicolour settings.

What buyers should check

At ₦4,200 for the smaller version, some households may find it affordable enough to install several units: one on the staircase, another in the corridor and perhaps two around the entrance. However, buyers should check the battery life, charging method, weather resistance and detection range before deciding where to install it.

A growing RATEL range

What stands out about this latest RATEL product is the idea behind it. VDM appears to be paying attention to the everyday inconveniences Nigerians face, especially those caused by poor electricity supply. From power stations to smaller household gadgets, he is gradually expanding his product range.

There is something worth appreciating about businesses that identify ordinary problems and offer affordable solutions. You don't always need to spend hundreds of thousands of naira to make your home more comfortable, and sometimes even a small gadget can save you the stress of walking around in darkness.

Imagine having four of these lights around your house. NEPA takes light, but as you move around, the lights come on automatically. Small enjoyment!
