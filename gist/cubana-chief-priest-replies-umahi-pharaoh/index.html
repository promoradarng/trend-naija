"Pharaoh, let my people go!": Cubana Chief Priest replies Umahi, accuses him of giving Tinubu fake promises on South-East votes

9 Nov 2026, 11:14 GMT

Socialite and businessman Cubana Chief Priest has replied Minister of Works David Umahi, a day after the minister mocked his famous "money na water" catchphrase and asked how many people he has given scholarships.

"Pharaoh, let my people go"

Borrowing from the Bible story of Moses and Pharaoh, Cubana Chief Priest gave the minister a new nickname, and accused him of making promises to President Bola Tinubu that he cannot deliver.

"Pharaoh, let my people go. Stop giving Tinubu fake promises on votes. What project are you using to convince South-East people to vote for him?" he said.

The governorship claim

He went further, claiming that Umahi is preparing his son to become governor of Ebonyi State after the current governor, Francis Nwifuru.

Speaking partly in Pidgin, he said, in effect: "You're busy preparing your son to be governor after Nwifuru. God won't let that happen. You want to use Ebonyi State to settle your child. Let the young man come and meet us on the street so we can teach him how to make 'money na water'."

This is Cubana Chief Priest's claim, and Umahi has not yet responded to this latest attack.

How it started

The exchange began weeks ago when Cubana Chief Priest described Umahi as the second-worst minister from the South-East. Umahi hit back by telling him he was "becoming very noisy", comparing him unfavourably with Dangote and Otedola, and asking, "How many people have you given scholarships?"

Now the socialite has turned the argument back to politics, questioning what Umahi has done to win South-East votes for the President.

Over to you

Who is winning this war of words: the minister or the socialite? And is Cubana Chief Priest right to question what the South-East has to show for supporting Tinubu?

Lagos West senator and former deputy governor Idiat Adebule dies at 56

28 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Senator Idiat Adebule, who represented Lagos West and was formerly deputy governor of Lagos State, has reportedly died at 56. Her death reportedly came less than a day after her birthday.

Read more

Senator Idiat Adebule, who represented the Lagos West Senatorial District in the Nigerian Senate, has reportedly died at the age of 56.

What happened

Adebule was reportedly taken to a private hospital in the GRA area of Ikeja after she fell ill, where she was later pronounced dead. Her death is said to have come less than a day after she marked her 56th birthday. No official cause of death has been announced.

Her career

Adebule served as deputy governor of Lagos State before she was elected to the Senate on the platform of the All Progressives Congress (APC). At the time of her death, she was reportedly seeking re-election to a second term in the Senate in the 2027 elections.

What happens next

Her family, the Lagos State government and the Senate are expected to make formal statements and announce burial arrangements. Tributes are expected from across the political divide.

Davido and cousins turn heads at Governor Adeleke's second-term inauguration

Osun Governor Ademola Adeleke was sworn in for a second term on Friday in Osogbo. His nephew Davido and other family members drew attention with a coordinated appearance at the ceremony.

Osun State Governor Ademola Adeleke was inaugurated for a second term on Friday, 27 November 2026, at the Osogbo Township Stadium.

Family affair

Afrobeats star Davido, the governor's nephew, drew almost as much attention as the ceremony itself. He arrived with his brother Adewale Adeleke and cousins Shina Adeleke, known as Shina Rambo, and Adebayo Adeleke, known as B-Red. Background

Shettima mocks Atiku and Obi at Enugu rally, suggests Obi for South-East commission job

28 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Vice President Kashim Shettima told the APC's South-East rally in Enugu that Atiku and Obi are not in the race to win. He suggested Tinubu appoint Obi to chair the South-East Development Commission, a remark that drew criticism online.

Read more

Vice President Kashim Shettima took aim at opposition candidates Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi on Saturday, 28 November 2026, during the APC's South-East presidential campaign rally in Enugu.

What he said

Shettima told supporters that the two men were not in the 2027 race to win, but for money and to cause trouble after losing. He said the APC would 'retire' Atiku to Yola, his home in Adamawa State.

Turning to Obi, the vice president said the NDC candidate had visited 17 states, including the five South-East states, most of the South-South, Kaduna and Lagos, and argued that a presidential election cannot be won with support from only a few regions. He then suggested that President Tinubu appoint Obi as chairman of the South-East Development Commission, saying it was about the only job he was fit for.

How people reacted

The remarks drew criticism from many Nigerians on X, with some accusing the vice president of attacking Obi instead of highlighting the Tinubu administration's achievements. Shettima has made similar jabs at both men at earlier rallies.

The opposition

Atiku's camp has repeatedly said he is not stepping down for anyone. Opposition parties, including Atiku's ADC, Obi's NDC and Makinde's APM, are due to meet at a summit in Abuja on Monday, 30 November.

Pope Leo appoints Nigerian-born priest Benedict Koledoye as bishop in Wales

28 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Pope Leo XIV has reportedly appointed Nigerian-born Canon Benedict Oladipo Koledoye as auxiliary bishop of Cardiff-Menevia in Wales. He is said to be the first black Latin Rite priest to be made a bishop in England and Wales.

Read more

Pope Leo XIV has reportedly appointed Nigerian-born Canon Benedict Oladipo Koledoye as Auxiliary Bishop of the Archdiocese of Cardiff-Menevia in Wales.

A historic first

The appointment, announced on Saturday, 28 November 2026, by the Catholic Bishops' Conference of England and Wales, reportedly makes him the first black Latin Rite priest to be ordained a bishop in England and Wales.

Who he is

Koledoye, 55, was born on 21 April 1971 in Ado-Ekiti, Ekiti State, and has family roots in the Ilaje-Ese Odo area of Ondo State. He has served as a priest in South Wales for about 15 years.

His reaction

Koledoye said the news, which was delivered to him by the Apostolic Nuncio, took him by surprise. He thanked the Pope, acknowledged the weight of the role and said he looked forward to working with the Archbishop of Cardiff-Menevia, Mark O'Toole, who described the appointment as a moment of joy.

How Nigerians reacted

Video of the announcement has been trending among Nigerians online, with many celebrating the appointment as a proud moment.
