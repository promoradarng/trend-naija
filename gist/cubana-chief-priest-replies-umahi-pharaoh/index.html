"Pharaoh, let my people go!": Cubana Chief Priest replies Umahi, accuses him of giving Tinubu fake promises on South-East votes

9 Nov 2026, 11:14 GMT

Socialite and businessman Cubana Chief Priest has replied Minister of Works David Umahi, a day after the minister mocked his famous "money na water" catchphrase and asked how many people he has given scholarships.

"Pharaoh, let my people go"

Borrowing from the Bible story of Moses and Pharaoh, Cubana Chief Priest gave the minister a new nickname, and accused him of making promises to President Bola Tinubu that he cannot deliver.

"Pharaoh, let my people go. Stop giving Tinubu fake promises on votes. What project are you using to convince South-East people to vote for him?" he said.

The governorship claim

He went further, claiming that Umahi is preparing his son to become governor of Ebonyi State after the current governor, Francis Nwifuru.

Speaking partly in Pidgin, he said, in effect: "You're busy preparing your son to be governor after Nwifuru. God won't let that happen. You want to use Ebonyi State to settle your child. Let the young man come and meet us on the street so we can teach him how to make 'money na water'."

This is Cubana Chief Priest's claim, and Umahi has not yet responded to this latest attack.

How it started

The exchange began weeks ago when Cubana Chief Priest described Umahi as the second-worst minister from the South-East. Umahi hit back by telling him he was "becoming very noisy", comparing him unfavourably with Dangote and Otedola, and asking, "How many people have you given scholarships?"

Now the socialite has turned the argument back to politics, questioning what Umahi has done to win South-East votes for the President.

Goodluck Jonathan calls for review of Nigeria's democracy and institutions

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a broad review of Nigeria's democratic structures, how politicians are recruited and the state of public institutions, saying the country owes a debt to its founding nationalists.

Read more

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a wide-ranging review of Nigeria's democratic structures, political recruitment and public institutions.

What he said

Chairing a colloquium at the Nigerian Institute of International Affairs in Lagos to mark the 162nd birth anniversary of nationalist Herbert Macaulay, reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Jonathan said the country's founding nationalists did not merely fight to end colonial rule, but imagined a sovereign, united and prosperous Nigeria where every citizen would enjoy equal rights, dignity and opportunity. He said independence was also meant to bring economic freedom, industrialisation, quality education, healthcare and social justice.

Honesty about failures

While acknowledging progress in some areas, Jonathan said true patriotism requires honesty about the country's failures. He pointed to leadership failures and weak institutions, and urged a review of how the country's democracy works and how leaders emerge.

Background

Jonathan, who governed from 2010 to 2015, is widely remembered for conceding defeat to Muhammadu Buhari in the 2015 election, a first for a sitting Nigerian president. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

State police bill clears 32 state assemblies, heads for Tinubu's assent

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele says 32 state assemblies have approved the constitutional amendment to create state police, enough to send it to President Tinubu for assent. The remaining four states have until Tuesday to report.

Read more

The constitutional amendment to create state police in Nigeria has cleared a major hurdle, with 32 state Houses of Assembly approving it.

What the Senate said

Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele said in Abuja on Sunday, 15 November 2026, that 32 state assemblies had approved the State Police (Constitution Alteration) Bill, 2026, which is more than the two-thirds required under Section 9(2) of the 1999 Constitution. He gave the remaining four assemblies until Tuesday, 17 November, to submit their reports, but said the National Assembly now has a duty to send the bill to President Bola Tinubu for assent whether or not they meet the deadline.

What the bill would do

The amendment would replace Nigeria's single, centralised police force with a Federal Police Service and State Police Services, allowing states to set up their own forces.

Background

Calls for state police have grown stronger in recent years amid security threats including banditry and kidnapping. The senior civil servants' union this week also called for state police to be created.

After 20 years of marriage, Kwankwaso's daughter Zainab files for divorce from Kano Governor Abba Yusuf, offers to refund dowry

14 Nov 2026, 17:44 GMT

Zainab Musa Kwankwaso, daughter of Senator Rabiu Kwankwaso, has reportedly filed for divorce from her husband, Kano State Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf, at an Upper Area Court in Abuja, and offered to refund her dowry. The governor has been summoned to appear on 17 November.

Read more

Zainab Musa Kwankwaso, daughter of former Kano governor and senator Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso, has reportedly filed for divorce from her husband, Kano State Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf, after 20 years of marriage.

The court case

The case was filed at the Upper Area Court in Gudu, Abuja. A civil summons directs Governor Yusuf to appear in court on Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

Zainab is reported to be seeking khul'i, a process under Islamic law that allows a wife to end her marriage, and has offered to refund the dowry Yusuf paid when they married.

The summons is not a divorce order, and the court has yet to rule on the matter.

Her reasons

According to the filing, Zainab cited a long absence of mutual care, love and emotional connection in the marriage, and said reconciliation efforts had failed.

The couple married on 29 July 2006, when Zainab was working as an aide to her father, who was then governor of Kano State. They have six children.

The political backdrop

The marriage once symbolised the close bond between Kwankwaso and Yusuf, his long-time political protégé. That relationship has since broken down.

In January 2026, Governor Yusuf left Kwankwaso's party, the New Nigeria Peoples Party (NNPP), for the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC), a move Kwankwaso's camp described as a betrayal.

Some reports have linked the political split to tension in the marriage, but the reasons given in the court filing focus on the breakdown of the relationship itself.

No comment yet

As of Saturday, neither Governor Yusuf, Zainab nor Senator Kwankwaso had made a public statement on the divorce, and the Kano State Government had not commented.

"Your approach was inappropriate": South African presidency fires back at Fani-Kayode over livestream complaint

14 Nov 2026, 09:27 GMT

South Africa's presidency has accused Nigeria's High Commissioner, Femi Fani-Kayode, of misrepresenting what happened when his speech was cut off at the ceremony where he presented his credentials to President Cyril Ramaphosa, describing his public complaint as inappropriate.

Read more

The South African presidency has criticised Nigeria's High Commissioner to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, over his public complaint that his speech was cut off during the ceremony where he presented his letters of credence to President Cyril Ramaphosa.

Fani-Kayode's complaint

Fani-Kayode had raised concerns about the incident, suggesting that he was interrupted while addressing the gathering during the official diplomatic ceremony.

South Africa responds

The South African presidency has rejected his account. Responding to the controversy, President Ramaphosa's spokesperson, Vincent Magwenya, accused Fani-Kayode of misrepresenting the situation and of wrongly dragging the South African President into a matter that did not require his involvement.

Magwenya also described the Nigerian diplomat's handling of the issue as inappropriate.

A routine ceremony turned row

The disagreement has drawn attention to what should have been a routine diplomatic ceremony between two African countries.

Diplomatic engagements are usually guided by established protocols, and disagreements over procedure are generally expected to be handled through official channels rather than in public.

For a newly appointed high commissioner, the public exchange has raised questions about diplomatic conduct, and about how misunderstandings between representatives of friendly nations should be resolved.
