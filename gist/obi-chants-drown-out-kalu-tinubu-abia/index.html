Crowd chants 'Obi' as Deputy Speaker Kalu campaigns for Tinubu at Abia festival

12 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives Benjamin Kalu was reportedly met with chants of Peter Obi's name as he campaigned for President Bola Tinubu at a festival in Abia State.

What happened

Kalu, an All Progressives Congress (APC) lawmaker who represents Bende Federal Constituency, was addressing the Item Okpi Festival, an annual cultural celebration in Bende Local Government Area, on Saturday. According to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the audience responded with loud and sustained chants of Obi's name rather than applause. When a woman on stage began a pro-Obi song, Kalu reportedly asked her to switch to a pro-Tinubu song, but the chanting continued. The Deputy Speaker was said to have remarked in Igbo that the crowd did not seem to want a university the President plans to build for them, and suggested some of those chanting were not from Item.

Background

Abia is governed by Alex Otti of the Labour Party, a former ally of Obi who has said he supports Tinubu as the sitting president while acknowledging that his party has its own candidate. Obi, now the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), remains popular in the South-East, where the APC is working hard to win support ahead of the 16 January 2027 election. Kalu has not publicly commented on the video.

  • The crowd chanted Obi's name during Kalu's speech.
  • Kalu is campaigning for Tinubu in the South-East.
  • The festival took place in Bende, Abia State.

ADC tells Tinubu to resign or face impeachment over Kano governor's interview

25 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The African Democratic Congress has reportedly demanded that President Tinubu resign or face impeachment, citing Kano Governor Abba Yusuf's recent interview about the 2023 Supreme Court ruling. The ADC says it points to possible gross misconduct.

Read more

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has reportedly issued an ultimatum to President Bola Tinubu, demanding that he either resign or face impeachment proceedings in the National Assembly.

Why the ADC made the demand

The demand follows Kano State Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf's recent Hausa-language interview, in which he spoke about a promise to rejoin the APC if the president did not interfere in his Supreme Court case over the 2023 governorship election. The ADC argued that the governor's remarks suggested a link between the court case and a political deal, and that this could amount to gross misconduct, a constitutional ground for removing a president.

Background

Yusuf defected from the NNPP to the APC in January 2026. In his interview, he claimed that his former mentor, Rabiu Kwankwaso, sent him to make the promise to Tinubu. The Kwankwasiyya Movement has rejected the claim as false and said the governor offered no evidence.

The other side

No response from the presidency or the APC to the ADC's demand was included in the reports.

Obi defends Kwankwasiyya supporters who carry sticks to rallies

25 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has reportedly defended Kwankwasiyya members who bring sticks to political events, saying the items have never been used to hurt anyone. He spoke in Jalingo, Taraba State.

Read more

Peter Obi, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate, has reportedly defended members of the Kwankwasiyya movement who carry sticks to political gatherings.

What he said

Speaking in a television interview in Jalingo, Taraba State, on Tuesday evening, 24 November 2026, after a town hall meeting, Obi is said to have explained that the movement, led by his running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso, has a long tradition of supporters attending political events with sticks. He said there was no record of the sticks being used to harm anyone.

He also said that calls against electoral malpractice were being misread as opposition to campaigning.

Background

Political violence has become a major concern ahead of the January 2027 elections, following Monday's deadly attack on an NDC campaign event in Delta State. At the Taraba town hall, Obi led a minute of silence for the party members killed.

NDC boycotts IGP's security meeting, says its members are not safe

24 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Nigeria Democratic Congress has reportedly refused to attend a security meeting called by the Inspector-General of Police, accusing security agencies of failing to protect its members from attacks. It blamed thugs it said were loyal to the APC.

Read more

The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has announced that it will boycott a security meeting convened by the Inspector-General of Police, citing attacks on its members ahead of the 2027 elections.

The party's reasons

In a statement on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, signed by its National Publicity Secretary, the party said it no longer felt safe under the Nigeria Police Force and would not lend legitimacy to a security process that had failed to protect its members.

The NDC accused security agencies of failing to prevent repeated attacks it alleged were carried out by thugs loyal to the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC). It pointed to incidents in Benue, Edo, Cross River, Enugu and Delta states, including what it described as an attempt to stop its presidential candidate, Peter Obi, from entering Benue State.

The Delta attack

The boycott follows Monday's deadly attack on the campaign team of the party's Delta Central senatorial candidate, Ovie Omo-Agege, in Ughelli North. The party alleged that security personnel were complicit. Police have since invited Omo-Agege and others for questioning as part of their investigation.

The other side

No response from the APC or the police to the NDC's allegations was included in the reports.

Court voids APC's Bayelsa West senatorial candidacy

24 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly voided Fidelis Agbiki's candidacy as the APC's Bayelsa West senatorial candidate. The court upheld claims that his name was missing from the party's membership register.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has reportedly nullified the candidacy of High Chief Fidelis Agbiki, the All Progressives Congress (APC) senatorial candidate for Bayelsa West in the 2027 elections.

The case

The petitioners raised two main objections to his candidacy. First, they argued that his name did not appear in the APC membership register submitted to INEC, meaning he was not a recognised member of the party. Second, they claimed he did not resign from a political appointment before taking part in the senatorial primary.

The ruling

Justice Mohammed Garba Umar upheld the petition, finding that the primary breached the Electoral Act.

Background

The judgment came on the same day a Federal High Court in Warri voided the NDC candidacy of former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege for Delta Central on similar grounds relating to membership registers. Pre-election court cases are common in Nigeria, and both rulings can be appealed.
