Gunmen abduct 14 Islamiyya students and their teacher on Niger farm

3 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Suspected bandits have abducted 14 students of an Islamic school and their teacher in Rafi Local Government Area of Niger State, police have confirmed.

What happened

The attack happened at about 12.30pm on Saturday, 31 October 2026, in Tungan-Bako village near Pandogari. According to the police, the students were helping their teacher harvest maize on his farm when armed men stormed the area and took them away.

Police statement

The spokesman for the Niger State Police Command, Wasiu Abiodun, confirmed the incident on Monday, 2 November. He said the number of students abducted was 14, correcting earlier reports that about 50 pupils had been seized. He said a joint team of security forces had been deployed to rescue the victims and that further updates would be given.

Background

Rafi and nearby local government areas in Niger State have suffered repeated attacks by armed groups in recent years, including mass abductions from schools and villages. Kidnapping for ransom remains a serious problem across north-west and north-central Nigeria, and farmers are often targeted during the harvest season. On the same day the abduction was confirmed, bandits ambushed a bus carrying police officers in neighbouring Zamfara State.

What happens next

Families and community leaders are calling for the quick and safe release of the students.

  • The students were harvesting maize on their teacher's farm.
  • Police say 14 students were taken, not 50.
  • A joint rescue operation is under way.

Jimmy Odukoya sues sister and Fountain of Life trustees over his removal

21 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Pastor Jimmy Odukoya has filed a lawsuit at the Federal High Court in Lagos against his sister, Tolu Odukoya, and members of the Fountain of Life Church board over his removal as senior pastor.

Read more

Pastor Jimmy Odukoya has gone to court over his removal as senior pastor of The Fountain of Life Church, filing a suit against his sister and several members of the church's board of trustees.

The suit

The suit was filed at the Federal High Court in Lagos on Friday, 20 November 2026. Eight people are named as respondents, including his sister Tolu Odukoya, interim leader Pastor Rotimi Okpaise and other trustees.

The filing marks a significant escalation in the leadership dispute at the Ilupeju church founded by his late father, Taiwo Odukoya.

Background

Earlier this week, the trustees announced that Jimmy had been removed because his appointment did not reflect his father's succession wishes, and released a 2021 video in which the late founder reportedly named Pastor Kunle Areogun as his successor. Jimmy has insisted his removal is invalid. A Lagos State High Court had already granted the trustees an order barring him from acting as senior pastor while their own suit is heard.

What happens next

The court is expected to fix a date to hear the case. The trustees had not responded publicly to the new suit in the reports.

NELFUND's Managing Director, Akin Sawyer, said in a television interview on Friday, 20 November 2026, that the allowance was being reviewed in light of the economic pressures facing students.

Suspected insurgents bomb power lines linking Kainji Dam to Jebba

21 Nov 2026, 14:41 GMT

Suspected Boko Haram insurgents reportedly blew up two electricity transmission towers connecting the Kainji Dam power plant to Jebba in Niger State. No lives were lost, but the attack raised fears over the safety of power infrastructure.

Read more

Suspected Boko Haram insurgents have reportedly bombed two electricity transmission towers connecting the Kainji Dam power station to Jebba in Niger State.

What happened

The explosions reportedly happened at about 2pm on 17 November 2026, between Kanti community in Mashegu Local Government Area and Kanya community in Mokwa Local Government Area. The attackers are said to have targeted the transmission infrastructure, raising concerns that the Kainji Dam power plant itself could be at risk.

No lives were lost, according to the reports, but the blasts caused panic in nearby communities. Security monitoring has continued in the area.

Why it matters

Kainji and Jebba are hydropower stations that feed the national grid.

Background

The incident follows a series of attacks on and vandalism of electricity infrastructure across the country. This month, the House of Representatives resolved to investigate the destruction of 10 transmission towers within five months.

Nigeria begins building $70m passport factory, local production from May 2027

21 Nov 2026, 14:41 GMT

The federal government has started building a $70 million passport production plant in Abuja. Interior Minister Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo says it will start producing passport booklets locally from May 2027.

Read more

Nigeria has started building a $70 million passport production plant in Abuja, as the federal government moves to end its reliance on foreign suppliers for passport booklets.

The announcement

Minister of Interior Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo announced the project at a groundbreaking ceremony at the headquarters of the Nigeria Immigration Service. He described it as a major step towards a self-sufficient passport system and said local production should begin by May 2027.

The plant

The facility is being built in partnership with IRIS Smart Technologies and will initially produce six million electronic passport booklets a year, with capacity that could later rise to 10 million.

Why it matters

The plant is meant to end Nigeria's reliance on foreign suppliers for passport booklets.
