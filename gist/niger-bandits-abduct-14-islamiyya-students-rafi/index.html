Gunmen abduct 14 Islamiyya students and their teacher on Niger farm

3 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Suspected bandits have abducted 14 students of an Islamic school and their teacher in Rafi Local Government Area of Niger State, police have confirmed.

What happened

The attack happened at about 12.30pm on Saturday, 31 October 2026, in Tungan-Bako village near Pandogari. According to the police, the students were helping their teacher harvest maize on his farm when armed men stormed the area and took them away.

Police statement

The spokesman for the Niger State Police Command, Wasiu Abiodun, confirmed the incident on Monday, 2 November. He said the number of students abducted was 14, correcting earlier reports that about 50 pupils had been seized. He said a joint team of security forces had been deployed to rescue the victims and that further updates would be given.

Background

Rafi and nearby local government areas in Niger State have suffered repeated attacks by armed groups in recent years, including mass abductions from schools and villages. Kidnapping for ransom remains a serious problem across north-west and north-central Nigeria, and farmers are often targeted during the harvest season. On the same day the abduction was confirmed, bandits ambushed a bus carrying police officers in neighbouring Zamfara State.

What happens next

Families and community leaders are calling for the quick and safe release of the students.

  • The students were harvesting maize on their teacher's farm.
  • Police say 14 students were taken, not 50.
  • A joint rescue operation is under way.

Dangote Refinery considers bigger IPO after ₦6 trillion in subscriptions

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering enlarging its share sale by up to 30% after investors put in nearly ₦6 trillion. That could take the offer to about $2.1 billion, one of Africa's largest.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering expanding its initial public offering (IPO) by as much as 30%, after strong investor demand pushed subscriptions to nearly ₦6 trillion, or about $4.5 billion.

The numbers

The refinery, owned by billionaire Aliko Dangote, initially planned to sell 4.1 billion shares. Demand is said to have exceeded the shares on offer by nearly three times, prompting talks about increasing the allocation.

If the expansion goes ahead, the value of the share sale could rise to about $2.1 billion, potentially making it one of the biggest stock market offerings on the continent.

Why it matters

The refinery is the largest in Africa, and its pricing decisions have recently driven changes in pump prices across Nigeria.

CBN and NNPC sign new agreement to tighten oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue

18 Nov 2026, 14:54 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement covering foreign-exchange inflows, remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing and more. It promises a more structured system, but the full agreement has not been released and no revenue targets have been set.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement aimed at tightening oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue.

What the agreement covers

The agreement covers foreign-exchange inflows, the remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing, digital payments, regulatory compliance and cybersecurity.

Officials say it will replace the old practice of dealing with issues one transaction at a time with a more structured system, including joint committees and performance monitoring.

Why it matters

Oil remains Nigeria's biggest source of foreign exchange and a major part of government revenue. For years, questions have been raised about how much oil money actually reaches government accounts, and how quickly.

Stronger coordination between the central bank and the national oil company could, in theory, improve transparency, boost foreign-exchange supply and help stabilise the naira.

Promises versus results

It sounds promising, but Nigerians will need to see measurable results. The full agreement has not been made public, and no target has been given for additional revenue or foreign-exchange inflows.

Without published targets and regular public reporting, it will be difficult for citizens to judge whether the new arrangement is working.

The real test

The real test is simple: will more oil money reach government accounts, and will Nigerians be able to see it?

FG moves to start direct flights between Nigeria and Ireland

18 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo says Nigeria is working towards direct flights to Ireland, which could open a new route for local airlines such as Air Peace and Enugu Air.

Read more

Nigeria is taking steps to establish direct flights to Ireland, a move that could open a new international route for local airlines including Air Peace and Enugu Air.

What was said

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, disclosed the plan during a meeting with the Irish Ambassador to Nigeria, Lesley Ní Bhriain. He said Nigeria was ready to deepen aviation cooperation with Ireland, citing growing demand for travel between the two countries.

Why it matters

The proposed arrangement is expected to strengthen trade, tourism, education and business ties between the two countries, and make travel easier for Nigerians living in Ireland.

What happens next

Details such as the timeline for the first flights and which airlines will operate the route have not been announced.
