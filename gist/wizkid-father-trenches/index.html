Odumodublvck claims his song made Declan Rice famous in Nigeria

2 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Rapper Odumodublvck has reportedly claimed that Nigerians did not know Arsenal midfielder Declan Rice until he released a song about him. Many fans mocked the claim, pointing out that Rice was already a well-known England player.

Read more

Rapper Odumodublvck has sparked amused reactions online after claiming he introduced England and Arsenal midfielder Declan Rice to Nigerians.

What he said

In comments shared on Monday, 2 November 2026, the rapper reportedly said that before he sang about Rice, nobody in Nigeria knew him, and that Rice blew up after the song, after which Arsenal discovered and signed him. He presented the claim as proof of how his music connects football and music fans.

The song

Odumodublvck released his hit 'Declan Rice' in 2023, and it became one of the most popular Nigerian rap songs of that year, helping to make him one of the country's best-known young rappers. Rice, who was then captain of West Ham United and already an England international, joined Arsenal in 2023 in a British record transfer.

How people reacted

Many fans mocked the claim, saying football followers in Nigeria knew Rice long before the song and that it was the rapper who benefited from the player's name, not the other way round. Others said he was joking or simply promoting himself, while some called it harmless banter.

Background

Odumodublvck is known for bold statements and a confident persona, which have helped keep him in the spotlight. Arsenal is one of the most widely supported English clubs in Nigeria.

Namibian Afrobeats singer Nally dies at 33 in crash that killed seven

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

Namibian singer Nally Simson has died at the age of 33 in a head-on collision that killed seven people in northern Namibia on Sunday. His last Instagram post, promoting his song 'Untouchable', has gone viral as fans mourn.

Read more

The Namibian music industry is in mourning after Afrobeats singer Nally Simson, widely known as Nally, died in a road accident on Sunday, 1 November 2026. He was 33.

What happened

Nally was one of seven people killed in a head-on collision between a pickup truck and an SUV at the Elim Curve on the Okahao-Oshakati main road in the north of the country. The regional police commander for Omusati, Commissioner Ismael Basson, said the crash happened at about 4am, and that six occupants of one vehicle and the driver of the SUV died at the scene. Police have not given further details about the cause.

Family statement

The singer's brother, Benjamin Simson, confirmed his death and asked for privacy as the family grieves.

His last post

A day before his death, on Saturday, 31 October, Nally shared a clip of his latest song, 'Untouchable', on Instagram. In the song he addresses critics, saying their words have no power over him. Fans who returned to the post after the news broke filled the comments with tributes, saying the lyrics now felt very emotional. A separate video in which a fan appeared to predict tragedy for him hours before the crash also spread online, drawing shocked reactions.

Background

Nally was one of Namibia's best-known Afrobeats artists and had built a following across southern Africa. Fellow musicians, fans and public figures in Namibia have paid tribute to him on social media.

What happens next

Funeral arrangements have not yet been announced.

Toluwanising burial row resolved as widow and father agree, Woli Arole calls for calm

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

A close associate of the late gospel singer Toluwanising says his widow and father have agreed to bury him at his Ibadan house, ending their dispute. Comedian-turned-pastor Woli Arole has urged people to stop using the singer's death for online attention.

Read more

The dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried appears to have been settled, according to one of his closest colleagues.

The agreement

In an Instagram video shared on Sunday, 1 November 2026, singer Lanre Alewilese said the widow, Damilola Olayemi, and her father-in-law had agreed that the singer should be buried at his house in Ibadan, Oyo State. He described the disagreement as a misunderstanding and asked Nigerians to let the family settle the matter in peace. He also appealed to people to stop attacking the widow, describing her as a young woman going through a very difficult time, and said members of the singer's band could vouch for her character.

Woli Arole speaks

On Monday, 2 November, comedian-turned-pastor Bayegun Oluwatoyin, known as Woli Arole, posted a video in which he condemned people seeking social media attention from the tragedy. Quoting Proverbs 31:8 on speaking up for those who cannot speak for themselves, he described Toluwanising as a rare talent, and questioned why some of those now talking about him had shown little interest in his music while he was alive. He urged the public to support the widow and children, and said no dispute could take away the music the singer left behind. His comments came after unproven claims made online by a preacher based in Canada named him and other gospel figures in connection with the death.

Background

Toluwanising, 32, died suddenly on 14 October 2026, and a night of tributes was held for him in Ibadan on 27 October. His father had insisted that he be buried at the family residence or in his hometown near his late mother, and relatives had claimed that the widow preferred a cemetery. His younger brother later defended the widow, as did media personality Nancy Umeh.

What happens next

A burial date has not yet been announced.

Don Jazzy says he blew his first big music money on 50 bottles of champagne

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

Mavin Records boss Don Jazzy says he spent his first big earnings from music on champagne and clubbing before he started investing in new artistes. He says that decision means he no longer has to work, and urged young people to invest early.

Read more

Music producer and Mavin Records founder Michael Collins Ajereh, better known as Don Jazzy, has admitted that he wasted his first major earnings from music, and says the lesson changed his life.

What he said

Speaking on an episode of the 'On The Grind' podcast, in comments that spread on Monday, 2 November 2026, Don Jazzy said that when he got his first big money from music, he bought 50 bottles of champagne and went clubbing. He said he quickly pulled himself together and began investing in signing new artistes, adding that, as a result, he no longer has to work.

His advice

The producer urged young people to put the money they earn in their youth into profitable businesses rather than spending it on luxuries. He said they did not necessarily need to start a record label, but should find something lucrative to invest in.

Background

Don Jazzy co-founded Mo' Hits Records with D'banj in 2004 and produced many of the label's biggest hits. After the label split in 2012, he set up Mavin Records, which has gone on to sign and develop stars such as Tiwa Savage, Rema, Ayra Starr, Johnny Drille and Crayon. The label attracted a major investment from Universal Music Group in 2024. He is also known for his generosity online and recently announced a technology scholarship for young Nigerians.

How people reacted

Many social media users praised his honesty and said the advice was useful for young people who start earning early. Others joked that 50 bottles of champagne would be a dream night out, while some pointed out that his success depended on spotting talent, which not every investor can do.
