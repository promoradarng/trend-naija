Three dead, 14 in hospital after drinking herbal mixtures in Benue

6 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

At least three people have reportedly died and 14 others have been taken to hospital after drinking herbal mixtures suspected to contain harmful substances in Benue State.

What happened

According to reports on Friday, 6 November 2026, three men hawking the herbal preparations sold them to residents during a market day in Oju Township, Oju Local Government Area, last weekend. A resident said panic broke out on Monday, 2 November, when several people who had taken the mixtures began showing similar symptoms. One person died on Tuesday, a second early on Wednesday and a third on Thursday. Fourteen others are being treated at the General Hospital in Oju, four of them reportedly in a critical condition.

Suspects arrested

The chairman of Oju Local Government Area, Jackson Ominyi, confirmed the incident and said the suspected sellers had been arrested and taken to the state police headquarters in Makurdi. He said three deaths had been recorded as of Thursday, but that he did not yet have a final figure. The suspects have not been charged in court, and the substances in the mixtures have not been officially identified.

Background

Unregulated herbal mixtures are widely sold in markets and on the streets across Nigeria, often with claims that they cure many illnesses. Health authorities, including the food and drug regulator NAFDAC, have repeatedly warned Nigerians against buying unregistered medicines from hawkers.

What happens next

Residents are urging the authorities to investigate and to stop the sale of unregulated herbal products in the area.

  • The suspected sellers have been arrested.

DSS intercepts weapons smuggled on camels from Niger Republic into Sokoto

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The DSS has reportedly intercepted a consignment of weapons, including 21 AK-47 rifles, four machine guns and 825 magazines, allegedly being smuggled on camels from Niger Republic to bandits in north-west Nigeria.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) has intercepted a large consignment of weapons and ammunition allegedly being smuggled from Niger Republic to armed bandits in north-west Nigeria.

What was seized

According to security sources quoted in reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the interception took place on Tuesday, 10 November, at Dawakawa in Goronyo Local Government Area of Sokoto State. Operatives reportedly recovered 21 AK-47 rifles, four PKT machine guns and 825 magazines. The suspected smugglers were said to have been using camels to move the weapons along remote routes to avoid detection.

Why it matters

Security analysts described the seizure as a significant blow to an alleged cross-border arms supply network that is suspected of arming bandit groups in the North-West. The use of camels shows how criminal networks are using unconventional methods to move weapons across the long, porous border with Niger. Some reports described the operation as foiling a major terror plot.

Arrests

It is not clear whether any suspects were arrested.

Cooking gas now up to ₦1,800 per kg in Lagos, Ogun and Oyo

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Retail cooking gas prices have reportedly jumped from about ₦1,200 to ₦1,800 per kilogramme in parts of Lagos, Ogun and Oyo within a month, after depot prices rose by nearly 32 per cent. Some households say they are turning to charcoal.

Read more

Households in Lagos, Ogun and Oyo states are paying much more for cooking gas after a sharp rise in prices over the past month.

The new prices

According to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026, retail prices of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) at several outlets rose from around ₦1,200 to ₦1,800 per kilogramme. In parts of Ogun State, including Magboro, Mowe and Ibafo, some residents said they were paying as much as ₦1,900 per kg. In Oyo State, prices reportedly range from about ₦1,450 per kg at direct outlets to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,650 at neighbourhood retailers.

Why prices rose

The increase follows a reported 31.8 per cent rise in depot prices, from ₦1,100 to ₦1,450 per kg, which has pushed up costs along the supply chain.

Impact on households

Some households say they are considering returning to charcoal because they can no longer afford gas.

Background

The latest increase comes even as petrol prices have eased slightly, with Dangote Refinery reportedly cutting its petrol price this week.

Court remands journalist Daniel Ojukwu in Abuja prison over cybercrime charges

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu has been remanded in prison in Abuja on a 12-count cybercrime charge linked to his reports about a presidential aide, pending a bail hearing. The decision has renewed concerns about press freedom.

Read more

A court in Abuja has ordered that investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu be held in prison pending a hearing on his bail application.

The charges

Ojukwu, who works for the Foundation for Investigative Journalism, was arraigned on a 12-count charge of allegedly breaching Nigeria's Cybercrimes Act, and the court ordered his remand on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The charges relate to reports he published about Adejoke Orelope-Adefulire, who was senior special assistant on the Sustainable Development Goals under former President Muhammadu Buhari and now holds the same role under President Bola Tinubu. The charges have not been tested at trial.

Press freedom concerns

The decision drew widespread criticism from media freedom advocates, who have long argued that the cybercrimes law is used to silence journalists. According to the Committee to Protect Journalists, at least 29 journalists have been prosecuted under the law since it was enacted in 2015. Nigeria is ranked 112th out of 180 countries in the 2026 World Press Freedom Index compiled by Reporters Without Borders.

Background

Ojukwu was previously detained by police for several days in 2024 over a report on the same official, a case that also drew national and international criticism.

Bishop David Oyedepo, founder of Living Faith Church Worldwide, has drawn wide reactions online after a video showed him criticising fake pastors in Nigeria.

What he said

In the clip, which spread on social media on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the cleric was addressing a gathering that included several dignitaries. He said the number of fraudulent ministers in the country was alarming and that genuine Christians want to be counted as people of faith. He also reflected on his own ministry, which he said spans nearly 50 years. The occasion appeared to be a formal church celebration, but his comments on fake pastors became the main talking point.

How people reacted

The comments drew mixed reactions on social media.

Background

Oyedepo is one of Nigeria's most influential church leaders, and his comments regularly spark debate. Last week he warned politicians that the evil they fail to stop will eventually stop them, and told anyone with plans to Islamise Nigeria to drop them.
