Tinubu defends new loans, says debt should be judged by what it builds

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has responded to criticism of his government's borrowing plans, saying the real test of debt is what it produces rather than how large it is. His essay, titled 'From Reforms to Delivery', was released in Abuja on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and shared on X by his adviser on information and strategy, Bayo Onanuga.

What he said

Tinubu reportedly argued that the key question is not how much Nigeria borrows but what the country builds and leaves behind. He listed projects including the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway, the Sokoto–Badagry Superhighway, the Trans-Sahara Highway, upgrades to the electricity grid and the modernisation of ports in Lagos, as well as spending on health, education and skills. He said the coastal highway alone could cut logistics costs on the corridor by 15 to 20 per cent, add up to 2 per cent to GDP and create about 100,000 construction jobs.

The figures he cited

The President pointed to growth of 4.43 per cent in the second quarter, inflation of 14.63 per cent in September compared with about 21 per cent a year earlier, external reserves of about $55 billion and a tax-to-GDP ratio of 12.5 per cent. He credited the removal of the petrol subsidy, the unification of exchange rates and tax reforms. He said a $1 trillion economy by 2030 cannot run on infrastructure built for a much smaller economy, and named food affordability and energy prices among his priorities for the next phase.

The criticism

The essay follows the President's request to the Senate for approval to borrow about $20.5 billion and €1 billion. Atiku Abubakar, the ADC presidential candidate, said the request proved an earlier warning he gave, and has accused the government of piling up debt, which he says now stands at about ₦167 trillion. He has asked for details of how previous loans were spent.

What happens next

The Senate is yet to approve the new borrowing plan, and the debate over debt is expected to be a major theme of the 2027 campaign.

  • Tinubu says loans should be judged by what they build.
  • He cites inflation of 14.63% and reserves of about $55bn.
  • Atiku says debt has climbed to about ₦167 trillion.

US Vice-President JD Vance is reportedly reconsidering whether to run for president in 2028.

What is reported

According to reports on Friday, 13 November 2026, citing people close to him, Vance has not made a final decision about entering the race after the Republican Party's poor performance in the midterm elections earlier this month. Allies and donors are said to be questioning whether the party can hold on to the White House. A bigger question

Sweden returns 39 Benin Bronzes to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Sweden has handed back 39 Benin artefacts taken during the British invasion of Benin in 1897, at a ceremony in Lagos. Six of the objects will stay in Sweden on a 10-year loan.

Read more

Sweden has returned 39 Benin Bronzes and other artefacts to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted from the Benin Kingdom.

The handover

Representatives of Sweden's National Museums of World Culture formally handed over the objects at a ceremony in Lagos on Thursday, 12 November 2026. They will be received by Nigeria's National Commission for Museums and Monuments. The collection includes cultural objects known as Emwin Arre. Six of the 39 artefacts will remain in Sweden under a 10-year loan arrangement.

Why it matters

The objects were among thousands taken by British forces who attacked and burned Benin City in 1897 before bringing the kingdom under British rule. Michael Barrett, curator for Africa at Sweden's National Museums of World Culture, described the return as an important step in addressing colonial-era looting of cultural property.

Background

Nigeria has been campaigning for decades for the return of the Benin Bronzes, which are held in museums and private collections around the world. The Netherlands returned 119 objects in 2025, and Germany handed over 20 Benin Bronzes in 2022. Many more remain abroad.

ECOWAS names ex-Ghana president Akufo-Addo to lead observers for Nigeria's 2027 polls

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

ECOWAS has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Akufo-Addo to head its election observation mission for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections on 16 January 2027.

Read more

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to lead its election observation mission to Nigeria.

The appointment

The appointment was announced in a statement from Akufo-Addo's office on Thursday, 12 November 2026. He will oversee the deployment of ECOWAS observers for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections, scheduled for 16 January 2027. The mission will monitor the electoral process and report on how the polls are conducted.

Who is Akufo-Addo

Akufo-Addo served two terms as Ghana's president, from 2017 to January 2025.

Background

International attention on Nigeria's election is growing. The European Union has raised concerns about violence and hate speech, and a senior US State Department official told Congress this week that the vote could spur violence. INEC says preparations are on track, with non-sensitive materials already being moved to the states.
