Pro-Tinubu group warns APC that Imo West row could cost it South East support

31 Oct 2026, 20:42 GMT

A pro-Tinubu support group has warned the All Progressives Congress (APC) that the dispute over its candidate for the Imo West Senatorial District could hurt the party in the South East ahead of 2027.

What the group said

In a statement signed by its national president, Gbenga Owojori, and reported on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the Renewed Hope Foot Soldiers for Tinubu reacted to the Federal High Court ruling that recognised former governor Rochas Okorocha, rather than Governor Hope Uzodimma, as the APC candidate for Imo West. The group argued that shutting out candidates from the South East could weaken the coalition President Bola Tinubu needs for re-election.

Its demands

The group called on party leaders to resolve the matter through inclusive dialogue and transparent party processes, and said Uzodimma should be given a fair chance to pursue his ambitions, including a possible bid for the Senate presidency. It also made claims, which it did not support with evidence, that some senior political figures were working against Uzodimma. Those named have not responded to the claims.

Background

Okorocha went to court claiming he won the APC primary on 18 May, while the party's returning officer declared Uzodimma the winner. The court ordered INEC to publish Okorocha's name, and former APC national chairman Abdullahi Ganduje later visited Okorocha to congratulate him. The case also raises the question of whether a sitting governor can contest a Senate seat while still in office. Uzodimma has not said publicly whether he will appeal.

What happens next

The dispute is expected to continue in court and within the party, with both camps seeking the backing of the national leadership.

  • The group says the row could cost the APC South East support.
  • It wants Uzodimma to be allowed to pursue his ambitions.
  • A court recognised Okorocha as the APC candidate.

Four killed as gunmen attack Omo-Agege's NDC campaign in Delta

23 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Gunmen opened fire on former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege and his campaign team in Uduere, Ughelli North, Delta State, on Monday. His campaign says four people were killed and four others critically injured, and that the senator escaped unhurt.

Read more

Four people were killed and several others injured when gunmen attacked a Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) campaign event in Uduere, Ughelli North Local Government Area of Delta State, on Monday, 23 November 2026.

What happened

According to his campaign organisation, armed men opened fire at close range on former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege, the NDC's Delta Central senatorial candidate, and his entourage while he was addressing a crowd that had gathered to welcome him. The campaign said the senator was not hit, although bullets passed close to him.

The victims

The Omo-Agege Campaign Organisation said four people died and four others were critically injured and receiving treatment. It named two of the dead as aides of the campaign.

'Assassination attempt'

In a statement by its media adviser, the campaign described the incident as a targeted attempt on the senator's life rather than a political clash, citing eyewitnesses who said the attackers made clear he was their target.

What happens next

There was no word in the reports on arrests or a police statement. The attack comes as campaigns for the 2027 elections gather pace.

Tinubu tells Igbo leaders in Lagos he was upset over 2023 votes but they have settled

23 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

President Tinubu reportedly told Igbo stakeholders in Lagos that he was angry that some of them did not vote for him in 2023, but said the matter had been settled. He urged them to collect their PVCs and vote for him on 16 January.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has reportedly told Igbo leaders and stakeholders in Lagos that he expects their votes in the coming presidential election.

What he said

Speaking in an informal tone at a meeting on Monday, 23 November 2026, the president acknowledged that some of those present did not vote for him in 2023. He said he had been upset about it at the time, but that both sides had since settled their differences.

He urged them to register, collect their Permanent Voter Cards and vote on 16 January, reminding them of what he described as an agreement between them, and stressing that he is the APC's candidate.

Background

Lagos has a large Igbo population. The NDC's Peter Obi is among Tinubu's main challengers for 2027.

APC says letter asking INEC to guarantee Tinubu's 2027 win is fake

23 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The APC has dismissed a letter circulating online that claimed its national chairman asked INEC to ensure President Tinubu is declared winner in 2027. The party called it fake and malicious.

Read more

The All Progressives Congress (APC) has rejected a letter circulating on social media that claimed to be from its National Chairman, Professor Nentawe Yilwatda, asking the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to guarantee President Bola Tinubu's victory in the 2027 election.

What the letter claimed

The document, dated 22 November 2026 and addressed to INEC Chairman Professor Joash Amupitan, purported to ask the commission to use its administrative and operational machinery to make sure Tinubu was declared winner of the next presidential election.

The party's response

In a statement on Monday, the chairman's special adviser on media described the letter as fake and malicious, and a calculated attempt to damage the reputation of the party's leadership and weaken trust in democratic institutions. The party said the letter did not come from Yilwatda or his office, and pointed to features missing from the document that would appear on genuine correspondence.

Background

The row comes as campaigns for the January 2027 elections intensify.

Abure-led Labour Party faction endorses Tinubu for 2027

23 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Labour Party faction led by Julius Abure has reportedly endorsed President Tinubu for re-election. Abure said the faction would mobilise the votes the party won in 2023 behind the president.

Read more

A faction of the Labour Party led by Julius Abure has reportedly endorsed President Bola Tinubu for re-election in the 2027 general election.

The endorsement

Abure announced the decision on Monday, 23 November 2026, after a meeting of the faction's National Executive Council in Abuja. The faction is said to have described Tinubu as the most suitable candidate to lead Nigeria beyond 2027, pointing to his political experience and his time as governor of Lagos State.

Abure also reportedly pledged to mobilise the six million votes the Labour Party claims it won in the 2023 presidential election in support of the president.

Background

Earlier this month, Abure said he would not support Peter Obi, the party's 2023 presidential candidate, who is now running on the NDC platform. He had said he was open to working with either Tinubu or Atiku Abubakar, depending on how the party's leadership dispute was resolved. The Labour Party has been split by a long-running leadership tussle.

How people reacted

The endorsement drew criticism and sarcastic comments on social media, with many users questioning the faction's motives.
