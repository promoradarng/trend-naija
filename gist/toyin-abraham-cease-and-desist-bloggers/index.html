Toyin Abraham issues cease-and-desist notice to bloggers over 'false' posts

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Nollywood actress and filmmaker Toyin Abraham has warned bloggers and social media users that she will take legal action over publications she considers false and damaging.

The notice

On Thursday, 5 November 2026, the actress shared on X a cease-and-desist notice issued by her lawyer, Tope Salami, according to reports. The notice, addressed to the general public and particularly bloggers, content creators and influencers, demanded that people stop circulating unverified claims about her and remove existing posts. It warned that digital materials could be used as evidence in possible court proceedings. The notice did not specify which posts or claims it was referring to.

Background

Toyin Abraham has been the subject of much online discussion in recent weeks, including over a reported rift with fellow actress Funke Akindele after an old TikTok collaboration resurfaced. This week she also asked fans to call her by her former name, Toyin Aimakhu, as she promotes her new film 'Bestie', which is due in cinemas in December. Several celebrities, including Senator Ned Nwoko on behalf of Regina Daniels, have recently threatened legal action against people spreading false stories online.

How people reacted

Some fans backed her decision, saying celebrities deserve protection from lies, while others said public figures should expect criticism.

  • Her lawyer issued the notice on 5 November.
  • It demands that false posts be removed.
  • It warns of possible court action.

Nollywood actor IK Ogbonna has publicly criticised Edo State governor Monday Okpebholo over the condition of roads in the state.

What he said

In posts on his Instagram Stories on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the actor said he had just travelled by road through Edo and was shocked by what he saw. "Your approach was inappropriate": South African presidency fires back at Fani-Kayode over livestream complaint

14 Nov 2026, 09:27 GMT

South Africa's presidency has accused Nigeria's High Commissioner, Femi Fani-Kayode, of misrepresenting what happened when his speech was cut off at the ceremony where he presented his credentials to President Cyril Ramaphosa, describing his public complaint as inappropriate.

Read more

The South African presidency has criticised Nigeria's High Commissioner to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, over his public complaint that his speech was cut off during the ceremony where he presented his letters of credence to President Cyril Ramaphosa.

Fani-Kayode's complaint

Fani-Kayode had raised concerns about the incident, suggesting that he was interrupted while addressing the gathering during the official diplomatic ceremony.

South Africa responds

The South African presidency has rejected his account. Responding to the controversy, President Ramaphosa's spokesperson, Vincent Magwenya, accused Fani-Kayode of misrepresenting the situation and of wrongly dragging the South African President into a matter that did not require his involvement.

Magwenya also described the Nigerian diplomat's handling of the issue as inappropriate.

A routine ceremony turned row

The disagreement has drawn attention to what should have been a routine diplomatic ceremony between two African countries.

Diplomatic engagements are usually guided by established protocols, and disagreements over procedure are generally expected to be handled through official channels rather than in public.

For a newly appointed high commissioner, the public exchange has raised questions about diplomatic conduct, and about how misunderstandings between representatives of friendly nations should be resolved.
