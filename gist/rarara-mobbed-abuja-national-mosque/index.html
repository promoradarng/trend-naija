Tinubu's praise singer Rarara mobbed at Abuja National Mosque

21 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Popular Hausa praise singer Dauda Kahutu, widely known as Rarara, was attacked by an angry crowd at the Abuja National Mosque during Friday prayers on 20 November 2026.

What happened

Rarara, a prominent supporter of President Bola Tinubu, was entering the mosque with plain-clothed and uniformed security personnel when a group of people rushed at him. Security operatives tried to hold the crowd back but could not fully contain them. At one point, the singer removed his cap, which carried Tinubu's insignia.

A video of the incident has spread widely online.

Context

The incident came a week after President Tinubu's convoy was pelted with stones after Friday prayers at a mosque in Garki, Abuja, an attack for which police arrested 78 people before the Presidency directed their release.

Reactions

Paul Ibe, media adviser to ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar, condemned the violence on X while also making a political point about the incident.

APC reaction

Former Imo East APC senatorial aspirant Belusochukwu Enwere condemned the attack, describing it as part of a growing pattern of hostility towards supporters of the ruling party and a threat to democratic stability ahead of 2027.

  • The attack happened during Friday prayers.
  • Security operatives struggled to contain the crowd.
  • It came a week after the Tinubu convoy incident.

El-Rufai tells court he never tapped Ribadu's phone

25 Nov 2026, 11:41 GMT

Former Kaduna Governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly told a Federal High Court in Abuja that he never intercepted the phone calls of National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu. He opened his defence on Wednesday.

Read more

Former Kaduna State Governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly told a Federal High Court in Abuja that he never intercepted the telephone conversations of National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu, and never asked anyone to do so for him.

In court

El-Rufai opened his defence on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, before Justice Joyce Abdulmalik, after his no-case submission was dismissed in October. Led in evidence by his lawyer, he told the court that he had never owned equipment capable of intercepting calls and had no technical knowledge of how such interception works.

He also denied having any access to the NSA's communication networks or personal devices.

How the case began

The case arises from remarks El-Rufai made in a television interview on 13 February 2026. He denies the charges.

What happens next

The trial continues as El-Rufai presents his defence.

'Political rhetoric'

El-Rufai told the court that his 'tapped' remark was political rhetoric. The court later adjourned the trial to February to allow his defence to continue.

Court freezes $100m linked to Governors' Forum in Paris Club fees dispute

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

An Abuja High Court has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum, pending a dispute over legal and consultancy fees tied to Paris Club refunds. The order does not decide who owns the money.

Read more

A High Court of the Federal Capital Territory in Abuja has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum (NGF), in a dispute over unpaid legal and consultancy fees connected to the Paris Club refunds.

The order

Justice O.C. Agbaza granted the interim freezing order on 19 November 2026, following an application by a lawyer against the NGF's incorporated trustees and 29 commercial banks. The order covers a single total sum of $100 million, about ₦135.6 billion, across the affected accounts.

What it means

The order preserves the disputed money until the court hears an application for an injunction or makes a further order. It does not decide who owns the funds.

The claim

The lawyer is seeking payment under an indemnity he says the NGF issued in December 2018, after the federal government paid $100 million to the forum to hold pending a decision on liability for professional fees. No response from the NGF was included in the reports.

Brymo urges Tinubu to name National Theatre after Pete Edochie

25 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Singer Brymo has called on President Tinubu to rename the National Theatre in Lagos after the late Pete Edochie, while saying Wole Soyinka does not deserve the honour. His remarks have sparked heated debate.

Read more

Singer Brymo has sparked a heated online debate after calling on President Bola Tinubu to rename the National Theatre in Lagos in honour of the late Nollywood legend Pete Edochie.

What he said

In a video posted on Instagram on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, Brymo addressed the president directly, urging him to immortalise Edochie by attaching his name to the iconic landmark.

He also argued that Nobel laureate Wole Soyinka, whose name has been linked with the National Theatre, should not receive the honour, and spoke critically of the writer.

Background

Pete Edochie died in Enugu early on Wednesday at the age of 79, after a long battle with prostate cancer. Tributes have poured in from President Tinubu, state governors and colleagues across the film industry.

How people reacted

Brymo's comments have sparked heated debate online.
