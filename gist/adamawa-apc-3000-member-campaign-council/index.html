Adamawa APC inaugurates 3,000-member campaign council, receives ADC and Labour defectors

31 Oct 2026, 09:42 GMT

The All Progressives Congress (APC) in Adamawa State has inaugurated a campaign council of more than 3,000 members for the 2027 general elections, according to reports on Saturday, 31 October 2026.

What happened

Governor Ahmadu Umaru Fintiri reportedly inaugurated the council at the Banquet Hall of Government House in Yola on Thursday, 29 October. Retired General Buba Marwa was named co-chairman, and the council includes representatives from the state's three senatorial zones and its communities. At the same event, a group of defectors from the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and the Labour Party was received into the APC and presented to the state party chairman, Hamza Bello Madagali, by the party's North-East national vice chairman, Idris Shuaibu.

What was said

Speakers reportedly said the party would leave nothing to chance in its bid to win the state, and Fintiri warned that anti-party behaviour would not be tolerated. Madagali acknowledged that some stakeholders had been left off the initial list and appealed for patience.

Background

Adamawa is the home state of former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, the ADC presidential candidate, which makes the contest there especially significant. Buba Marwa is a former military governor of Lagos State and has headed the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency. Party switching has been common across the country in the run-up to the elections, with governors and lawmakers moving between parties.

What happens next

The council is expected to begin campaigning across the state's local government areas in the coming weeks.

  • Defectors from the ADC and Labour Party were received.

Court voids ADC governorship nomination in Nasarawa, orders fresh primary in Karu

6 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has nullified the nomination of retired Major General Nuhu Angbazo as the ADC governorship candidate in Nasarawa State, ruling that the primary was incomplete because Karu LGA was left out.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has set aside the nomination of retired Major General Nuhu Angbazo as the African Democratic Congress (ADC) governorship candidate in Nasarawa State.

The judgment

In a ruling reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Justice Mohammed Umar held that the party's governorship primary was inconclusive because results from Karu Local Government Area were not included. The judge said shutting out party members in Karu meant the exercise could not validly produce a candidate. The case was brought by a former Minister of Environment, Mohammed Abdullahi, who took part in the primary alongside Angbazo and Senator Mohammed Onawo, who represents Nasarawa South.

Orders

The court ordered the ADC to hold a fresh primary in Karu, restrained Angbazo from presenting himself as the party's candidate, and barred the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) from recognising him as such. The plaintiff's lawyer welcomed the ruling and said the party must now complete the primary properly before any candidate can be recognised.

Background

The ruling adds to the ADC's legal troubles. This week, another Federal High Court judgment removed the party's David Mark-led national leadership and voided Atiku Abubakar's presidential nomination, a ruling the party has said it will challenge. Nasarawa is governed by the APC's Abdullahi Sule, who is completing his second term. Angbazo has not publicly said whether he will appeal.

What happens next

Unless the ruling is overturned on appeal, the ADC will have to return to Karu to finish the primary before it can field a governorship candidate in the state.

Nollywood actor and politician Kenneth Okonkwo says the NDC is facing more serious problems than the ADC, and insists Atiku Abubakar will be on the ballot in 2027. He spoke days after a Federal High Court ruling nullified the emergence of the ADC's national chairman.

Read more

Nigeria's opposition politics is getting more interesting as the battle for relevance ahead of the 2027 general elections continues.

What Okonkwo said

Veteran Nollywood actor and politician Kenneth Okonkwo has stirred another round of debate after claiming that the NDC is facing more serious problems than the African Democratic Congress (ADC).

According to Okonkwo, former Vice President Atiku Abubakar will still be on the ballot in the 2027 presidential election.

"NDC is facing more problems than ADC. Atiku Abubakar will be on the ballot," he said.

An interesting twist

Okonkwo is making this prediction at a time when the leadership of his own party, the ADC, is facing a major legal setback after a Federal High Court in Abuja nullified the emergence of its national chairman and the actions taken under his leadership.

That raises an obvious question. If his own party is battling a leadership crisis in court, how certain can anyone be about what will happen to other opposition parties before 2027?

Politics full of surprises

Nigerian politics has a way of surprising even those who believe they understand the game. Alliances can change overnight, party leaders can fall out, and court judgments can completely change the direction of an election.

For Atiku, who has contested the presidency several times, whether he will appear on the ballot again remains a major subject of discussion. Okonkwo's prediction should be understood as his political opinion rather than confirmation of Atiku's candidacy.

As 2027 approaches, Nigerians are watching closely to see whether opposition politicians will eventually unite behind a common candidate or continue to pursue separate presidential ambitions.

Wike mocks Peter Obi: 'Vote for him as president of social media, not Nigeria'

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has told Rivers voters that Peter Obi is popular on social media but lacks the capacity to lead Nigeria, also mocking the NDC candidate's habit of carrying his own bag. Obi's supporters hit back online.

Read more

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has taken another swipe at Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC).

What Wike said

Speaking at a town hall meeting in Abonnema, Akuku-Toru Local Government Area of Rivers State, on Thursday, 5 November 2026, Wike reportedly said that people who wanted a president of social media should vote for Obi, but that he should not be voted for as president of Nigeria. He also mocked Obi's well-known habit of carrying his own bag, saying it did not show that he had the capacity to rule the country.

Background

Wike, a former Rivers governor, leads the Rainbow Coalition, a group of PDP and other politicians backing President Bola Tinubu's re-election in the presidential election scheduled for 16 January 2027. He has repeatedly criticised Obi in recent weeks. Obi, a former Anambra governor, has a large following among young voters, especially online, and is running with Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate. Wike made the comments during a busy week in which he also spoke about Governor Siminalayi Fubara's political future and denied any role in the court ruling on the Imo West Senate ticket.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters responded angrily online, with many saying Wike could not campaign without mentioning Obi and describing the former governor's simple lifestyle as a strength. Supporters of the minister said Obi's online popularity had not translated into votes in Rivers State in the past. Obi's campaign had not publicly responded to the latest remarks at the time of writing.

Former INEC director asks how 1.5 million questionable records got into the voter register

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, a former INEC director of voter education, says it should not have been possible for about 1.5 million questionable entries to enter the voter register, and wants INEC to explain how they were detected and how they got in.

Read more

A former senior official of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has questioned how about 1.5 million questionable records found their way into the country's voter register.

What he said

Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, a former Director of Voter Education and Publicity at INEC, said in a television interview reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, that such problems should not happen given the safeguards the commission has put in place. He noted that the law on who can register is simple: a Nigerian citizen aged 18 or above who turns up in person to register. Because registration requires physical presence and the capture of fingerprints and photographs, he said, he could not understand how so many questionable entries had been made.

His questions for INEC

Osaze-Uzzi said the commission, led by its chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, had not explained what period its review of the register covered or how the problem entries were first detected. He said tools such as the Automated Fingerprint Identification System and artificial intelligence can help to remove duplicates, but that INEC should still give a clear account of how the records got into the database in the first place.

Background

INEC regularly cleans up the register to remove duplicate, underage and other invalid entries before elections, and the issue of the accuracy of the register has long been debated. The 2027 general elections are due in January, with the presidential poll on 16 January. INEC also urged young Nigerians this week to turn their online enthusiasm into votes, saying votes cannot be counted on social media.

Why it matters

A clean voter register is central to public confidence in elections. Civil society groups have urged INEC to publish more detail on the clean-up so that eligible voters are not wrongly removed.
