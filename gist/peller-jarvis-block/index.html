VeryDarkMan brings out cash counting machine after claiming record ₦90 million sales in one day

1 Nov 2026, 16:09 GMT

VeryDarkMan has brought out a cash counting machine to count money reportedly received from sales of his RATEL products, after saying the business made ₦90 million in a single day. He has also promised to make his bank statement public to clear any doubts.

Read more

Social media activist and businessman VeryDarkMan (VDM) has once again got people talking, this time after bringing out a cash counting machine to count money reportedly received from the sale of his RATEL products.

Photos show him and a colleague surrounded by bundles of naira notes, with an open suitcase full of cash, stacks of money on cartons of RATEL power stations and a counting machine beside them.

A record day

According to VDM, the business recorded about ₦90 million in sales in one day, the biggest single-day figure since the products were launched.

Ready to show proof

VDM knows how Nigerians think. Once you mention ₦90 million in one day, some people will immediately start asking questions, while others will simply refuse to believe it.

To clear the doubts, he has promised to go a step further and make his bank account statement public so people can see the transactions for themselves. He appeared confident about the figures and made it clear he was ready to back up his claims with evidence.

A fast-growing brand

What makes this development interesting is how quickly the RATEL business appears to be gaining attention. From importing products from China to demonstrating how they perform in public, VDM has continued to attract customers and supporters.

However, the ₦90 million figure has yet to be independently verified, and sales revenue should not be confused with profit.

Social media clout without financial growth is a wasted opportunity: what Daniel Regha can learn from VDM's RATEL

1 Nov 2026, 09:07 GMT

Daniel Regha has questioned VeryDarkMan's move from activism into business with his RATEL brand. But VDM's shift raises a bigger question for every Nigerian influencer: what are you building with all that attention?

Read more

Daniel Regha has weighed in on VeryDarkMan's growing RATEL business, writing on X that VDM had gone "from activist, to NGO business, to CEO" and "from criticizing business owners to joining the same businesses to make profit."

His post drew mixed reactions. One user replied that anyone who has not yet understood VDM is to be pitied, while another asked what VDM is supposed to do with the money he makes from social media: give it all away and own nothing because he is an activist?

Influence without a business

Sometimes one wonders whether Daniel Regha sees the business opportunities sitting right in front of him. For someone who commands so much attention on X, sparks conversations almost daily and has built a considerable following, you would expect him to have turned that influence into something financially rewarding by now.

Perhaps the fear of what people will say is holding him back.

You are only a tenant

Many influencers forget that they do not own these platforms. They are simply tenants. Facebook, X, TikTok and Instagram can suspend an account, restrict its reach or change their policies overnight, and years of gathering followers could suddenly amount to very little.

What VDM seems to understand

VeryDarkMan appears to understand something many influencers have yet to figure out: popularity has an expiry date. The same people cheering you today may move on to another personality tomorrow.

Instead of depending entirely on public attention, he is gradually converting his influence into a business. He is introducing products, building a customer base and creating something that could keep generating income long after the social media excitement fades. That is smart thinking.

People will always talk

Imagine spending years building an audience of hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of people, only to have nothing financially sustainable to show for it.

If you have the audience, build a business. Sell products, offer services, create something valuable and earn legitimate money from the influence you have worked hard to establish. If you sell something, people will criticise you. If you remain broke, those same people will still have something to say. The people whose opinions you fear will not come together to pay your rent, feed your family or settle your bills.

The real question

The advice to Daniel Regha and other social media personalities is simple: stop treating online popularity as a lifetime achievement, and find a way to turn that attention into lasting value. VDM may have his critics, but his decision to venture into business deserves recognition.

Because when the followers stop cheering and the cameras stop rolling, the real question will be: what did you build with all that influence?

What do you think? Should influencers concentrate on entertaining their followers, or should they use their popularity to build businesses?

Police officers feared dead as bandits ambush bus heading to Zamfara APC primary

3 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Bandits ambushed a bus carrying police officers on the Sokoto–Gusau road in Zamfara State on Monday. The officers were travelling to provide security for an APC senatorial primary, and some are feared dead while others were reportedly abducted.

Read more

Several police officers are feared dead after suspected bandits attacked a bus carrying them along the Sokoto–Gusau road in Zamfara State on Monday, 2 November 2026.

What happened

The officers were travelling in a state mass transit bus, known locally as Bazamfara, towards Gusau, the state capital. According to police sources, they were on their way to provide security for the All Progressives Congress (APC) primary election for the Zamfara North senatorial district, which was taking place in Kaura Namoda Local Government Area. Sources said some officers were killed in the ambush and others were abducted by the attackers. The exact number of casualties had not been confirmed at the time of reporting.

Official response

The spokesman for the police zone covering the area reportedly confirmed the attack, but said he was in hospital and would issue an official statement later. The identities of the victims have not been released.

Background

Zamfara is one of the states worst affected by banditry in north-west Nigeria, with frequent attacks on travellers, villages and security personnel. Police officers are often deployed to provide security at party primaries and campaign events, and several political gatherings have been attacked in recent weeks as the 2027 elections approach. In neighbouring Niger State, gunmen abducted 14 Islamiyya students and their teacher at the weekend.

What happens next

Security agencies are expected to launch an operation to rescue the abducted officers, and the police are expected to release casualty figures.
