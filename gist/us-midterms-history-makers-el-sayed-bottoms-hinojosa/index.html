First Muslim senator and first Black woman governor among US midterm history-makers

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The 2026 US midterm elections produced several historic firsts, according to results and projections reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

First Muslim senator

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed reportedly defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race, becoming the first Muslim member of the US Senate. A former Detroit health director, he ran on a progressive platform including Medicare for All and higher taxes on billionaires, and won the seat left by retiring Democrat Gary Peters. His campaign survived a bitter contest in which a Democratic rival he beat in the primary endorsed his Republican opponent.

First Black woman governor

In Georgia, former Atlanta mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms, 56, defeated Republican businessman Rick Jackson, becoming the first Black woman elected governor of any US state and Georgia's first female governor. Her victory reportedly ends 24 years of Republican control of the office. Atlanta-born Nigerian singer Davido shared the news on his Instagram Story.

Texas turns blue

In Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa reportedly defeated Republican governor Greg Abbott, who was seeking a fourth term, by about 53.5 per cent to 46.5 per cent. When she takes office in January 2027, the 52-year-old will be the state's first Latina governor and its first Democratic governor since Ann Richards left office in 1995.

More firsts

In New Hampshire, Democratic congressman Chris Pappas, 46, defeated former Republican senator John E. Sununu to become the first openly gay man elected to the US Senate, keeping the seat of retiring Democrat Jeanne Shaheen. In New Mexico, Democrat Deb Haaland, a member of Laguna Pueblo and a former US interior secretary, won the governorship, becoming the first Native American woman elected governor of a US state.

Kansas breaks a long streak

In Kansas, Democrat Adam Hamilton, a pastor who leads the largest United Methodist church in the country, reportedly defeated Republican senator Roger Marshall, becoming the first Democrat elected to the US Senate from the state since 1932. He ran as a moderate and won endorsements from some prominent Kansas Republicans.

Background

The results came as Democrats were projected to win control of both the House and the Senate, in what many analysts described as a rebuke of President Donald Trump's second-term agenda amid concern over the economy, tariffs and the conflict with Iran.

  • Abdul El-Sayed is the first Muslim US senator.
  • Keisha Lance Bottoms is the first Black woman elected governor of a US state.
  • Chris Pappas and Deb Haaland also made history.

JD Vance reportedly reconsidering 2028 presidential run after midterm losses

13 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

US Vice-President JD Vance is reportedly rethinking whether to run for president in 2028, after the Republicans' heavy defeat in this month's midterm elections led allies and donors to question the party's chances.

US Vice-President JD Vance is reportedly reconsidering whether to run for president in 2028.

What is reported

According to reports on Friday, 13 November 2026, citing people close to him, Vance has not made a final decision about entering the race after the Republican Party's poor performance in the midterm elections earlier this month. Allies and donors are said to be questioning whether the party can hold on to the White House. People close to him say he remains committed to the political movement that returned Donald Trump to power, but is increasingly asking whether he should be the one to lead it.

A bigger question

One ally reportedly said Vance has been wrestling with whether any Republican can win the presidency in the current political climate. Another noted that at 42, he could afford to wait for a better opportunity.

Background

Vance had been widely seen as the leading contender for the 2028 Republican nomination. In the 3 November midterms, the Democrats were projected to win control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate, and Trump has since replaced his chief of staff. Vance has not publicly commented on the reports.

Microsoft outage disrupts Outlook, Teams and Copilot in 74 countries

13 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

A global Microsoft 365 outage that began on Thursday has disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot for users in 74 countries and territories, with nearly 140,000 problem reports logged at its peak. Microsoft says it is working to restore services.

Read more

Microsoft is working to restore its services after a global outage disrupted Outlook email, Teams and its Copilot AI assistant for users around the world.

What happened

The outage began on Thursday, 12 November 2026, and affected Microsoft 365 services in 74 countries and territories, according to reports on Friday. Many users were unable to load their inboxes or send and receive emails, while others had trouble with Teams calls and Copilot. Reports of Outlook problems on an outage-tracking website peaked at nearly 140,000 worldwide.

Microsoft's explanation

Microsoft initially blamed the problem on unusually high traffic across parts of its infrastructure, which caused connection errors. The company said it had paused some deployments while it worked on a fix.

Why it matters

Outlook and Teams are widely used by businesses and organisations around the world, including in Nigeria.

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu becomes New Jersey's lieutenant governor

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu has been appointed lieutenant governor of the US state of New Jersey, becoming the first immigrant to hold the post. She will also serve as Secretary of State.

Read more

Shirley U. Emehelu, a lawyer born in Nigeria, has been appointed lieutenant governor of New Jersey in the United States.

The appointment

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill appointed Emehelu on Monday, 9 November 2026, making her the state's fifth lieutenant governor and the first immigrant to hold the office. She was sworn in at the Statehouse in Trenton by the state's Chief Justice, Stuart Rabner. The appointment also means that, for the first time in New Jersey's history, women hold the state's two highest executive offices at the same time. Emehelu will also serve as Secretary of State, a role that covers government administration and oversight of elections.

Why she was chosen

Sherrill described Emehelu as an experienced public servant and pointed to her record prosecuting financial crimes, protecting consumers, defending civil rights and tackling government waste. The governor also praised her leadership as acting Secretary of State, including overseeing the state's recent elections.

Her Nigerian roots

Emehelu, who moved from Nigeria to the United States as a child, said she was proud of what the appointment means for Black women, immigrants and people who share her Nigerian heritage, and expressed hope that it would inspire young people to serve. She said she would work with the governor on the cost of living and economic opportunity.
