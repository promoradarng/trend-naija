First Muslim senator and first Black woman governor among US midterm history-makers

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The 2026 US midterm elections produced several historic firsts, according to results and projections reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

First Muslim senator

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed reportedly defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race, becoming the first Muslim member of the US Senate. A former Detroit health director, he ran on a progressive platform including Medicare for All and higher taxes on billionaires, and won the seat left by retiring Democrat Gary Peters. His campaign survived a bitter contest in which a Democratic rival he beat in the primary endorsed his Republican opponent.

First Black woman governor

In Georgia, former Atlanta mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms, 56, defeated Republican businessman Rick Jackson, becoming the first Black woman elected governor of any US state and Georgia's first female governor. Her victory reportedly ends 24 years of Republican control of the office. Atlanta-born Nigerian singer Davido shared the news on his Instagram Story.

Texas turns blue

In Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa reportedly defeated Republican governor Greg Abbott, who was seeking a fourth term, by about 53.5 per cent to 46.5 per cent. When she takes office in January 2027, the 52-year-old will be the state's first Latina governor and its first Democratic governor since Ann Richards left office in 1995.

More firsts

In New Hampshire, Democratic congressman Chris Pappas, 46, defeated former Republican senator John E. Sununu to become the first openly gay man elected to the US Senate, keeping the seat of retiring Democrat Jeanne Shaheen. In New Mexico, Democrat Deb Haaland, a member of Laguna Pueblo and a former US interior secretary, won the governorship, becoming the first Native American woman elected governor of a US state.

Kansas breaks a long streak

In Kansas, Democrat Adam Hamilton, a pastor who leads the largest United Methodist church in the country, reportedly defeated Republican senator Roger Marshall, becoming the first Democrat elected to the US Senate from the state since 1932. He ran as a moderate and won endorsements from some prominent Kansas Republicans.

Background

The results came as Democrats were projected to win control of both the House and the Senate, in what many analysts described as a rebuke of President Donald Trump's second-term agenda amid concern over the economy, tariffs and the conflict with Iran.

  • Abdul El-Sayed is the first Muslim US senator.
  • Keisha Lance Bottoms is the first Black woman elected governor of a US state.
  • Chris Pappas and Deb Haaland also made history.

Iran reportedly gives singer Parastoo Ahmadi 74 lashes for performing without hijab

24 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Iranian authorities have reportedly flogged singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her team over an online concert she performed without a headscarf. Rights groups say they also received two-year bans on artistic work.

Read more

Iranian authorities have reportedly administered 74 lashes to the singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four members of her production team over a concert she performed without wearing the mandatory Islamic headscarf.

What happened

According to a human rights lawyer and monitoring groups, the punishment was carried out on Monday, 23 November 2026, at the prosecutor's office in the city of Qom.

The concert

The case stems from a virtual concert Ahmadi recorded in December 2024 at a historic caravanserai in Qom and streamed on YouTube. In the video, which was widely viewed, she performed traditional Persian music with male musicians while wearing a sleeveless dress with her hair uncovered, defying rules that ban women from singing solo in public and appearing without a hijab.

The sentence

A court found Ahmadi and four male musicians and crew members guilty of offending public decency by publishing what it called immoral content online. As well as the lashes, each of the five was reportedly given a two-year ban on artistic activity.

US adds 24 more Nigerians to 'worst of the worst' deportation list

24 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The US Department of Homeland Security has reportedly added 24 Nigerians to its list of foreign nationals arrested for serious crimes, bringing the number of Nigerians on it to 157. The allegations range from fraud to drug offences.

Read more

The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has reportedly added 24 Nigerians to its deportation list, bringing the total number of Nigerian nationals named to 157.

The list

DHS updated its 'Worst of the Worst' initiative on Monday, 23 November 2026. The list identifies foreign nationals arrested by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) for what it describes as serious crimes, and now includes more than 45,000 people.

The allegations

The people listed face allegations ranging from drug and weapons offences and violent crimes to fraud and the misuse of public benefits. Many of the Nigerians named were arrested over alleged wire fraud, mail fraud or impersonation in states including Texas, Virginia, New Jersey, Georgia and Indiana.

Background

Being named on the list does not mean a person has been convicted.

Fire breaks out at Will Smith and Jada Pinkett Smith's Calabasas estate

A fire destroyed a maintenance shed on Will Smith and Jada Pinkett Smith's $42 million Calabasas estate early on Monday. No one was hurt and officials said the cause was accidental.

A fire broke out early on Monday, 23 November 2026, at Will Smith and Jada Pinkett Smith's $42 million estate in Calabasas, California.

What happened

Sheriff's deputies were called shortly after midnight when security staff reported flames on the 150-acre property. They found a maintenance shed fully ablaze. Firefighters spent about two hours putting out the fire, which officials said did not spread to any other buildings.

No injuries

A sheriff's spokesperson said no one was injured and that the cause of the fire was accidental.

Background

It is the latest in a string of fires to affect the family's properties over the past eight years. Their biggest loss came in January 2025, when the Palisades Fire tore through parts of Malibu.

Democrats win biggest US House majority in more than three decades

23 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

US Democrats now hold 258 of 435 House seats after a recount in North Carolina, their largest majority since 1992. They also won the Senate in this month's midterm elections.

Read more

The Democratic Party has secured its largest majority in the US House of Representatives in more than 30 years, according to media projections.

The deciding race

US networks projected on Sunday, 22 November 2026, that Democrat Chuck Hubbard had defeated Republican incumbent Virginia Foxx in North Carolina after a recount requested by Foxx. The win gives Democrats 258 seats in the 435-member chamber, matching the number they won in 1992, the year Bill Clinton was elected president. Hubbard said Foxx had conceded and thanked her for her decades of service.

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries celebrated on X, saying the party now held the largest majority of any party in Congress this century.

Senate too

Democrats also won control of the Senate in this month's midterm elections, a major setback for President Donald Trump and the Republican Party.

What it means

Control of both chambers gives Democrats more power to block Trump's legislative agenda and launch investigations during the final two years of his presidency. The results were widely seen as reflecting voter frustration over living costs, tariffs and the conflict with Iran.
