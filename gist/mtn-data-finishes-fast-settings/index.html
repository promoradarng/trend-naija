Ebonyi women parade man in wheelbarrow over alleged repeated beating of his wife

7 Nov 2026, 18:58 GMT

Women in Ishiagu, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly paraded a man around the community in a wheelbarrow over claims that he repeatedly beat his wife. The unusual punishment has sparked debate about domestic violence and community justice.

Read more

Women in Ishiagu community, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly taken an unusual step to show their anger over domestic violence, publicly parading a man accused of repeatedly beating his wife.

Pushed around in a wheelbarrow

According to reports circulating online, the women placed the man in a wheelbarrow and pushed him around the community, apparently determined to make an example of him over his alleged treatment of his wife.

Photos show a large group of women surrounding the man, who sits in the wheelbarrow in a yellow T-shirt as several women push and guide it along a dirt path, with one carrying a calabash.

"Enough is enough"

The incident has drawn attention because of the unusual punishment the women chose. They appeared to have reached a point where they could no longer tolerate the alleged abuse.

In many communities, domestic violence is still treated as a private family matter, and victims are sometimes encouraged to endure abuse for the sake of their marriage. The women of Ishiagu reportedly decided that enough was enough.

Justice or humiliation?

Their action raises an important question about how communities should respond when a woman is repeatedly subjected to violence in her own home.

Many people may applaud the women for standing up against domestic abuse. Others may question whether publicly humiliating an accused person, who has not been tried, is the right approach.

The full circumstances of the incident, including the man's side of the story and whether the authorities have stepped in, remain unclear.

Jerry Eze's protocol officer sparks outrage at Ibiyeomie's church dedication

7 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A video of a member of Pastor Jerry Eze's security team reacting roughly to a man who knelt to greet the cleric at the Hand of God Cathedral dedication in Port Harcourt has drawn criticism online.

Read more

A video from the dedication of Pastor David Ibiyeomie's 120,000-seat Hand of God Cathedral in Port Harcourt has sparked debate online, after a member of Pastor Jerry Eze's security team was seen reacting roughly to a worshipper.

What the video shows

The footage, shared on Facebook on Friday, 6 November 2026, shows the founder of Streams of Joy International arriving at the event. A man dropped to his knees as a sign of respect as the cleric walked past, and Pastor Eze briefly acknowledged him. However, one of his protocol officers then reportedly acted towards the man in a way many viewers felt was unnecessarily aggressive and disrespectful.

Background

The dedication of the Hand of God Cathedral, led by Bishop David Oyedepo, drew many of Nigeria's best-known pastors. Another widely shared video from the same event showed Jerry Eze kneeling to greet Oyedepo, Ibiyeomie and other senior ministers. Eze is known for his popular online prayer programme, 'New Season Prophetic Prayers and Declarations', which draws large audiences on YouTube and social media.

How people reacted

Many Nigerians criticised the protocol officer's behaviour, saying it was out of place at a celebratory religious event and that church leaders' security teams should treat members of the public with respect. Pastor Eze had not publicly commented at the time of writing.

Flamingos get $50,000 reward, but no FIFA prize money for World Cup win

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nigeria's U-17 world champions, the Flamingos, have received a $50,000 cash gift from a sponsor of the National Sports Commission at a reception in Rabat. FIFA does not pay prize money for the tournament because it is classed as a developmental competition.

Read more

Nigeria's Flamingos have received a $50,000 cash reward after winning the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup, but the team will not receive any official prize money from FIFA.

The reward

The gift was presented by Alhaji Saleh Ahmadu, a sponsor of the National Sports Commission (NSC), at a reception held in the team's honour in Rabat, Morocco, on Saturday night, 7 November 2026, according to reports. The event was hosted by NSC chairman Shehu Dikko and director-general Bukola Olopade, and was also attended by the Comptroller-General of the Nigeria Customs Service, Bashir Adewale Adeniyi. The sponsor said the gift recognised the players' outstanding performance, commitment and determination.

No FIFA prize money

FIFA classifies the U-17 Women's World Cup as a developmental tournament and does not attach official prize money to it, unlike the senior World Cups. That means the champions receive the trophy and medals but no payment from world football's governing body.

How the final was won

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final on Saturday. Awawu Bashiru opened the scoring when her looping effort slipped through the hands of Japan's goalkeeper, and Oluebube Umejiaku doubled the lead with a lob after a through ball from Esther Stephen. Japan's Fuki Ito pulled one back with ten minutes left, but Nigeria held on to become the first African winners of the tournament.

Tinubu to inaugurate sections of Kaduna–Kano and Kano–Maradi railways on 3 December

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

President Tinubu will inaugurate completed sections of the Kaduna–Kano and Kano–Katsina–Maradi railway projects on 3 December, according to his campaign council. It says the Kaduna–Kano line is about 90% complete.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has fixed 3 December 2026 for the inauguration of completed sections of two major railway projects in northern Nigeria.

The projects

According to a statement on Sunday, 8 November 2026, by Dele Alake, spokesman of the APC Presidential Campaign Council, the 203-kilometre Kaduna–Kano Standard Gauge Railway is about 90 per cent complete, while the 400-kilometre Kano–Katsina–Maradi line, which will link Nigeria with the Republic of Niger, is about 70 per cent complete. The 190-kilometre Jaji-to-Kano segment of the Kaduna–Kano line and the 250-kilometre Dawanu–Kano–Kazaure–Daura–Katsina stretch of the Kano–Maradi line are due to be inaugurated on 3 December.

Progress claims

The statement said that when Tinubu took office in 2023, the Kano–Maradi project was barely 5 per cent complete and the Kaduna–Kano line under 30 per cent. It said a federal inspection team led by the Permanent Secretary of the Federal Ministry of Transportation, Funso Adebiyi, recently assessed both corridors and carried out a test run. The figures come from the President's campaign and have not been independently verified.

Background

The Kaduna–Kano line is part of the planned Lagos–Kano standard gauge railway, which already runs from Lagos to Ibadan and from Abuja to Kaduna. The Kano–Maradi project was started under former President Muhammadu Buhari.

Why it matters

The inauguration will come just weeks before the 16 January 2027 presidential election, and infrastructure is a key part of the President's re-election campaign.
