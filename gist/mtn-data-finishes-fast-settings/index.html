Ebonyi women parade man in wheelbarrow over alleged repeated beating of his wife

7 Nov 2026, 18:58 GMT

Women in Ishiagu, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly paraded a man around the community in a wheelbarrow over claims that he repeatedly beat his wife. The unusual punishment has sparked debate about domestic violence and community justice.

Read more

Women in Ishiagu community, Ivo Local Government Area of Ebonyi State, have reportedly taken an unusual step to show their anger over domestic violence, publicly parading a man accused of repeatedly beating his wife.

Pushed around in a wheelbarrow

According to reports circulating online, the women placed the man in a wheelbarrow and pushed him around the community, apparently determined to make an example of him over his alleged treatment of his wife.

Photos show a large group of women surrounding the man, who sits in the wheelbarrow in a yellow T-shirt as several women push and guide it along a dirt path, with one carrying a calabash.

"Enough is enough"

The incident has drawn attention because of the unusual punishment the women chose. They appeared to have reached a point where they could no longer tolerate the alleged abuse.

In many communities, domestic violence is still treated as a private family matter, and victims are sometimes encouraged to endure abuse for the sake of their marriage. The women of Ishiagu reportedly decided that enough was enough.

Justice or humiliation?

Their action raises an important question about how communities should respond when a woman is repeatedly subjected to violence in her own home.

Many people may applaud the women for standing up against domestic abuse. Others may question whether publicly humiliating an accused person, who has not been tried, is the right approach.

The full circumstances of the incident, including the man's side of the story and whether the authorities have stepped in, remain unclear.

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has reportedly promised a ₦10 million reward to each councillor in Nigeria whose ward votes for President Bola Tinubu in the 2027 presidential election.

What he said

Wike made the pledge during a political engagement in Kwali Area Council of the Federal Capital Territory, according to reports on Monday, 9 November 2026. He also praised his Rainbow Coalition, saying it was bringing together politicians from across the country, and urged residents of Kwali and the wider FCT to back the President's re-election, arguing that a second term would bring more development to Abuja.

Background

Wike, a former Rivers governor and PDP chieftain, leads the Rainbow Coalition of politicians from the PDP and other parties backing Tinubu. He has been one of the President's most vocal campaigners, frequently criticising opposition candidates Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar.

Why it matters

INEC this week also warned political actors against inflammatory rhetoric ahead of the elections.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

INEC condemns 'rig and die' slogan, warns of sanctions for electoral offenders

9 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

INEC has condemned the 'rig and die' slogan used by some political actors, warning that it could put election workers at risk, and urged anyone disputing results to use lawful channels.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has condemned the use of the slogan 'rig and die' by some political actors ahead of the 2027 elections.

What INEC said

Speaking at a forum in Enugu for journalists and media executives from the South-East, South-South and South-West, reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Kudu Haruna said threats and inflammatory language could endanger presiding officers, temporary election staff, party agents and observers. He urged anyone who disputes an election result to use lawful channels, including complaints to INEC or security agencies and the courts. He also warned that election officials, including ad hoc staff, would face legal consequences if found to have compromised the process.

Background

The 'rig and die' slogan has been used by some opposition supporters to warn against electoral fraud, but critics say it can be read as a threat of violence. INEC recently said it had uncovered a plot to threaten and kidnap election workers, with suspects arrested and charged. Violence has already been reported at several campaign events, including a clash at an NDC rally in Asaba in which one person died.

What happens next

Haruna called for closer cooperation between INEC and the media to counter misinformation before the elections, which begin with the presidential poll on 16 January 2027.

Tunde Bakare says he turned down Tinubu's offer of a ministerial post

9 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Pastor Tunde Bakare says President Tinubu offered him a cabinet position, which he declined because he felt too old for the job, preferring to advise the government from the background. He says he is not part of the opposition.

Read more

Pastor Tunde Bakare, leader of the Citadel Global Community Church, says President Bola Tinubu offered him a ministerial position, which he turned down.

What he said

Speaking at an event in Leicester, United Kingdom, on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Bakare said he told the President he was too old for the demands of the office and would rather work quietly in the background. He said he offered instead to give advice and raise concerns wherever he believed government policy had fallen short. He insisted that not being in the cabinet did not make him part of the opposition, describing his approach as a 'propositional platform' focused on identifying problems and proposing solutions.

Background

Bakare was Muhammadu Buhari's running mate in the 2011 presidential election and sought the APC presidential ticket in 2022, which Tinubu won. He has been an outspoken commentator on national affairs for decades and has had a mixed relationship with successive governments.
