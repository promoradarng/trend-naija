Flamingos beat Venezuela to reach U-17 Women's World Cup semi-finals, face Brazil next

2 Nov 2026, 09:44 GMT

Nigeria's Flamingos beat Venezuela 1-0 in Morocco through a late penalty from captain Azeezat Oduntan to reach the semi-finals of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup. They are Africa's last team in the tournament and will play Brazil on Wednesday.

Read more

Nigeria's under-17 women's team, the Flamingos, are into the semi-finals of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup in Morocco.

The match

The Flamingos beat Venezuela 1-0 in the quarter-final on Sunday, 1 November 2026, at the Mohammed VI Football Academy in Salé. Captain Azeezat Oduntan scored the winner from the penalty spot in the 89th minute after Oluebube Umejiaku was fouled in the box, a decision confirmed by a VAR review.

What comes next

Nigeria will face Brazil in the semi-final on Wednesday, 4 November, at the Moulay El Hassan Stadium in Rabat. They are the only African side left in the competition after Ghana lost 1-0 to Japan in their quarter-final.

Reactions

The Confederation of African Football congratulated the team on social media, celebrating their place in the last four. Nigerian fans also shared videos of the players dancing and celebrating after earlier victories in the tournament.

Background

Nigeria has a strong record in women's youth football and has reached the later stages of age-group World Cups several times. The senior team, the Super Falcons, are the most successful side in African women's football and won another Women's Africa Cup of Nations title this year. The Flamingos' run has raised hopes of Nigeria's first-ever U-17 women's world title.

Havertz goal gives Arsenal first league win at Anfield in 14 years, sends them top

1 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Arsenal beat Liverpool 1-0 at Anfield on Sunday through Kai Havertz's early goal, moving to the top of the Premier League with 24 points. It was the Gunners' first league win at Anfield in 14 years.

Read more

Arsenal have moved to the top of the Premier League after ending a long wait for a league win at Anfield.

The match

Kai Havertz scored the only goal in the 16th minute on Sunday, 1 November 2026, turning in a cross from Declan Rice at the back post. Liverpool controlled long spells of the game and pushed hard for an equaliser, with two penalty appeals turned down and Florian Wirtz hitting the crossbar from a free-kick. Arsenal goalkeeper David Raya denied Alexander Isak, while at the other end Alisson made a late save to stop substitute Christos Tzolis from doubling the lead.

The table

The result takes Arsenal to 24 points, three ahead of Manchester City, who reportedly lost to Brighton on the same day. It was Arsenal's first league win at Anfield in 14 years, ending a run of 13 league visits without victory.

What Arteta said

Manager Mikel Arteta told the BBC that the win was for everyone connected to the club and that 14 years without a victory at Anfield was a long time. He praised his players' performance but said it was too early to talk about the title race, adding that the team would now prepare for a big European match in Prague.

Why it matters

Arsenal have finished as runners-up in recent seasons and are hoping to win their first league title since 2004. The win at one of the hardest grounds in English football will boost their belief, while Liverpool will be disappointed not to have taken anything from a game they dominated for long periods.

Dangote cuts petrol gantry price again, now ₦1,210 per litre

2 Nov 2026, 09:44 GMT

The Dangote Refinery has reportedly reduced its petrol gantry price by ₦55 to ₦1,210 per litre, citing a fall in European petrol benchmark prices. Depot prices in Lagos have also dropped, averaging about ₦1,259 per litre.

Read more

Petrol prices are falling again in Nigeria after the Dangote Refinery announced another cut.

The new price

According to reports on Monday, 2 November 2026, the refinery reduced its petrol gantry price by ₦55, from ₦1,265 to ₦1,210 per litre, a drop of about 4.35 per cent. The cut was announced through marketers linked to the refinery. Industry sources said it followed a fall in the European petrol benchmark that the refinery uses to set its prices, as well as better supply.

Depot prices

Other Lagos depots had already reduced their prices at the end of last week. Depot owners including African Terminal, Rainoil, Ardova and NIPCO were selling petrol at about ₦1,260 per litre, with Aiteo slightly lower at ₦1,255. Prices also fell in Port Harcourt and Calabar. Diesel prices have moved in the opposite direction after the refinery raised its diesel gantry price last week.

What it means for motorists

Gantry and depot prices are what marketers pay before selling at filling stations, so the cuts could lead to lower pump prices in the coming days. However, actual pump prices also depend on transport costs and how quickly retailers pass on savings.

Background

This is the refinery's second petrol price cut in less than a week. Since fuel subsidies were removed in 2023, prices have been set by the market, and the Dangote Refinery has become a major influence on what Nigerians pay at the pump.
