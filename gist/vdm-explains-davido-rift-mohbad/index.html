VDM says his friendship with Davido began and ended over the fight for Mohbad

5 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has given his account of how his friendship with Davido broke down.

What he said

In a video released on Thursday, 5 November 2026, VDM said the same issue that brought them together eventually tore them apart: the campaign for justice for the late singer Mohbad. He recalled that he became heavily involved in demanding answers after Mohbad's death in September 2023, when questions about the circumstances of the death and the singer's earlier complaints about his former record label drew huge public attention. VDM said Davido followed him on social media in early 2024, which excited him, and that they became friends, with VDM supporting and promoting some of the singer's music. He said their relationship later changed, with the Mohbad matter at the heart of the fallout.

The current feud

The explanation comes amid an escalating public feud. This week VDM dared Davido's camp to show proof that the singer ever helped him financially, warned Zlatan Ibile after Davido was filmed singing along to Zlatan's song 'Inferno', and released a preview of his own diss track aimed at Davido. Several celebrities, including Oyemykke and Roskey, have weighed in.

Fresh swipe

On Friday, 6 November, VDM shared another video in which he boasted that he owns social media and, addressing Zlatan Ibile, hinted that he would soon turn his attention to the rapper. Some of his followers cheered him on, while others mocked the declaration.

Background

Mohbad, whose real name was Ilerioluwa Aloba, died in Lagos in September 2023 at the age of 27. His death led to protests and a police investigation, and a coroner's inquest was held into the circumstances.

How people reacted

Fans were divided, with some saying VDM's account explained the bitterness, and others urging both men to end the public quarrel.

Laide Bakare revisits feud with Eniola Badmus in new interview despite apology

6 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Actress Laide Bakare has spoken about her broken friendship with Eniola Badmus in an interview, saying Badmus once told people not to film them together. She acknowledged an apology but made a remark about Badmus's looks that has drawn criticism.

Read more

Nollywood actress Laide Bakare has reopened her disagreement with colleague Eniola Badmus in a new interview.

What she said

Speaking in an interview with media personality Chude Jideonwo, reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, Bakare recalled an incident in which Badmus allegedly told people not to film the two of them together because they were not friends. Bakare said she was equally not interested in a friendship with someone who did not want to be associated with her. She acknowledged that Badmus had later apologised, but appeared to question the circumstances of the apology. She also made a remark about Badmus's appearance, saying she did not find her attractive even with make-up, filters and weight loss.

A friendship that broke down

Bakare also spoke about how close the two once were. She said Badmus knew her family well and often visited their home, where Bakare's mother would cook for them.

Background

Both women are well-known actresses in the Yoruba and English-language film industries. Badmus has been open about her weight-loss journey in recent years. Badmus has not publicly responded to the latest comments.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised Bakare's comment about Badmus's looks, saying it was unkind and unnecessary, especially after an apology. Others said the actress was entitled to share her side of the story and urged both women to settle their differences privately.

Funke Akindele's 'The Four' sells out London premiere at Odeon Leicester Square

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The London premiere of Funke Akindele's new film 'The Four' at the Odeon Luxe Leicester Square on 29 November has sold out, its distributor says. Akindele called it a historic moment for Nigerian cinema.

Read more

Funke Akindele's new film, 'The Four', has sold out its London premiere ahead of its release in Nigerian cinemas.

The premiere

The film's distributor, FilmOne Entertainment, announced on Thursday, 5 November 2026, that tickets for the premiere at the Odeon Luxe Leicester Square, one of London's most famous cinemas, were no longer available. The premiere is scheduled for 29 November. Akindele had earlier described the event as a historic moment for Nigerian cinema.

Background

Akindele is Nollywood's most commercially successful filmmaker, with several of her films topping the Nigerian box office, including 'Battle on Buka Street', 'A Tribe Called Judah' and 'Everybody Loves Jenifa'. 'A Tribe Called Judah' became the first Nollywood film to earn more than ₦1 billion at the box office. 'The Four' features several well-known actors, including Wumi Toriola, and is expected in Nigerian cinemas in December, traditionally the busiest period for local releases.

Why it matters

A sold-out premiere at a major London venue shows the growing international audience for Nollywood films, particularly among the Nigerian diaspora in the UK.

How fans reacted

Fans congratulated the actress and said they could not wait for the film's release in Nigeria.

Toyin Abraham issues cease-and-desist notice to bloggers over 'false' posts

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Actress Toyin Abraham's lawyer has issued a cease-and-desist notice warning bloggers, influencers and social media users to stop circulating claims she says are false and damaging, and to remove existing posts or face legal action.

Read more

Nollywood actress and filmmaker Toyin Abraham has warned bloggers and social media users that she will take legal action over publications she considers false and damaging.

The notice

On Thursday, 5 November 2026, the actress shared on X a cease-and-desist notice issued by her lawyer, Tope Salami, according to reports. The notice, addressed to the general public and particularly bloggers, content creators and influencers, demanded that people stop circulating unverified claims about her and remove existing posts. It warned that digital materials could be used as evidence in possible court proceedings. The notice did not specify which posts or claims it was referring to.

Background

Toyin Abraham has been the subject of much online discussion in recent weeks, including over a reported rift with fellow actress Funke Akindele after an old TikTok collaboration resurfaced. This week she also asked fans to call her by her former name, Toyin Aimakhu, as she promotes her new film 'Bestie', which is due in cinemas in December. Several celebrities, including Senator Ned Nwoko on behalf of Regina Daniels, have recently threatened legal action against people spreading false stories online.

How people reacted

Some fans backed her decision, saying celebrities deserve protection from lies, while others said public figures should expect criticism.

Apostle Suleman says he tested Nathaniel Bassey with money, and was impressed

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Apostle Johnson Suleman has reportedly told his church that he once offered gospel singer Nathaniel Bassey money to test his character before a convention, and that Bassey's response taught him a lesson about humility and spiritual covering.

Read more

Apostle Johnson Suleman, founder of Omega Fire Ministries, has shared a story about gospel singer Nathaniel Bassey that has drawn wide attention.

What he said

Speaking during a Sunday service, in comments shared this week, Suleman reportedly said he first met Bassey on a plane ahead of his church's convention. According to him, Bassey said he wanted to attend the convention not as a paid performer but to support his spiritual father, whom Suleman named as Pastor Deboye. Curious about his reasons, Suleman said he deliberately offered the singer money to see how he would respond, as a way of studying his character and values.

The response

Suleman said Bassey's response left him with a powerful lesson about mentorship and respect for spiritual leaders, though reports did not detail exactly what the singer said.

Background

Nathaniel Bassey is one of Africa's most popular gospel ministers and recently became the first African gospel artist to reach 1 billion views on YouTube. He is known for his worship sessions and the annual Hallelujah Challenge. Suleman is a prominent and sometimes controversial Pentecostal preacher based in Auchi, Edo State.

How people reacted

Many Christians praised Bassey's humility, while others debated whether it was right to test someone with money.
