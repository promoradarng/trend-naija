Tiwa Savage hits back at critic who mocked her age over 'African Baddie' single

29 Oct 2026, 20:44 GMT

Afrobeats star Tiwa Savage has fired back at a man who mocked her age over the title of her new single, 'African Baddie', in an exchange that quickly went viral.

What happened

On Thursday, 29 October 2026, a social media user reportedly questioned why a woman of 40 would call herself a 'baddie' and suggested she was not serious. Replying in a single post, the singer corrected him, saying she is actually 46, listed her achievements and described herself as unbothered. She asked what exactly his problem was and used the same post to promote her new release.

The new single

'African Baddie' is scheduled for release on Friday, 30 October. The singer has long presented herself as Africa's 'bad girl', and the title builds on that image.

Background

Tiwa Savage is one of Africa's most successful female artists. She has won major awards, signed with Universal Music Group and performed at the coronation concert of King Charles III in 2023. Her career spans more than a decade of hit songs, and she has frequently spoken about ageism and the pressure on women in the music industry.

How people reacted

Fans rallied behind the singer, praising her confidence and saying age should not limit how women express themselves. Others joked that the critic had given her free promotion a day before the song's release.

  • Tiwa says she is 46 and 'unbothered'.
  • 'African Baddie' is due out on Friday, 30 October.
  • Fans praised her response to the critic.

Social media activist Martins Vincent Otse, popularly known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has been hospitalised in China after being diagnosed with what he described as life-threatening malaria.

What he said

VDM, who is in China on a business trip, shared the health update in a video on Instagram on Thursday, 19 November 2026. He said his condition got worse despite his attempts to treat the illness himself.

Naira weakens to ₦1,363 per dollar in official market

19 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The naira lost ₦7.43 against the dollar on Wednesday to close at ₦1,363.23 in the official market, and fell to ₦1,430 on the parallel market. Analysts point to reduced dollar inflows and rising demand.

Read more

The naira weakened against the US dollar in both the official and parallel foreign exchange markets on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, amid reduced foreign exchange inflows and rising demand for dollars.

Official market

Data from the Nigerian Autonomous Foreign Exchange Market (NAFEM) showed the naira fell by ₦7.43, or 0.54%, to close at ₦1,363.23 to the dollar, from ₦1,355.80 the previous day. It also lost ground against the euro, closing at ₦1,495.60, but gained slightly against the British pound to ₦1,795.24.

Parallel market

On the parallel market, the naira slipped by ₦10 to trade at ₦1,430 to the dollar, while at one major bank's foreign exchange desk it closed at ₦1,368.

Why it matters

The fall comes a day after the naira lost about ₦25 against the dollar, and as the Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC move to boost foreign exchange inflows.
