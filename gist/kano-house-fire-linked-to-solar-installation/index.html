House in Kano State catches fire, blaze linked to solar panel installation

9 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

A house in Kano State has caught fire, with the incident being linked to its solar power installation.

Flames on the roof

Photos from the scene show thick smoke rising from the roof of a two-storey building, with flames breaking through beside a row of solar panels. A man can be seen climbing up to an upper-floor balcony as a crowd of neighbours gathers below, some filming on their phones and others watching in shock.

A growing safety concern

The incident raises serious concerns about the safety of solar installations, especially as more Nigerians turn to solar energy because of unreliable electricity supply.

Poor wiring, faulty batteries, overloaded inverters and improper installation can all create fire hazards. Experts often advise homeowners to use qualified installers, buy certified equipment and have their systems checked regularly.

Cause not yet confirmed

The exact cause of this particular fire has not yet been independently confirmed.

Ebola: 28 contacts still under watch in Nigeria, 34 cleared, says NCDC

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The NCDC says 28 people linked to Nigeria's single imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a type of Ebola, remain under surveillance, while 34 others have been cleared after 21 days of monitoring. No further cases have been confirmed.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says 28 people linked to the country's imported Ebola case are still being monitored.

The update

In its national health security update released on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said 34 other contacts had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. As of 9 November, Nigeria had confirmed only one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, with no further confirmed cases.

The response

Idris said the response involves the Federal Ministry of Health, the FCT Administration, state authorities and development partners, with contact tracing, investigation of reported cases, laboratory testing, infection prevention and control, safe patient care and stronger screening at entry points.

Background

Ebola is a severe and often deadly viral disease spread through contact with the body fluids of infected people. The 21-day monitoring period reflects the maximum incubation time of the virus. The case in Nigeria is linked to an outbreak in Central Africa; the United States has just barred travellers who have been in the Democratic Republic of Congo in the past 21 days. Nigeria contained an Ebola outbreak in 2014.

Advice

Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness, vomiting or unexplained bleeding, especially after travel or contact with a suspected case, to report to a health facility immediately.

Edo governor Okpebholo vows to sign death warrants for convicted kidnappers, 'even my father'

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Edo governor Monday Okpebholo says he will sign the death warrants of anyone convicted of kidnapping under the state's law, even if it were his own father, as his government moves to enforce the death penalty for the crime.

Read more

Edo State governor Monday Okpebholo says he will approve the execution of convicted kidnappers, regardless of who they are.

What he said

In a video shared on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, the governor said he would sign death warrants for anyone found guilty of kidnapping, even if it were his own father or a close relative. He said the state needed to remove criminal elements from society, arguing that kidnappers were the ones killing people.

The law

Edo State has a law that prescribes the death penalty for kidnapping, and the governor's administration says it is moving to enforce it. Under Nigerian law, state governors must sign death warrants before executions can take place.

Background

Executions in Nigeria are rare because governors have generally been reluctant to sign death warrants, and human rights groups oppose the death penalty.

Mele Kyari denies ₦210 trillion went missing at NNPC under his watch

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

Former NNPC boss Mele Kyari has told a Senate committee that no money was lost under his leadership, saying the disputed ₦210 trillion in the company's accounts came from a misreading of its financial records.

Read more

Former Group Managing Director of the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPCL), Mele Kyari, has denied that ₦210 trillion went unaccounted for while he led the company.

What happened

Kyari appeared before the Senate Committee on Public Accounts, which is examining queries raised by the Auditor-General of the Federation over NNPCL's audited financial statements for 2017 to 2023, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The disputed figure is made up of ₦103 trillion recorded as accrued expenses and ₦107 trillion listed as sundry receivables. Kyari told the committee that no money was lost under his watch and that the figures arose from a misreading of the company's records.

The committee's position

The committee began examining the figures in June 2025 and has repeatedly rejected the company's explanations, insisting that NNPCL properly account for the entries.

Background

Kyari led NNPC from 2019 until 2025, overseeing its conversion into a limited liability company under the Petroleum Industry Act.

Gunmen kill Miyetti Allah chairman in Takum, Taraba State

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

Gunmen have reportedly killed Alhaji Ibrahim Dio, chairman of the Miyetti Allah Cattle Breeders Association in Takum LGA of Taraba State, at his home shortly after he returned from the cattle market.

Read more

Gunmen have reportedly killed the chairman of the Miyetti Allah Cattle Breeders Association (MACBAN) in Takum Local Government Area of Taraba State.

What happened

According to reports, Alhaji Ibrahim Dio was attacked at his home in the Abuja Quarters area of Takum on the evening of Sunday, 8 November 2026, shortly after returning from the Takum cattle market. The gunmen reportedly arrived on a motorcycle and opened fire, killing him.

Who he was

Residents described Dio as a dedicated community leader who worked for the welfare of Fulani residents and others in Takum, and who was said to have maintained good relations with other communities in the area.

Background

MACBAN is the main association representing cattle breeders in Nigeria. The police had not publicly commented on the killing or any arrests at the time of writing.
