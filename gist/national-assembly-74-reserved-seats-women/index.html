National Assembly considering 74 extra seats reserved for women, says Senate leader

2 Nov 2026, 18:01 GMT

The National Assembly is considering a constitutional amendment that would create 74 new legislative seats reserved for women, Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele has said.

The proposal

Speaking on Monday, 2 November 2026, at a Law Week event organised by the Ekiti State branch of the International Federation of Women Lawyers, Bamidele reportedly said the plan would add 37 seats in the Senate and 37 in the House of Representatives, giving each state and the Federal Capital Territory one extra women-only seat in each chamber. He said the constitution review committee has also proposed three extra seats for women in each state house of assembly, and stressed that the new seats would be added to the existing ones rather than replacing any.

How long it would last

Bamidele, who is vice-chairman of the Senate committee reviewing the 1999 Constitution, said the arrangement would run for 16 years, or four election cycles, before a full review. He described this as a sunset clause meant to allow a more inclusive political culture to develop.

What happens next

Constitutional amendments need the approval of two-thirds of members of both chambers of the National Assembly and of at least 24 of the 36 state houses of assembly. Bamidele said the National Assembly was engaging state lawmakers to secure their support.

Background

Women hold only a small share of seats in Nigeria's National Assembly, one of the lowest rates in Africa. A similar bill on special seats for women failed in 2022, which led to protests by women's groups. Supporters say reserved seats would give women a stronger voice in law-making, while critics argue they would add to the cost of governance.

  • The plan would add 37 women-only seats in each chamber.
  • State assemblies would get three extra seats for women.
  • The arrangement would run for 16 years before review.

Court freezes $100m linked to Governors' Forum in Paris Club fees dispute

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

An Abuja High Court has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum, pending a dispute over legal and consultancy fees tied to Paris Club refunds. The order does not decide who owns the money.

Read more

A High Court of the Federal Capital Territory in Abuja has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum (NGF), in a dispute over unpaid legal and consultancy fees connected to the Paris Club refunds.

The order

Justice O.C. Agbaza granted the interim freezing order on 19 November 2026, following an application by a lawyer against the NGF's incorporated trustees and 29 commercial banks. The order covers a single total sum of $100 million, about ₦135.6 billion, across the affected accounts.

What it means

The order preserves the disputed money until the court hears an application for an injunction or makes a further order. It does not decide who owns the funds.

The claim

The lawyer is seeking payment under an indemnity he says the NGF issued in December 2018, after the federal government paid $100 million to the forum to hold pending a decision on liability for professional fees. No response from the NGF was included in the reports.

Nollywood legend Pete Edochie dies at 79

25 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Veteran actor Pete Edochie, famous for playing Okonkwo in Things Fall Apart, has died at the age of 79. His family says he died in Enugu early on Wednesday after a long battle with prostate cancer.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actor Pete Edochie has died at the age of 79, his family has announced.

The announcement

In a statement shared on Instagram by his son, Leo Pete Edochie, on behalf of the family, the family said the actor died at 12:30am on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, in Enugu, after a prolonged battle with prostate cancer. The statement, titled 'An Iroko Has Fallen', described him as a beloved husband, father, brother and kinsman.

He is survived by his wife, Josephine Edochie, his children, including actors Yul and Linc Edochie, his siblings and other relatives. The family said funeral arrangements would be announced in due course.

His life and career

Pete Edochie hailed from Akamnato, Ifite Nteje, in Anambra State. He began his career in broadcasting in 1967 before becoming one of Nigeria's best-known actors. His major breakthrough came in 1987, when he played Okonkwo in the Nigerian Television Authority's adaptation of Chinua Achebe's Things Fall Apart, a role that brought him recognition far beyond Nigeria.

Known for his commanding voice and powerful screen presence, he became famous for portraying traditional patriarchs and kings in countless Nollywood films. He was honoured with the national award of Member of the Order of the Niger (MON).

His final weeks

In October, during a visit to Ghana, he spoke openly about his declining health, saying walking had become difficult, and prayed that he would not become a burden to those around him. Just this week, actress Toyin Abraham and her crew knelt to welcome him on her film set, and he had spoken publicly about his son Yul days earlier.

Tributes

News of his death has drawn an outpouring of grief from fans, many of whom described it as the end of an era for Nigerian film.
