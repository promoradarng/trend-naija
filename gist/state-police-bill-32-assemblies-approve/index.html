All 36 state assemblies approve state police bill, says National Assembly clerk

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

The constitutional amendment to create state police in Nigeria has cleared a major hurdle, with 32 state Houses of Assembly approving it.

What the Senate said

Senate Leader Opeyemi Bamidele said in Abuja on Sunday, 15 November 2026, that 32 state assemblies had approved the State Police (Constitution Alteration) Bill, 2026, which is more than the two-thirds required under Section 9(2) of the 1999 Constitution. He gave the remaining four assemblies until Tuesday, 17 November, to submit their reports, but said the National Assembly now has a duty to send the bill to President Bola Tinubu for assent whether or not they meet the deadline.

All 36 states on board

On Monday, 16 November, the Clerk to the National Assembly, Kamoru Ogunlana, said all 36 state assemblies had approved the bill, describing it as a 100 per cent endorsement. Only 24 approvals are needed under Section 9(2) of the Constitution.

He said the bill had already met the legal requirements for transmission, but the National Assembly leadership had briefly held it back to allow for wider consensus with all the states. With verification now complete, he said, the bill will be sent to the President for assent.

What the bill would do

The amendment would replace Nigeria's single, centralised police force with a Federal Police Service and State Police Services, allowing states to set up their own forces.

Background

Calls for state police have grown stronger in recent years amid security threats including banditry and kidnapping. The senior civil servants' union this week also called for state police to be created.

2Baba says election riggers deserve severe punishment amid 'rig and die' debate

27 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Singer 2Baba has said he supports tough punishment for anyone who manipulates election results, as debate continues over the 'rig and die' slogan. He said the slogan reflects Nigerians' frustration.

Read more

Music star Innocent Idibia, popularly known as 2Baba, has weighed in on the controversial 'rig and die' slogan ahead of the 2027 elections.

What he said

Speaking in a radio interview on Thursday, 26 November 2026, the African Queen singer said he supported severe punishment for anyone who tries to manipulate election results, arguing that serious consequences would discourage others from electoral malpractice.

He described the national mood as tense, and said the slogan reflected the desperation and frustration many Nigerians feel. He also said he did not see anything wrong with the slogan itself.

Background

The phrase 'rig and die' has spread widely online and at rallies. Earlier this week, INEC warned that it was dangerous, saying a slogan linking an electoral offence to death could incite mob attacks at polling units and put voters and election staff at risk. A civic group has also recorded 124 cases of electoral violence since July.
