Rufai Oseni challenges Umahi to win six million votes before comparing himself to Obi

3 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Television presenter Rufai Oseni has responded sharply to Minister of Works Dave Umahi's recent comments about NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi.

What Umahi said

At an APC town hall in Abakaliki, Ebonyi State, on Monday, 2 November 2026, Umahi reportedly questioned Obi's political relevance, described him as a 'nobody' and called himself the 'Lion of the South East'. He has also asked what makes Obi better than past South East leaders such as Sam Egwu and Anyim Pius Anyim, and has argued that the region should back President Bola Tinubu so that an Igbo candidate can later get eight years rather than four.

Oseni's response

Speaking on his breakfast television programme on Tuesday, 3 November, Oseni reportedly said self-given titles mean little without results. He said that before Umahi can compare himself with Obi, he should obtain a presidential nomination form, contest a general election and win at least six million votes, roughly the number Obi received in the 2023 presidential election.

Background

Umahi, a former two-term governor of Ebonyi State, is a leading APC figure in the South East and has campaigned hard for Tinubu's re-election. Obi, a former Anambra governor, came third in the 2023 presidential election as the Labour Party candidate and is now running on the NDC ticket with Rabiu Kwankwaso. The South East is seen as an important battleground in 2027, and four governors from the zone recently declared support for Tinubu.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters cheered Oseni's comments, while APC supporters said Umahi's record as governor and minister speaks for itself.

Crowd erupts in 'Obi' chants as Deputy Speaker Ben Kalu campaigns for Tinubu at Item Okpi festival in Aba

7 Nov 2026, 20:53 GMT

As Deputy Speaker Benjamin Kalu urged the crowd at the Item Okpi cultural festival in Aba to back President Tinubu, sections of the audience reportedly broke into loud chants of "Obi! Obi! Obi!", even as he declared, "On your mandate we shall stand."

Read more

What was meant to be a moment of political mobilisation for President Bola Ahmed Tinubu reportedly took an unexpected turn at the Item Okpi cultural festival in Aba, Abia State, when members of the crowd began chanting the name of Peter Obi.

"Obi! Obi! Obi!"

The Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives, Benjamin Kalu, was addressing a large gathering at the cultural event when he turned to national politics and began appealing to the people to support President Tinubu.

The response from sections of the audience was not what he appeared to be hoping for. As Kalu spoke in support of the President, loud chants of "Obi! Obi! Obi!" reportedly rose from the crowd.

Even when the Deputy Speaker repeated the familiar declaration, "On your mandate we shall stand," the chants continued, creating an unusual scene in which the politician's message and the crowd's response seemed to be moving in opposite directions.

Why it matters

The moment has drawn attention because of Abia State's political significance and the continuing debate about the popularity of Nigeria's major political figures ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Peter Obi, who contested the 2023 presidential election under the Labour Party and is now the NDC presidential candidate, continues to command a strong following in sections of the electorate, particularly in the South-East.

For Kalu, an influential APC politician from Abia State, the incident highlights the challenge of promoting the ruling party's candidate in a region where political loyalties remain divided.

A cultural festival turned political

Chants from a crowd at a cultural gathering are not a scientific measure of how a state or region will vote. But public reactions like this can still offer a glimpse into the political conversations happening among ordinary Nigerians.

What makes this incident stand out is the setting. The gathering was mainly a cultural celebration, yet it became a scene of competing political expressions once the Deputy Speaker raised the subject of presidential support.

Over to you

Were the chants simply a spontaneous reaction from enthusiastic Obi supporters, or a sign of wider political sentiment in the area? One thing is clear: Ben Kalu was talking about Tinubu, but some voices in the crowd wanted to talk about Obi.

Tinubu asks how Atiku built a university while he was vice-president

7 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

At an APC rally in Port Harcourt, President Tinubu said he was cleaning up a mess created by people complaining about hunger, naming Atiku Abubakar, and questioned how the ADC candidate built a university while in office.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has launched one of his sharpest attacks yet on Atiku Abubakar, presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC).

What he said

Speaking at an All Progressives Congress (APC) rally in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Tinubu dismissed critics who point to economic hardship under his government, saying he was cleaning up the mess left by those who came before him and that Atiku was one of them. He also questioned how Atiku had built a university while he was vice-president, a remark widely seen as raising questions about how the former vice-president acquired his wealth while in public office.

Background

Atiku served as vice-president from 1999 to 2007 under Olusegun Obasanjo. He founded the American University of Nigeria in Yola, Adamawa State, and has long said his wealth comes from legitimate private business interests. He has repeatedly blamed Tinubu's policies, including the way fuel subsidy removal was handled, for rising prices. Tinubu's Rivers visit follows his trip to Akwa Ibom on Friday, where he said the naira would have become worthless without his reforms. FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, whose Rainbow Coalition backs the President, is a key ally in Rivers, and Governor Siminalayi Fubara has also declared support for Tinubu.

Atiku replies

Later on Saturday, Atiku hit back in a statement issued by his campaign spokesman, Phrank Shaibu. He said his investment in education began long before he entered public office, when he laid the foundation for ABTI Academy in 1993, which later grew into the American University of Nigeria. In the same statement he referred to a 1993 US court forfeiture involving funds in an account linked to Tinubu, a matter the President's supporters have long said was a civil case that did not involve any criminal conviction. Atiku also said Tinubu's policies had pushed millions of Nigerians into extreme poverty, and rejected the President's claim that the economy is being rescued.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027, and campaigns by all the major parties are expected to intensify in the coming weeks.

Thugs reportedly destroy vehicles of NDC supporters at Delta rally

7 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Videos from a Nigeria Democratic Congress rally in Delta State reportedly show suspected thugs smashing vehicles belonging to party supporters, with some supporters said to have fought back. Police have not commented.

Read more

Tension broke out at a rally of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) in Delta State on Saturday, 7 November 2026, after suspected thugs were reportedly filmed destroying vehicles belonging to party supporters.

What happened

Footage shared on social media appears to show a group of men vandalising vehicles at or near the rally venue. According to reports, some NDC supporters retaliated after the attack, raising tensions further. It is not clear who the attackers were, how many vehicles were damaged or whether anyone was hurt. The police had not publicly commented on the incident at the time of writing.

Background

The NDC is the party of presidential candidate Peter Obi and his running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso. Delta is governed by Sheriff Oborevwori, who joined the APC and has backed President Bola Tinubu's re-election. Political violence has already been reported on several sides ahead of the January 2027 elections: Obi's convoy was attacked in Calabar earlier this week, stones were thrown at a stage during a governor's campaign rally in Nasarawa, and APC supporters were attacked and a campaign vehicle burnt ahead of a rally in Katsina State last week. INEC has also warned of a plot to threaten and kidnap election workers.

How people reacted

Many social media users condemned the violence and expressed concern for the safety of supporters at political events, while some warned that the same thugs could be used to disrupt the elections.

Wike and his Rainbow Coalition raise eyebrows as minister appears visibly angry at Rivers campaign ground

7 Nov 2026, 17:19 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike was reportedly seen checking how many people each of his aides had brought to the presidential campaign ground in Rivers State, looking far from pleased. The turnout has become a talking point, and so has the old question of paid crowds.

Read more

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike and his Rainbow Coalition campaign have got people talking again, this time after he appeared visibly angry at the presidential campaign ground in Rivers State.

Counting heads

Wike was reportedly seen checking how many people each of his aides had brought to the venue. Photos show the minister, in his black and red hat and a rainbow "Coalition" sash bearing the APC logo, deep in conversation with aides and looking far from amused.

One can only feel sorry for any aide who collected mobilisation money and failed to deliver the expected crowd.

The turnout question

The expectation may have been a stadium filled to capacity, but the turnout has now become a talking point on social media.

That brings us to Peter Obi, whose supporters often describe his campaign crowds as organic, with people attending out of personal conviction rather than financial inducement.

Can anyone fill a stadium for free?

Crowd mobilisation, from buses to refreshments to small payments, has long been a feature of Nigerian political rallies across parties. Large crowds make for impressive pictures, but they do not always reflect how people will vote.

So one question remains: can any Nigerian politician genuinely fill a stadium without spending money on crowd mobilisation?
