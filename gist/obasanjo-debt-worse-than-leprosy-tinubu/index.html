Obasanjo says debt is worse than leprosy, calls Tinubu's borrowing remark 'nonsense'

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

Former President Olusegun Obasanjo has criticised President Bola Tinubu's attitude to government borrowing, reportedly describing the President's past comparison of debt to leprosy as nonsense.

What he said

Speaking at a public forum in Abeokuta, Ogun State, on Friday, 30 October 2026, Obasanjo said debt was worse than leprosy because leprosy destroys only one person, while debt destroys generations. He was responding to a remark Tinubu made in April, when the President said borrowing was not leprosy and that the country simply had to work hard to repay its loans.

Other points

Obasanjo reportedly recalled that when he started his farm 47 years ago, one naira was worth more than a dollar, compared with about ₦1,500 to the dollar today, and asked what Nigeria had done with that time. He questioned whether the country's leaders understood its needs, criticised Nigeria's reliance on the World Bank and International Monetary Fund, and said Nigeria was advised not to subsidise its farmers while rich countries support theirs. He also warned against relying too heavily on foreign-trained economists whose advice may not suit Nigeria. The forum was focused on using artificial intelligence to solve grassroots problems.

Background

Obasanjo, who led Nigeria from 1999 to 2007, is credited by supporters with securing relief from much of the country's foreign debt in 2005. His comments came a day after Tinubu published an essay defending his government's new external loans, arguing that debt should be judged by what it builds and pointing to falling inflation and rising reserves. The President has asked the Senate to approve new borrowing of about $20.5 billion and €1 billion, which opposition figures including Atiku Abubakar have criticised.

What happens next

The Presidency had not responded to Obasanjo's remarks at the time of the report, and the debate over debt is likely to feature strongly in the 2027 campaign.

  • Obasanjo says debt destroys generations, while leprosy destroys one person.
  • He questioned reliance on the World Bank and IMF.
  • His remarks came a day after Tinubu defended new loans.

Political violence: Jonathan sounds alarm, Senate asks INEC to disqualify violent candidates as APC and ADC trade blame

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that some campaigns are being run like cult groups, while the Senate urged police to probe recent attacks and INEC to disqualify candidates linked to violence. The APC accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, a claim the ADC rejected.

Read more

Concern over violence ahead of the 2027 elections dominated political discussion on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, with a former president, the Senate and the main parties all weighing in.

Jonathan's warning

Speaking at a peace and security conference organised by his foundation in Abuja, former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that rising political intolerance and violence could threaten the elections. He said some political groups were running their campaign teams like cult groups, and that Nigerians at home and abroad had expressed fears about possible crises after the polls. He also condemned thuggery and the use of young people as tools of violence.

The Senate's resolution

The Senate, acting on a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi (ADC, Bauchi Central), condemned recent attacks on political figures. These included the stoning of President Bola Tinubu's motorcade after Friday prayers in Abuja, reported attacks on NDC candidate Peter Obi in Edo State, a disrupted NDC procession in Asaba on 7 November that left one person dead, and an attack on ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar's convoy in Sokoto.

Lawmakers asked the Inspector-General of Police to carry out impartial investigations and prosecute those responsible, and urged INEC to apply the law disqualifying candidates linked to electoral violence. The motion was seconded by Senator Ali Ndume (APC, Borno South).

APC and ADC trade accusations

The APC presidential campaign council, through its spokesperson Dele Alake, accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, deepfakes and hired protests to damage President Tinubu's campaign, pointing to the convoy incident in Abuja.

The ADC campaign council, through Phrank Shaibu, dismissed the claim as a distraction from the government's economic record. It challenged the APC to provide evidence or withdraw the allegation, and asked why no one had been prosecuted if the ruling party had proof. The NDC's response was not included in the reports.

The chants

The APC statement also blamed the opposition for youths chanting "Tinubu Must Go" and the Hausa phrase "Ba mayi", meaning roughly "we are not supporting you", at some of the ruling party's rallies.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Nigeria and South Africa hold talks after Fani-Kayode diplomatic row

17 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nigeria and South Africa have held talks in Pretoria over tensions following a public dispute involving Nigeria's ambassador, Femi Fani-Kayode. South Africa had summoned the envoy over his conduct after he presented his credentials.

Read more

Nigeria and South Africa have held talks to ease tensions in their relationship, days after a public dispute involving Nigeria's ambassador to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, and officials of the South African government.

The summons

South Africa's Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) summoned the envoy over his conduct following the presentation of his credentials. The row followed Fani-Kayode's complaint about being left out of a presidential livestream, which drew a response from the South African presidency.

The meeting

Fani-Kayode shared the outcome of the meeting on his X account, posting a clip of himself at DIRCO's head office in Pretoria. He said both sides raised concerns about the state of relations and the events that sparked the controversy.

He said the two countries look forward to building on the meeting and strengthening their long-standing bilateral ties.

Senate Chief Whip Monguno and APC leaders reportedly escape Boko Haram attack in Borno

17 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Senate Chief Whip Mohammed Tahir Monguno reportedly escaped a Boko Haram attack during a campaign tour of Kukawa, Borno State. Troops are said to have moved quickly to repel the attackers.

Read more

Senate Chief Whip Mohammed Tahir Monguno, who represents Borno North, has reportedly escaped a Boko Haram attack during a 2027 campaign tour of Kukawa Local Government Area of Borno State on Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

What happened

Monguno is said to have travelled to Kukawa with former governor and senator Maina Maaji Lawan and other APC figures to campaign when the insurgents tried to overrun the area. Troops of Operation Hadin Kai, acting on intelligence, reportedly moved quickly to repel the attackers.

The campaign tour

The APC entourage had earlier gathered in Monguno town on Sunday, where the senator joined Governor Babagana Zulum for the launch of the party's campaign in the area. The governor returned to Maiduguri by helicopter on Monday, while Monguno and others continued to Kukawa.
