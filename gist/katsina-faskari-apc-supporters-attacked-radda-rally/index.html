APC supporters reportedly attacked and vehicle burnt before Katsina governor's rally

1 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Violence has again marred campaigning in Katsina State, with supporters of the All Progressives Congress (APC) reportedly attacked on their way to a rally by Governor Dikko Umaru Radda.

What happened

According to reports on Sunday, 1 November 2026, suspected hoodlums ambushed APC supporters in Yankara town, Faskari Local Government Area, on Saturday as they travelled to a rally for President Bola Tinubu and the governor's 2027 campaigns. The attackers reportedly blocked a major road used by the campaign convoy and set fire to a campaign vehicle belonging to a support group, the Musa Adamu Network. The violence reportedly followed a religious procession in the area, which the attackers are said to have exploited. Several people were injured. A witness claimed there were deaths, but this has not been independently confirmed.

Reactions

The governor went ahead with the rally. The secretary of the Musa Adamu Network condemned the attack and called on security agencies to identify and prosecute those responsible, while the state APC chairman, Bishir Saulawa, pledged a peaceful, issue-based campaign. The police had not commented at the time of the report.

Background

It is the second reported attack on an APC campaign in Katsina in less than a week, after suspected thugs disrupted a rally in Malumfashi. Opposition figures have also reported attacks, including on AAC presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore in Ekiti and the ADC governorship candidate in Sokoto. Atiku Abubakar and other leaders have called for investigations and warned against political violence.

What happens next

Security agencies are under pressure to ensure safe campaigning as parties step up their activities ahead of the January 2027 elections.

Hamzat vows to enforce one-year rent limit as Lagos governorship candidates debate

9 Nov 2026, 06:44 GMT

Lagos APC governorship candidate Obafemi Hamzat says the state will enforce the legal limit on advance rent to one year, warning landlords against demanding two or three years upfront. He spoke at a televised governorship debate.

Read more

Lagos State Deputy Governor Obafemi Hamzat, the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate for governor, says the state government will enforce the legal limit on advance rent payments.

What he said

Speaking at a governorship debate organised by a coalition of media organisations on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Hamzat said the one-year cap on advance rent is already contained in the Lagos State Real Estate Registration Agency law passed in 2025, and that the focus now is making sure it is followed. He warned landlords against demanding two or three years' rent upfront, and said estate agents are required to register with the state and appear on an official database, urging residents to check it before dealing with agents.

The law

The Lagos State Tenancy Law of 2011 already makes it unlawful for landlords or their agents to demand more than one year's rent in advance from a new tenant, and limits advance payments from sitting tenants. Despite this, demands for two or more years' rent have remained a widespread concern.

Background

Hamzat is seeking to succeed Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu in the 2027 governorship election. Former governor Akinwunmi Ambode recently pledged to mobilise support for him. Housing costs are one of the biggest concerns for Lagos residents.

Okowa says he left Atiku for Tinubu because he has 'seen the light'

8 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former Delta governor Ifeanyi Okowa, Atiku Abubakar's running mate in 2023, told an APC rally in Port Harcourt that he switched to backing President Tinubu because he has seen the light, crediting the government's fiscal reforms.

Read more

Former Delta State governor Ifeanyi Okowa has explained why he abandoned former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar to support President Bola Tinubu ahead of the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at an All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Okowa reportedly said he had 'seen the light'. He argued that Tinubu inherited a country with foundations weakened by decades of mismanagement, and that the current administration was making tough but necessary decisions to fix them. He credited the government's fiscal reforms with increasing the revenue shared with states and local governments, saying the results could be seen in better project delivery and salary payments. He also compared Tinubu's record as Lagos governor with his performance as President, saying the same transformation was now happening across the country.

Background

Okowa was Atiku's vice-presidential running mate on the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) ticket in the 2023 election, which Tinubu won. He later left the PDP for the APC, following his successor in Delta, Governor Sheriff Oborevwori. Atiku is now the candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), although a Federal High Court ruling last week voided his nomination, a decision the party has appealed. At the same rally, Tinubu questioned how Atiku built a university while he was vice-president, prompting a sharp reply from Atiku's campaign.

What happens next

Atiku's campaign had not publicly responded to Okowa's remarks at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Amaechi warns court ruling against ADC could trigger unrest if mishandled

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

ADC vice-presidential candidate Rotimi Amaechi has warned that the court judgment removing the party's leadership and voiding Atiku Abubakar's nomination could lead to serious unrest, even civil war, if the dispute is not properly managed.

Read more

Rotimi Amaechi, the vice-presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has warned that the court ruling against the party's leadership could cause serious unrest if it is not handled carefully.

What he said

In comments reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Amaechi said the judgment that nullified the party's leadership and the process that produced Atiku Abubakar as presidential candidate could lead to civil war if the dispute is not properly managed. He did not set out in detail how he believed the crisis should be resolved.

The ruling

On Thursday, Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja ruled that the national working committee led by former Senate President David Mark was invalid. The judgment also nullified the emergence of former Interior Minister Rauf Aregbesola as national secretary, and decisions taken by the Mark-led leadership, including the process that produced Atiku as candidate. The court directed INEC not to recognise decisions taken at the party's national executive committee meeting of 27 July 2025. The suit was filed by Nafiu Gombe, a former deputy national chairman of the party.

Background

The ADC has appealed the judgment and asked INEC not to act on it until the appeal is heard. Atiku has vowed to stay in the race and accused the government of using the courts against the opposition. Amaechi, a former Rivers governor and transport minister, this week also said former President Muhammadu Buhari had promised in writing to back him in 2022.

What happens next

Attention is now on the Court of Appeal and on how INEC responds before the presidential election on 16 January 2027.

Obi asks Tinubu to account for subsidy savings and over $100bn in loans

8 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

NDC candidate Peter Obi has challenged President Tinubu to explain what his government has done with the ₦15.8 trillion reportedly saved from fuel subsidy removal and more than $100 billion in borrowing, a day after Tinubu criticised Obi's record in Anambra.

Read more

Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has called on President Bola Tinubu to account for money saved from the removal of the fuel subsidy and from government borrowing.

What Obi said

In a statement on his X account on Saturday, 7 November 2026, titled 'Good Day, Mr President: Nigerians Deserve Accountability', Obi said the large sums flowing into government coffers, including higher tax revenues, should be producing clear improvements in Nigerians' daily lives. He urged the President to visit the remaining 33 states he has not yet toured, spend time with the people and explain what his administration had done with the ₦15.8 trillion he said had been saved from subsidy removal, the more than $100 billion he said had been borrowed, and increased revenue from taxes.

The background to the exchange

Obi's statement came a day after Tinubu, speaking in Akwa Ibom, criticised the former Anambra governor's record on erosion in the state. At a reception in Uyo the same day, Tinubu said the naira would have become worthless without his reforms and that Nigeria would never go back to the subsidy. Vice-President Kashim Shettima also told an APC rally in Uyo that Obi had run himself out of the race.

The figures

Obi did not give a breakdown of how he arrived at the figures. The World Bank reported this week that Nigeria's total external debt, including private-sector debt, rose to about $145 billion in 2025. The government says its borrowing is needed to fund infrastructure and that its reforms have saved the economy from collapse.

What happens next

The Presidency had not publicly responded to Obi's statement at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
