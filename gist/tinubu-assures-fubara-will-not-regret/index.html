"Fubara, you will not regret what you have done as long as all of us are alive": Tinubu assures Rivers governor

7 Nov 2026, 16:46 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has given Rivers State Governor Siminalayi Fubara a strong personal assurance, telling him he will not regret the decision he has taken.

What Tinubu said

"Fubara, you will not regret what you have done as long as all of us are alive," the President said during his presidential campaign rally in the state.

Photos from the rally show Fubara smiling beside FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, both wearing red caps bearing Tinubu's image, a striking picture given the long-running feud between the two men.

Why the words matter

The statement is attracting attention because of the political tension that has surrounded Rivers State and the long-running power struggle between Governor Fubara and his political opponents.

For anyone who has followed Rivers politics closely, those words carry real weight. Fubara has faced some of the toughest political battles of his administration, with fights over political loyalty, control of the state legislature and the influence of powerful figures shaping events in the state.

A sacrifice, a deal, or a new understanding?

With the President offering such a personal assurance, questions are being raised. The President did not spell out exactly what decision he was referring to, or what he intends to do for the governor in return.

Was Tinubu acknowledging a major political sacrifice by Fubara? Was he reassuring him over a difficult decision? Or could this signal a new understanding within the political leadership of Rivers State ahead of 2027?

INEC warns against 'Rig and Die' slogan ahead of 2027 elections, raises concerns over electoral violence

9 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

INEC has raised serious concern over the growing use of the "Rig and Die" slogan by some political actors ahead of the 2027 elections, warning that such language could encourage violence. But the slogan also points to a deeper problem: Nigerians' fear that their votes will not count.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has expressed serious concern over the growing use of the controversial "Rig and Die" slogan by some political actors ahead of Nigeria's 2027 general elections.

A dangerous slogan

According to the electoral body, such language is dangerous and could encourage political violence, putting innocent Nigerians at risk as the country prepares for another major election.

Why it has come to this

The controversy also raises a question that cannot be ignored: why has Nigeria's electoral system reached a point where political actors feel the need to use such threatening language?

For years, Nigerians have complained about electoral malpractice, vote buying, intimidation, ballot box snatching and alleged manipulation of results. Many voters have grown frustrated, especially when they believe their votes do not count.

With the 2027 elections approaching, political tensions are already building.

Confidence in the process

INEC is right to warn against inflammatory statements that could endanger lives. But the commission must also recognise that public confidence in the electoral process remains a major concern.

Nigerians want elections where votes are counted honestly, results are transmitted transparently, and those caught manipulating the process face real consequences.

Political leaders, too, have a responsibility to avoid statements that could turn ordinary citizens into victims of electoral violence.

No election should cost an innocent Nigerian their life. At the same time, electoral offenders must never be allowed to operate without consequences.

Should INEC be more concerned about the slogan "Rig and Die", or about fixing the electoral weaknesses that make Nigerians fear rigging in the first place?

Wike made the pledge during a political engagement in Kwali Area Council of the Federal Capital Territory, according to reports on Monday, 9 November 2026. Obi says NFF officials stopped him from greeting world champion Flamingos

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

NDC candidate Peter Obi says Nigeria Football Federation officials refused to let him congratulate the Flamingos when he saw them at Abuja airport on their return with the U-17 World Cup trophy. The NFF had not responded.

Read more

Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has accused officials of the Nigeria Football Federation (NFF) of stopping him from congratulating the Flamingos after their World Cup triumph.

What Obi said

Obi said he was at the airport in Abuja at about 7am on Monday, 9 November 2026, to catch a flight to Enugu, when he saw the Flamingos, who had just arrived with the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup trophy. He said he asked his protocol officer to find out if he could go over and welcome the players, but was told that NFF officials had refused, reportedly warning that anyone who allowed him near the team would lose their job.

'Not the first time'

Obi said this was not the first time he had been prevented from supporting a national team. He recalled travelling to Uyo in March 2025 to watch a World Cup qualifier against Zimbabwe, and said that although he was warmly received before the match, he was not allowed the same access afterwards.

Background

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final in Rabat on Saturday to become the first African team to win the tournament, and were welcomed back to Nigeria on Monday. President Bola Tinubu and the First Lady have both congratulated the team.

Presidency responds

The Presidency accused Obi of trying to politicise national sports teams. In a post on X on Monday, the President's special assistant on social media, Dada Olusegun, said the national teams, including the Under-17 girls, represent the whole country and not any party or individual's ambitions, and that protocol rules around national teams are designed to protect young athletes from political exploitation, commercial distractions and media pressure. The NFF itself had not publicly commented.

Ebonyi governor Francis Nwifuru told President Tinubu at an APC rally in Abakaliki that Ebonyi is an APC state and that 80% of its votes are assured for him in 2027.

Ebonyi State governor Francis Nwifuru has promised President Bola Tinubu 80 per cent of the state's votes in the 2027 presidential election.

What he said

Speaking at the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential rally in Abakaliki on Monday, 9 November 2026, Nwifuru told the President that Ebonyi was an APC state, that 80 per cent of its votes were assured for him and that he would win by a landslide.

Background

The rally is part of Tinubu's campaign tour of the South-East and South-South, which has included stops in Akwa Ibom and Rivers states in the past week. Several South-East governors have declared support for the President, and the APGA in Anambra has also adopted him as its candidate. The South-East, however, is the home region of NDC candidate Peter Obi, who won most of the region's votes in 2023, and his party has set ambitious targets of its own, including 98 per cent of Anambra's votes.

How people reacted

The governor's pledge drew mixed reactions on social media. Supporters of the President welcomed it, while others questioned whether any governor could guarantee how residents would vote.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
