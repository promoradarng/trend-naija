"Fubara, you will not regret what you have done as long as all of us are alive": Tinubu assures Rivers governor

7 Nov 2026, 16:46 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has given Rivers State Governor Siminalayi Fubara a strong personal assurance, telling him he will not regret the decision he has taken.

What Tinubu said

"Fubara, you will not regret what you have done as long as all of us are alive," the President said during his presidential campaign rally in the state.

Photos from the rally show Fubara smiling beside FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, both wearing red caps bearing Tinubu's image, a striking picture given the long-running feud between the two men.

Why the words matter

The statement is attracting attention because of the political tension that has surrounded Rivers State and the long-running power struggle between Governor Fubara and his political opponents.

For anyone who has followed Rivers politics closely, those words carry real weight. Fubara has faced some of the toughest political battles of his administration, with fights over political loyalty, control of the state legislature and the influence of powerful figures shaping events in the state.

A sacrifice, a deal, or a new understanding?

With the President offering such a personal assurance, questions are being raised. The President did not spell out exactly what decision he was referring to, or what he intends to do for the governor in return.

Was Tinubu acknowledging a major political sacrifice by Fubara? Was he reassuring him over a difficult decision? Or could this signal a new understanding within the political leadership of Rivers State ahead of 2027?

Former All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has denied reports that he endorsed Peter Obi ahead of Nigeria's 2027 presidential election.

"The claim is false"

In a public statement dated 18 November 2026, Garba described the claim circulating online as false.

He said he has withdrawn from partisan politics and is now focusing on his business ventures and on Africa's digital economy.

His new focus

Garba named Crowwe, Tablars and Justitia SI among the projects currently receiving his attention.

He also urged the public to rely only on his official channels for accurate information about his activities.

Where did the story come from?

The origin of the endorsement claim remains unclear. No post, interview or public statement showing Garba endorsing Obi has been found.

What is clear is that Garba has publicly disputed the claim.

A familiar pattern

With the 2027 elections approaching, false claims about endorsements are spreading quickly on social media. It is the second time this week that a story about backing for Obi has been corrected, after Obi himself clarified that his Washington visit was not an endorsement from the US Congress.

Peter Obi corrects claims that his Washington visit was an endorsement from the US Congress

18 Nov 2026, 16:43 GMT

Peter Obi says his recent trip to Washington involved talks on Nigeria's development, citizens' welfare and democracy, and that he neither sought nor announced any endorsement, after social media posts claimed the US Congress had backed him.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has corrected claims that his recent visit to Washington amounted to an endorsement from the United States Congress.

What Obi said

According to Obi, the trip involved discussions about Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and the state of democracy. He said he neither sought nor announced any endorsement.

He said he met serving and former US lawmakers and other stakeholders during the visit.

How the confusion started

The confusion followed a viral video in which retired US Army Colonel Albert Short praised Obi.

Some social media posts presented those personal remarks as if they were official backing from the US Congress, and the claim spread quickly online.

What the facts show

There is no public evidence that the US Congress as an institution has endorsed Obi's 2027 presidential bid. Congress does not endorse candidates in other countries' elections.

A meeting with politicians, or kind words from an individual, is not the same as an endorsement from their institution.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections drawing closer, claims about foreign support for candidates are likely to keep spreading on social media. Obi's correction is a reminder for supporters on all sides to check claims before sharing them.

Soludo says Tinubu doesn't need to rig 2027; Dele Momodu says Obi will beat him in Anambra

20 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Anambra Governor Charles Soludo told an APC rally in Awka that the 2027 election is already decided and Tinubu does not need to rig it. ADC chieftain Dele Momodu fired back that Peter Obi will win Anambra and embarrass the governor.

Read more

Anambra State Governor Charles Soludo and ADC chieftain Dele Momodu have clashed over the 2027 presidential race in Anambra, a day after Soludo's party, APGA, adopted President Bola Tinubu as its candidate.

Soludo's claim

Speaking at the APC presidential campaign rally at Alex Ekwueme Square in Awka on Thursday, 19 November 2026, Soludo said the election was already won and lost and that President Tinubu does not need to rig it to secure a second term.

He argued that opposition candidates had strong support only in some parts of the country, while the Tinubu-Shettima ticket had a presence in every state.

Momodu's response

Reacting the same evening on a television programme, Dele Momodu dismissed Soludo's prediction and said Peter Obi, the NDC candidate, would defeat Tinubu in Anambra and embarrass the governor. He pointed to Obi's 2023 performance, when he won both Anambra and Lagos States.

Background

Anambra is Obi's home state, where he served as governor. APGA, which controls the state, formally adopted Tinubu as its candidate on Thursday, with Soludo handing the party's flag to Vice President Kashim Shettima.

The election

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
