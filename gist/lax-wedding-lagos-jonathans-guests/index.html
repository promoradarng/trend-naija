Goodluck and Patience Jonathan among guests at singer L.A.X's Lagos wedding

2 Nov 2026, 09:44 GMT

Singer L.A.X's wedding celebration in Lagos turned into a gathering of political, royal and entertainment heavyweights.

The wedding

The singer, whose real name is Damilola Afolabi, held his wedding reception on Victoria Island on Sunday, 1 November 2026. It was hosted by his father, Taiwo Afolabi, chairman of the SIFAX Group. The couple had earlier held a civil ceremony in Malta in September and an Islamic wedding ceremony on 29 October.

The guests

Former President Goodluck Jonathan and his wife, Patience Jonathan, were among the guests, along with the Emir of Kano, Muhammadu Sanusi II, several traditional rulers and stars from the entertainment industry. Lagos monarch Oba Saheed Elegushi and his wife were also pictured at the event.

How people reacted

Photos and videos of the former First Lady drew warm and nostalgic reactions online. Many users recalled her time in office with affection, while others remembered the viral moments from her public appearances. Fans also congratulated L.A.X on his marriage and remarked on the high-profile guest list.

Background

L.A.X rose to fame in the mid-2010s with hits such as 'Caro' with Wizkid. His father, Taiwo Afolabi, is a prominent businessman in the maritime sector and a well-known figure in Nigerian society.

Sweden returns 39 Benin Bronzes to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Sweden has handed back 39 Benin artefacts taken during the British invasion of Benin in 1897, at a ceremony in Lagos. Six of the objects will stay in Sweden on a 10-year loan.

Read more

Sweden has returned 39 Benin Bronzes and other artefacts to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted from the Benin Kingdom.

The handover

Representatives of Sweden's National Museums of World Culture formally handed over the objects at a ceremony in Lagos on Thursday, 12 November 2026. They will be received by Nigeria's National Commission for Museums and Monuments. The collection includes cultural objects known as Emwin Arre. Six of the 39 artefacts will remain in Sweden under a 10-year loan arrangement.

Why it matters

The objects were among thousands taken by British forces who attacked and burned Benin City in 1897 before bringing the kingdom under British rule. Michael Barrett, curator for Africa at Sweden's National Museums of World Culture, described the return as an important step in addressing colonial-era looting of cultural property.

Background

Nigeria has been campaigning for decades for the return of the Benin Bronzes, which are held in museums and private collections around the world. The Netherlands returned 119 objects in 2025, and Germany handed over 20 Benin Bronzes in 2022. Many more remain abroad.
