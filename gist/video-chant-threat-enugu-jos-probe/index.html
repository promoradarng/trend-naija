JIBWIS and Christian group call for DSS probe of video allegedly threatening worshippers in Enugu and Jos

26 Oct 2026, 10:42 GMT

Update: JIBWIS national chairman Sheikh Abdullahi Bala Lau has urged the DSS, the Nigeria Police Force and other security agencies to identify the individual in the video. The Northern Christian Association has also called on security agencies to establish whether the footage is authentic and identify those featured.

There are conflicting claims online about when the video was recorded, with some users saying it dates back to 2020 and others saying it was posted only days ago. Social media users have also named a man they claim is the speaker, and there are unconfirmed reports that he has been suspended by the movement. None of these claims has been independently verified, and no official has named anyone.

Of particular concern is the apparent presence of children and young people during the chant. Critics fear that exposing minors to messages that appear to encourage religious violence could contribute to radicalisation and deepen religious tensions.

A disturbing video reportedly filmed somewhere in Northern Nigeria has raised serious concerns over alleged threats of religious violence, and is now being reported by several Nigerian news outlets.

The footage shows young men in brown uniforms and blue berets marching and chanting in Hausa during what appears to be a drill. News reports have linked the uniforms to the first-aid volunteer wing of the Islamic movement JIBWIS, whose members help coordinate Friday Jumu'ah prayers.

According to a Hausa-to-English translation circulating with the video, the drill leader allegedly calls for AK-47 rifles so the group can travel to Enugu and attack Christians during Sunday worship. He reportedly refers to the worshippers as "infidels", imitates the sound of gunfire, and also mentions Jos as another target.

Lere Olayinka, media aide to the FCT Minister, has called on the DSS and the police to investigate the video.

However, the location, date, identity of the speaker, the group's actual affiliation and the accuracy of the translation have not been independently verified. No response from the group had been reported at the time of writing.

If the translation is accurate, this is an extremely serious matter. Nobody should be allowed to use religion as an excuse to threaten innocent people, whether they are Christians or Muslims.

If someone openly calls for weapons to attack people during worship, shouldn't that be enough to trigger an immediate security investigation? Nigeria has suffered enough religious violence, and credible threats against worshippers cannot be treated as ordinary speech.

NAFDAC shuts Abuja's Zuba International Market over fake products worth ₦300m

27 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

NAFDAC has shut Zuba International Market in Abuja after a joint operation with police and the DSS reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome goods worth more than ₦300 million. Over 160 shops were inspected.

Read more

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has shut down Zuba International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

The operation

The closure followed a joint enforcement operation with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services, which reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome products worth more than ₦300 million. The exercise covered more than 160 shops in the market.

Why now

NAFDAC said the closure was part of a wider crackdown on substandard and fake goods ahead of the festive season.

Naira gains ₦19 to ₦1,378 per dollar after CBN raises BDC limit

27 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The naira strengthened by ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar on the official market on Thursday, after the CBN raised the weekly dollar sales limit for bureaux de change. It had lost over ₦43 in the previous two weeks.

Read more

The naira recovered strongly against the US dollar on the official foreign exchange market on Thursday, 26 November 2026.

The rebound

The currency gained ₦19 to close at ₦1,378 per dollar, up from ₦1,397 on Wednesday. The recovery reversed some of the losses it suffered between 13 and 25 November, when it fell by ₦43.50 amid rising demand for dollars.

Why

The gain followed the Central Bank of Nigeria's decision to raise the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators from $150,000 to $1 million, aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the market.

Background

The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 23%.

Dangote cuts cooking gas price to ₦1,000 per kg, but retail prices stay high

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its cooking gas gantry price to ₦1,000 per kg, with its marketers selling at ₦1,100. Consumers, however, are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kg at retail outlets.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery has reportedly reduced the wholesale price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), commonly known as cooking gas, to ₦1,000 per kilogram.

The new prices

Marketers linked to the refinery have lowered their selling price to ₦1,100 per kilogram, according to a market survey.

The retail gap

Despite the cuts, consumers are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kilogram at retail outlets, raising concerns about the gap between wholesale and retail prices. Earlier this week, cooking gas was reported to be selling for up to ₦2,000 per kg in parts of Abuja.

Background

The refinery has also cut its petrol and diesel prices in recent weeks, most recently reducing diesel by ₦125 to ₦1,595 per litre.
