JIBWIS and Christian group call for DSS probe of video allegedly threatening worshippers in Enugu and Jos

26 Oct 2026, 10:42 GMT

Update: JIBWIS national chairman Sheikh Abdullahi Bala Lau has urged the DSS, the Nigeria Police Force and other security agencies to identify the individual in the video. The Northern Christian Association has also called on security agencies to establish whether the footage is authentic and identify those featured.

There are conflicting claims online about when the video was recorded, with some users saying it dates back to 2020 and others saying it was posted only days ago. Social media users have also named a man they claim is the speaker, and there are unconfirmed reports that he has been suspended by the movement. None of these claims has been independently verified, and no official has named anyone.

Of particular concern is the apparent presence of children and young people during the chant. Critics fear that exposing minors to messages that appear to encourage religious violence could contribute to radicalisation and deepen religious tensions.

A disturbing video reportedly filmed somewhere in Northern Nigeria has raised serious concerns over alleged threats of religious violence, and is now being reported by several Nigerian news outlets.

The footage shows young men in brown uniforms and blue berets marching and chanting in Hausa during what appears to be a drill. News reports have linked the uniforms to the first-aid volunteer wing of the Islamic movement JIBWIS, whose members help coordinate Friday Jumu'ah prayers.

According to a Hausa-to-English translation circulating with the video, the drill leader allegedly calls for AK-47 rifles so the group can travel to Enugu and attack Christians during Sunday worship. He reportedly refers to the worshippers as "infidels", imitates the sound of gunfire, and also mentions Jos as another target.

Lere Olayinka, media aide to the FCT Minister, has called on the DSS and the police to investigate the video.

However, the location, date, identity of the speaker, the group's actual affiliation and the accuracy of the translation have not been independently verified. No response from the group had been reported at the time of writing.

If the translation is accurate, this is an extremely serious matter. Nobody should be allowed to use religion as an excuse to threaten innocent people, whether they are Christians or Muslims.

If someone openly calls for weapons to attack people during worship, shouldn't that be enough to trigger an immediate security investigation? Nigeria has suffered enough religious violence, and credible threats against worshippers cannot be treated as ordinary speech.

Reps bill would let regulators shut hospitals that refuse emergency patients

5 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

A bill to amend the National Health Act has passed second reading in the House of Representatives. It would require hospitals to begin triage within 15 minutes and stabilise emergency patients within 30 minutes, or risk losing their licences.

Read more

A bill that would allow regulators to suspend or revoke the licences of hospitals that refuse or delay emergency treatment has passed its second reading in the House of Representatives.

What the bill says

The bill, sponsored by House spokesman Akin Rotimi and debated on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, seeks to amend the National Health Act 2014 by setting mandatory timelines for emergency care. Under the proposal, facilities would have to begin assessing a patient within 15 minutes of arrival and provide basic stabilisation within 30 minutes. Where a life-threatening condition is identified, staff would have to start lifesaving treatment immediately, and hospitals with a free bed would have to assign it to an emergency patient.

Why it is needed

Section 20 of the existing Act already bans health workers and facilities from refusing emergency treatment, with a fine of ₦100,000, up to six months in prison, or both. However, some hospitals have continued to turn emergency patients away or demand payment before treatment. Rotimi said delays, administrative hurdles, upfront payment demands and poor referrals were still causing avoidable complications and deaths, and that the amendment would make clear who is responsible and how quickly they must act.

Background

There have been many reports in Nigeria of accident and gunshot victims, pregnant women and others dying after being refused care because they could not pay a deposit or provide a police report. The government has previously said hospitals must treat gunshot victims without waiting for police clearance.

Senate version

A similar bill sponsored by Senator Abdulaziz Yar'Adua is before the Senate. It proposes up to six months in prison, a fine of 300,000 naira, or both, for hospitals and health workers that fail to provide emergency care. It would also require hospitals to assess patients within 15 minutes, begin stabilisation within 30 minutes, provide a bed within two hours where available, and dispatch an ambulance or other transport within 30 minutes when a referral is needed. The medical and nursing councils would be asked to set up a national emergency care curriculum.

What happens next

The bill will now go to committee for further work before returning to the House for third reading.

Government says ₦50,000 NERSI beneficiaries will be chosen from official records, not applications

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The federal government says beneficiaries of its ₦50,000 monthly National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention will be selected through verified government records and set criteria, not by applying or being nominated. It warned against scams.

Read more

The federal government has explained how it will choose beneficiaries for its National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention (NERSI), which pays ₦50,000 a month to vulnerable Nigerians.

How people will be selected

According to reports on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, the government said beneficiaries will be identified strictly through verified official records and defined eligibility criteria, rather than through applications or nominations. Only people who meet the set criteria will be considered.

Scam warnings

Officials have warned Nigerians not to share their PINs or one-time passwords with anyone who calls claiming to represent the programme, and have explained who may collect the payment on a beneficiary's behalf. Several digital banks and payment platforms have also published guidance on how to check eligibility.

Background

NERSI is one of several government schemes aimed at cushioning the effect of high living costs, alongside the HoPE cash transfer programme, which pays ₦25,000 a month for three months to eligible households, and the CREDICORP consumer credit scheme. The government says it is using the national social register to target the poorest households. Critics have questioned whether the payments reach the right people and say the amounts are small compared with rising prices.

What it means

People who have not been contacted through official channels are being advised to treat any request for payment or personal details with suspicion.

Soldier rewarded with ₦500,000 for rejecting kidnap suspect's bribe in Plateau

4 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Nigerian Army says Private Utim Terwase refused a ₦500,000 bribe from a kidnapping suspect he had arrested in Plateau State. His commander ordered that he receive a reward equal to the sum he turned down.

Read more

The Nigerian Army has rewarded a soldier with ₦500,000 for refusing a bribe of the same amount from a suspected kidnapper in Plateau State.

What happened

According to the Army, Private Utim Terwase arrested a suspected kidnapper after foiling an escape attempt. The suspect then reportedly offered him ₦500,000 to let him go. The soldier refused, handed the suspect over to the appropriate authorities and reported the bribe offer through proper channels.

The reward

The General Officer Commanding the Army's 3 Division, which runs Operation Enduring Peace in Plateau State, praised the soldier's integrity and directed that he be given a cash reward equal to the bribe he rejected. He urged other soldiers to follow his example.

Background

Plateau State has suffered repeated attacks by armed groups and kidnappers, and security forces are deployed across the state. Bribery is a long-standing concern in Nigeria's security services.

How people reacted

Many Nigerians praised the soldier as a role model and said such acts of honesty should be celebrated more often.

57 Nigerians at risk as Gambia orders banks to replace foreign staff

4 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigerians in Diaspora Commission says 57 Nigerian professionals in four Nigerian-owned banks in The Gambia could lose their jobs after the Gambian central bank ordered banks to replace foreign staff with Gambians by 31 December. The FG has urged a rethink.

Read more

The federal government has raised concern over a directive by The Gambia's central bank ordering commercial banks to replace foreign employees with Gambian nationals, a move that could cost dozens of Nigerians their jobs.

The directive

According to reports, the Central Bank of The Gambia issued a circular dated 16 September 2026 giving banks until 31 December 2026 to replace non-Gambian staff with qualified Gambians.

Nigeria's concern

The Nigerians in Diaspora Commission (NiDCOM) said 57 Nigerian professionals working in four Nigerian-owned banks in The Gambia could be affected. It urged the Gambian authorities to reconsider the directive and to engage with the banks and other stakeholders to reach a fair solution.

Background

Nigerian banks have expanded across West Africa over the past two decades, with several operating subsidiaries in The Gambia. Both countries are members of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS), whose protocols promote the free movement of people and the right to work across member states. Many West African countries, however, have rules encouraging the employment of their own citizens in key sectors.

Why it matters

The move comes as several countries tighten rules on foreign workers. Nigerians abroad have recently faced stricter immigration policies in the UK, Portugal and elsewhere.

What happens next

It is not yet clear whether The Gambia will change or delay the policy.
