JIBWIS and Christian group call for DSS probe of video allegedly threatening worshippers in Enugu and Jos

26 Oct 2026, 10:42 GMT

Update: JIBWIS national chairman Sheikh Abdullahi Bala Lau has urged the DSS, the Nigeria Police Force and other security agencies to identify the individual in the video. The Northern Christian Association has also called on security agencies to establish whether the footage is authentic and identify those featured.

There are conflicting claims online about when the video was recorded, with some users saying it dates back to 2020 and others saying it was posted only days ago. Social media users have also named a man they claim is the speaker, and there are unconfirmed reports that he has been suspended by the movement. None of these claims has been independently verified, and no official has named anyone.

Of particular concern is the apparent presence of children and young people during the chant. Critics fear that exposing minors to messages that appear to encourage religious violence could contribute to radicalisation and deepen religious tensions.

A disturbing video reportedly filmed somewhere in Northern Nigeria has raised serious concerns over alleged threats of religious violence, and is now being reported by several Nigerian news outlets.

The footage shows young men in brown uniforms and blue berets marching and chanting in Hausa during what appears to be a drill. News reports have linked the uniforms to the first-aid volunteer wing of the Islamic movement JIBWIS, whose members help coordinate Friday Jumu'ah prayers.

According to a Hausa-to-English translation circulating with the video, the drill leader allegedly calls for AK-47 rifles so the group can travel to Enugu and attack Christians during Sunday worship. He reportedly refers to the worshippers as "infidels", imitates the sound of gunfire, and also mentions Jos as another target.

Lere Olayinka, media aide to the FCT Minister, has called on the DSS and the police to investigate the video.

However, the location, date, identity of the speaker, the group's actual affiliation and the accuracy of the translation have not been independently verified. No response from the group had been reported at the time of writing.

If the translation is accurate, this is an extremely serious matter. Nobody should be allowed to use religion as an excuse to threaten innocent people, whether they are Christians or Muslims.

If someone openly calls for weapons to attack people during worship, shouldn't that be enough to trigger an immediate security investigation? Nigeria has suffered enough religious violence, and credible threats against worshippers cannot be treated as ordinary speech.

Petrol prices fall at filling stations after Dangote cuts rate by ₦51

18 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Major marketers including AA Rano, NIPCO and AP Ardova have reportedly reduced pump prices to between ₦1,350 and ₦1,385 per litre. It follows a ₦51 per litre cut by Dangote Refinery.

Read more

Motorists are beginning to see some relief at filling stations as major marketers reduce petrol prices following a ₦51 per litre cut by the Dangote Petroleum Refinery.

The new prices

A market survey on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, reportedly showed that AA Rano, the Nigerian Independent Petroleum Company (NIPCO) and AP Ardova had lowered their petrol prices to between ₦1,350 and ₦1,385 per litre. Several filling stations are said to have cut prices by between ₦20 and ₦60 per litre, with NNPC and other marketers also adjusting their rates.

Why prices are falling

The reductions come as the cost of importing petrol has dropped, with the landing cost of imported fuel reportedly falling to about ₦1,067 per litre, below Dangote Refinery's own rate. This has put pressure on local prices.

The survey suggested more stations could follow with price cuts in the coming days.

Kwara suspends children's hospital chief after mothers and sick children were locked out

18 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The Kwara State Government has suspended the chief medical director of the Children's Specialist Hospital in Ilorin, after a viral video showed mothers and sick children allegedly locked out for arriving by 7am. The board says a shortage of doctors caused a backlog.

Read more

The Kwara State Government has reportedly suspended the Chief Medical Director of the Children's Specialist Hospital, Centre Igboro, Ilorin, Dr Mosunmola Folorunsho, after mothers and their sick children were allegedly locked out of the hospital.

What happened

A video that went viral showed mothers with sick children who said they had been shut out for arriving by 7am, reportedly because they had not arrived by 5:30am. Following the outrage, the Executive Secretary of the Kwara State Hospitals Management Board, Dr Abdulraheem Malik, and members of the management team made an emergency visit to the hospital.

The government's action

In a statement on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the board said the CMD had been suspended and given 48 hours to explain her role in the incident. She was directed to hand over to the most senior doctor at the hospital pending further directives.

The board's explanation

The board said its findings showed the hospital had a temporary shortage of doctors, after one of the doctors scheduled for the morning shift was away at a malaria training programme. This created a backlog of patients waiting to be seen.

TRCN gives all teachers until end of 2027 to get licensed

18 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria says every teacher must be certified and licensed by December 2027. Licences will have to be renewed every three years.

Read more

The Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria (TRCN) has given teachers across the country until December 2027 to obtain professional certification and practising licences.

What the council said

The council's Registrar, Dr Ronke Soyombo, announced the deadline while speaking to journalists in Abuja on Tuesday, 17 November 2026. She said the council will no longer tolerate people teaching without the required credentials and is strengthening registration, licensing and qualifying examinations.

How it works

Soyombo explained that professional certification is obtained once, while practising licences must be renewed every three years, with evidence that the teacher has taken part in continuous professional development programmes.

Education students are now required to sit the TRCN examination before completing their training, while serving teachers must also meet the requirements.

Help for non-education graduates

The council is introducing a faster route to professional status for teachers who do not hold education degrees, so they can get the training they need before the deadline.
