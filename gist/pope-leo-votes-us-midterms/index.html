Pope Leo XIV casts mail-in ballot in US midterm elections

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

Pope Leo XIV, the first American pope, has voted in the United States midterm elections, the Vatican has confirmed.

What happened

The Holy See press office said on Friday, 30 October 2026, that the Pope had cast a mail-in ballot in Illinois, where he was born. It said he had exercised his right to vote as a US citizen whenever he was eligible, and that he believes citizens have both a right and a duty to take part in democratic processes. His ballot is expected to include races for the US Senate, the governorship of Illinois and the House of Representatives. He is believed to be the first pope ever to vote in an American election.

Background

The Pope was born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago and holds both American and Peruvian citizenship, having spent many years as a missionary and bishop in Peru. He was elected pope in May 2025 after the death of Pope Francis. Public records show he voted regularly in Illinois before becoming pope, including in the 2024 presidential election. The Vatican did not say who he voted for.

Why it matters

The US midterm elections in November will decide control of Congress, and the Pope's decision to vote has drawn wide attention because of his role as a global religious leader. Supporters praised him for setting an example of civic duty, while others questioned whether a pope should take part in a national election.

What happens next

The Pope is expected to continue his public schedule, including a planned visit to South America.

Iran says two oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines in Strait of Hormuz

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines while using unauthorised routes through the Strait of Hormuz. The vessels were not named. The incident comes days after US strikes on Iranian targets.

Read more

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps says two oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

What Iran said

In a statement carried by Iran's state news agency on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the Guards said one tanker struck a mine in the eastern part of the strait and the other elsewhere in the waterway, while trying to cross using routes not authorised by Tehran. The statement did not name the vessels or give their nationalities, and accused them of violations including switching off their navigation and tracking systems. There was no independent confirmation of the incidents or of any casualties.

Background

Iran has sharply restricted shipping through the strait since a war began in late February, requiring ships to follow routes it has approved. The United States responded with a naval blockade of Iranian ports and has helped tankers pass through the waterway, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil. Iran has reported several ships catching fire in the strait in recent weeks. The incident comes days after US forces struck an Iranian air defence site on Qeshm Island, an operation President Donald Trump described as minor.

Why it matters

Disruption in the Strait of Hormuz affects global oil supply and prices, which in turn affect Nigeria's oil revenue and the cost of imported fuel.

US strikes Iranian air defence site as Trump insists 'we are not at war'

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

US forces struck an Iranian air defence site on Qeshm Island on Friday, the second round of strikes this week, hours after Washington offered up to $20 million for information on Iran's nuclear programme. Trump described the strike as minor.

Read more

US forces struck an Iranian air defence site on Friday, 6 November 2026, the second round of American strikes on Iran in a week.

What happened

President Donald Trump told reporters the operation was minor, saying Iran had built an anti-aircraft device and US forces destroyed it in a matter of minutes. He insisted the United States was not at war with Iran, pointing to the absence of recent US casualties. Iranian state media reported two explosions in the Suza area of Qeshm Island, close to the Strait of Hormuz, a major route for the world's oil shipments. Earlier in the week, on Monday, US forces carried out strikes that Washington described as defensive, with explosions reported on the islands of Qeshm and Hengam and near the naval hub of Bandar Abbas.

Reward offer

Hours before Friday's strike, the US State Department offered up to $20 million and possible relocation for information about Iran's nuclear programme, including its scientists, technology and the organisations involved.

Talks

Trump said Washington remained in contact with Tehran. Regional officials said Iran was considering new proposals brokered by Qatar to ease tensions, but that it would not negotiate over its nuclear programme until the US met several conditions, including lifting sanctions and ending a blockade of Iranian ports.

Why it matters

Trump acknowledged that the conflict had contributed to his party's losses in this week's midterm elections, partly because it pushed up petrol prices. Tensions in the Gulf can also affect global oil prices, which matter for Nigeria's revenue and fuel costs.

Paraguay's Santiago Peña impeached and placed under house arrest

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Paraguay's Senate voted unanimously to remove President Santiago Peña over the collapse of Ueno Bank, a day after he offered to resign. He was placed under house arrest, and Vice-President Pedro Alliana was sworn in.

Read more

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has been removed from office by the Senate and placed under house arrest over the collapse of Ueno Bank.

What happened

On Friday, 6 November 2026, all 44 senators present, including members of Peña's own Colorado Party, voted to remove him; one senator was absent. He was arrested minutes after the vote, and Vice-President Pedro Alliana was sworn in as interim president, expected to serve until 2028. Peña had offered to resign on Thursday, but senators went ahead with the impeachment, which strips him of the immunity normally enjoyed by former presidents. Senate President Basilio Núñez said Peña had been found guilty of dereliction of duty.

The allegations

The impeachment charges included conflicts of interest, manipulation of regulatory oversight, diversion of pension funds and involvement in a cover-up involving the central bank. Peña, who until last year held shares in the company that owned Ueno Bank, has denied wrongdoing. Prosecutors said he was due to be questioned.

Background

The collapse of Ueno Bank left large numbers of Paraguayans unable to access their savings and pensions, triggering a political crisis. Peña, an economist and former finance minister, took office in August 2023. Earlier this week, he apologised to Paraguayans in a video message.

What happens next

Investigators are expected to continue their inquiries into the bank's collapse, while depositors wait to learn whether they will recover their money.

Armed man arrested near White House after allegedly saying voices told him to kill Trump

7 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The US Secret Service arrested a Venezuelan man near the White House after he allegedly said voices told him to kill President Trump. Agents found a rifle, two handguns and ammunition in the car, which had been reported stolen.

Read more

The US Secret Service has arrested a man near the White House after he allegedly told agents that voices in his head had instructed him to travel to Washington and kill President Donald Trump.

What happened

According to US officials, agents spotted the man behaving suspiciously in a car parked beside the White House on Thursday, 5 November 2026. During questioning, he allegedly said voices had directed him to come to the capital and assassinate the President. Agents found that the car had been reported stolen, and a search turned up a rifle, two handguns and dozens of rounds of ammunition.

The suspect

The Department of Homeland Security said the man is a Venezuelan national who entered the United States illegally in 2024. He has been charged with several firearms offences, as well as unauthorised use of a vehicle and receiving stolen property, and is being held by the US Marshals Service. He has not yet been tried.

Secret Service comments

Secret Service Director Sean Curran said the arrest showed the kind of security environment his agents deal with every day. Officials said the White House has seen a significant rise in incidents linked to mental health problems, as the presidency often becomes a focus for people experiencing such conditions.

Background

Trump survived two assassination attempts during the 2024 election campaign. The arrest came in the same week that the President's party suffered heavy losses in the midterm elections and he named Stephen Miller as his new chief of staff.
