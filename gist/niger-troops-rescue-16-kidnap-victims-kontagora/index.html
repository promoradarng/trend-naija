Troops rescue 16 kidnap victims in Niger State, recover weapons and cash

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

The Nigerian Army says its troops have rescued 16 kidnapped people during operations against armed groups in Niger State.

What the army said

In a statement on Friday, 6 November 2026, Captain Adebayo Samuel Adewumi of the army's 18 Brigade said the operations took place between 3 and 5 November. In the main operation on 5 November, troops acting on intelligence about armed men moving from Mashegu Local Government Area towards Rijiyan Nagwamatse in Kontagora Local Government Area set up an ambush. The army said it clashed with the group, which had abducted local residents, killed an unconfirmed number of the attackers and freed 16 victims.

Items recovered

Troops said they recovered two AK-47 rifles, seven magazines, three rocket-propelled grenade bombs, 33 motorcycles, ammunition and about ₦4.1 million in cash. In a separate operation on 3 November, troops intercepted armed men moving from Kebbi State into Rijau Local Government Area of Niger State, forcing them to retreat, and recovered five motorcycles, a phone and about ₦644,000. The rescued victims received first aid and were handed over to the authorities to be reunited with their families.

Background

Niger State has suffered repeated attacks and kidnappings by armed groups. Separately, the families of Islamiyya students abducted in Rafi Local Government Area last week say the kidnappers have demanded more than ₦100 million. The army did not say whether any of those students were among the people rescued.

What happens next

The army said operations would continue across the state.

  • Sixteen kidnap victims were rescued.
  • Troops recovered rifles, RPG bombs and 33 motorcycles.
  • The operations took place in Kontagora and Rijau LGAs.

Sweden returns 39 Benin Bronzes to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Sweden has handed back 39 Benin artefacts taken during the British invasion of Benin in 1897, at a ceremony in Lagos. Six of the objects will stay in Sweden on a 10-year loan.

Read more

Sweden has returned 39 Benin Bronzes and other artefacts to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted from the Benin Kingdom.

The handover

Representatives of Sweden's National Museums of World Culture formally handed over the objects at a ceremony in Lagos on Thursday, 12 November 2026. They will be received by Nigeria's National Commission for Museums and Monuments. The collection includes cultural objects known as Emwin Arre. Six of the 39 artefacts will remain in Sweden under a 10-year loan arrangement.

Why it matters

The objects were among thousands taken by British forces who attacked and burned Benin City in 1897 before bringing the kingdom under British rule. Michael Barrett, curator for Africa at Sweden's National Museums of World Culture, described the return as an important step in addressing colonial-era looting of cultural property.

Background

Nigeria has been campaigning for decades for the return of the Benin Bronzes, which are held in museums and private collections around the world. The Netherlands returned 119 objects in 2025, and Germany handed over 20 Benin Bronzes in 2022. Many more remain abroad.

Four UNN students die after off-campus party in Nsukka, six in hospital

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka has confirmed that four students died after a party held to celebrate a friend's final exams, after allegedly consuming a mix of alcohol and drugs. Six others are in hospital, and the university has declared three days of mourning.

Read more

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka (UNN) has confirmed that four of its students have died after an off-campus party in Nsukka, Enugu State.

What happened

Speaking to journalists on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Vice-Chancellor Professor Simon Ortuanya said 10 students were involved in the incident at the party on Wednesday, 11 November, which had been organised to celebrate a friend finishing his final-year examinations. He said the four who died allegedly consumed substances believed to be a mixture of alcohol and drugs. The other six are receiving treatment in different hospitals. Earlier reports had put the death toll as high as seven.

University responds

The Vice-Chancellor described the deaths as one too many and declared three days of mourning in honour of the students. He also corrected reports that all the students were from the Faculty of Pharmaceutical Sciences, saying they came from different departments. The exact substances involved have not been officially identified, and it is not clear whether the police have opened an investigation.

Police investigate

The Enugu State Police Command said on Thursday evening that it had opened an investigation into the deaths. It said the four victims, two men and two women, fell ill after allegedly drinking alcoholic beverages at a gathering at a hostel in Nsukka and were confirmed dead on arrival at hospital. Police said the drinks had been recovered from the scene and the bodies taken to a mortuary for autopsy.

Background

Drug and alcohol misuse among young people has become a growing public health concern in Nigeria.

US grounds all departing flights nationwide after telecom outage

14 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The US Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide ground stop on all departing flights on Friday, citing safety. More than 2,000 flights were cancelled and over 10,000 delayed, and officials blamed a telecommunications outage.

Read more

The United States briefly halted all departing flights across the country on Friday, 13 November 2026, causing widespread disruption.

What happened

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a sudden nationwide ground stop at 3.54pm Eastern time, citing extraordinary circumstances and safety, and said all departing traffic should remain on the ground until further notice. It said inbound international flights would be handled case by case. At the time, more than 4,000 flights bound for US destinations were in the air, according to flight-tracking data.

The impact

Within hours, more than 2,000 flights had been cancelled and more than 10,000 delayed, according to flight-tracking figures, and lawmakers demanded an explanation.

The cause

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication so far of anything malicious, and that the problem appeared to be a telecommunications outage. He said President Donald Trump had been briefed.
