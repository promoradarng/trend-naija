A recent episode of comedian Isbae U's interview show 'Curiosity Made Me Ask' took a dramatic turn when actor Aremu Afolayan reportedly slapped the host and tore his clothes on camera.

Enioluwa apologises after backlash over comments on young Nigerians and work

17 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Influencer Enioluwa Adeoluwa has apologised after a clip from a youth conference, in which he appeared to suggest young people don't like to work, drew wide criticism. BBNaija's Nelly Mbonu was among those who pushed back.

Influencer Enioluwa Adeoluwa has issued a public apology after remarks he made about young Nigerians at a youth conference sparked a backlash online.

What happened

A clip from the Emergence of Stars Port Harcourt Youth Conference showed Enioluwa making remarks that many viewers felt painted young Nigerians as unwilling to work. The video spread quickly on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, drawing criticism from people who said he ignored unemployment, systemic barriers and the economic struggles young Nigerians face.

Many critics accused him of speaking from a position of privilege. BBNaija star Nelly Mbonu was among those who publicly challenged him, rejecting the idea that young people simply choose not to work hard.

The apology

Later the same day, Enioluwa posted a statement on X addressing the reaction, apologising for the remarks and acknowledging his privilege.

Aremu Afolayan reportedly clashes with Isbae U on Curiosity Made Me Ask

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Actor Aremu Afolayan's appearance on Isbae U's show Curiosity Made Me Ask reportedly ended in a heated exchange. He took offence when the host compared their late fathers.

Read more

Nollywood actor Aremu Afolayan has become a talking point online after his appearance on comedian Isbae U's interview show, Curiosity Made Me Ask, reportedly ended in a heated exchange.

What happened

Isbae U, known for his sarcastic interview style, is said to have questioned whether Aremu was still active in Nollywood and made jokes about his body and how he earns a living given his few recent film roles.

Aremu reportedly explained that he has stayed away from film sets because of insecurity, saying travelling for shoots had become dangerous and he was not willing to risk his life for a role paying ₦2 million or ₦3 million.

Where it went wrong

The mood reportedly changed when Isbae U brought up his own late father, actor Sir K. Kamoru, and compared him with Aremu's late father, the veteran filmmaker Ade Love Afolayan. Aremu is said to have taken offence and warned the host against such comparisons, insisting his father's contribution to the film industry was not up for debate. He also reportedly questioned Isbae U's own achievements as an actor.

Political violence: Jonathan sounds alarm, Senate asks INEC to disqualify violent candidates as APC and ADC trade blame

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that some campaigns are being run like cult groups, while the Senate urged police to probe recent attacks and INEC to disqualify candidates linked to violence. The APC accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, a claim the ADC rejected.

Read more

Concern over violence ahead of the 2027 elections dominated political discussion on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, with a former president, the Senate and the main parties all weighing in.

Jonathan's warning

Speaking at a peace and security conference organised by his foundation in Abuja, former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that rising political intolerance and violence could threaten the elections. He said some political groups were running their campaign teams like cult groups, and that Nigerians at home and abroad had expressed fears about possible crises after the polls. He also condemned thuggery and the use of young people as tools of violence.

The Senate's resolution

The Senate, acting on a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi (ADC, Bauchi Central), condemned recent attacks on political figures. These included the stoning of President Bola Tinubu's motorcade after Friday prayers in Abuja, reported attacks on NDC candidate Peter Obi in Edo State, a disrupted NDC procession in Asaba on 7 November that left one person dead, and an attack on ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar's convoy in Sokoto.

Lawmakers asked the Inspector-General of Police to carry out impartial investigations and prosecute those responsible, and urged INEC to apply the law disqualifying candidates linked to electoral violence. The motion was seconded by Senator Ali Ndume (APC, Borno South).

APC and ADC trade accusations

The APC presidential campaign council, through its spokesperson Dele Alake, accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, deepfakes and hired protests to damage President Tinubu's campaign, pointing to the convoy incident in Abuja.

The ADC campaign council, through Phrank Shaibu, dismissed the claim as a distraction from the government's economic record. It challenged the APC to provide evidence or withdraw the allegation, and asked why no one had been prosecuted if the ruling party had proof. The NDC's response was not included in the reports.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
