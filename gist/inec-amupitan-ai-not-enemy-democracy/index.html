INEC chairman says AI is not an enemy of democracy, but warns of deepfakes

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

The Chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Amupitan, says artificial intelligence (AI) is not an enemy of democracy, even though its misuse could create serious problems for elections.

What he said

Speaking at the Beyond the Ballot Summit in Abuja on Monday, 2 November 2026, Amupitan reportedly said election bodies around the world use machine-assisted processes to find duplicate records in large voter registers, plan logistics, analyse data and work more efficiently. However, he warned that generative AI has put powerful tools in the hands of people who want to disrupt elections or mislead voters, and that abilities which once needed a lot of money and technical skill can now be obtained very cheaply. He gave examples such as cloning the voices of public figures and creating realistic fake recordings of election officials making false announcements.

Background

His comments come days after INEC's ICT director admitted that some registration officers had entered AI-generated and poor-quality photos into the preliminary voter register, which was published online on 26 October. INEC says it detected the problem with its biometric identification system and is cleaning up the register before it is certified on 15 December. Opposition parties and civil society groups have called for a full audit, and ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has demanded a forensic review. Amupitan had earlier announced that INEC would use AI in parts of the electoral system ahead of the 2027 elections.

Why it matters

Fake videos and audio have already been shared during the campaign, and several politicians have accused rivals of using deepfakes. Observers say voters will need to be careful about what they share as the January 2027 elections approach.

  • Amupitan says AI helps find duplicate records and plan logistics.
  • He warned about voice cloning and fake announcements.
  • The voter register is due to be certified on 15 December.

Kwankwaso accuses Kano Governor Abba Yusuf of using EFCC to target him

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

NDC vice-presidential candidate Rabiu Kwankwaso has accused his former protégé, Kano Governor Abba Yusuf, of trying to bring him down through anti-corruption agencies ahead of 2027. He claimed the governor asked the EFCC to act against him.

Read more

Rabiu Kwankwaso, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) vice-presidential candidate and former governor of Kano State, has publicly accused Kano Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf of plotting against him through anti-corruption agencies ahead of the 2027 elections.

The accusation

In a statement on his X account on Friday, 27 November 2026, Kwankwaso claimed that the governor, who served under him for decades as an aide and commissioner, had formally asked the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) to act against him. He alleged that when that effort failed, the governor turned to a little-known group to stage protests at the EFCC's office to put pressure on the agency.

Kwankwaso said the governor wanted to damage his reputation.

Background

The two men, once close allies, have fallen out since Yusuf defected from the NNPP to the APC in January 2026. In recent days the governor claimed that Kwankwaso once sent him to promise President Tinubu they would rejoin the APC, a claim the Kwankwasiyya Movement has rejected. Yusuf has also spoken publicly about the end of his marriage to Kwankwaso's niece.

The other side

No response from Governor Yusuf or the EFCC to Kwankwaso's claims was included in the reports.

Fashola says no one should die for rigging elections

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Former Lagos Governor Babatunde Fashola has condemned the 'rig and die' slogan, saying death is not the penalty for electoral offences. He backed tougher prison terms instead.

Read more

Former Lagos State Governor Babatunde Fashola has condemned the 'rig and die' slogan ahead of the 2027 elections, insisting that nobody should lose their life over electoral offences.

What he said

In a television interview that aired on Friday, 27 November 2026, Fashola, a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, said the punishment for rigging under the Electoral Act is not death. He added that even if it were, which he would not support, there must be a process, including a charge, a trial and a chance to defend oneself, before a life is taken.

He nevertheless said electoral manipulation should attract serious consequences, suggesting tougher prison sentences without the option of a fine for offences that undermine democracy, and urged politicians to campaign rather than rig.

Background

The slogan has sparked a national debate. INEC has warned it could incite mob attacks at polling units, while singer 2Baba said this week he saw nothing wrong with it and backed severe punishment for riggers. Fashola's comments drew mixed reactions online.

INEC names nine states as hotspots for campaign violence

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

INEC Chairman Joash Amupitan has listed Lagos, Edo, Ekiti, Enugu, Ebonyi, Delta, Cross River, Oyo and Benue as states hit by campaign violence and political intolerance. He warned that the unrest could threaten preparations for the 2027 elections.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence and political intolerance ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The states

INEC Chairman Professor Joash Amupitan listed Lagos, Edo, Ekiti, Enugu, Ebonyi, Delta, Cross River, Oyo and Benue. He spoke on Wednesday in Abuja at a strategic election security conference organised with the Office of the National Security Adviser and attended by the heads of security agencies.

What he said

Amupitan said campaign convoys, town hall meetings, party offices and supporters had all come under attack in the listed states. He warned that the growing unrest could jeopardise preparations for the elections, and also raised concern about harassment, intimidation and assault of INEC officials and staff.

Background

The warning follows a series of violent incidents this month, including deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states. A civic group this week recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths since July, and INEC has also cautioned against the 'rig and die' slogan.

Shettima says Obi failed to transform his own hometown as governor

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Vice President Kashim Shettima has reportedly mocked Peter Obi, saying he governed Anambra for eight years without transforming his hometown, Agulu. He praised Governor Chukwuma Soludo for dualising the road there.

Read more

Vice President Kashim Shettima has reportedly taken a swipe at NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, questioning why his hometown, Agulu, was not transformed during his eight years as governor of Anambra State.

What he said

Shettima made the remarks at a town hall meeting with traditional rulers in Isuofia, Anambra State, where he praised Governor Chukwuma Soludo for infrastructure projects. He credited Soludo, who is from Isuofia, with dualising the road linking Agulu to Ekwulobia, saying it took another governor to transform the road to Obi's hometown.

Background

Shettima has been campaigning in Anambra for President Tinubu's re-election. He recently said he would keep firing back at Atiku Abubakar as long as he attacks the president.

The other side

No response from Obi or the NDC was included in the reports.
