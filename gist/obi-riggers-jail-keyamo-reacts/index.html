Obi says election riggers will go to jail under him as Keyamo mocks his Nasarawa remarks

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has said anyone who rigs elections under his government will go to prison. He made the comments at a party town hall in Nasarawa State on Wednesday, 28 October 2026, during a campaign visit that has also drawn a sharp response from the ruling party.

What Obi said

Asked what his party would do about rigging in 2027, Obi said that under his presidency INEC officials or anyone else who manipulated elections would be jailed. He said strengthening political parties and the electoral process would make rigging impossible, and that the law would apply equally to everyone, from the President to the ordinary citizen. He also proposed that candidates for public office should prove they attended primary and secondary school, with records open to public checks, and said anyone found to have forged certificates would face prison.

Keyamo's reaction

In a radio interview during the same visit, Obi argued that Nigeria would be much richer if public money had been kept in banks earning interest instead of being mismanaged. He also said the results of government projects should speak for themselves without ceremonies or television coverage. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo seized on those comments in a post on X on Thursday, describing them in strongly mocking terms and saying he did not know whether to laugh or cry. He claimed the remarks explained what he described as a fall in Obi's popularity.

Background

Obi, a former Anambra governor, came third in the 2023 presidential election as the Labour Party candidate and is now running on the NDC ticket with Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate. Exchanges between his camp and senior APC figures have grown more heated since campaigns began this month, with both sides trading personal attacks online.

What happens next

Obi is expected to continue his tour of the North Central, while the APC campaign has indicated it will keep challenging his record in office.

  • Obi says INEC officials who rig elections will be jailed if he wins.
  • He wants candidates' school records to be open to public checks.
  • Keyamo mocked his comments about keeping public money in banks.

Reps urge Tinubu to consider state of emergency in Delta if violence worsens

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The House of Representatives has asked President Tinubu to closely monitor security in Delta State, warning that a state of emergency may become necessary if political violence continues. It also told the police to set up a special investigative team.

Read more

The House of Representatives has called on President Bola Tinubu to closely watch the security situation in Delta State, warning that a state of emergency may be needed if conditions keep getting worse.

The motion

The resolution on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, followed a motion by Representative Benedict Etanabene, who pointed to a series of politically motivated attacks in the state. Lawmakers expressed concern that the violence was spreading fear and threatening peace among communities.

Directive to the police

The House also directed the Inspector-General of Police to identify flashpoints across the state and set up a special team to investigate the attacks.

Background

The resolution comes two days after gunmen attacked an NDC campaign event led by former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege in Uduere, Ughelli North, killing several people. Police have invited Omo-Agege and others for questioning, and the Inspector-General has ordered an investigation. Delta Governor Sheriff Oborevwori has called for everyone involved to be arrested, whatever their party.

ADC tells Tinubu to resign or face impeachment over Kano governor's interview

25 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The African Democratic Congress has reportedly demanded that President Tinubu resign or face impeachment, citing Kano Governor Abba Yusuf's recent interview about the 2023 Supreme Court ruling. The ADC says it points to possible gross misconduct.

Read more

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has reportedly issued an ultimatum to President Bola Tinubu, demanding that he either resign or face impeachment proceedings in the National Assembly.

Why the ADC made the demand

The demand follows Kano State Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf's recent Hausa-language interview, in which he spoke about a promise to rejoin the APC if the president did not interfere in his Supreme Court case over the 2023 governorship election. The ADC argued that the governor's remarks suggested a link between the court case and a political deal, and that this could amount to gross misconduct, a constitutional ground for removing a president.

Background

Yusuf defected from the NNPP to the APC in January 2026. In his interview, he claimed that his former mentor, Rabiu Kwankwaso, sent him to make the promise to Tinubu. The Kwankwasiyya Movement has rejected the claim as false and said the governor offered no evidence.

The other side

No response from the presidency or the APC to the ADC's demand was included in the reports.

Peter Obi, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate, has reportedly defended members of the Kwankwasiyya movement who carry sticks to political gatherings.

What he said

Speaking in a television interview in Jalingo, Taraba State, on Tuesday evening, 24 November 2026, after a town hall meeting, Obi is said to have explained that the movement, led by his running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso, has a long tradition of supporters attending political events with sticks. He said there was no record of the sticks being used to harm anyone.

He also said that calls against electoral malpractice were being misread as opposition to campaigning.

Background

Political violence has become a major concern ahead of the January 2027 elections, following Monday's deadly attack on an NDC campaign event in Delta State. At the Taraba town hall, Obi led a minute of silence for the party members killed.
