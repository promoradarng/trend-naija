Kemi Badenoch hits back after being called a 'Nigerian scammer' online

30 Oct 2026, 11:49 GMT

Kemi Badenoch, the Nigerian-raised leader of Britain's Conservative Party, has condemned online attacks that referred to her heritage, saying they show what kind of people her critics are.

What happened

According to reports on Friday, 30 October 2026, Raheem Kassam, a former adviser linked to Nigel Farage's camp, repeatedly called Badenoch a scammer in social media posts over the weekend, mocking her Nigerian background and warning people not to give her their bank details. He also accused her of using her heritage to advance her career when it suited her.

What Badenoch said

Speaking on a British breakfast television programme, Badenoch said some people in Reform UK appeared to think that attacking her race would help them. She said Britain is not a racist country and that such attacks would not work, and urged former Conservative voters who had switched to Reform to come back.

Reform's response

Robert Jenrick, a former Conservative MP who has defected to Reform UK, reportedly declined to call the comments racist, describing them as perhaps rude or unpleasant. Badenoch said Jenrick was now Nigel Farage's problem. Farage said that mentioning that someone grew up in Nigeria was not racist, but later described Kassam's comment as over the top. A Labour spokesperson criticised Jenrick for not condemning the remarks.

Background

Badenoch was born in London, grew up partly in Lagos and moved back to Britain as a teenager. She became Conservative leader in 2024. Reform UK has been rising in opinion polls, and several former Conservatives have joined the party, increasing tensions between the two.

Trump envoys Kushner and Witkoff meet Putin as Ukraine peace push continues

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held more than two hours of talks with Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin, a day after Russia and Ukraine agreed a prisoner exchange in their first direct talks in nine months. No breakthrough was announced.

Read more

US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner have met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow as efforts to end the war in Ukraine continue.

The meeting

The envoys spent more than two hours with Putin in the Kremlin on Thursday, 12 November 2026. Kushner reportedly pushed for European countries to join the talks so they could become multilateral, something Moscow has so far resisted. Putin said Russia has never opposed dialogue with Europe but insisted there could be only one dividing line with Ukraine, a state border. No breakthrough was announced, and a Kremlin adviser said Putin would continue the discussion with President Donald Trump at the APEC summit in China later this month.

Abu Dhabi talks

The meeting came a day after talks in Abu Dhabi, the first direct negotiations between Russian and Ukrainian delegations in nine months, which produced a prisoner exchange deal but no agreement on territory.

What happens next

The envoys were due to travel to Dublin to brief Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, who has spoken of a window of opportunity for a deal. The European Union is preparing a new package of sanctions against Russia.

Ghana unveils new smaller cedi notes, to circulate from 1 December

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Bank of Ghana has launched a redesigned series of cedi banknotes with better security features, smaller sizes and Adinkra symbols, which will enter circulation on 1 December.

Read more

Ghana has unveiled a new series of banknotes as part of efforts to modernise its currency.

The new notes

The Bank of Ghana launched the Ghana Cedi Heritage Series on Thursday, 12 November 2026, with the notes due to enter circulation on 1 December. The bank says they have improved security features, last longer and are smaller and easier to carry. The designs feature historical figures, national heroes, historic sites and monuments, and include traditional Adinkra symbols on Ghanaian banknotes for the first time.

What the governor said

Bank of Ghana Governor Dr Johnson Pandit Asiama said a currency is more than a means of exchange and reflects a country's ability to manage its own monetary affairs. He said confidence in a currency depends on sound economic policy, strong institutions and public trust.

Background

Nigeria's own attempt to redesign the naira in late 2022 caused severe cash shortages in early 2023.

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu becomes New Jersey's lieutenant governor

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu has been appointed lieutenant governor of the US state of New Jersey, becoming the first immigrant to hold the post. She will also serve as Secretary of State.

Read more

Shirley U. Emehelu, a lawyer born in Nigeria, has been appointed lieutenant governor of New Jersey in the United States.

The appointment

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill appointed Emehelu on Monday, 9 November 2026, making her the state's fifth lieutenant governor and the first immigrant to hold the office. She was sworn in at the Statehouse in Trenton by the state's Chief Justice, Stuart Rabner. The appointment also means that, for the first time in New Jersey's history, women hold the state's two highest executive offices at the same time. Emehelu will also serve as Secretary of State, a role that covers government administration and oversight of elections.

Why she was chosen

Sherrill described Emehelu as an experienced public servant and pointed to her record prosecuting financial crimes, protecting consumers, defending civil rights and tackling government waste. The governor also praised her leadership as acting Secretary of State, including overseeing the state's recent elections.

Her Nigerian roots

Emehelu, who moved from Nigeria to the United States as a child, said she was proud of what the appointment means for Black women, immigrants and people who share her Nigerian heritage, and expressed hope that it would inspire young people to serve. She said she would work with the governor on the cost of living and economic opportunity.

Salvadoran troops reportedly launch deadly operation against Haitian gang, leaving 20 dead

11 Nov 2026, 12:50 GMT

At least 20 people have reportedly been killed in an operation involving troops from El Salvador against suspected gang members in Haiti, where armed groups have taken control of large areas and forced thousands from their homes.

Read more

At least 20 people have reportedly been killed in an operation involving Salvadoran troops targeting suspected gang members in Haiti.

A country held hostage

The reported incident comes as Haiti continues to struggle with widespread gang violence, kidnappings and insecurity, particularly in areas where armed groups have taken control of communities and forced thousands of residents to flee.

For years, ordinary Haitians have lived under the constant threat of armed gangs, with many families unable to go about their daily lives without fear.

Foreign forces on the ground

Soldiers from El Salvador have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an internationally backed mission to help the Haitian police fight the gangs, alongside forces from other countries.

The reported operation has raised fresh questions about the growing role of foreign security forces in efforts to restore order in the troubled Caribbean nation.

What we don't know

The circumstances of the reported deaths, including the identities of those killed and the exact role played by the Salvadoran troops, have not been independently confirmed.
