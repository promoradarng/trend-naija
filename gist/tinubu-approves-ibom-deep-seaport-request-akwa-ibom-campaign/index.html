Tinubu approves Ibom Deep Seaport request during Akwa Ibom campaign, promises jobs and economic development

6 Nov 2026, 20:21 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has approved a request linked to the long-awaited Ibom Deep Seaport, announcing the decision while addressing supporters at his presidential campaign rally in Uyo, Akwa Ibom State.

"I look, and I approve it"

Speaking from the podium, flanked by party leaders and security officials, Tinubu told the crowd that the state's leadership had brought a request to him.

"Now your leader makes a request, for international export status. I look, and I approve it," he said, to cheers from supporters.

The Ibom Deep Seaport has been one of Akwa Ibom's biggest ambitions for years, with successive state governments pushing for it as a way to open the state to international trade and create jobs.

Promises of jobs and skills

Tinubu also used the occasion to make several promises about the state's economic future.

"I promise you skill development. I promise you jobs," he said. "We are going to plant a lot of economic trees."

He pointed to the palm tree, a major part of the region's economy, as an opportunity. "There is no reason why we cannot use the value of palm trees and the palm here," he said, adding that there was no reason the country could not create skills development that is "everlasting".

"Development must be even"

The President said growth must reach every part of the country. "Development must be even, inclusive and reasonable. It must bring progress and create economic value," he said.

What it means

If the approval leads to real progress on the seaport, it could be a major boost for Akwa Ibom's economy, bringing shipping, logistics and related businesses to the state.

But campaign promises are easy to make at rallies. Nigerians will be watching to see whether the approval is followed by funding, construction and the jobs that have been promised.

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has reportedly promised a ₦10 million reward to each councillor in Nigeria whose ward votes for President Bola Tinubu in the 2027 presidential election.

What he said

Wike made the pledge during a political engagement in Kwali Area Council of the Federal Capital Territory, according to reports on Monday, 9 November 2026. He also praised his Rainbow Coalition, saying it was bringing together politicians from across the country, and urged residents of Kwali and the wider FCT to back the President's re-election, arguing that a second term would bring more development to Abuja.

Background

Wike, a former Rivers governor and PDP chieftain, leads the Rainbow Coalition of politicians from the PDP and other parties backing Tinubu. He has been one of the President's most vocal campaigners, frequently criticising opposition candidates Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar.

INEC this week also warned political actors against inflammatory rhetoric ahead of the elections.

INEC condemns 'rig and die' slogan, warns of sanctions for electoral offenders

9 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

INEC has condemned the 'rig and die' slogan used by some political actors, warning that it could put election workers at risk, and urged anyone disputing results to use lawful channels.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has condemned the use of the slogan 'rig and die' by some political actors ahead of the 2027 elections.

What INEC said

Speaking at a forum in Enugu for journalists and media executives from the South-East, South-South and South-West, reported on Monday, 9 November 2026, INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Kudu Haruna said threats and inflammatory language could endanger presiding officers, temporary election staff, party agents and observers. He urged anyone who disputes an election result to use lawful channels, including complaints to INEC or security agencies and the courts. He also warned that election officials, including ad hoc staff, would face legal consequences if found to have compromised the process.

Background

The 'rig and die' slogan has been used by some opposition supporters to warn against electoral fraud, but critics say it can be read as a threat of violence. INEC recently said it had uncovered a plot to threaten and kidnap election workers, with suspects arrested and charged. Violence has already been reported at several campaign events, including a clash at an NDC rally in Asaba in which one person died.

What happens next

Haruna called for closer cooperation between INEC and the media to counter misinformation before the elections, which begin with the presidential poll on 16 January 2027.

Obi says NFF officials stopped him from greeting world champion Flamingos

9 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

NDC candidate Peter Obi says Nigeria Football Federation officials refused to let him congratulate the Flamingos when he saw them at Abuja airport on their return with the U-17 World Cup trophy. The NFF had not responded.

Read more

Peter Obi, presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has accused officials of the Nigeria Football Federation (NFF) of stopping him from congratulating the Flamingos after their World Cup triumph.

What Obi said

Obi said he was at the airport in Abuja at about 7am on Monday, 9 November 2026, to catch a flight to Enugu, when he saw the Flamingos, who had just arrived with the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup trophy. He said he asked his protocol officer to find out if he could go over and welcome the players, but was told that NFF officials had refused, reportedly warning that anyone who allowed him near the team would lose their job.

'Not the first time'

Obi said this was not the first time he had been prevented from supporting a national team. He recalled travelling to Uyo in March 2025 to watch a World Cup qualifier against Zimbabwe, and said that although he was warmly received before the match, he was not allowed the same access afterwards.

Background

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final in Rabat on Saturday to become the first African team to win the tournament, and were welcomed back to Nigeria on Monday. President Bola Tinubu and the First Lady have both congratulated the team.

Presidency responds

The Presidency accused Obi of trying to politicise national sports teams. In a post on X on Monday, the President's special assistant on social media, Dada Olusegun, said the national teams, including the Under-17 girls, represent the whole country and not any party or individual's ambitions, and that protocol rules around national teams are designed to protect young athletes from political exploitation, commercial distractions and media pressure. The NFF itself had not publicly commented.

Ebonyi governor Francis Nwifuru told President Tinubu at an APC rally in Abakaliki that Ebonyi is an APC state and that 80% of its votes are assured for him in 2027.

Ebonyi State governor Francis Nwifuru has promised President Bola Tinubu 80 per cent of the state's votes in the 2027 presidential election.

What he said

Speaking at the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential rally in Abakaliki on Monday, 9 November 2026, Nwifuru told the President that Ebonyi was an APC state, that 80 per cent of its votes were assured for him and that he would win by a landslide.

Background

The rally is part of Tinubu's campaign tour of the South-East and South-South, which has included stops in Akwa Ibom and Rivers states in the past week. Several South-East governors have declared support for the President, and the APGA in Anambra has also adopted him as its candidate. The South-East, however, is the home region of NDC candidate Peter Obi, who won most of the region's votes in 2023, and his party has set ambitious targets of its own, including 98 per cent of Anambra's votes.

How people reacted

The governor's pledge drew mixed reactions on social media. Supporters of the President welcomed it, while others questioned whether any governor could guarantee how residents would vote.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
