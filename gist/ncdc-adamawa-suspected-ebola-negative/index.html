Two suspected Ebola cases in Adamawa test negative, NCDC says

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says two people investigated for suspected Ebola virus disease in Adamawa State have tested negative.

What happened

NCDC Director-General Jide Idris said the Adamawa State Public Health Emergency Operations Centre received a report in the early hours of 30 October 2026 after two people in Yola developed symptoms that needed further investigation. The two were isolated, public health measures were started and samples were collected. Idris said the samples came back negative for Ebola after two rounds of testing.

Nigeria's only case

Idris said the case confirmed earlier in the Federal Capital Territory remained the only laboratory-confirmed Ebola case in Nigeria. That patient had recently travelled to Uganda and was later evacuated to Switzerland for treatment, while dozens of contacts were monitored. He praised the Adamawa Commissioner for Health and the state response team for acting quickly, and said the NCDC would keep supporting the state.

Contacts being monitored

In its latest situation report, published on Monday, 2 November, the NCDC said 62 contacts of the confirmed case had been identified and are being followed up. Their monitoring periods end on different dates between 31 October and 13 November 2026, so 13 November is the latest date on which follow-up of the current contacts is expected to finish. The agency said it had trained more contact tracers and that surveillance continues.

Advice to the public

The NCDC urged people to stay calm, avoid spreading unverified claims and rely on official channels for updates. Anyone with fever, severe weakness, headache, muscle pain, vomiting, diarrhoea or unexplained bleeding, especially after possible exposure, should seek medical help immediately, tell health workers about any travel or contact with sick people, and avoid self-medication.

Background

The current outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain, began in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May and has recorded more than 10,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,000 deaths, mostly in the DR Congo. Africa CDC has warned that the continent is not winning the fight, and African health ministers have agreed to strengthen border surveillance.

FG moves to start direct flights between Nigeria and Ireland

18 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo says Nigeria is working towards direct flights to Ireland, which could open a new route for local airlines such as Air Peace and Enugu Air.

Read more

Nigeria is taking steps to establish direct flights to Ireland, a move that could open a new international route for local airlines including Air Peace and Enugu Air.

What was said

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, disclosed the plan during a meeting with the Irish Ambassador to Nigeria, Lesley Ní Bhriain. He said Nigeria was ready to deepen aviation cooperation with Ireland, citing growing demand for travel between the two countries.

Why it matters

The proposed arrangement is expected to strengthen trade, tourism, education and business ties between the two countries, and make travel easier for Nigerians living in Ireland.

What happens next

Details such as the timeline for the first flights and which airlines will operate the route have not been announced.

Nigerian stock market loses ₦2.27 trillion in one day

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Investors lost about ₦2.27 trillion on the Nigerian Exchange on Tuesday as the All-Share Index fell 1.38%. Trading volume and deals also dropped.

Read more

The Nigerian stock market closed sharply lower on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, as sell-offs in major stocks wiped about ₦2.27 trillion off market value in a single trading session.

The numbers

Data from the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) showed the All-Share Index fell by 3,491.13 points, or 1.38%, to close at 249,318.65, down from 252,809.78 in the previous session. Total market capitalisation dropped by the same percentage, from ₦164.15 trillion to ₦161.88 trillion.

Trading activity

Activity also slowed. Investors traded 564.04 million shares, down 16.26% from 673.6 million in the previous session, while the number of deals fell by 18.91% to 30,992.

Bright spots

The losses were partly cushioned by buying interest in consumer goods and insurance stocks, with Cadbury Nigeria among the top gainers.

NCC gives MTN, Airtel and others until 31 December to use idle spectrum or lose it

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigerian Communications Commission has told telecom operators to prove they are using their allocated spectrum by 31 December 2026 or risk losing it. It wants better network quality for subscribers.

Read more

The Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) has given telecommunications operators, including MTN and Airtel, until 31 December 2026 to show they are effectively using their allocated spectrum or risk having it taken back.

What spectrum is

Spectrum refers to the radio frequencies operators use to carry calls, internet data and other wireless services. Because it is a limited national resource, the regulator wants to make sure allocations are supporting real network deployment.

Why the NCC is acting

The directive is aimed at ensuring valuable frequencies are not left idle while many Nigerians complain of poor network quality, congestion and limited broadband coverage.

What operators must do

Under the directive, operators must show that their assigned frequencies are being actively used through investment in network infrastructure and improved services. Unused spectrum could be reclaimed and reassigned.
