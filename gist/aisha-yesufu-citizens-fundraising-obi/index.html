Aisha Yesufu launches citizens' fundraising campaign for Peter Obi

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Activist Aisha Yesufu has launched a fundraising drive asking ordinary Nigerians to contribute to Peter Obi's 2027 presidential campaign.

What she said

In a video posted on X on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Yesufu said Obi cannot do it alone, and that people who want to take back their country must take ownership of the campaign. She argued that the arrangement in which Obi personally funds most campaign activities is no longer sustainable, and urged supporters to give whatever they can afford. She said the citizens' fund is separate from the account of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Obi's party, and promised full transparency in how the money is used.

Background

Yesufu is a co-founder of the Bring Back Our Girls movement and one of Obi's most prominent supporters. Obi, a former Anambra governor, is running with Rabiu Kwankwaso on the NDC ticket. His campaign has presented itself as funded by ordinary citizens in contrast to what it describes as money politics. His convoy was attacked by thugs in Calabar on Wednesday. Other candidates, including President Bola Tinubu of the APC and Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, are backed by large party structures and support groups.

Rules on campaign funding

Nigeria's Electoral Act sets limits on how much candidates can spend and how much individuals can donate, and requires parties to account for campaign funds.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters welcomed the appeal and shared it widely, while critics questioned how the fund would be managed and audited.

  • The citizens' fund is separate from the NDC account.

Adamu Garba denies endorsing Peter Obi for 2027, says he has quit partisan politics

19 Nov 2026, 17:04 GMT

Former APC presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has dismissed reports that he endorsed Peter Obi for the 2027 presidential election, saying he has withdrawn from partisan politics to focus on his businesses and Africa's digital economy.

Former All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has denied reports that he endorsed Peter Obi ahead of Nigeria's 2027 presidential election.

"The claim is false"

In a public statement dated 18 November 2026, Garba described the claim circulating online as false.

He said he has withdrawn from partisan politics and is now focusing on his business ventures and on Africa's digital economy.

His new focus

Garba named Crowwe, Tablars and Justitia SI among the projects currently receiving his attention.

He also urged the public to rely only on his official channels for accurate information about his activities.

Where did the story come from?

The origin of the endorsement claim remains unclear. No post, interview or public statement showing Garba endorsing Obi has been found.

What is clear is that Garba has publicly disputed the claim.

A familiar pattern

With the 2027 elections approaching, false claims about endorsements are spreading quickly on social media. It is the second time this week that a story about backing for Obi has been corrected, after Obi himself clarified that his Washington visit was not an endorsement from the US Congress.

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has told supporters in Anambra State that Nigerians will feel a difference from the very first day if he and Rabiu Kwankwaso win the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking in Anambra alongside the former Kano governor, Obi reportedly said their administration would prioritise accountability, transparency and the responsible management of public funds.

He said the two of them would fight corruption and make sure public money is used for the public good, adding that Nigerians would know from day one that a new team was in charge.

The context

Obi's comments came a day after Anambra Governor Charles Soludo hosted an APC rally in Awka for President Bola Tinubu and said the election was already decided. ADC chieftain Dele Momodu responded that Obi would win Anambra comfortably.

The race

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027, with Tinubu (APC), Atiku Abubakar (ADC), Obi (NDC) and other candidates in the race.

Fact check: Viral video claiming bandits attacked Tinubu is old and misleading

20 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A video shared on Facebook claiming bandits tried to attack President Tinubu is false. The footage reportedly dates back to a June 2025 incident in Kaduna and does not show a bandit attack.

Read more

A video circulating on Facebook since 27 October 2026, claiming that bandits attempted to attack President Bola Tinubu, has been found to be misleading.

The claim

The video shows Tinubu speaking at a podium and flinching as a man runs beside him, while two uniformed soldiers move in to shield him. A caption on the clip reads that a bandit attacked the president, and a narrator repeats the claim.

The facts

A fact-check found that the footage dates back to a June 2025 incident in Kaduna and does not show an attack by bandits. The video has been recirculated with a false caption ahead of the 2027 elections.

Why it matters

Misleading videos and AI-generated images have become a growing concern in the 2027 campaign, with the main parties accusing each other of spreading false content online.
