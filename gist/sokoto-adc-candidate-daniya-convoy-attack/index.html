Sokoto ADC governorship candidate says his convoy was attacked near airport

31 Oct 2026, 06:42 GMT

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) candidate for governor of Sokoto State, Manir Muhammad Dan'iya, has alleged that his convoy was attacked near the Sultan Abubakar III International Airport in Sokoto on Friday night, 30 October 2026.

What he says happened

According to his media aide, Aminu Abdullahi, the incident happened at about 9pm, shortly after Dan'iya had seen off former governor Aminu Waziri Tambuwal at the airport. He alleged that members of the Sokoto State Marshal and Community Guard blocked the convoy, saying they were acting on orders to keep ADC members out of the state. He said efforts to calm the situation failed before shots were fired. Dan'iya reportedly escaped unhurt, but his bulletproof vehicles were damaged, and he said two Department of State Services officers attached to his security team were taken away. He also said he had noticed unusual deployments at several entry points into the state earlier in the day.

Official response

The Sokoto State Police Command spokesperson, DSP Ahmad Rufae, said the command would take necessary action and that anyone found responsible would face the law. The Sokoto State Government and the Marshal and Community Guard had not publicly responded at the time of the report, and the allegations have not been independently verified.

What he is asking for

Dan'iya reported the matter to the state Commissioner of Police and asked the Inspector-General of Police and the head of the DSS to investigate and secure the release of the two officers. He also called on the state government to explain the alleged orders.

Background

The incident is the latest in a string of reported attacks on opposition figures as campaigning for the January 2027 elections intensifies. On the same day, AAC presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore said he was attacked in Ado-Ekiti, and an APC rally in Katsina was disrupted earlier this week.

  • Dan'iya says his convoy was blocked and shot at; he was not hurt.
  • He blames state security personnel; the state government has not responded.
  • Police say anyone found responsible will face the law.

Former Osun governor Oyinlola says his endorsement of Tinubu 'has expired'

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Former Osun governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has reportedly withdrawn his 2025 endorsement of President Tinubu for 2027, saying it was a political move to resolve Osun's local government crisis that did not work.

Read more

Former Osun State governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has said his endorsement of President Bola Tinubu for the 2027 election is no longer valid.

What he said

Asked in a television interview, reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026, whether he still stood by the endorsement, Oyinlola reportedly replied that it had expired. He explained that he and other Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) stakeholders in Osun endorsed the President on 20 July 2025 as a strategic move, because of problems in the state that they believed only the President could solve, including missing local government funds and a dispute over who should occupy the local council offices. He said the endorsement was meant to help resolve those issues, but the solution had not come.

Background

Oyinlola governed Osun State from 2003 to 2010. Osun has been at the centre of a long-running dispute over control of its local governments, involving the PDP-led state government of Governor Ademola Adeleke and APC-backed council officials, with court rulings and disagreements over the release of council funds. The PDP itself is divided, with some members backing Tinubu, others joining opposition coalitions, and a court battle over its national leadership.

Why it matters

The withdrawal is a setback for the President's campaign in Osun, a swing state in the South West. The APC says it remains confident of winning the state, while opposition parties hope to benefit from discontent there.

What happens next

Oyinlola did not say which candidate, if any, he would now support.

Obidient mobiliser Serah Ibrahim backs Makinde's presidential bid

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Serah Ibrahim, a well-known grassroots mobiliser linked to the Obidient Movement, has reportedly endorsed Oyo governor Seyi Makinde for president and says she helped write his Reset Agenda. Peter Obi responded by calling her his daughter.

Read more

Serah Ibrahim, a grassroots mobiliser widely associated with Peter Obi's Obidient Movement, has publicly backed Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde's bid for the presidency.

What happened

According to reports, Ibrahim appeared at the Allied Peoples Movement's presidential manifesto event in Abuja, where she introduced Makinde to the audience and described him as the most outstanding Nigerian on the ballot. She said she had helped develop his presidential policy document, called the Reset Agenda, which focuses on the economy, security, education and governance.

Obi's response

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, addressed speculation about a rift with Ibrahim in a video, describing her as his daughter. He did not criticise her decision.

Background

Makinde, who is completing his second term as Oyo governor, is the APM's presidential candidate for 2027. The opposition is split between several candidates, including Obi of the NDC and Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, while President Bola Tinubu seeks a second term on the APC platform. Several of Makinde's own officials have resigned from his government in recent weeks.

How people reacted

Some Obi supporters criticised Ibrahim, while others said she was free to support any candidate. Makinde's supporters welcomed her backing.

Thugs attack Peter Obi's convoy in Calabar, one arrested

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Suspected thugs attacked NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi's convoy in Calabar on Wednesday, throwing stones and bottles. Police say one suspect has been arrested. Obi blamed APC supporters, a claim the party has not answered.

Read more

The convoy of Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate Peter Obi was attacked by suspected political thugs in Calabar, Cross River State, on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

What happened

According to reports, the attack happened around the Watt Market area as Obi's convoy moved through the city towards a hotel where the party had scheduled a presidential town hall meeting. Obi was in Calabar with his wife, Margaret, and his running mate, Rabiu Kwankwaso. Attackers threw stones, bottles and other objects at vehicles and supporters who had lined the route. Several supporters were reportedly injured and vehicles were damaged. An aide to Obi, Esther Umoh, said one supporter was hit by a stray bullet.

Police response

The Cross River State Police Command confirmed the incident and said a 27-year-old suspect had been arrested. Police said investigations were continuing to identify those behind the attack.

What Obi said

Speaking after the incident, Obi alleged that APC supporters threw stones at his convoy, forcing people to run for safety. He said such attacks happen because Nigerians tolerate them, and vowed that it would not be allowed in 2027. The APC had not responded to the claim at the time of reporting.

Obi overruled his security

Obi later said he ignored advice from his security team during the tense moment and went ahead with the town hall at the hotel. Police said they stepped in to prevent a confrontation between NDC and APC supporters at the venue.

Background

Cross River is governed by the APC. Attacks on campaign events have increased as the January 2027 elections approach. Earlier this week, ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar accused the DSS of plotting to disrupt his Sokoto rally, and APC supporters were attacked ahead of a rally in Katsina. Civil society groups and religious leaders have urged all parties to restrain their supporters.

Obasanjo's son Olujuwon joins pro-Tinubu movement, backs President for 2027

4 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Olujuwon Obasanjo, a son of former President Olusegun Obasanjo, has reportedly joined the PBAT Door-to-Door Movement as Special Adviser on Political Affairs and pledged to support President Tinubu's re-election. His father has been a fierce critic of the government.

Read more

A son of former President Olusegun Obasanjo, Olujuwon Obasanjo, has thrown his support behind President Bola Tinubu's bid for a second term.

What happened

According to reports on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Olujuwon joined the PBAT Door-to-Door Movement as Special Adviser on Political Affairs during a visit to its headquarters in Abuja, where he was received by the National Coordinator, Sunday Asuku, and shown around the facility. He described the appointment as an honour, praised the movement's community outreach and said he had followed its work for some time.

What he said

Olujuwon reportedly said the President had started on a good note and urged Nigerians, especially young people, to support the continuation of the government's reforms. He said the country had made progress in several areas and pledged to contribute to the President's re-election.

Background

The move stands out because his father has been one of the government's harshest critics. Last week, the former President described the government's borrowing as 'absolute nonsense'. The PBAT movement, whose grand patron is former Niger Delta militant leader Government Ekpemupolo (Tompolo), recently launched its Ogun State chapter and donated 26 campaign vehicles. Members of political families supporting different sides is common in Nigerian politics.

How people reacted

APC supporters welcomed the news, while some critics said it would not change the former President's views or influence voters in Ogun State, where both Obasanjo and the President's allies are active.
