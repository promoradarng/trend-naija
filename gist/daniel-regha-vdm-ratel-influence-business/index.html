Social media clout without financial growth is a wasted opportunity: what Daniel Regha can learn from VDM's RATEL

1 Nov 2026, 09:07 GMT

Daniel Regha has weighed in on VeryDarkMan's growing RATEL business, writing on X that VDM had gone "from activist, to NGO business, to CEO" and "from criticizing business owners to joining the same businesses to make profit."

His post drew mixed reactions. One user replied that anyone who has not yet understood VDM is to be pitied, while another asked what VDM is supposed to do with the money he makes from social media: give it all away and own nothing because he is an activist?

Influence without a business

Sometimes one wonders whether Daniel Regha sees the business opportunities sitting right in front of him. For someone who commands so much attention on X, sparks conversations almost daily and has built a considerable following, you would expect him to have turned that influence into something financially rewarding by now.

Perhaps the fear of what people will say is holding him back.

You are only a tenant

Many influencers forget that they do not own these platforms. They are simply tenants. Facebook, X, TikTok and Instagram can suspend an account, restrict its reach or change their policies overnight, and years of gathering followers could suddenly amount to very little.

What VDM seems to understand

VeryDarkMan appears to understand something many influencers have yet to figure out: popularity has an expiry date. The same people cheering you today may move on to another personality tomorrow.

Instead of depending entirely on public attention, he is gradually converting his influence into a business. He is introducing products, building a customer base and creating something that could keep generating income long after the social media excitement fades. That is smart thinking.

People will always talk

Imagine spending years building an audience of hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of people, only to have nothing financially sustainable to show for it.

If you have the audience, build a business. Sell products, offer services, create something valuable and earn legitimate money from the influence you have worked hard to establish. If you sell something, people will criticise you. If you remain broke, those same people will still have something to say. The people whose opinions you fear will not come together to pay your rent, feed your family or settle your bills.

The real question

The advice to Daniel Regha and other social media personalities is simple: stop treating online popularity as a lifetime achievement, and find a way to turn that attention into lasting value. VDM may have his critics, but his decision to venture into business deserves recognition.

Because when the followers stop cheering and the cameras stop rolling, the real question will be: what did you build with all that influence?

What do you think? Should influencers concentrate on entertaining their followers, or should they use their popularity to build businesses?

Nkem Owoh denies saying Yoruba founded Nollywood, credits them with pioneering films

8 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Veteran actor Nkem Owoh, known as Osuofia, has said on a popular interview show that he never claimed the Yoruba founded Nollywood, pointing out that the word did not exist when he started acting, but credited Yoruba filmmakers such as Hubert Ogunde with pioneering the tradition.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actor Nkem Owoh, popularly known as Osuofia, has addressed comments attributed to him about who founded Nigeria's film industry.

What he said

Speaking on the YouTube interview show 'Curiosity Made Me Ask', hosted by content creator Isbae U, in an episode uploaded on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Owoh rejected reports that he had said the Yoruba people started Nollywood rather than the Igbo. He explained that the word 'Nollywood' did not even exist when he and his colleagues first began making films. However, he said Yoruba filmmakers had indeed pioneered the home-video tradition in Nigeria, giving particular credit to the late theatre and film pioneer Hubert Ogunde. When the host jokingly suggested Ogunde might have been Igbo, Owoh insisted that he was Yoruba.

The wider debate

The question of who built Nollywood has been debated for years. In August 2026, the late actor Francis Duru argued that Igbo businesspeople were the real architects of the home-video boom of the 1990s and early 2000s, while accepting that the Yoruba film community had become more dominant through stronger organisation. Owoh was reported at the time to have pointed out that Yoruba filmmakers were active in theatre and film long before the video boom. Duru died in October 2026.

Background

Owoh is one of Nollywood's best-loved comic actors, famous for the 'Osuofia in London' films and the song 'I Go Chop Your Dollar'. 'Curiosity Made Me Ask' is one of Nigeria's most popular celebrity interview shows.

CBN sets ₦15bn capital rule for dollar-backed stablecoins in new crypto rules

8 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has set minimum capital of ₦10 billion for naira-backed stablecoin issuers and ₦15 billion for those offering dollar or other foreign-currency tokens, under new rules for crypto operators. Other crypto service providers will need ₦1 billion.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has introduced tougher rules for cryptocurrency and stablecoin businesses, including minimum capital requirements of up to ₦15 billion.

The new requirements

Under the regulations, reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026, issuers of naira-backed stablecoins must have at least ₦10 billion in paid-up capital, while operators offering tokens backed by foreign currencies, such as dollar-pegged stablecoins, will need ₦15 billion. Other virtual asset service providers, including digital wallet, payment, transfer and custody businesses, must have at least ₦1 billion. The CBN said it may demand more capital from larger or riskier operators.

Licensing and foreign firms

The rules make it illegal for individuals or companies to issue, exchange, distribute or facilitate regulated virtual assets in Nigeria without CBN approval. Foreign operators targeting Nigerian customers must incorporate and obtain licences locally, regardless of approvals elsewhere. Companies already licensed by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) will face extra capital requirements if they offer services under the CBN's oversight, while digital assets classed as securities will remain under SEC supervision. The rules also set standards for reserve management and consumer protection.

Background

Nigeria has one of the world's largest communities of cryptocurrency users. The CBN banned banks from dealing with crypto firms in 2021 before lifting the restriction in 2023, and the SEC has since moved to register and regulate crypto exchanges. Stablecoins, which are designed to keep a steady value linked to a currency such as the dollar, are widely used by Nigerians for savings and payments.

MultiChoice shuts down GOtv Stream app months after closing Showmax

8 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

MultiChoice has discontinued its GOtv Stream mobile app, telling users to go back to their decoders, without giving a reason. It is the latest change since French group Canal+ took over the company, following the closure of Showmax in April.

Read more

MultiChoice has shut down its GOtv Stream app, which allowed subscribers to watch selected GOtv channels on phones and tablets.

What happened

A notice on the GOtv Stream website, reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026, confirmed the closure and directed customers back to their television decoders. The app has also been removed from the Google Play Store and Apple App Store; app-tracking data showed the Android version was removed on 21 October. Before the closure, it offered live channels, catch-up programmes and offline downloads, depending on the subscription package. MultiChoice has not given a reason for the shutdown or announced a replacement.

Background

GOtv Stream launched in Nigeria on 1 December 2023 as part of MultiChoice's effort to let subscribers watch TV away from their living rooms. The closure is the latest change since French media group Canal+ took control of MultiChoice in a deal valued at about $2 billion, which became unconditional in September 2025. MultiChoice announced in March 2026 that it would close its Showmax streaming service, citing heavy losses, and Showmax shut down in April, with subscribers directed to DStv Stream. The company has also recently dropped the Africa Magic Family channel after 12 years.

Ekiti politician Opeyemi Falegan has publicly apologised to Nollywood actress Nkechi Blessing Sunday, years after their much-discussed break-up.

What happened

The apology came during a TikTok Live session on Saturday, 7 November 2026, in which the former couple spoke face to face online about their 2022 separation. Nkechi made it clear that she wanted a genuine apology, reminding Falegan that remarks he made about her during a social media live session years ago were still circulating and being used against her. Falegan's response

Falegan apologised and said he had grown since their split, urging both of them to let the past go. He also spoke about what he described as emotional unavailability and temperament issues on both sides that made the relationship difficult.

Background

Nkechi Blessing is a popular actress and producer known for her outspoken personality on social media. Her relationship with Falegan was widely publicised before it ended in 2022 amid public exchanges online. The actress was also recently in the news after confronting comedian Carter Efe and his girlfriend.

How people reacted

Fans were divided, with some praising the two for making peace and others questioning why Nkechi chose to revisit the matter in public.
