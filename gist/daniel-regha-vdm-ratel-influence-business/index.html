Social media clout without financial growth is a wasted opportunity: what Daniel Regha can learn from VDM's RATEL

1 Nov 2026, 09:07 GMT

Daniel Regha has weighed in on VeryDarkMan's growing RATEL business, writing on X that VDM had gone "from activist, to NGO business, to CEO" and "from criticizing business owners to joining the same businesses to make profit."

His post drew mixed reactions. One user replied that anyone who has not yet understood VDM is to be pitied, while another asked what VDM is supposed to do with the money he makes from social media: give it all away and own nothing because he is an activist?

Influence without a business

Sometimes one wonders whether Daniel Regha sees the business opportunities sitting right in front of him. For someone who commands so much attention on X, sparks conversations almost daily and has built a considerable following, you would expect him to have turned that influence into something financially rewarding by now.

Perhaps the fear of what people will say is holding him back.

You are only a tenant

Many influencers forget that they do not own these platforms. They are simply tenants. Facebook, X, TikTok and Instagram can suspend an account, restrict its reach or change their policies overnight, and years of gathering followers could suddenly amount to very little.

What VDM seems to understand

VeryDarkMan appears to understand something many influencers have yet to figure out: popularity has an expiry date. The same people cheering you today may move on to another personality tomorrow.

Instead of depending entirely on public attention, he is gradually converting his influence into a business. He is introducing products, building a customer base and creating something that could keep generating income long after the social media excitement fades. That is smart thinking.

People will always talk

Imagine spending years building an audience of hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of people, only to have nothing financially sustainable to show for it.

If you have the audience, build a business. Sell products, offer services, create something valuable and earn legitimate money from the influence you have worked hard to establish. If you sell something, people will criticise you. If you remain broke, those same people will still have something to say. The people whose opinions you fear will not come together to pay your rent, feed your family or settle your bills.

The real question

The advice to Daniel Regha and other social media personalities is simple: stop treating online popularity as a lifetime achievement, and find a way to turn that attention into lasting value. VDM may have his critics, but his decision to venture into business deserves recognition.

Because when the followers stop cheering and the cameras stop rolling, the real question will be: what did you build with all that influence?

What do you think? Should influencers concentrate on entertaining their followers, or should they use their popularity to build businesses?

VeryDarkMan questions INEC chairman's photo with APC UK chapter chairman

14 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Activist VeryDarkMan has questioned why INEC chairman Joash Amupitan was photographed with the APC's UK chapter chairman during his London trip, asking whether other parties' UK leaders were given similar access.

Read more

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has raised questions about a photograph showing the chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) with a leader of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) in the UK.

What he said

In a post reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, VDM clarified that the man wearing an APC-branded cap in the photo was not INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan, but Tunde Doherty, chairman of the APC's UK chapter. He asked what the APC official was doing with the INEC chairman and whether the UK leaders of other parties, including the NDC, Labour Party, PDP and ADC, had been given the same opportunity to meet him. He went on to claim that INEC was compromised.

Background

Amupitan was in London this week to speak at Chatham House about preparations for the 2027 elections. Protesters confronted him outside the venue, accusing the commission of bias, and he pledged to reject any political appointment after leaving office to show his independence. INEC has also denied reports that he plans to resign. INEC had not responded to VDM's post at the time of writing, and the circumstances of the photo are not clear.

APC UK chairman responds

Tunde Doherty, chairman of the APC in the UK, later rejected claims that he was part of the INEC chairman's entourage at Chatham House. He said opposition figures had deliberately misrepresented a photo of him taken outside the venue, that he had never held any position or role in INEC and had no affiliation with it, and that as a party leader he had a right to attend public policy discussions there.

Tinubu campaign unveils five-year defence plan: 28,000 soldiers, 50,000 police

16 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The APC presidential campaign says President Tinubu has launched a five-year defence plan, with approval to recruit 28,000 more soldiers and 50,000 police officers, new army divisions and training depots.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu's campaign has outlined a five-year strategic defence plan it says will guide efforts to tackle insecurity.

What was announced

In a statement on Sunday, 15 November 2026, APC presidential campaign spokesperson Dele Alake said the plan brings together defence priorities, operational needs, capability development and spending to respond to threats including terrorism, banditry and kidnapping. The campaign said the government had approved the recruitment of 28,000 additional soldiers and 50,000 new police officers, created four new army divisions and approved two new army depots in Osogbo, Osun State, and Amasiri-Edda, Ebonyi State, each able to train up to 6,000 recruits at a time. It also pointed to a 700-unit barracks project in Ikere-Ekiti.

Claims of progress

The campaign claimed that military operations over the past three years had killed more than 15,000 insurgents and bandits, and that over 130,000 Boko Haram fighters and their dependants had surrendered under the Operation Safe Corridor rehabilitation programme. These figures could not be independently verified.

Background

Insecurity is one of the main issues in the 2027 campaign. ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar this week cited tracker data showing 638 people were killed in October, and recent attacks in Zamfara and Kano have heightened concern. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
