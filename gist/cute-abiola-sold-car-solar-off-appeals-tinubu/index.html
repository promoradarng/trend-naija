Cute Abiola says he sold one of his cars as he appeals to Tinubu over power and hardship

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Comedian and skit maker The Cute Abiola has joined Nigerians complaining about poor electricity supply and economic hardship, in videos addressed directly to President Bola Tinubu.

What he said

In one video shared online on Friday, 6 November 2026, the content creator said his solar power system had gone off because of heavy use during the long power outages in his area. He appealed to the President to help Nigerians, saying everywhere was hot, there was no light and the suffering was too much. In another video, he said he had just sold one of his cars to ease his financial situation, explaining that he owed people money and still needed funds to shoot new content. He added that he hoped things would not get to the point where he had to sell his house.

Background

Abiola, whose real name is Abdulgafar Ahmed, became popular for his comedy skits and is a former member of the Nigerian Navy. His complaints come as consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states report renewed power outages, which the federal government has blamed on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced hydropower generation. The government has also started paying a ₦50,000 monthly relief grant to selected households.

How people reacted

Many fans sympathised and said his videos reflected what millions of Nigerians are going through. Some critics said he had not praised the government when things were going well for him and was now blaming the President, while others said public figures should be free to speak about their struggles.

VDM praised as Oyo corps members finally receive delayed allowance

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Oyo State Batch 25C corps members have reportedly received their delayed final ₦77,000 allowance, two days after VeryDarkMan raised the alarm. He shared a video of a corps member celebrating the payment.

Read more

Corps members of the Oyo State Batch 25C have reportedly received their long-delayed final allowance, after social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM), whose real name is Martins Vincent Otse, publicly called out the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC).

What happened

On Wednesday, 18 November 2026, VDM shared a video on Instagram showing a corps member celebrating after receiving a credit alert and thanking him for taking up the matter.

How it started

VDM first raised the issue on 16 November, after receiving messages from more than 100 affected corps members. He said they had completed their service and attended their passing-out parade at the end of October, but had not been paid their final ₦77,000 allowance. He called on the NYSC Director-General to act.

How people reacted

Many social media users praised the activist for taking up the corps members' complaint.

Influencer Enioluwa Adeoluwa has issued a public apology after remarks he made about young Nigerians at a youth conference sparked a backlash online.

What happened

A clip from the Emergence of Stars Port Harcourt Youth Conference showed Enioluwa making remarks that many viewers felt painted young Nigerians as unwilling to work. The video spread quickly on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, drawing criticism from people who said he ignored unemployment, systemic barriers and the economic struggles young Nigerians face.

The apology

Later the same day, Enioluwa posted a statement on X addressing the reaction, apologising for the remarks and acknowledging his privilege.

Thousands march in Haiti to praise El Salvador's President Bukele as his troops take on gangs

18 Nov 2026, 14:49 GMT

Thousands of Haitians took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other cities on Tuesday to praise El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his soldiers, who are leading a tough new offensive against the gangs that have terrorised Haiti for years.

Read more

Thousands of people took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other parts of Haiti on Tuesday, praising El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his troops as they take on the country's powerful gangs.

"Viv Bukele"

Photos from the marches show huge crowds filling the streets, waving Haitian flags and holding up posters of the Salvadoran president. Many carried banners reading "Viv Bukele", Haitian Creole for "Long live Bukele".

Why Haitians are cheering

For years, armed gangs have controlled large parts of Port-au-Prince and beyond, killing, kidnapping and forcing hundreds of thousands of people from their homes.

Salvadoran troops have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an international mission, and recently launched their first major offensive against the gangs, with reports of heavy fighting and deaths.

Many marchers said Bukele is doing what the United Nations and the United States failed to do for years, while ordinary Haitians continued to suffer.

The Bukele model

Bukele became popular at home by cracking down hard on El Salvador's gangs, sharply reducing violence in a country once known as one of the most dangerous in the world. Many Haitians hope his forces can bring the same results.

His methods have also faced criticism from human rights groups, who say the crackdown in El Salvador involved mass arrests and abuses. But for Haitians who have lived in fear for years, many say any hope of relief is welcome.

Over to you

Peter Obi says his US trip was not to seek American endorsement

18 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has dismissed reports that he went to Washington to seek the backing of US lawmakers for 2027. He said his meetings focused on Nigeria's democracy and development.

Read more

Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate Peter Obi has dismissed reports that his recent trip to Washington, DC, was aimed at winning the backing of United States lawmakers for his 2027 bid.

What Obi said

In a statement on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, the former Anambra governor said media interpretations of his meetings with US lawmakers and policy figures had been inaccurate. He said he did not ask for an endorsement and did not present the meetings as formal support from the US Congress or any individual legislator.

He explained that the purpose of the visit was to exchange views with congressional leaders and other American stakeholders on Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and, above all, the strengthening of its democracy. He said the talks focused on democratic institutions, accountability and making sure elections are free, fair and credible.

His advice to others

Obi encouraged other Nigerian politicians to hold similar international discussions on governance, and urged that public debate on Nigeria's democratic future should not be reduced to claims of foreign endorsement.

Background

Claims that the US Congress had endorsed Obi circulated online after the trip, and a US lobbyist also commented on the matter.
