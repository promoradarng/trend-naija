CAN disowns cleric claiming to be its general secretary, says it backs no candidate

4 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Christian Association of Nigeria (CAN) has distanced itself from a cleric who presented himself as its general secretary and tried to link the body to support for a candidate in the 2027 elections.

What CAN said

In a statement signed by its president, Archbishop Daniel Okoh, and reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, CAN said Archbishop Chivir Chianson does not hold any position in the association and has no authority to speak or act on its behalf. It said he had tried to identify himself as CAN's general secretary and to associate the body with what he described as a church mobilisation in support of a political candidate. Okoh warned the public and the media to disregard any claim that Chianson or his group represents CAN.

Neutrality

The statement was widely read as reaffirming CAN's position that it does not endorse any candidate in the race between President Bola Tinubu of the APC, Atiku Abubakar of the ADC and Peter Obi of the NDC.

Background

Religious leaders and groups have become increasingly involved in the 2027 campaign. A bishops' conference has reportedly endorsed Tinubu for a second term, while a video of a Catholic priest leading his congregation in a song for Obi and Kwankwaso went viral this week. Religion has long been a sensitive issue in Nigerian elections, especially after the APC's Muslim-Muslim ticket in 2023.

Why it matters

CAN is the umbrella body for most Christian denominations in Nigeria, and its position can influence many voters.

  • CAN says Chianson is not its general secretary.
  • It warned the public to disregard his claims.
  • CAN is seen as neutral in the 2027 race.

Tinubu takes swipe at Peter Obi in Akwa Ibom, says he confronted the Atlantic Ocean while Obi ran away from erosion in Anambra

6 Nov 2026, 16:47 GMT

Speaking in Akwa Ibom, President Bola Tinubu accused NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi of failing to tackle erosion in Anambra State, while claiming he himself "tamed" the Atlantic Ocean, an apparent reference to the Eko Atlantic project in Lagos.

Read more

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has taken a swipe at former Anambra State Governor Peter Obi, comparing their records on tackling environmental challenges.

What Tinubu said

Speaking in Akwa Ibom State, Tinubu accused Obi of failing to confront the serious erosion problems affecting Anambra State during his time as governor, and pointed out that Obi is now running for president under the NDC.

"Peter Obi is in one party they call NDC where he's running for president. He ran away from erosion in Anambra. He couldn't confront erosion. I, Bola Ahmed Tinubu, confronted the Atlantic Ocean. I tamed it for you," Tinubu said.

The President appeared to be referring to efforts to reclaim and protect land along the Lagos coastline, particularly the Eko Atlantic project, which began during his time as Lagos State governor.

Can the two really be compared?

Anambra has struggled with severe gully erosion for decades, with communities losing farmland, roads and homes. Tackling it requires substantial funding, engineering work and cooperation between different levels of government.

The Eko Atlantic project, on the other hand, involved major private investment and extensive coastal engineering to reclaim land from the Atlantic Ocean.

Both involve serious environmental challenges, but their circumstances, funding arrangements and engineering requirements are quite different.

The road to 2027

As the 2027 presidential election draws closer, exchanges like this are likely to become more common, with leaders publicly questioning one another's records in office.

Nigerians may also want to look closely at the actual results of these projects and the roles played by the leaders involved.

Court voids ADC governorship nomination in Nasarawa, orders fresh primary in Karu

6 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has nullified the nomination of retired Major General Nuhu Angbazo as the ADC governorship candidate in Nasarawa State, ruling that the primary was incomplete because Karu LGA was left out.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has set aside the nomination of retired Major General Nuhu Angbazo as the African Democratic Congress (ADC) governorship candidate in Nasarawa State.

The judgment

In a ruling reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Justice Mohammed Umar held that the party's governorship primary was inconclusive because results from Karu Local Government Area were not included. The judge said shutting out party members in Karu meant the exercise could not validly produce a candidate. The case was brought by a former Minister of Environment, Mohammed Abdullahi, who took part in the primary alongside Angbazo and Senator Mohammed Onawo, who represents Nasarawa South.

Orders

The court ordered the ADC to hold a fresh primary in Karu, restrained Angbazo from presenting himself as the party's candidate, and barred the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) from recognising him as such. The plaintiff's lawyer welcomed the ruling and said the party must now complete the primary properly before any candidate can be recognised.

Background

The ruling adds to the ADC's legal troubles. This week, another Federal High Court judgment removed the party's David Mark-led national leadership and voided Atiku Abubakar's presidential nomination, a ruling the party has said it will challenge. Nasarawa is governed by the APC's Abdullahi Sule, who is completing his second term. Angbazo has not publicly said whether he will appeal.

What happens next

Unless the ruling is overturned on appeal, the ADC will have to return to Karu to finish the primary before it can field a governorship candidate in the state.

Nigeria's opposition politics is getting more interesting as the battle for relevance ahead of the 2027 general elections continues.

What Okonkwo said

Veteran Nollywood actor and politician Kenneth Okonkwo has stirred another round of debate after claiming that the NDC is facing more serious problems than the African Democratic Congress (ADC).

According to Okonkwo, former Vice President Atiku Abubakar will still be on the ballot in the 2027 presidential election.

"NDC is facing more problems than ADC. Atiku Abubakar will be on the ballot," he said.

An interesting twist

Okonkwo is making this prediction at a time when the leadership of his own party, the ADC, is facing a major legal setback after a Federal High Court in Abuja nullified the emergence of its national chairman and the actions taken under his leadership.

That raises an obvious question. If his own party is battling a leadership crisis in court, how certain can anyone be about what will happen to other opposition parties before 2027?

Politics full of surprises

Nigerian politics has a way of surprising even those who believe they understand the game. Alliances can change overnight, party leaders can fall out, and court judgments can completely change the direction of an election.

For Atiku, who has contested the presidency several times, whether he will appear on the ballot again remains a major subject of discussion. Okonkwo's prediction should be understood as his political opinion rather than confirmation of Atiku's candidacy.

As 2027 approaches, Nigerians are watching closely to see whether opposition politicians will eventually unite behind a common candidate or continue to pursue separate presidential ambitions.
