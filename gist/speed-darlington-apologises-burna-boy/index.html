Speed Darlington apologises to Burna Boy, begs him to drop court charges

12 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Singer Speed Darlington has made a public apology to Burna Boy, appealing to the Grammy-winning star to withdraw the court charges filed against him.

What he said

In a video posted on Instagram on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Speed Darlington, whose real name is Darlington Okoye, admitted that his words may have caused Burna Boy pain and embarrassment. He said he had wrongly assumed that other people shared his own temperament and tolerance, and pledged to stop all comments about the singer, direct or indirect, if the charges are withdrawn. He also promised to stop mentioning Burna Boy's name altogether once the matter is settled.

How the feud began

The dispute escalated in October 2024, when Speed Darlington posted videos questioning Burna Boy's Grammy win for his album 'Twice as Tall' and making disparaging remarks about him. The matter later became a legal case, with Speed Darlington facing charges filed after a complaint from Burna Boy's camp.

How people reacted

The apology drew a wave of reactions online, with many users surprised by Speed Darlington's change of tone. Burna Boy had not publicly responded at the time of writing.

  • Speed Darlington asked Burna Boy to drop the charges.
  • He promised never to mention the singer again.
  • Burna Boy has not responded.

2Baba says election riggers deserve severe punishment amid 'rig and die' debate

27 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Singer 2Baba has said he supports tough punishment for anyone who manipulates election results, as debate continues over the 'rig and die' slogan. He said the slogan reflects Nigerians' frustration.

Read more

Music star Innocent Idibia, popularly known as 2Baba, has weighed in on the controversial 'rig and die' slogan ahead of the 2027 elections.

What he said

Speaking in a radio interview on Thursday, 26 November 2026, the African Queen singer said he supported severe punishment for anyone who tries to manipulate election results, arguing that serious consequences would discourage others from electoral malpractice.

He described the national mood as tense, and said the slogan reflected the desperation and frustration many Nigerians feel. He also said he did not see anything wrong with the slogan itself.

Background

The phrase 'rig and die' has spread widely online and at rallies. Earlier this week, INEC warned that it was dangerous, saying a slogan linking an electoral offence to death could incite mob attacks at polling units and put voters and election staff at risk. A civic group has also recorded 124 cases of electoral violence since July.

INEC names nine states as hotspots for campaign violence

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

INEC Chairman Joash Amupitan has listed Lagos, Edo, Ekiti, Enugu, Ebonyi, Delta, Cross River, Oyo and Benue as states hit by campaign violence and political intolerance. He warned that the unrest could threaten preparations for the 2027 elections.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence and political intolerance ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The states

INEC Chairman Professor Joash Amupitan listed Lagos, Edo, Ekiti, Enugu, Ebonyi, Delta, Cross River, Oyo and Benue. He spoke on Wednesday in Abuja at a strategic election security conference organised with the Office of the National Security Adviser and attended by the heads of security agencies.

What he said

Amupitan said campaign convoys, town hall meetings, party offices and supporters had all come under attack in the listed states. He warned that the growing unrest could jeopardise preparations for the elections, and also raised concern about harassment, intimidation and assault of INEC officials and staff.

Background

The warning follows a series of violent incidents this month, including deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states. A civic group this week recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths since July, and INEC has also cautioned against the 'rig and die' slogan.
