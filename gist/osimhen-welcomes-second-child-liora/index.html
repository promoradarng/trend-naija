Harry Kane becomes first Ballon d'Or winner left out of FIFPRO World XI

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Bayern Munich striker Harry Kane, who won the 2026 Ballon d'Or after scoring 73 goals in 65 games, has been left out of the FIFPRO Men's World XI, the first time a reigning winner has missed out since 2005.

Read more

Harry Kane has become the first Ballon d'Or winner to be left out of the FIFPRO Men's World XI in the same year.

What happened

The team, voted for by professional players around the world and announced on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, did not include the Bayern Munich and England striker, who won the 2026 Ballon d'Or after scoring 73 goals in 65 appearances for club and country. Erling Haaland and Kylian Mbappé were among the forwards chosen ahead of him.

A first

It is the first time since the FIFPRO World XI was introduced in 2005 that the reigning Ballon d'Or winner has failed to make the team.

Background

The Ballon d'Or is voted for by journalists, while the FIFPRO World XI is chosen by fellow players, and the two awards do not always agree. Kane joined Bayern Munich from Tottenham Hotspur in 2023 and has been one of the most prolific goalscorers in Europe since.

Senate sets aside ₦100 million reward for World Cup-winning Flamingos

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

Senate President Godswill Akpabio has announced a ₦100 million reward for the Flamingos after their FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup win, following a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi, who said the team had received too little recognition.

Read more

The Senate has set aside ₦100 million for the Flamingos, Nigeria's under-17 women's football team, after their victory at the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup.

What happened

Senate President Godswill Akpabio announced the reward during plenary on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, as the chamber considered a congratulatory motion moved by Senator Abdul Ningi of the ADC, who represents Bauchi Central. Ningi described the team's win as historic, saying the young players had achieved what many senior national teams had not, despite going into the tournament with little public attention. He said the players deserved far more publicity and a proper reception on their return.

Background

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final in Rabat, Morocco, on 7 November to become the first African team to win the tournament, and Nigeria's first FIFA world champions in women's football at any level. FIFA does not pay prize money for the competition, but a sponsor of the National Sports Commission gave the team $50,000 in Rabat. NDC candidate Peter Obi said NFF officials stopped him from greeting the team at Abuja airport, a claim the Presidency said politicised the national teams.

Nigeria wins both 100m golds at Youth Olympics as Rosemary Nwankwo and Gift Bright triumph

10 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Seventeen-year-old Rosemary Nwankwo won the girls' 100m at the Youth Olympic Games in Dakar in a wind-assisted 11.16 seconds, and Gift Bright won the boys' race, making Nigeria the first country to win both 100m titles at the same Games.

Read more

Nigeria has made history at the 2026 Youth Olympic Games in Dakar, Senegal, winning gold in both the girls' and boys' 100 metres.

The races

Rosemary Chigozie Nwankwo, a 17-year-old secondary school student, won the girls' 100m final in a wind-assisted 11.16 seconds, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. Arwa Ali of the United Arab Emirates took silver in 11.41 seconds and Dana Salem of Qatar won bronze in 11.48. Nwankwo had reached the final by winning her semi-final in 11.53 seconds. Her compatriot Gift Bright then won the boys' 100m in a personal best of 10.28 seconds.

A first

The double makes Nigeria the first country to win both 100m titles at the same edition of the Youth Olympic Games. Nwankwo's win was also Nigeria's first gold medal of the Dakar Games, and she becomes the third Nigerian woman to win the event, after Josephine Omaka in Singapore in 2010 and Rosemary Chukwuma in Buenos Aires in 2018.

Background

The Dakar Games are the first Olympic event ever held in Africa. The sprint golds come days after Nigeria's Flamingos won the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup in Morocco, adding to a remarkable run for Nigerian youth sport.

Ebola: 28 contacts still under watch in Nigeria, 34 cleared, says NCDC

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The NCDC says 28 people linked to Nigeria's single imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a type of Ebola, remain under surveillance, while 34 others have been cleared after 21 days of monitoring. No further cases have been confirmed.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says 28 people linked to the country's imported Ebola case are still being monitored.

The update

In its national health security update released on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said 34 other contacts had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. As of 9 November, Nigeria had confirmed only one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, with no further confirmed cases.

The response

Idris said the response involves the Federal Ministry of Health, the FCT Administration, state authorities and development partners, with contact tracing, investigation of reported cases, laboratory testing, infection prevention and control, safe patient care and stronger screening at entry points.

Background

Ebola is a severe and often deadly viral disease spread through contact with the body fluids of infected people. The 21-day monitoring period reflects the maximum incubation time of the virus. The case in Nigeria is linked to an outbreak in Central Africa; the United States has just barred travellers who have been in the Democratic Republic of Congo in the past 21 days. Nigeria contained an Ebola outbreak in 2014.

Advice

Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness, vomiting or unexplained bleeding, especially after travel or contact with a suspected case, to report to a health facility immediately.
