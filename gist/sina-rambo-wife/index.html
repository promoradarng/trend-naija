Okowa says he left Atiku for Tinubu because he has 'seen the light'

8 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former Delta governor Ifeanyi Okowa, Atiku Abubakar's running mate in 2023, told an APC rally in Port Harcourt that he switched to backing President Tinubu because he has seen the light, crediting the government's fiscal reforms.

Read more

Former Delta State governor Ifeanyi Okowa has explained why he abandoned former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar to support President Bola Tinubu ahead of the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at an All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally in Port Harcourt, Rivers State, on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Okowa reportedly said he had 'seen the light'. He argued that Tinubu inherited a country with foundations weakened by decades of mismanagement, and that the current administration was making tough but necessary decisions to fix them. He credited the government's fiscal reforms with increasing the revenue shared with states and local governments, saying the results could be seen in better project delivery and salary payments. He also compared Tinubu's record as Lagos governor with his performance as President, saying the same transformation was now happening across the country.

Background

Okowa was Atiku's vice-presidential running mate on the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) ticket in the 2023 election, which Tinubu won. He later left the PDP for the APC, following his successor in Delta, Governor Sheriff Oborevwori. Atiku is now the candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), although a Federal High Court ruling last week voided his nomination, a decision the party has appealed. At the same rally, Tinubu questioned how Atiku built a university while he was vice-president, prompting a sharp reply from Atiku's campaign.

What happens next

Atiku's campaign had not publicly responded to Okowa's remarks at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Six Nigerians deported from the US arrive in Lagos

8 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The National Emergency Management Agency says it received six Nigerians deported from the United States at Lagos airport on 4 November. The five men and one woman were profiled by immigration officers and given resettlement support.

Read more

Six Nigerians deported from the United States have arrived in Lagos, the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) says.

What happened

In a statement on its X account on Sunday, 8 November 2026, NEMA said the five men and one woman, all adults, landed at the cargo terminal of Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Ikeja at about 10.13pm on Wednesday, 4 November, aboard a small chartered aircraft. NEMA's Lagos office received them in partnership with the US Consulate, the Federal Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Nigeria Immigration Service and an aviation services company. Immigration officers documented and profiled each returnee before clearing them, and NEMA said it coordinated support to help them resettle.

Background

The deportations are part of the United States' wider crackdown on undocumented migrants under President Donald Trump, whose administration has carried out mass removals, often on chartered flights. Groups of Nigerians have been returned on several occasions over the past year. The agency did not give the reasons for the individual deportations. Trump this week named Stephen Miller, the architect of his deportation programme, as White House chief of staff.

What happens next

NEMA said the federal government would continue to work with other agencies to manage the return of Nigerians from abroad in an orderly and dignified way.

All 10 passengers abducted from commercial bus in Ogun regain freedom, one suspected kidnapper killed

8 Nov 2026, 18:52 GMT

All 10 passengers kidnapped from a commercial bus at Orita Fufu, Awa-Ijebu, Ogun State, on 3 November have been released unharmed, police say. One suspected kidnapper was killed in a follow-up operation, and an AK-47, ammunition and ₦1.37 million were recovered.

Read more

There is good news from Ogun State: all 10 passengers abducted from a commercial bus at Orita Fufu, Awa-Ijebu, have regained their freedom.

Released unharmed

The passengers were taken on Tuesday, 3 November 2026. Police say they were released unharmed on the night of Saturday, 7 November, following sustained security pressure and intelligence-led efforts.

Suspect killed, weapons and cash recovered

During a follow-up operation, police say one suspected kidnapper was killed. An AK-47 rifle, ammunition, ₦1.37 million in cash and a mobile phone were reportedly recovered.

Photos of the recovered items show bundles of naira notes, an AK-47 magazine and a mobile phone laid out on the ground in the bush.

Questions still unanswered

Police have not said whether any ransom was paid for the passengers' release.

Reports also differ on whether any of the passengers was a serving or recently discharged member of the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC).

Safe at last

The most important update is that all 10 passengers are alive and safely out of captivity, after five days that must have been terrifying for them and their families.

The incident is another reminder of the danger travellers face on Nigeria's highways, and of the need for security agencies to keep up pressure on kidnapping gangs.

Flamingos get $50,000 reward, but no FIFA prize money for World Cup win

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nigeria's U-17 world champions, the Flamingos, have received a $50,000 cash gift from a sponsor of the National Sports Commission at a reception in Rabat. FIFA does not pay prize money for the tournament because it is classed as a developmental competition.

Read more

Nigeria's Flamingos have received a $50,000 cash reward after winning the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup, but the team will not receive any official prize money from FIFA.

The reward

The gift was presented by Alhaji Saleh Ahmadu, a sponsor of the National Sports Commission (NSC), at a reception held in the team's honour in Rabat, Morocco, on Saturday night, 7 November 2026, according to reports. The event was hosted by NSC chairman Shehu Dikko and director-general Bukola Olopade, and was also attended by the Comptroller-General of the Nigeria Customs Service, Bashir Adewale Adeniyi. The sponsor said the gift recognised the players' outstanding performance, commitment and determination.

No FIFA prize money

FIFA classifies the U-17 Women's World Cup as a developmental tournament and does not attach official prize money to it, unlike the senior World Cups. That means the champions receive the trophy and medals but no payment from world football's governing body.

How the final was won

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final on Saturday. Awawu Bashiru opened the scoring when her looping effort slipped through the hands of Japan's goalkeeper, and Oluebube Umejiaku doubled the lead with a lob after a through ball from Esther Stephen. Japan's Fuki Ito pulled one back with ten minutes left, but Nigeria held on to become the first African winners of the tournament.
