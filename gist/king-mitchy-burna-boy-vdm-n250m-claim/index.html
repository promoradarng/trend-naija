Senator Seriake Dickson dives into River Forcados, urges youths to learn to swim

Former Bayelsa governor Seriake Dickson has shared a video of himself diving into the River Forcados near the community where he grew up, encouraging young people in riverine areas to learn to swim as a life skill.

Read more

Senator Henry Seriake Dickson has drawn reactions online after sharing a video of himself swimming in the River Forcados in Bayelsa State.

The video

In the post, shared on Thursday, 12 November 2026, the former Bayelsa governor said he had returned to the river near the community where he grew up until the age of 20, and that swimming there is something he does from time to time. He said his children also know how to swim.

His message

Dickson urged young people, especially young men in riverine communities, to learn to swim, describing it as an essential life skill rather than just a pastime.

How people reacted

Many social media users praised the senator, with some calling him a lifesaver for promoting the skill.

Background

Dickson represents Bayelsa West in the Senate and is the national leader of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), the party of presidential candidate Peter Obi.

Images of recharge cards bearing the logo of the All Progressives Congress (APC), reportedly handed out at President Bola Tinubu's campaign rally in Rivers State, have sparked debate on social media.

What happened

A man who came across pictures of the ₦200 airtime cards questioned online what anyone could achieve with such a small amount, and his comment, shared on Thursday, 12 November 2026, quickly drew reactions. The cards were said to have circulated among people who attended the rally.

How people reacted

The APC had not publicly responded to the recharge card images at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Reps order probe into attacks on political rallies ahead of 2027 polls

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The House of Representatives has ordered an investigation into violence at political rallies and meetings across the country, citing deaths and injuries in Delta, Nasarawa, Katsina and Enugu. Committees are to report back within three weeks.

Read more

The House of Representatives has resolved to investigate violence at political rallies, meetings and processions ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The motion

On Thursday, 12 November 2026, the House adopted a motion of urgent public importance sponsored by Minority Leader Frederick Agbedi and 16 other lawmakers. It directed its committees on Police Affairs and on National Security and Intelligence to examine the causes of the violence and recommend ways to prevent further attacks. A principal officer of the House will lead the inquiry, and the committees are to report within three weeks.

Incidents cited

Presenting the motion, Agbedi pointed to an attack during a political procession in Asaba, Delta State, on 7 November, which police said left one person dead and five injured; violence at an APC rally in Lafia, Nasarawa State, on 4 November; an attack on APC supporters travelling to a rally in Katsina State on 28 October, after which police reportedly arrested 14 suspects; and the disruption of political meetings in Enugu State in September.

Background

The motion came on the same day suspected thugs stormed a Nigeria Democratic Congress town hall in Lagos. The APC presidential campaign council condemned that attack and other recent attacks on supporters across party lines, and urged security agencies to arrest those responsible. The Inspector-General of Police and the European Union have also raised concerns about rising political violence. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Protesters heckle INEC chairman Amupitan in London; he denies running pro-APC X account

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Diaspora protesters surrounded INEC chairman Joash Amupitan's car outside Chatham House in London, accusing the commission of bias. Amupitan, who pledged to refuse any political appointment after his tenure, has again denied operating an X account that once backed the APC.

Read more

Protesters confronted the chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Amupitan, in London on Thursday, 12 November 2026.

What happened

Amupitan was in London to speak at a panel on Nigeria's preparations for the 2027 elections, hosted by Chatham House and the Kimpact Development Initiative. Nigerian diaspora activists who had mobilised on social media gathered outside the venue and reportedly surrounded his vehicle, accusing the commission of favouring President Bola Tinubu and the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) and demanding his resignation. They cited duplicate entries and AI-generated images in the preliminary voters' register and alleged fake polling units.

Amupitan's response

At the panel, Amupitan pledged to turn down any political or diplomatic appointment after leaving INEC, saying he wanted to demonstrate his impartiality, and said the commission was introducing technical safeguards. INEC has said it removed 1.6 million invalid records from the register, including entries with manipulated images, and flagged officials in 10 states. Amupitan also again denied operating an X account that had allegedly posted support for the APC during the 2023 elections, saying he had never had an X account and that it was never linked to his email. INEC said in April that the claims were fabricated and that fake accounts had been created in his name.

Background

At the same event, Amupitan said INEC would obey the final court ruling on Atiku Abubakar's candidacy. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
