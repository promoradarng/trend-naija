Sowore calls for legal cannabis, says Nigeria could earn up to ₦2.86 trillion a year

17 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

African Action Congress (AAC) presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore has called on the federal government to legalise cannabis, arguing that Nigeria is missing out on billions of naira in revenue by keeping it illegal.

What he said

In a post on X on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, Sowore said the country should build a regulated cannabis industry instead of spending resources arresting people and destroying plantations. He said he has backed legalisation since 2018.

The figures

He pointed to regulated markets abroad, citing about CA$2.2 billion in government revenue from recreational cannabis in Canada in 2023-2024, around $1.067 billion in cannabis tax in California in 2024, and roughly $255.4 million in Colorado.

Using those examples, he projected that Nigeria could earn between $550 million and $2.2 billion a year, or about ₦715 billion to ₦2.86 trillion at an exchange rate of ₦1,300 to the dollar. He acknowledged that actual earnings would depend on how any such market is designed and run.

He also argued that legalisation could create jobs in farming, pharmaceutical production and industrial hemp, and support research and exports.

Background

Cannabis remains illegal in Nigeria, and the NDLEA regularly carries out raids on farms and arrests dealers. Sowore, a long-time activist, is running for president in 2027 on the AAC platform.

Peter Obi says his US trip was not to seek American endorsement

18 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has dismissed reports that he went to Washington to seek the backing of US lawmakers for 2027. He said his meetings focused on Nigeria's democracy and development.

Read more

Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate Peter Obi has dismissed reports that his recent trip to Washington, DC, was aimed at winning the backing of United States lawmakers for his 2027 bid.

What Obi said

In a statement on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, the former Anambra governor said media interpretations of his meetings with US lawmakers and policy figures had been inaccurate. He said he did not ask for an endorsement and did not present the meetings as formal support from the US Congress or any individual legislator.

He explained that the purpose of the visit was to exchange views with congressional leaders and other American stakeholders on Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and, above all, the strengthening of its democracy. He said the talks focused on democratic institutions, accountability and making sure elections are free, fair and credible.

His advice to others

Obi encouraged other Nigerian politicians to hold similar international discussions on governance, and urged that public debate on Nigeria's democratic future should not be reduced to claims of foreign endorsement.

Background

Claims that the US Congress had endorsed Obi circulated online after the trip, and a US lobbyist also commented on the matter.

Political violence: Jonathan sounds alarm, Senate asks INEC to disqualify violent candidates as APC and ADC trade blame

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that some campaigns are being run like cult groups, while the Senate urged police to probe recent attacks and INEC to disqualify candidates linked to violence. The APC accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, a claim the ADC rejected.

Read more

Concern over violence ahead of the 2027 elections dominated political discussion on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, with a former president, the Senate and the main parties all weighing in.

Jonathan's warning

Speaking at a peace and security conference organised by his foundation in Abuja, former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that rising political intolerance and violence could threaten the elections. He said some political groups were running their campaign teams like cult groups, and that Nigerians at home and abroad had expressed fears about possible crises after the polls. He also condemned thuggery and the use of young people as tools of violence.

The Senate's resolution

The Senate, acting on a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi (ADC, Bauchi Central), condemned recent attacks on political figures. These included the stoning of President Bola Tinubu's motorcade after Friday prayers in Abuja, reported attacks on NDC candidate Peter Obi in Edo State, a disrupted NDC procession in Asaba on 7 November that left one person dead, and an attack on ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar's convoy in Sokoto.

Lawmakers asked the Inspector-General of Police to carry out impartial investigations and prosecute those responsible, and urged INEC to apply the law disqualifying candidates linked to electoral violence. The motion was seconded by Senator Ali Ndume (APC, Borno South).

APC and ADC trade accusations

The APC presidential campaign council, through its spokesperson Dele Alake, accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, deepfakes and hired protests to damage President Tinubu's campaign, pointing to the convoy incident in Abuja.

The ADC campaign council, through Phrank Shaibu, dismissed the claim as a distraction from the government's economic record. It challenged the APC to provide evidence or withdraw the allegation, and asked why no one had been prosecuted if the ruling party had proof. The NDC's response was not included in the reports.

The chants

The APC statement also blamed the opposition for youths chanting "Tinubu Must Go" and the Hausa phrase "Ba mayi", meaning roughly "we are not supporting you", at some of the ruling party's rallies.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
