US court rejects bid to seek evidence from Tinubu in FOIA case, Presidency says

31 Oct 2026, 17:44 GMT

A federal court in Washington has reportedly refused a request by US researcher Aaron Greenspan to seek evidence from President Bola Tinubu and a lobbying firm in his records lawsuit against the FBI and DEA. The Presidency welcomed the ruling, while the underlying case continues.

Read more

A United States federal court has turned down an attempt to obtain evidence from President Bola Tinubu as part of a long-running records lawsuit, according to a statement from the Presidency on Saturday, 31 October 2026.

What the court decided

The case, before the US District Court for the District of Columbia, is a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit filed by American researcher Aaron Greenspan against the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) and the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA). On 30 October, the court reportedly denied Greenspan's request for limited discovery from President Tinubu and the lobbying firm DCI Group, which he had linked to alleged cyberattacks on his website. The court found the request was not relevant enough to the main records dispute. It also rejected a request by a third party to file a friend-of-the-court brief, refused a related request for judicial notice, and denied Greenspan's motion for summary judgment, meaning the main case continues.

The Presidency's reaction

The President's adviser on information and strategy, Bayo Onanuga, announced the development. Lawyers quoted in the statement said the ruling kept the case focused on the issues properly before the court and set clear limits on what it would consider.

Background

Greenspan has for several years sought the release of US government records he says relate to investigations involving the President in the 1990s. The President's lawyers have consistently opposed the release of the records, and the Presidency has dismissed the claims linked to them. The case has drawn wide attention in Nigeria, particularly from opposition supporters.

What happens next

The FOIA case itself will continue, and further rulings are expected. Greenspan had not publicly responded to the decision at the time of the report.

Content creator De Peters questions how VeryDarkMan presents his power station business

31 Oct 2026, 17:44 GMT

Content creator De Peters has criticised VeryDarkMan for presenting his power station venture as a favour to Nigerians when he is selling the units for profit. He also accused VDM of criticising other influencers for promotions while advertising his own products.

Read more

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) is facing fresh criticism over his power station business, this time from content creator De Peters.

What De Peters said

In comments reported on Saturday, 31 October 2026, De Peters reportedly asked why VDM was acting as though he was giving Nigerians free power stations, when he was in fact selling them to make money. He argued that VDM often criticises other influencers for promoting cryptocurrency, betting platforms and other products, yet was now promoting his own goods. He also said other businesses already sell power stations in Nigeria, some with similar capacity and specifications at lower prices.

Background

VDM has spent recent weeks promoting his Ratel power stations, imported from China, with videos from a warehouse and demonstrations of the units. He has said buyers will be limited to two units each and has clashed with businessman Blord, whom he accuses of copying the idea. Other commentators have praised VDM for bringing more affordable solar options to Nigerians, with one claiming that prices of power stations have fallen since the Ratel brand launched. The debate comes as federal agencies warn celebrities they will be held responsible for promoting fraudulent products, although there is no suggestion that VDM's products are fraudulent.

How people reacted

The comments sparked a wider conversation about influencer marketing. Some users agreed that VDM should not present a business as charity, while his supporters said he was offering a useful product at a fair price. VDM had not publicly responded at the time of the report.

Three kidnapped Zamfara clerics rescued after weeks in captivity

31 Oct 2026, 17:44 GMT

Security forces have reportedly rescued three of six Islamic clerics who were kidnapped in Zamfara State in September while travelling to a prayer session for President Tinubu. The clerics are said to be unharmed and will be handed over to the state government.

Read more

Three Islamic clerics who were kidnapped in Zamfara State more than six weeks ago have been freed, according to reports on Saturday, 31 October 2026.

What happened

The clerics were among six imams abducted on 12 September 2026 on the Dakko–Damri road in Bakura Local Government Area, near Sububu Forest, while travelling to Talata Mafara for a prayer gathering for President Bola Tinubu's government. Their kidnappers reportedly demanded a ransom of ₦30 million.

The rescue

The three were reportedly rescued through intelligence-led operations by the Office of the National Security Adviser and the Department of State Services, working with the police. They were said to be unharmed and in stable condition after medical checks and security debriefings, and will be handed over to the Zamfara State Government to be reunited with their families. The fate of the other three clerics was not reported, and it was not said whether any ransom was paid.

Background

Zamfara is one of the states worst affected by banditry in the North West, with frequent kidnappings on rural roads and in villages. Security agencies have stepped up operations in the region, but abductions for ransom remain common. Insecurity is a key issue in the 2027 election campaign, and opposition candidates have promised tougher action against kidnappers.

What happens next

Security agencies say efforts are continuing to secure the release of the remaining captives.

Joke Silva thanks supporters as 40-day mourning for Olu Jacobs ends

Veteran actress Joke Silva has thanked Nigerians for their prayers, visits and support as the family marks the end of the 40-day mourning period for her husband, the late screen legend Olu Jacobs.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actress Joke Silva has issued a heartfelt message of thanks as her family marks the end of the 40-day mourning period for her late husband, Olu Jacobs.

What she wrote

In an Instagram post on Saturday, 31 October 2026, accompanied by a photo of herself and her husband, Joke Silva wrote on behalf of the Jacobs family to thank everyone who had supported them. She thanked those who sent prayers, messages and calls, those who visited in person, and those who gave gifts or contributed towards the funeral. She said the 40 days had come and gone, and that the family was humbled and deeply grateful. She signed the message as Ajoke Silva, on behalf of the Jacobs family.

Who was Olu Jacobs

Olu Jacobs was one of the most respected actors in Nigerian film history, with a career spanning stage, television and cinema in Nigeria and the United Kingdom. He appeared in many Nollywood classics and international productions and was widely regarded as a father figure in the industry. He and Joke Silva, herself an acclaimed actress and co-founder of the Lufodo Academy of Performing Arts, were one of Nollywood's most admired couples.

How fans reacted

Fans and colleagues responded with messages of love and prayers for the family, praising Joke Silva for her strength and dignity during the period of mourning.
