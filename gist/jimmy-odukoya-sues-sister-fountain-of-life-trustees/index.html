Jimmy Odukoya sues sister and Fountain of Life trustees over his removal

21 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Pastor Jimmy Odukoya has gone to court over his removal as senior pastor of The Fountain of Life Church, filing a suit against his sister and several members of the church's board of trustees.

The suit

The suit was filed at the Federal High Court in Lagos on Friday, 20 November 2026. Eight people are named as respondents, including his sister Tolu Odukoya, interim leader Pastor Rotimi Okpaise and other trustees.

The filing marks a significant escalation in the leadership dispute at the Ilupeju church founded by his late father, Taiwo Odukoya.

Background

Earlier this week, the trustees announced that Jimmy had been removed because his appointment did not reflect his father's succession wishes, and released a 2021 video in which the late founder reportedly named Pastor Kunle Areogun as his successor. Jimmy has insisted his removal is invalid. A Lagos State High Court had already granted the trustees an order barring him from acting as senior pastor while their own suit is heard.

What happens next

The court is expected to fix a date to hear the case. The trustees had not responded publicly to the new suit in the reports.

Rousing welcome

At Sunday service on 22 November, Jimmy Odukoya received loud cheers and applause from the congregation as he arrived, despite the court order barring him from presenting himself as senior pastor. There was an unusually heavy security presence around the church.

'I have not been served'

Speaking to the congregation on Sunday, Jimmy said he had heard about the injunction against him but had not seen it or been formally served, so legally it had not reached him. He pledged to carry on with his ministry. The Lagos State High Court order was granted on 18 November, and the matter was adjourned to 1 December.

Bishop Mike Okonkwo visits

Bishop Mike Okonkwo, who helped found the church, also attended a service and told worshippers the church had been battered on social media in recent weeks. He said he could not be separated from the Fountain of Life Church.

Wife breaks silence

Jimmy's wife, Mo Jasmine Daniel, ended her silence on social media by resharing her husband's post after his Sunday welcome, a move widely seen as a show of support.

'Church is not a political party'

In a sermon clip posted on Sunday, Bishop Mike Okonkwo told worshippers not to take sides with Tolu, Jimmy or the board, reminding them that none of them died for them and that the church is not a political party.

Court summons

On Monday, 23 November, the Lagos State High Court ordered Jimmy to appear on Thursday, 26 November, to answer contempt proceedings brought by the trustees. The judge allowed the trustees to serve him by substituted means, including newspaper publication, pasting at his last known address and electronic delivery, after normal service proved difficult. The notice warns of the consequences of disobeying the court's 18 November order, but it is not a finding of guilt.

  • The suit names eight respondents, including his sister.
  • It was filed at the Federal High Court in Lagos.
  • A separate court order bars him from acting as senior pastor.

CBN raises weekly dollar limit for BDCs from $150,000 to $1m

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has reportedly raised the weekly dollar purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators to $1 million to ease festive pressure on the naira. Banks have begun implementing the change.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has reportedly raised the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change (BDC) operators from $150,000 to $1 million, in a move aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the foreign exchange market.

How it will work

Sources said commercial banks had started contacting BDC operators to set up arrangements for the new limit. Banks will act only as intermediaries between BDCs and the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market, using a purchase tracking platform.

Background

The change is a major relaxation of restrictions the CBN introduced in February. It comes as the naira has weakened against the dollar in recent days and the country's external reserves have fallen. The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate at 23%.

NNPC says $2.04bn in crude oil proceeds is not missing

25 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

NNPC has dismissed reports that $2.04 billion in crude oil proceeds linked to its former trading arm Duke Oil is missing. It says the figure is the gross value of 29 cargoes and that all transactions were properly accounted for.

Read more

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC) has dismissed reports that $2.04 billion in crude oil proceeds involving its trading subsidiary, formerly known as Duke Oil, is missing.

What NNPC said

In a statement on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, NNPC's chief corporate communications officer said the $2.04 billion represented the gross value of 29 crude oil cargoes, not missing or unpaid revenue. The statement was titled 'Duke Oil cargoes are fully accounted for'.

The company maintained that all transactions under the Direct Sale Direct Purchase (DSDP) programme were properly documented. It said the apparent gaps in dollar receipts were due to the way the programme's settlements were structured.

Background

Reports suggesting the money could not be traced had sparked concern online.
