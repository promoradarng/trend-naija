Akwa Ibom announces 13th-month pension bonus and ₦10,000 payment for retirees

31 Oct 2026, 20:42 GMT

Pensioners in Akwa Ibom State are set to receive an extra month's pension, Governor Umo Eno has announced.

What was announced

Speaking at the Ibom Hall Grounds in Uyo, in remarks reported on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the governor said all pensioners in the state would receive a 13th-month pension bonus. He also announced a separate ₦10,000 welfare payment, which will be paid together with November 2026 pensions. The exact amount and payment date of the 13th-month bonus were not stated in reports.

Clearing the backlog

The governor reportedly said his government had paid ₦100 billion of a ₦111 billion gratuity backlog it inherited and planned to clear the remainder before the end of its first term. He also urged pensioners aged 65 and above to register for the state's free health insurance scheme for older people.

Background

At the event, the Nigeria Union of Pensioners presented the governor with a merit award. Unpaid pensions and gratuities are a long-standing problem in many Nigerian states, and retirees have often protested over delays. Some states have recently announced extra payments to help workers and pensioners cope with rising living costs, while the Federal Government this week unveiled a ₦50,000 monthly cash relief scheme for vulnerable households.

How people reacted

Pensioners welcomed the announcement, while some residents called on other states to follow the example.

  • All pensioners in the state will get a 13th-month bonus.
  • A ₦10,000 welfare payment comes with November pensions.
  • ₦100bn of a ₦111bn gratuity backlog has reportedly been paid.

Medical academy urges Nigerians to take precautions against new COVID variant PJ.2.1

23 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Academy of Medical Sciences of Nigeria has warned that festive travel and gatherings could spread a new COVID-19 variant, PJ.2.1. The WHO says the global risk is low and there is no evidence it causes more severe illness.

Read more

The Academy of Medical Sciences of Nigeria has advised Nigerians to take precautions against a new COVID-19 variant, PJ.2.1, warning that festive season travel and large gatherings could help it spread.

What the advisory says

In a public health advisory signed by its president and the chair of its rapid response committee, the Academy said the World Health Organization has classified PJ.2.1 as a Variant Under Monitoring, while rating its additional global public health risk as low. It stressed that evidence about the variant is still limited.

Where it has been found

The variant was first detected in Ontario, Canada, on 2 May 2026, and has since been reported in countries including the United States, Spain, France, the Netherlands, Israel, Singapore, Brazil, Argentina, Australia and New Zealand. WHO had recorded sequences from 18 countries as of 8 November.

How serious is it?

Early studies suggest PJ.2.1, a descendant of the Omicron JN.1 family, may spread more efficiently than some other Omicron sub-lineages. However, there is currently no evidence that it causes more severe illness, and early laboratory findings suggest antibodies from updated COVID-19 vaccines can neutralise it.

At least 21 people, including eight soldiers and 13 construction workers, were reportedly killed by insurgents during the reconstruction of the Mayanti community in Bama Local Government Area of Borno State.

How it came to light

The deaths were disclosed on Saturday, 21 November 2026, by Borno's Commissioner for Education, Abba Wakilbe, who heads the community's resettlement committee, during the inauguration of the rebuilt community. Among those killed was a major, Major Mairiga, along with seven other soldiers.

Wakilbe said work on the project began in August 2025 and was completed in October 2026. He paid tribute to those who died, saying their sacrifice made the return of the community possible.

What was built

Governor Babagana Zulum inaugurated 500 houses, 2,000 semi-permanent shelters and a school at Mayanti. He praised the committee for finishing the project despite repeated attacks, recalling that the community was attacked four or five times while construction was going on.

Background

Many communities in Borno State were destroyed during years of insurgency, and the state government has been rebuilding and resettling displaced residents in several areas.

NCC warns 'London-used' phones may be cut off if they fail device checks

23 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Imported used phones flagged by the Nigerian Communications Commission could lose network service after 30 days, under its device checker rules. The regulator has been sending text alerts to affected users since October.

Read more

Nigerians who buy imported used phones, popularly called 'London-used', could reportedly lose mobile service if their devices fail checks by the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC).

How it works

Under the regulator's Device Checker and Type Approval Compliance framework, flagged devices may be disconnected from local networks after 30 cumulative days.

The NCC began sending text alerts under the sender name NCC-Checker on 10 October 2026. The messages advise affected users to contact the seller or check their device using the regulator's device checker. Alerts have reportedly gone to users of iPhones, Google Pixels, TECNO, Infinix and other brands.

Existing phones

About 231 million devices that were already active on Nigerian networks before the rollout were captured automatically.
