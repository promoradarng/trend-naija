Akwa Ibom announces 13th-month pension bonus and ₦10,000 payment for retirees

31 Oct 2026, 20:42 GMT

Pensioners in Akwa Ibom State are set to receive an extra month's pension, Governor Umo Eno has announced.

What was announced

Speaking at the Ibom Hall Grounds in Uyo, in remarks reported on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the governor said all pensioners in the state would receive a 13th-month pension bonus. He also announced a separate ₦10,000 welfare payment, which will be paid together with November 2026 pensions. The exact amount and payment date of the 13th-month bonus were not stated in reports.

Clearing the backlog

The governor reportedly said his government had paid ₦100 billion of a ₦111 billion gratuity backlog it inherited and planned to clear the remainder before the end of its first term. He also urged pensioners aged 65 and above to register for the state's free health insurance scheme for older people.

Background

At the event, the Nigeria Union of Pensioners presented the governor with a merit award. Unpaid pensions and gratuities are a long-standing problem in many Nigerian states, and retirees have often protested over delays. Some states have recently announced extra payments to help workers and pensioners cope with rising living costs, while the Federal Government this week unveiled a ₦50,000 monthly cash relief scheme for vulnerable households.

How people reacted

Pensioners welcomed the announcement, while some residents called on other states to follow the example.

  • All pensioners in the state will get a 13th-month bonus.
  • A ₦10,000 welfare payment comes with November pensions.
  • ₦100bn of a ₦111bn gratuity backlog has reportedly been paid.

Mele Kyari denies ₦210 trillion went missing at NNPC under his watch

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

Former NNPC boss Mele Kyari has told a Senate committee that no money was lost under his leadership, saying the disputed ₦210 trillion in the company's accounts came from a misreading of its financial records.

Read more

Former Group Managing Director of the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPCL), Mele Kyari, has denied that ₦210 trillion went unaccounted for while he led the company.

What happened

Kyari appeared before the Senate Committee on Public Accounts, which is examining queries raised by the Auditor-General of the Federation over NNPCL's audited financial statements for 2017 to 2023, according to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The disputed figure is made up of ₦103 trillion recorded as accrued expenses and ₦107 trillion listed as sundry receivables. Kyari told the committee that no money was lost under his watch and that the figures arose from a misreading of the company's records.

The committee's position

The committee began examining the figures in June 2025 and has repeatedly rejected the company's explanations, insisting that NNPCL properly account for the entries.

Background

Kyari led NNPC from 2019 until 2025, overseeing its conversion into a limited liability company under the Petroleum Industry Act.

NERC approves ₦30bn to give free meters to remaining Band A customers

10 Nov 2026, 20:44 GMT

The electricity regulator NERC has approved ₦30 billion to provide free meters to unmetered Band A customers and speed up installations for Band B customers, with all installations due by 31 May 2027.

Read more

The Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission (NERC) has approved ₦30 billion to provide meters to electricity customers who still do not have them.

The plan

In a statement on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, NERC said the money, Tranche C of its Meter Acquisition Fund, took effect on 2 November. It will be used to give free meters to the remaining unmetered Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs for at least 20 hours of supply a day, and to speed up metering for Band B customers. All installations must be completed by 31 May 2027.

How it will work

The money will be shared among the 11 electricity distribution companies in proportion to their contributions to the fund, and meters must be spread fairly across the states in each company's area. The order is part of the third phase of the Presidential Metering Initiative and requires the companies to use transparent procurement involving eligible local meter makers, assemblers and providers.

Background

Many Nigerian electricity customers are still billed by estimate, a long-standing source of complaints. The announcement comes after recent power cuts in Lagos, Abuja and other states that the government blamed on gas shortages, maintenance and sabotage.

Air Peace offloads passenger who allegedly threatened his anger could 'burn down' plane

10 Nov 2026, 17:47 GMT

An Air Peace flight to Asaba returned to the ramp after a passenger who refused to stop taking photos allegedly told crew his anger could 'burn down' the plane, the NCAA says. He was removed and later said he had no bad intentions.

Read more

An Air Peace flight to Asaba, Delta State, returned to the ramp before take-off after a passenger allegedly threatened the crew.

What happened

According to the Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority's director of public affairs and consumer protection, Michael Achimugu, who described the incident on X on Monday, 9 November 2026, the trouble began when the passenger refused repeated requests from cabin crew to stop taking photographs and put his device away. During the dispute, he allegedly said his anger could 'burn down' the plane. The crew reported the threat to the captain, who ordered the aircraft back to the ramp at 3.57pm, and the passenger was offloaded. His travelling companion also chose to leave the plane.

The passenger's explanation

Achimugu said the passenger cooperated during the offloading. The man later told authorities that his frustration had built up after he missed an earlier flight, and that he had no intention of causing harm.

Background

Under aviation rules, passengers must follow the instructions of cabin crew, and threats of any kind on board are treated seriously.
