CBN warns banks to store payment data in Nigeria by January 2027

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has told banks and other financial institutions that the deadline for keeping critical payment transaction data inside Nigeria remains 1 January 2027.

What happened

The CBN Deputy Governor for Financial System Stability, Lamido Abubakar Yuguda, gave the warning at a regulatory meeting in Lagos organised with the National Information Technology Development Agency (NITDA), reported on Thursday, 29 October 2026. He said the date had not changed and that institutions that break the rule would face sanctions. Deliberate attempts to frustrate the policy would not be tolerated, he added.

Why the rule exists

The CBN says storing payment data in the country will improve supervision, make the financial system more resilient and protect sensitive customer information, as more Nigerians rely on mobile apps, online banking and fintech services. The meeting focused on the National Sovereign Cloud Initiative, which aims to keep government and critical data on infrastructure within Nigeria.

What banks must do

Yuguda reportedly stressed that simply moving data into the country will not make it safe. Banks must also improve their cybersecurity, plans for keeping services running during outages, disaster recovery and risk management. They will remain responsible for risks linked to cloud providers and other technology partners.

What it means for customers

For ordinary customers, the change should be mostly invisible, but the regulator says it should make it harder for sensitive financial data to be exposed and easier for authorities to investigate fraud.

  • The deadline is 1 January 2027 and will not change.
  • Banks must also strengthen cybersecurity and backup plans.
  • Firms that break the rule face sanctions.

Troops rescue 18 of 22 children abducted in Kaduna, search on for four

23 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Soldiers have rescued 18 children who were abducted by suspected terrorists in Kachia/Kajuru, Kaduna State, early on Sunday. The army says the search for the remaining four continues.

Read more

The Nigerian Army says its troops have rescued 18 children who were abducted by suspected terrorists in Kaduna State early on Sunday, 22 November 2026, and are searching for four others.

What happened

According to the army, armed men intercepted 22 members of the Boys' and Girls' Brigade in the Mother-Cat community, in the Kachia/Kajuru area of the state, while they were on an early-morning group road walk, and took them away.

The rescue

The army's acting director of public relations, Colonel Appolonia Anele, said troops of 1 Division operating under Operation FANSAN YAMMA moved immediately after receiving a distress call and pursued the abductors. Troops advanced towards a riverside area, where some of the children were found after the fleeing attackers abandoned them. In all, 18 were rescued.

What happens next

The army said operations were continuing to rescue the remaining four children. The attack comes days after troops foiled a suspected kidnap attempt at a school in another part of Kaduna State.

About 20 correctional officers are reportedly under investigation after two death-row inmates escaped from the Oke-Kura custodial centre in Ilorin. The service has confirmed the probe but not the number of officers involved.

About 20 officers of the Nigerian Correctional Service are reportedly under investigation after two death-row inmates escaped from the Medium Security Custodial Centre at Oke-Kura, Ilorin, Kwara State, last Wednesday.

The probe

Those affected are said to include members of the armed squad and duty officers responsible for supervising inmates around the clock. Sources described the breach as a coordinated escape attempt involving seven inmates, two of whom got away.

What the service said

The Kwara State Command of the service confirmed on Sunday, 22 November 2026, that an investigation had begun on the orders of the Controller-General of Corrections. Its spokesperson said the probe would be thorough, but declined to confirm how many officers had been questioned, saying it would be premature while the investigation was ongoing.

Gunmen reportedly set secondary school on fire in Plateau's Bokkos

22 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Suspected gunmen reportedly set fire to Government Secondary School, Masharkut, in Bokkos, Plateau State, on Sunday afternoon. No casualties were reported, and local officials said the attackers had not been identified.

Read more

Suspected gunmen reportedly set fire to Government Secondary School, Masharkut, in the Butura District of Bokkos Local Government Area, Plateau State, on Sunday, 22 November 2026, causing panic among residents.

What happened

According to a community member, the attack began at about 1pm. Residents noticed smoke rising from the school shortly after returning from church, and realised about 30 minutes later that the building was on fire.

Official response

The chairman of Bokkos Local Government Area confirmed that the council had received reports of the fire. He said the circumstances and the identity of those responsible had not yet been established, and that the damage to the school building was the authorities' immediate concern.

Security agencies had not commented at the time of the reports, and no casualties were mentioned.

Background

Bokkos and other parts of Plateau State have suffered repeated attacks on rural communities in recent years.
