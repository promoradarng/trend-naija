Osimhen closes in on Galatasaray's record for goals by a foreign player

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

Super Eagles striker Victor Osimhen is moving quickly up Galatasaray's list of all-time foreign goalscorers, and the club record is now within reach.

Where he stands

Osimhen's two goals in Monday's 2-1 derby win over Fenerbahçe took his tally for the Istanbul club to 68, according to reports on Thursday, 29 October 2026. That puts him third among foreign players in the club's history. Romanian great Gheorghe Hagi is second with 72 goals, while Argentine striker Mauro Icardi holds the record with 77, just nine more than Osimhen.

His form this season

The 27-year-old has reportedly scored nine goals and provided three assists in nine appearances in all competitions this season under coach Okan Buruk. His derby double was celebrated widely by Galatasaray fans and by Nigerians on social media.

Background

Osimhen first joined Galatasaray on loan from Napoli and became a fan favourite after a prolific first season that helped the club win the Turkish league title. He later completed a permanent move. The former African Footballer of the Year remains Nigeria's main attacking threat, and his form will be closely watched by Super Eagles fans.

What happens next

At his current rate, Osimhen could pass Hagi within weeks, and could challenge Icardi's record before the end of the season.

  • Osimhen has 68 goals for Galatasaray.
  • Mauro Icardi holds the foreign-player record with 77.
  • He scored twice in the derby win over Fenerbahçe.

CAS restores Senegal as AFCON 2025 champions, overturning CAF ruling

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Court of Arbitration for Sport has confirmed Senegal as winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. It overturned in full the CAF Appeal Board decision that had handed the title to Morocco.

Read more

Senegal are once again Africa's champions after the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) overturned the decision that had stripped them of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations title.

The verdict

CAS delivered its ruling on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, reversing in full the decision of CAF's Appeal Board, which had awarded the title to hosts Morocco. With the verdict, Senegal are officially recognised as the 2025 AFCON champions.

How it started

Senegal beat Morocco 1-0 after extra time in the final at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat on 18 January 2026, with Pape Gueye scoring the only goal. During the match, Senegal's players walked off the pitch for about 15 minutes in protest at a late penalty awarded to Morocco, which was later saved.

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation then took legal action, and on 17 March CAF's Appeal Board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match, awarding Morocco a 3-0 win and the trophy. The Senegalese Football Federation refused to accept the decision and appealed to CAS.

What it means

The ruling ends months of legal uncertainty over the continent's top football title. Before the verdict, CAF President Patrice Motsepe had said the body would accept whatever CAS decided.

Morocco to fight on

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation said it rejected the ruling and would challenge it through every legal channel available, citing what it described as procedural failures. Senegal star Sadio Mane welcomed the decision.

CAF responds

In a short statement, CAF acknowledged the ruling and pledged to act on it. The trophy, medals and prize money were never taken back from Senegal during the dispute and will remain with the team.

Former Super Eagles captain and coach Augustine Eguavoen is reportedly emerging as the leading candidate to take interim charge of Nigeria's national team in early 2027.

Chelle's contract

Eric Chelle was appointed in January 2025 on a two-year deal that expires in January 2027. He had reportedly agreed in principle on an extension to August 2028, but the deal was never formally signed after NFF President Ibrahim Gusau and his board resigned and federation elections were postponed.

Limited powers

A five-member FIFA Normalisation Committee is now running the federation until new elections, due by 31 May 2027. It is said to lack the authority to commit the NFF to multi-year contracts that go beyond its temporary mandate, leaving Chelle likely to leave when his current deal ends.

Qualifying pressure

The uncertainty comes as Nigeria sit in a difficult position in their 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying group.

FIFA confirms US-led bid for 2031 Women's World Cup, UK to host 2035

24 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

FIFA has confirmed the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica as hosts of the 2031 Women's World Cup, the first with 48 teams. The four UK nations will host the 2035 edition.

Read more

FIFA has officially named the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica as joint hosts of the 2031 Women's World Cup, while England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland will host the 2035 tournament.

The decision

The hosts were confirmed on Monday, 23 November 2026, at an Extraordinary FIFA Congress held virtually with representatives of its 211 member associations. Both joint bids were approved by acclamation, having been the only candidates.

A bigger tournament

The 2031 edition will be the first Women's World Cup with 48 teams and 104 matches, bringing it in line with the men's tournament, and the first to be hosted across four countries.

It will be the third time the US has hosted the Women's World Cup, after 1999 and 2003, and the first time for Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica.

ADC tells Tinubu to resign or face impeachment over Kano governor's interview

25 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The African Democratic Congress has reportedly demanded that President Tinubu resign or face impeachment, citing Kano Governor Abba Yusuf's recent interview about the 2023 Supreme Court ruling. The ADC says it points to possible gross misconduct.

Read more

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has reportedly issued an ultimatum to President Bola Tinubu, demanding that he either resign or face impeachment proceedings in the National Assembly.

Why the ADC made the demand

The demand follows Kano State Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf's recent Hausa-language interview, in which he spoke about a promise to rejoin the APC if the president did not interfere in his Supreme Court case over the 2023 governorship election. The ADC argued that the governor's remarks suggested a link between the court case and a political deal, and that this could amount to gross misconduct, a constitutional ground for removing a president.

Background

Yusuf defected from the NNPP to the APC in January 2026. In his interview, he claimed that his former mentor, Rabiu Kwankwaso, sent him to make the promise to Tinubu. The Kwankwasiyya Movement has rejected the claim as false and said the governor offered no evidence.

The other side

No response from the presidency or the APC to the ADC's demand was included in the reports.
