Russia detains teenager over planned attack on Moscow museum after US warning

1 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The United States Embassy in Moscow has issued an urgent warning about a possible attack at one of the Russian capital's best-known historic sites.

What the embassy said

In a statement on Saturday, 31 October 2026, the embassy said it was aware of a credible threat of an event that could cause mass casualties at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve. It advised American citizens to avoid the site that day, stay alert and avoid crowds, and said the US government was coordinating with Russian authorities.

Russia's response

Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) confirmed it had received information from its American counterparts about possible preparations for a terrorist attack in Moscow and said it was taking appropriate action. Russian media reported that the museum reserve was temporarily closed and that police restricted access to the surrounding park.

Background

Tsaritsyno is a large 18th-century palace and park complex in the south of Moscow, popular with tourists and families. The warning is reminiscent of March 2024, when the US Embassy warned of a possible attack on large gatherings in Moscow shortly before gunmen attacked the Crocus City Hall concert venue, killing more than 140 people. The rare cooperation between US and Russian security services comes at a time of high tension between Russia and the West over Ukraine and Europe.

Suspect detained

On Monday, 2 November, reports said the FSB had detained a minor suspected of planning the attack on the museum reserve, acting on information from US law enforcement. The suspect was not named, and Russian authorities did not say exactly when the arrest took place.

What happens next

Russian authorities are expected to give more details of the investigation. The case is a rare example of security cooperation between the two countries amid wider tensions.

  • The FSB says it detained a teenager over the planned attack.
  • It acted on information from US law enforcement.
  • The US Embassy had warned of a credible threat.

UK splits post-study work visa into two tiers from 2027

1 Nov 2026, 11:44 GMT

The UK is changing its Graduate visa from January 2027: most bachelor's and master's graduates will get 18 months instead of two years, while graduates of universities with strong compliance and job records will keep two years. Nigerians are among the biggest users of the route.

Read more

Nigerian students planning to study in the United Kingdom will face new rules on how long they can stay and work after graduation, under changes announced in the UK government's 2026 Budget.

What is changing

From 1 January 2027, the Graduate visa will reportedly be split into two tiers. Most bachelor's and master's graduates will be allowed 18 months to stay and work, down from two years. Graduates of universities with strong immigration compliance records and good graduate job outcomes will keep two years. Doctoral graduates will keep their existing three years. Applications submitted before 1 January 2027 will be decided under the current rules.

Unanswered questions

The UK government has not yet published the list of qualifying universities or the exact criteria that will be used. The change partly reverses an earlier plan to cut the visa to 18 months for everyone.

Why it matters for Nigerians

Nigeria was the third-largest source of sponsored study visas to the UK in the year to June 2026, with more than 26,000 main applicants, behind only China and India. More than 20,000 Nigerians were granted permission on the Graduate route in the same period. Prospective students are being advised to check a university's immigration compliance record and graduate employment outcomes, as well as fees and reputation.

Background

The Graduate visa cannot be extended, so graduates who want to stay longer must switch to another route, such as the Skilled Worker visa. The UK has tightened immigration rules in recent years, including restrictions on bringing family members on student visas.

US appeals court puts hold on order to reopen 2027 visa lottery

1 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

A US appeals court has reportedly paused a lower court order that required the State Department to open registration for the 2027 Diversity Visa lottery. Tens of thousands of African hopefuls now have no confirmed date to apply while the appeal continues.

Read more

Hopes of an imminent opening of the US Diversity Visa lottery for 2027 have been put on hold after a federal appeals court intervened, according to reports on Sunday, 1 November 2026.

What happened

The US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit reportedly granted an emergency stay on 29 October, pausing an order issued by District Judge Jinsook Ohta on 14 October. That order, made in a case brought by immigration law firm Red Eagle Law against Secretary of State Marco Rubio, had required the State Department to announce registration dates, open applications and notify selected entrants by 3 December. The lower court had set 29 October for publishing registration details and 1 November for opening the application window.

Why it was paused

The appeals judges reportedly found that the law firm had not shown that its financial interest in representing lottery winners was closely enough linked to the programme's goal of increasing immigration from under-represented countries. The court said its decision was not an endorsement of the State Department's failure to open the programme.

Who is affected

The lottery offers up to 55,000 US immigrant visas each year to people from countries with low rates of immigration to the United States, and African applicants have long made up a large share of winners. Tens of thousands of hopefuls across the continent now have no confirmed registration date. Nigeria has been excluded from the lottery for several years because of its high number of immigrants to the US, but many Nigerians follow the programme closely and some qualify through a spouse's or parent's country of birth.

What happens next

The registration window will remain closed until the appeal is decided or the State Department announces its own dates.

The video

A Facebook video reportedly showing Ozonna Soludo, the governor's son, dancing to and promoting a song associated with the NDC has sparked reactions online. In online discussions, the NDC has been linked to Peter Obi and Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso.

Flamingos beat Venezuela to reach U-17 Women's World Cup semi-finals, face Brazil next

2 Nov 2026, 09:44 GMT

Nigeria's Flamingos beat Venezuela 1-0 in Morocco through a late penalty from captain Azeezat Oduntan to reach the semi-finals of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup. They are Africa's last team in the tournament and will play Brazil on Wednesday.

Read more

Nigeria's under-17 women's team, the Flamingos, are into the semi-finals of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup in Morocco.

The match

The Flamingos beat Venezuela 1-0 in the quarter-final on Sunday, 1 November 2026, at the Mohammed VI Football Academy in Salé. Captain Azeezat Oduntan scored the winner from the penalty spot in the 89th minute after Oluebube Umejiaku was fouled in the box, a decision confirmed by a VAR review.

What comes next

Nigeria will face Brazil in the semi-final on Wednesday, 4 November, at the Moulay El Hassan Stadium in Rabat. They are the only African side left in the competition after Ghana lost 1-0 to Japan in their quarter-final.

Reactions

The Confederation of African Football congratulated the team on social media, celebrating their place in the last four. Nigerian fans also shared videos of the players dancing and celebrating after earlier victories in the tournament.

Background

Nigeria has a strong record in women's youth football and has reached the later stages of age-group World Cups several times. The senior team, the Super Falcons, are the most successful side in African women's football and won another Women's Africa Cup of Nations title this year. The Flamingos' run has raised hopes of Nigeria's first-ever U-17 women's world title.
