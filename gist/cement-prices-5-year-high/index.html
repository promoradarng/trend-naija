Cement hits ₦15,000 a bag, up over 300% in five years

19 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The price of a 50kg bag of cement in Nigeria has reportedly reached between ₦13,000 and ₦15,000, about 329% higher than in 2021, according to market price data.

How prices rose

A bag cost between ₦3,300 and ₦3,500 in 2021, rising to about ₦4,600 in 2022 and ₦5,000 in 2023. By 2024 buyers were paying ₦7,500 to ₦8,500, and prices climbed to ₦9,500 to ₦10,500 through most of 2025. There was brief relief at the start of 2026 before prices rose again.

Who is affected

The steep increase has put heavy pressure on construction budgets, hitting individual homebuilders and small-scale developers hardest.

  • Prices are up about 329% since 2021.
  • Small builders are hardest hit.

Bandits abduct six residents in Kaduna's Millennium City

Suspected bandits have reportedly abducted six residents in the Millennium City area of Chikun Local Government Area, Kaduna State.

What happened

The attackers stormed two houses at around midnight on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, shooting and breaking in before taking nine people away. Residents said they began hearing gunshots at about 11:30pm.

Three of those abducted, including an elderly woman and a mother with her toddler, were later released, leaving six people in captivity.

Residents' fears

A man whose wife is among those still held said he feared for her safety because she has difficulty walking, and appealed for help to rescue her.

Background

Kaduna has seen several kidnapping incidents in recent weeks, including a foiled attempt at a school in Zangon Kataf and the abduction of Boys' and Girls' Brigade members in Kachia/Kajuru, most of whom troops rescued.

CBN raises weekly dollar limit for BDCs from $150,000 to $1m

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has reportedly raised the weekly dollar purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators to $1 million to ease festive pressure on the naira. Banks have begun implementing the change.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has reportedly raised the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change (BDC) operators from $150,000 to $1 million, in a move aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the foreign exchange market.

How it will work

Sources said commercial banks had started contacting BDC operators to set up arrangements for the new limit. Banks will act only as intermediaries between BDCs and the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market, using a purchase tracking platform.

Background

The change is a major relaxation of restrictions the CBN introduced in February. It comes as the naira has weakened against the dollar in recent days and the country's external reserves have fallen. The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate at 23%.
