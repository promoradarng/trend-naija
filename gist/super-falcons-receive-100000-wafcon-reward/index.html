Super Falcons finally receive $100,000 each for 2024 WAFCON win

3 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Members of Nigeria's Super Falcons have reportedly begun receiving the cash reward promised by President Bola Tinubu after the team won the 2024 Women's Africa Cup of Nations (WAFCON).

What happened

According to reports, two players and a member of the technical crew confirmed that their accounts were credited on Monday, 2 November 2026. Each of the 24 players is said to have received the naira equivalent of $100,000, about ₦140 million, while each of the 11 members of the technical crew received the naira equivalent of $50,000. One player reportedly said she was grateful that the promise had finally been kept.

Other rewards

Apart from the cash, the President also approved national honours and three-bedroom apartments for the players and officials after the victory.

Background

The Super Falcons won a record-extending 10th continental title in Morocco in July 2025, coming from behind to beat the hosts in the final. The tournament, originally scheduled for 2024, was held a year later but kept its 2024 name. The win was celebrated across Nigeria, and the team was received by the President at the State House, where the rewards were announced. Delays in paying promised bonuses to national teams have been a long-running complaint in Nigerian sport, and some fans had questioned whether this reward would ever be paid.

How people reacted

Fans welcomed the news, saying the players deserved the money and calling on the government to pay other national teams promptly in future. Others said the long delay showed the need for clear and timely reward systems.

Flamingos beat Brazil 3-1 to reach first U-17 Women's World Cup final

5 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Nigeria's Flamingos have reached the final of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup for the first time after beating Brazil 3-1 in the semi-final. Awawu Bashiru scored twice and Queen Joseph once. They face Japan in the final.

Read more

Nigeria's under-17 women's team, the Flamingos, have made history by reaching the final of the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup for the first time.

The match

The Flamingos beat Brazil 3-1 in the semi-final on Wednesday night, 4 November 2026, according to reports. Queen Joseph opened the scoring in the 56th minute, and Awawu Bashiru doubled the lead in the 71st minute before adding her second goal in the 80th minute to put the game beyond Brazil's reach.

A record run

The win beats the team's previous best performance, a bronze medal at the 2022 edition of the tournament. On the way to the semi-final, the Flamingos beat Venezuela in the quarter-finals, a result that earned praise from the Confederation of African Football.

The final

The Flamingos will now face Japan in the final as they chase Nigeria's first FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup title. Japan, one of the strongest sides in women's youth football, won the tournament in 2014.

Background

The result comes at a difficult time for Nigerian women's football at senior level. The Super Falcons failed to qualify for the Women's World Cup for the first time, and no Nigerian player was shortlisted for this year's CAF Women's Player of the Year award. The Flamingos' success has been welcomed as a sign that a new generation of players is coming through.

FIFA's message

FIFA congratulated the Flamingos on qualifying for the final, a first for Nigeria at this level of the women's game.

How people reacted

Nigerians celebrated the win across social media, praising the young players and their coaches and urging the government and the football federation to support them ahead of the final.

Oyedepo arrives in Port Harcourt to dedicate Ibiyeomie's 120,000-seat Hand of God Cathedral

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Bishop David Oyedepo has arrived in Port Harcourt to lead the dedication of Salvation Ministries' Hand of God Cathedral, which is reported to seat up to 120,000 worshippers. Pastor David Ibiyeomie received him on Thursday evening.

Read more

Bishop David Oyedepo, founder of Living Faith Church Worldwide, has arrived in Port Harcourt to lead the dedication of a giant new church auditorium built by Pastor David Ibiyeomie's Salvation Ministries.

The arrival

Oyedepo reportedly landed in Rivers State by private jet on the evening of Thursday, 5 November 2026, and was received by Ibiyeomie and a delegation of ministers. The group then travelled in a motorcade to the cathedral complex, where church leaders welcomed them with cheers. Videos of the arrival have been widely shared online.

The cathedral

The dedication of the Hand of God Cathedral was scheduled for 10am on Friday, 6 November, at the ministry's headquarters on the Port Harcourt International Airport Road in Igwuruta. According to reports, the auditorium can seat up to 120,000 people and covers about 56,000 square metres, with a five-wing design meant to resemble an open hand. Reports have described it as bigger than the 109,000-capacity Ark auditorium built by Oyedepo's church. The church invited worshippers from across Nigeria and beyond to attend.

Background

Ibiyeomie founded Salvation Ministries in Port Harcourt and is widely seen as a spiritual son of Oyedepo, which has given the event special meaning for members of both churches. The pastor has been in the news this week over a sermon urging Christians to pay their tithes before spending money on Christmas clothes.

How people reacted

Church members celebrated the occasion online, calling it a historic moment. Others debated the cost of building such large church buildings at a time of economic hardship, while supporters said the project shows what a faith community can achieve.

Wike mocks Peter Obi: 'Vote for him as president of social media, not Nigeria'

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has told Rivers voters that Peter Obi is popular on social media but lacks the capacity to lead Nigeria, also mocking the NDC candidate's habit of carrying his own bag. Obi's supporters hit back online.

Read more

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has taken another swipe at Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC).

What Wike said

Speaking at a town hall meeting in Abonnema, Akuku-Toru Local Government Area of Rivers State, on Thursday, 5 November 2026, Wike reportedly said that people who wanted a president of social media should vote for Obi, but that he should not be voted for as president of Nigeria. He also mocked Obi's well-known habit of carrying his own bag, saying it did not show that he had the capacity to rule the country.

Background

Wike, a former Rivers governor, leads the Rainbow Coalition, a group of PDP and other politicians backing President Bola Tinubu's re-election in the presidential election scheduled for 16 January 2027. He has repeatedly criticised Obi in recent weeks. Obi, a former Anambra governor, has a large following among young voters, especially online, and is running with Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate. Wike made the comments during a busy week in which he also spoke about Governor Siminalayi Fubara's political future and denied any role in the court ruling on the Imo West Senate ticket.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters responded angrily online, with many saying Wike could not campaign without mentioning Obi and describing the former governor's simple lifestyle as a strength. Supporters of the minister said Obi's online popularity had not translated into votes in Rivers State in the past. Obi's campaign had not publicly responded to the latest remarks at the time of writing.

Former INEC director asks how 1.5 million questionable records got into the voter register

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, a former INEC director of voter education, says it should not have been possible for about 1.5 million questionable entries to enter the voter register, and wants INEC to explain how they were detected and how they got in.

Read more

A former senior official of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has questioned how about 1.5 million questionable records found their way into the country's voter register.

What he said

Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, a former Director of Voter Education and Publicity at INEC, said in a television interview reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, that such problems should not happen given the safeguards the commission has put in place. He noted that the law on who can register is simple: a Nigerian citizen aged 18 or above who turns up in person to register. Because registration requires physical presence and the capture of fingerprints and photographs, he said, he could not understand how so many questionable entries had been made.

His questions for INEC

Osaze-Uzzi said the commission, led by its chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, had not explained what period its review of the register covered or how the problem entries were first detected. He said tools such as the Automated Fingerprint Identification System and artificial intelligence can help to remove duplicates, but that INEC should still give a clear account of how the records got into the database in the first place.

Background

INEC regularly cleans up the register to remove duplicate, underage and other invalid entries before elections, and the issue of the accuracy of the register has long been debated. The 2027 general elections are due in January, with the presidential poll on 16 January. INEC also urged young Nigerians this week to turn their online enthusiasm into votes, saying votes cannot be counted on social media.

Why it matters

A clean voter register is central to public confidence in elections. Civil society groups have urged INEC to publish more detail on the clean-up so that eligible voters are not wrongly removed.
