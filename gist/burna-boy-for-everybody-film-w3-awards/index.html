Burna Boy's 'For Everybody' campaign film wins 15 awards

31 Oct 2026, 11:42 GMT

A campaign film featuring Burna Boy and several of Africa's best-known footballers has picked up a string of international awards.

What happened

'For Everybody', made by Sporty Group's production arm Sporty Studios together with RadicalMedia, reportedly won 15 awards at the 2026 w3 Awards, according to reports on Saturday, 31 October 2026. It won a gold award in the creator and brand collaboration category, as well as 14 silver awards in categories including music video, cinematography, brand storytelling, branded content, and sports and entertainment.

About the film

The film was directed by Dave Meyers, an American director best known for his music videos with major global stars, and was released in January 2026. It brings together music and sport to celebrate African talent and culture. Alongside Burna Boy, it features Nigerian football stars Asisat Oshoala and Kenneth Omeruo, as well as Spain's Nico Williams, France's Eduardo Camavinga, Ghana legend Michael Essien, Kenya's Victor Wanyama and South African rugby star Cheslin Kolbe.

Background

The w3 Awards recognise creative work in digital media, including websites, videos and online campaigns. Burna Boy, a Grammy winner, has increasingly worked on projects linking music and sport, and recently presented the Socrates Award at this year's Ballon d'Or ceremony. Oshoala is one of Africa's most decorated female footballers, having won the African Women's Footballer of the Year award a record number of times.

How fans reacted

Fans celebrated the recognition online, saying the film showed how African music and sport can work together to reach global audiences.

  • The film won one gold and 14 silver awards.
  • It was directed by Dave Meyers.
  • It also features Nico Williams, Camavinga and Michael Essien.

Tems opens up about living with ADHD

19 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Grammy-winning singer Tems has spoken about how ADHD affects her relationships, saying she finds it hard to keep in touch with people and sometimes switches topics mid-conversation.

Read more

Nigerian singer Temilade Openiyi, known as Tems, has opened up about living with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and how it affects her relationships with family and friends.

What she said

In a radio interview in Lagos, the singer said keeping in touch with people is something she finds difficult, even though she has many supportive people around her.

She explained that ADHD sometimes makes her switch topics suddenly during conversations, and that she often forgets to reach out to people. She admitted she has not always been consistent in communicating with people who matter to her, but said she is trying.

Background

Tems leads the Nigerian nominees for the 2027 Grammy Awards with four nominations.

IGP tells doctors: no police report needed to treat gunshot victims

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Inspector-General of Police Olatunji Disu has reminded doctors and hospitals that the law does not require a police report before treating gunshot victims. He urged health workers to put saving lives first.

Read more

The Inspector-General of Police, Olatunji Disu, has urged doctors and other health workers to treat gunshot victims immediately, without first demanding a police report.

What the IGP said

In a message posted on the Nigeria Police Force's official X account on Thursday, 19 November 2026, Disu stressed that the law does not require hospitals to obtain a police report before attending to people with gunshot wounds.

He appealed to doctors and health workers to prioritise saving lives instead of waiting for police documents before starting emergency treatment.

Help for the public

The IGP also encouraged members of the public not to hesitate to help injured people get to hospital.

Background

Nigeria has a law requiring hospitals to treat gunshot victims without first demanding a police report.
