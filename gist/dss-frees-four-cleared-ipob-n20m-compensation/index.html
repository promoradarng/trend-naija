DSS DG orders release of four men cleared of IPOB membership, approves ₦20 million compensation

4 Nov 2026, 04:00 GMT

The Director-General of the Department of State Services (DSS), Oluwatosin Adeola Ajayi, has ordered the release of four men who had been accused of belonging to the proscribed Indigenous People of Biafra (IPOB).

Who they are

The four men, Emele Uduma Ibe, Berefgagha Appah, Ondokeyilemi Aggry and Oyeinmokebebeton Aggry, were arrested in May 2022 in the Sampou and Akeddei communities of Kolokuma/Opokuma and Sagbama Local Government Areas of Bayelsa State. That means they spent about four and a half years in custody before their release.

₦5 million each

After they were cleared of the allegations, the DG approved an initial compensation package of ₦20 million, with each man receiving ₦5 million. The men are also expected to receive psychological and medical support and help returning to normal life.

Security sources said investigations showed the men had been lured by people claiming to be IPOB members, who promised them ₦100,000 a month under the Federal Government's Amnesty Programme.

"Where we admit our mistakes, we offer compensation, and seek ways to improve operations in tandem with the rule of law," Ajayi said. One of the freed men, Emele Uduma Ibe, described the day as the happiest of his life.

Not the first time

This is not the first such case under Ajayi. After he took office in 2024, he ordered a review of more than 300 inherited terrorism cases. Others who have since been compensated include a Jos businessman shot in error by DSS personnel, who received ₦20 million, and several people cleared of IPOB ties who received between ₦5 million and ₦10 million.

Can money repair the damage?

For anyone who has spent years in detention over an allegation they did not commit, freedom alone may not be enough to repair the damage. There is the emotional suffering, the disruption of family life, the loss of income and the stigma that often follows such accusations.

The ₦5 million payment may provide some financial relief, but it cannot replace the time lost.

Still, the decision sends an important message about accountability within Nigeria's security system. Security agencies have a duty to investigate threats to national security and protect citizens, but that duty must be carried out within the law and with respect for the rights of people who have not been convicted of any offence.

Many Nigerians will be watching to see whether similar attention is given to other people who may have been wrongly accused or detained, especially given the controversies around IPOB-linked arrests in the South-East and South-South.

The bigger question remains: if someone is arrested, detained and later cleared of wrongdoing, should compensation automatically follow? What do you think about the DSS Director-General's decision?

  • The four men were arrested in Bayelsa State in May 2022.
  • Each will receive ₦5 million, described as an initial payment, plus medical and psychological support.
  • The DG has ordered compensation in several earlier wrongful-arrest cases.

The rally

Adebayo made the pledge on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, at the SDP presidential campaign rally in Yola, Adamawa State, according to a statement from the party's campaign spokesman.

A sermon by Pastor Paul Eneche has gone viral, with many social media users interpreting it as a message aimed at President Bola Tinubu ahead of the 2027 election.

What he said

In the clip, posted on Thursday, 19 November 2026, the cleric said a leader who is not wanted by the people has no business seeking or holding power. He argued that leadership should come from the genuine wishes of the governed, not from money or manipulation.

He questioned why anyone would spend money to impose themselves on people who do not want them, and said such people should not contest at all.

Strong warning

He is also reported to have issued a warning to those he described as the architects of terrorism in Nigeria.

Reactions

The clip spread quickly online. While Eneche did not name anyone in the portion widely shared, many viewers linked his remarks to the President. No response from the Presidency was included in the reports.

ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has challenged President Bola Tinubu to release the full, unedited recording of a recent interview at the Presidential Villa.

The claims

In a statement issued on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, through his campaign's director of strategic communication, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku raised questions about whether the interview was funded through a $4.5 million foreign lobbying contract paid on Nigeria's behalf.

The former vice president called for the unedited recording to be made public and for the government to explain the lobbying arrangement.

US lobbyist Diana London has since deleted posts on X about her meeting with President Tinubu. State police bill moves to Tinubu's table after all 36 state assemblies approve it

18 Nov 2026, 14:51 GMT

The Senate has authorised its Clerk to send the constitutional amendment creating state police to President Bola Tinubu for assent, after all 36 state Houses of Assembly approved it. But state police will not start immediately: a separate National Policing Bill is still needed.

Read more

Nigeria has moved a step closer to state police. The Senate has authorised its Clerk to transmit the constitutional amendment establishing state police to President Bola Tinubu for his assent.

All 36 states on board

All 36 state Houses of Assembly have approved the proposal, completing the state-legislature stage of the constitutional amendment process.

The bill was originally sent to the National Assembly by President Tinubu himself, as part of efforts to tackle the country's worsening insecurity, so his assent is widely expected.

Not an overnight change

However, state police will not begin operating immediately once the President signs the amendment.

According to the Senate, a separate National Policing Bill is still needed to settle the key details: how state police will be funded, how officers will be recruited, who will command them and who will oversee them.

Why it matters

For years, many Nigerians, especially in states hit hard by banditry, kidnapping and communal violence, have argued that a single, centrally controlled police force is too stretched to protect every community.

Supporters say state police will bring security closer to the people, with officers who know the local terrain, language and culture.

Critics worry that governors could use state police against political opponents, journalists and critics, particularly at election time, and that poorer states may struggle to fund them properly.

The real test

Nigeria is now closer to decentralised policing, but the most important safeguards, the rules that will decide whether state police protect citizens or serve politicians, are still to be written.
