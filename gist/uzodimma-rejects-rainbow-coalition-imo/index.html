Uzodimma vs Wike? Imo governor rejects Rainbow Coalition, threatens to take back vehicles from APC members who work against the party

28 Oct 2026, 17:26 GMT

Political tension appears to be brewing in Imo State as Governor Hope Uzodimma sends a strong warning to members of the All Progressives Congress (APC), declaring that there is no room for a Rainbow Coalition in the state.

In remarks attributed to the governor, Uzodimma warned APC members against working outside the party's political structure, insisting that loyalty to the APC must remain their priority.

The governor reportedly went further by threatening to withdraw vehicles donated by Niger Delta leader High Chief Government Ekpemupolo, popularly known as Tompolo, from beneficiaries found supporting political arrangements outside the APC.

"No Rainbow Coalition in Imo State. If I see you work outside the APC party, I'll take the vehicles from you," Uzodimma was quoted as saying.

The governor also expressed appreciation to Tompolo for his generosity and pledged his support whenever the Niger Delta leader needed assistance in Imo State.

"I am very grateful to High Chief Tompolo. Anytime you need us in Imo State, let us know and we'll be there for you," he reportedly added.

Is Uzodimma drawing a political battle line against Wike?

The governor's reported declaration has raised questions about the political influence of the Minister of the Federal Capital Territory, Nyesom Wike, whose name has become associated with cross-party political alliances.

Wike, although a member of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP), serves in President Bola Tinubu's APC-led administration and has maintained political relationships across party lines. His political approach has often involved cooperation between politicians belonging to different parties, particularly in Rivers State.

However, Uzodimma's reported warning suggests that he is unwilling to accommodate a similar arrangement within the Imo APC.

Although the remarks could be interpreted as a message to Wike's political allies, they do not, on their own, establish that the two politicians are in a direct confrontation.

Questions over Tompolo's donated vehicles

Another interesting aspect of the governor's reported statement is his warning about withdrawing vehicles donated by Tompolo.

The remarks raise questions about whether the vehicles were intended as unconditional gifts or whether their continued use is tied to political loyalty. It also remains unclear what authority would govern the withdrawal of the vehicles from beneficiaries.

What does this mean for Imo politics?

With political calculations already taking shape ahead of the 2027 general elections, Uzodimma's reported position could signal an attempt to strengthen party discipline and prevent competing political alliances from gaining influence within the state.

For now, the bigger question is whether the governor's declaration is simply an internal warning to APC members or the beginning of a wider struggle over political influence.

One thing is certain: if these remarks are accurately reported, Governor Hope Uzodimma has made his position clear. He expects APC members in Imo State to remain loyal to the party.

But should vehicles donated to politicians be withdrawn simply because the beneficiaries choose to support a different political alliance? And could this be the beginning of a political disagreement between Uzodimma and Wike ahead of 2027?

Judgment details: why the court sacked the Mark-led ADC leadership

14 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The certified copy of the judgment that voided the David Mark-led ADC leadership shows the judge found a key resignation letter was fabricated and ruled that the party's executive committee could not appoint national officers without an election.

Read more

Details of the Federal High Court judgment that nullified the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) have emerged from the certified true copy of the ruling.

The resignation letter

Justice Peter Lifu, who delivered the judgment on 5 November 2026, examined three main issues. The first was a letter said to show that the party's deputy national chairman, Nafiu Bala Gombe, had resigned in May 2025, before the meeting that installed the new leadership. After comparing signatures, the judge found clear differences, described the letter as fictitious or fabricated and rejected it as evidence.

Powers of the executive committee

The ADC argued that its constitution allowed the National Executive Committee to dissolve the old leadership and appoint a new one. The judge accepted that parties may run their internal affairs, but held that this cannot override the Constitution and the Electoral Act, which require parties to elect their principal officers through periodic democratic elections. He ruled that the committee's administrative powers did not allow it to appoint national executives in place of an election.

No proof of a vote

The judge also found that the party could not show that members or delegates had been given the chance to vote for candidates of their choice, and said notifying INEC of a meeting was not the same as complying with the law.

Background

The judgment also voided the primary that produced Atiku Abubakar as the ADC's presidential candidate. The party has appealed, and INEC says it will obey the final court decision. Atiku and his running mate Rotimi Amaechi are continuing to campaign.

Atiku says nearly 40% of bread makers have shut down under Tinubu

14 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Campaigning in Bauchi, ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar said the Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria has lost close to 40 per cent of its members in two years, blaming soaring energy and financing costs under President Tinubu.

Read more

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has accused the Tinubu administration of pushing Nigeria's baking industry to the brink.

What he said

In a statement released in Bauchi on Saturday, 14 November 2026, by Phrank Shaibu, director of strategic communication for his campaign, Atiku said the Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria had lost close to 40 per cent of its members in two years. He was in the state as part of what his campaign calls its 'affordable life' tour.

Rising costs

According to the statement, energy accounts for roughly half of most bakeries' production costs. It said one operator's monthly diesel bill had risen from ₦9 million to ₦19 million, and that switching to alternative power could cost a small bakery ₦40 million to ₦50 million, money most cannot raise. The statement blamed high energy prices and expensive financing for the closures.

Bakers confirm

The Premium Breadmakers Association of Nigeria itself said at an exhibition in Lagos that about 40 per cent of its members had shut down in the past two years because of rising energy costs, expensive loans and economic conditions. Its president, Emmanuel Onuorah, called on the federal government to provide grants and single-digit interest loans to businesses in the real sector, warning that more closures could threaten jobs.

Background

Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has made the cost of living a central theme of his campaign. The government says its reforms, including the removal of fuel subsidy, were necessary to stabilise the economy and free up money for infrastructure. The Presidency had not responded to the statement at the time of writing. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
