Uzodimma vs Wike? Imo governor rejects Rainbow Coalition, threatens to take back vehicles from APC members who work against the party

28 Oct 2026, 17:26 GMT

Political tension appears to be brewing in Imo State as Governor Hope Uzodimma sends a strong warning to members of the All Progressives Congress (APC), declaring that there is no room for a Rainbow Coalition in the state.

In remarks attributed to the governor, Uzodimma warned APC members against working outside the party's political structure, insisting that loyalty to the APC must remain their priority.

The governor reportedly went further by threatening to withdraw vehicles donated by Niger Delta leader High Chief Government Ekpemupolo, popularly known as Tompolo, from beneficiaries found supporting political arrangements outside the APC.

"No Rainbow Coalition in Imo State. If I see you work outside the APC party, I'll take the vehicles from you," Uzodimma was quoted as saying.

The governor also expressed appreciation to Tompolo for his generosity and pledged his support whenever the Niger Delta leader needed assistance in Imo State.

"I am very grateful to High Chief Tompolo. Anytime you need us in Imo State, let us know and we'll be there for you," he reportedly added.

Is Uzodimma drawing a political battle line against Wike?

The governor's reported declaration has raised questions about the political influence of the Minister of the Federal Capital Territory, Nyesom Wike, whose name has become associated with cross-party political alliances.

Wike, although a member of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP), serves in President Bola Tinubu's APC-led administration and has maintained political relationships across party lines. His political approach has often involved cooperation between politicians belonging to different parties, particularly in Rivers State.

However, Uzodimma's reported warning suggests that he is unwilling to accommodate a similar arrangement within the Imo APC.

Although the remarks could be interpreted as a message to Wike's political allies, they do not, on their own, establish that the two politicians are in a direct confrontation.

Questions over Tompolo's donated vehicles

Another interesting aspect of the governor's reported statement is his warning about withdrawing vehicles donated by Tompolo.

The remarks raise questions about whether the vehicles were intended as unconditional gifts or whether their continued use is tied to political loyalty. It also remains unclear what authority would govern the withdrawal of the vehicles from beneficiaries.

What does this mean for Imo politics?

With political calculations already taking shape ahead of the 2027 general elections, Uzodimma's reported position could signal an attempt to strengthen party discipline and prevent competing political alliances from gaining influence within the state.

For now, the bigger question is whether the governor's declaration is simply an internal warning to APC members or the beginning of a wider struggle over political influence.

One thing is certain: if these remarks are accurately reported, Governor Hope Uzodimma has made his position clear. He expects APC members in Imo State to remain loyal to the party.

But should vehicles donated to politicians be withdrawn simply because the beneficiaries choose to support a different political alliance? And could this be the beginning of a political disagreement between Uzodimma and Wike ahead of 2027?

El-Rufai tells supporters to vote ADC at every level in 2027

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Former Kaduna governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly directed his supporters to back the African Democratic Congress in all elections in 2027. The message was delivered by an ally at a coalition meeting in Kaduna.

Read more

Former Kaduna State governor Nasir El-Rufai has instructed his supporters to vote for the African Democratic Congress (ADC) at every level in the 2027 elections, according to a close ally.

The message

Hajiya Hafsat Baba, the ADC's national vice-chairman for the North-West, reportedly conveyed the message at a meeting in Kaduna that brought together various El-Rufai support groups. She said the directive had been verified by El-Rufai himself and covered federal, state and local elections. The meeting was held to unite the groups and coordinate their activities ahead of the polls.

'Base still strong'

Another ally, Ibrahim Hussaini, rejected claims that El-Rufai had lost his political base, saying his loyalists remain strong across Kaduna State and the wider North.

Background

El-Rufai served two terms as governor of Kaduna from 2015 to 2023 as a member of the APC and was a key figure in President Bola Tinubu's 2023 campaign. He later fell out with the party and joined the opposition coalition that adopted the ADC, which has fielded former vice-president Atiku Abubakar as its presidential candidate. His relationship with his successor, Governor Uba Sani of the APC, has been tense. El-Rufai has also been involved in legal disputes in recent months and has appealed for a fair trial.

Why it matters

Kaduna is one of the most populous states in the North-West, a region with a large share of Nigeria's voters. The APC says it remains strong in the state, while the ADC hopes El-Rufai's support will help it make gains there.

Soludo says his family and Obi's are linked as godparents despite political rift

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Anambra governor Charles Soludo has reportedly said Peter Obi's wife is godmother to one of his children, and his own wife is confirmation godmother to Obi's daughter. He said their political differences have not broken their friendship.

Read more

Anambra State governor Charles Soludo has spoken about his close family ties with former governor and NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, despite their recent political differences.

What he said

In an interview with a BBC Igbo-language service on Monday, 2 November 2026, Soludo reportedly said that Obi's wife, Margaret, is the godmother of one of his children, and that his own wife, Nonye, is the confirmation godmother of Obi's daughter. He described Obi as a close friend and said no political disagreement had been enough to break the bond between their families.

Their differences

Soludo said the two men had disagreed on political matters, mainly over who should govern Anambra State. Their relationship became a subject of public debate after a dispute in September, and Soludo has said that his party, APGA, would not automatically support Obi's presidential bid. Soludo has also said he backs President Bola Tinubu for 2027.

Background

Obi governed Anambra from 2006 to 2014 as a member of APGA, the same party on which Soludo was elected in 2021. Both men are among the most prominent politicians in the South East. Soludo's son, Ozonna, recently released a song supporting the NDC, which drew attention because of his father's backing for Tinubu.

How people reacted

Many people welcomed the comments as a sign that politics should not destroy friendships, while others said the two men's public differences were more important for voters.

Atiku accuses DSS of plotting to disrupt ADC rally as Sokoto seals party office

3 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has alleged that the DSS is recruiting protesters to disrupt his party's rally in Sokoto. Separately, the Sokoto government sealed an ADC campaign office, saying the building's ownership was unclear and citing a disturbance.

Read more

Tension is rising in Sokoto State ahead of a planned campaign rally by the African Democratic Congress (ADC), with presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar accusing the Department of State Services (DSS) of plotting to disrupt it.

Atiku's allegation

In a statement on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Atiku alleged that the leadership of the DSS was recruiting people to stage protests in Sokoto in order to create disorder. He claimed hired protesters were being gathered at an office on Koko Road and sent out in trucks to key locations in the city. He also said public facilities had been blocked and logistical support withheld to frustrate the ADC's campaign, and warned that security officials who help suppress political freedom would be held to account under the law. He said the rally would go ahead. The DSS had not responded publicly to the claims at the time of reporting.

Campaign office sealed

Separately, the Sokoto State Government sealed an ADC campaign office on Ahmadu Bello Way on Monday, 2 November. Commissioner for Land and Housing Nasiru Dantsoho said records showed the building, once used by the defunct Savannah Bank and previously by the PDP, still belonged to the bank, and that the state had written to the Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation to clarify its ownership. He said the premises were sealed after a disturbance involving party supporters and others, to prevent further trouble. A media aide to the ADC governorship candidate confirmed the closure.

Background

Sokoto is governed by the APC. Opposition parties have complained of attacks and obstacles to their campaigns in several states, and an ADC candidate's convoy was attacked in Sokoto in recent weeks. The APC and state governments have generally denied using state agencies against opponents.

What happens next

Security agencies are expected to be on alert as the ADC presses ahead with its rally.

Rufai Oseni challenges Umahi to win six million votes before comparing himself to Obi

3 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

TV presenter Rufai Oseni has reportedly hit back at Works Minister Dave Umahi, who called Peter Obi a 'nobody' and described himself as the Lion of the South East. Oseni said Umahi should first run for president and win at least six million votes.

Read more

Television presenter Rufai Oseni has responded sharply to Minister of Works Dave Umahi's recent comments about NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi.

What Umahi said

At an APC town hall in Abakaliki, Ebonyi State, on Monday, 2 November 2026, Umahi reportedly questioned Obi's political relevance, described him as a 'nobody' and called himself the 'Lion of the South East'. He has also asked what makes Obi better than past South East leaders such as Sam Egwu and Anyim Pius Anyim, and has argued that the region should back President Bola Tinubu so that an Igbo candidate can later get eight years rather than four.

Oseni's response

Speaking on his breakfast television programme on Tuesday, 3 November, Oseni reportedly said self-given titles mean little without results. He said that before Umahi can compare himself with Obi, he should obtain a presidential nomination form, contest a general election and win at least six million votes, roughly the number Obi received in the 2023 presidential election.

Background

Umahi, a former two-term governor of Ebonyi State, is a leading APC figure in the South East and has campaigned hard for Tinubu's re-election. Obi, a former Anambra governor, came third in the 2023 presidential election as the Labour Party candidate and is now running on the NDC ticket with Rabiu Kwankwaso. The South East is seen as an important battleground in 2027, and four governors from the zone recently declared support for Tinubu.

How people reacted

Obi's supporters cheered Oseni's comments, while APC supporters said Umahi's record as governor and minister speaks for itself.
