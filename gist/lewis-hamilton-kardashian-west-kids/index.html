Paraguay's President Santiago Peña resigns over bank collapse scandal

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned after the lower house voted to begin impeachment proceedings over the collapse of a bank he was once linked to as a shareholder. He apologised to Paraguayans.

Read more

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned amid a political crisis caused by the collapse of a bank he was previously linked to.

What happened

In a video message posted on X, Peña said he was stepping down to protect the country's stability and democratic institutions, a day after opposition lawmakers requested his impeachment and the lower house of Congress voted to begin proceedings, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. He apologised to the people if he had failed them at any point, saying it was never his intention to betray their trust.

The bank collapse

The crisis began when Ueno Bank collapsed and froze its operations, reportedly leaving large numbers of customers unable to access their accounts. Lawmakers accused Peña of conflicts of interest, pointing to his past shareholding in the company that owned the bank.

Background

Peña, an economist and former finance minister, took office in August 2023 as a member of the long-dominant Colorado Party. Under Paraguay's constitution, the vice-president takes over when a president leaves office before the end of the term.

What happens next

Attention now turns to who will lead the country for the rest of the term, and to investigations into the bank's collapse and whether depositors will recover their money.

Spain's transport minister resigns after deadly train crashes

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Spain's Transport Minister Oscar Puente has announced his resignation after two major train crashes in less than a year, including an October collision south of Madrid that killed 69 people. He said staying on would be inconsistent with political dignity.

Read more

Spain's Transport Minister, Oscar Puente, has announced that he will resign following two serious train crashes in less than a year.

What he said

Puente told lawmakers on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, that a transport minister in a country like Spain could not stay in office after two accidents of such severity, regardless of whether he was directly responsible. He described the decision as a matter of political dignity, consistency and empathy, and said he had decided to step down within minutes of the latest crash but stayed on to manage the aftermath and introduce safety measures. He did not say exactly when he would leave office, and confirmed he would not stand in Spain's early general election on 29 November.

The latest crash

The resignation came six days after a crash on 28 October near Urda, south of Madrid, in which a high-speed train run by the state operator Renfe hit the back of a stationary train run by a private operator on the Madrid–Andalusia line. The crash reportedly killed 69 people and injured more than 200. Authorities have blamed a combination of human and computer failures, and Puente said a red signal should have stopped the train.

Background

Spain has one of Europe's largest high-speed rail networks. The two crashes have caused public anger over rail safety.

What happens next

Investigations into both crashes are continuing.

"I would have been worried if Amaechi stood a chance in Rivers State": Wike fires back at political rivals

6 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike has dismissed Rotimi Amaechi's chances in Rivers State, insisting he remains a major force there even though he is no longer governor. He also claimed the APC barely existed in Rivers before he backed it, and that he has "killed the Obi wave" in the FCT.

Read more

The Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, has once again spoken boldly about his political influence in Rivers State, dismissing the chances of former Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi and insisting he remains a major force in the state.

"You cannot wish me away"

According to Wike, he would only have reason to worry if Amaechi stood a realistic chance of a significant political comeback in Rivers State.

The former Rivers governor made it clear that leaving Government House in Port Harcourt has not reduced his influence or weakened the structures he built over the years.

"I am not a governor, I am a minister, but you cannot wish me away in Rivers State, because the people know what I stand for and because of the people I have raised," Wike said.

His statement appears to be a direct message to those who believe his appointment as FCT Minister has taken him away from the centre of Rivers politics.

Claims about the APC

Wike also claimed that the All Progressives Congress (APC) had little political strength in Rivers State before he decided to support the party.

"Before I decided to support the APC, the party did not exist in Rivers State," he said.

That is a bold claim, given the long rivalry between Wike and Amaechi, who both served as governors of Rivers State at different times.

"I have killed the Obi wave"

Wike did not limit his remarks to Rivers. He also turned his attention to the movement around NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, claiming he had already weakened its influence in the FCT.

"As for the NDC, I have killed the Obi wave in the FCT," he said.

The remarks reflect Wike's confidence in his political strength, although his claims about the APC and about Obi's support remain his own assessment.

The road to 2027

With the 2027 general elections approaching, the political battle in Rivers State is expected to draw even more attention. Wike, Amaechi and other influential figures may once again find themselves on opposing sides as parties prepare for another contest.

For now, Wike is making one thing clear to his opponents: he may no longer be governor, but he has no intention of surrendering his influence in Rivers State.

"If not for VeryDarkMan, I wouldn't be where I am today": Ratel Princess Jojooflele expresses gratitude from China

5 Nov 2026, 21:44 GMT

Social media personality Jojooflele, known as the Ratel Princess, has publicly thanked VeryDarkMan for changing her life, saying her following and opportunities grew after she met him. She spoke from China, a long way from the Nedu controversy that first brought her into the spotlight.

Read more

Popular social media personality Jojooflele, widely known as the Ratel Princess, has openly expressed her appreciation for VeryDarkMan (VDM), acknowledging the role he has played in changing her life.

"If not for VDM"

Speaking from China, Jojooflele reflected on how far she has come and how her association with VDM opened doors she probably never imagined would open so soon.

"If not for VDM, I wouldn't be where I am today," she said.

According to her, things took a completely different turn after she met VeryDarkMan. Her social media pages began attracting more attention, her followers increased, and opportunities started coming her way.

How it all started

What makes her story even more interesting is how it began. Many will remember the controversy involving Nedu, when Jojooflele found herself at the centre of public attention, with people discussing her personal life and questioning her actions.

For someone else, that kind of controversy could have been enough to damage their confidence or push them off social media completely.

Instead, Jojooflele turned the attention into something positive. Rather than letting the controversy define her future, she kept building her presence online and eventually became a familiar face among supporters of the Ratel Movement.

A lesson in gratitude

Today, she is in China, reflecting on her journey and publicly appreciating someone she believes contributed greatly to her success.

Jojooflele deserves credit for how she has handled her growing popularity. The right connections can open doors, but what you do with those opportunities matters just as much.

Her appreciation for VDM is also worth acknowledging. In a society where people sometimes forget those who supported them once they start to succeed, publicly recognising someone's contribution says a lot about gratitude.

Setbacks are not the end

Perhaps the biggest lesson here is never to be too quick to conclude that your present difficulties will decide your future. The situation people expect to destroy you can end up introducing you to opportunities you never knew existed.

What looks like a setback today may become part of the story you proudly tell tomorrow.
