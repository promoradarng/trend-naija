Fountain of Life trustees name interim leader, insist Jimmy Odukoya is out as senior pastor

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

There is confusion at The Fountain of Life Church in Ilupeju, Lagos, after an announcement that Jimmy Odukoya had been removed as senior pastor was quickly rejected by the church and by Odukoya himself.

The removal announcement

On Tuesday, 17 November 2026, a statement presented as coming from the church's board of trustees said Jimmy's tenure did not reflect the succession wishes of his late father and the church's founder, Taiwo Odukoya, who died in 2023. It said the board had reviewed the church's constitution and recordings of the founder's wishes, and that Jimmy was no longer recognised as senior pastor with immediate effect. The statement described the decision as a governance matter and not a personal judgement.

The church's response

Within hours, a statement posted on the church's official Instagram page and on Jimmy Odukoya's page rejected the announcement as having no constitutional weight. It said the announcement did not come from a properly constituted meeting of the Board of Trustees, but from a faction calling itself "Concerned Members of the Board".

The church said the individuals behind the faction had been formally asked to step down from the board on 14 March 2026 over what it described as repeated violations of the church's constitution and founding principles, and that the letter had been delivered to and acknowledged by them.

The founder's video

Later on Tuesday, a video said to have been recorded by the late Taiwo Odukoya in December 2021 emerged, with a transcript released by the board of trustees. In it, he reportedly named Pastor Kunle Areogun as the person who should lead the church if he died or was unable to continue.

He is said to have stated that Jimmy should serve as Areogun's immediate administrative assistant and lead the youth church. The video was reportedly recorded by Jimmy at his father's request and witnessed by his older brother, Pastor John Odukoya, and it has now become central to the dispute.

Interim leader named

Later on Tuesday, the board of trustees appointed Pastor Rotimi Okpaise as interim spiritual and administrative leader of the church. It said its resolution of 16 November removing Jimmy Odukoya remained in force and directed him to vacate the office and all its responsibilities. The trustees said Jimmy was present when his father's 2021 video was recorded.

Social media dispute

The trustees have also accused Jimmy Odukoya of taking control of the church's official social media accounts without approval and using them to share what they called misleading statements. They told members and the public to treat only statements signed on the board's behalf as authentic, and said they were taking steps to recover the accounts. Jimmy Odukoya had not responded to that claim in the reports.

Jimmy Odukoya speaks

On the evening of Wednesday, 18 November, Jimmy Odukoya addressed about 300 church workers at the church premises, giving his own account of the crisis for the first time. He said he had stayed quiet because he believed the matter could be settled privately, but felt he had to speak after the board released his late father's video to the media.

Background

Jimmy Odukoya, an actor and pastor, took over leadership of the church after his father's death. On 1 November he remarried and presented his new wife to the congregation. A video of the late founder said to show the role he intended for his son has also been circulating online amid the dispute.

What happens next

It is not yet clear how the leadership dispute will be resolved. The faction behind the removal announcement had not responded publicly to the church's statement in the reports.

  • Trustees named Pastor Rotimi Okpaise interim leader.
  • They told Jimmy Odukoya to vacate the office.
  • A statement on the church's page had called the removal invalid.

Cooking gas prices rise; refilling 12.5kg now costs up to ₦22,500

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Cooking gas has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kg to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800 depending on location. Some households are cutting back or switching to other fuels ahead of Christmas.

Read more

Nigerian households are facing fresh pressure as cooking gas prices rise across major cities ahead of the Christmas season.

The new prices

The price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kilogram to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800, depending on location. That means refilling a 12.5kg cylinder can now cost as much as ₦22,500, up from about ₦17,500.

How households are coping

Market findings suggest some Nigerians are now buying smaller quantities, cutting down on how much gas they use or switching to other cooking fuels.

FG to release stored rice and maize to bring down food prices

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Agriculture Minister Abubakar Kyari says the government will release rice, maize and other grains from its stocks into the market to cut food prices. It also plans to distribute three million bags of fertiliser within a month.

Read more

The federal government has announced plans to release stored rice, maize and other grains into the market in a bid to bring down food prices.

What was announced

Minister of Agriculture and Food Security Abubakar Kyari disclosed the plan on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, at the presentation of the 2026 Wet Season Agricultural Performance Survey Report in Abuja.

He said grains bought under the Bank of Agriculture's Guaranteed Minimum Price programme would soon be released to the market.

Fertiliser for farmers

The government also plans to distribute three million bags of fertiliser to farmers within the next month to boost production and improve food supply across the country.

Background

The Guaranteed Minimum Price scheme gives farmers an assured buyer for their produce.

Naira weakens to ₦1,363 per dollar in official market

19 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The naira lost ₦7.43 against the dollar on Wednesday to close at ₦1,363.23 in the official market, and fell to ₦1,430 on the parallel market. Analysts point to reduced dollar inflows and rising demand.

Read more

The naira weakened against the US dollar in both the official and parallel foreign exchange markets on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, amid reduced foreign exchange inflows and rising demand for dollars.

Official market

Data from the Nigerian Autonomous Foreign Exchange Market (NAFEM) showed the naira fell by ₦7.43, or 0.54%, to close at ₦1,363.23 to the dollar, from ₦1,355.80 the previous day. It also lost ground against the euro, closing at ₦1,495.60, but gained slightly against the British pound to ₦1,795.24.

Parallel market

On the parallel market, the naira slipped by ₦10 to trade at ₦1,430 to the dollar, while at one major bank's foreign exchange desk it closed at ₦1,368.

Why it matters

The fall comes a day after the naira lost about ₦25 against the dollar, and as the Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC move to boost foreign exchange inflows.

FG selects eight startups for over ₦1 billion iDICE funding

19 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The federal government has named eight Nigerian startups to receive about ₦134 million each in equity investment under the iDICE programme. They will also get mentorship and business support.

Read more

The federal government has unveiled eight Nigerian startups selected to receive about ₦134 million each under the Investment in Digital and Creative Enterprises (iDICE) programme.

The funding

The companies will share a combined investment of more than ₦1 billion through Growth Lab, the second phase of the iDICE Startup Bridge initiative. Each startup will receive $100,000 in equity investment, along with business development support, mentorship and access to industry experts.

The aim

The programme is designed to help Nigerian entrepreneurs grow their businesses, create jobs and compete more effectively in the digital and creative economy, and to help the startups attract further investment.
