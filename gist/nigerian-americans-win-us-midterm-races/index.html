Four Nigerian-Americans win seats in US midterm elections

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Nigerian-Americans have recorded a string of wins in the United States midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, according to reports.

Who won

Four Nigerian-Americans reportedly won state and local offices in Maryland, Texas, Washington DC and Pennsylvania. In Maryland, Democrat Will Jawando, whose father is Nigerian, defeated Republican Esther Wells to become Montgomery County executive, winning about 78 per cent of the vote with most ballots counted. He previously served on the Montgomery County Council. In Texas, 27-year-old Democrat Junior Ezeonu won the District 101 seat in the state House of Representatives. Born in Onitsha, Anambra State, he moved to Texas at the age of two, was elected to the Grand Prairie City Council in 2021 and became the city's youngest mayor pro tempore. He had defeated the incumbent in the Democratic primary and faced no Republican opponent. In Washington DC, Democrat Oye Owolewa won an at-large seat on the District of Columbia Council.

Still counting

A fifth Nigerian-American, Georgia state Representative Segun Adeyina, was reportedly leading in his race, which had not yet been called.

Background

The wins came on a night when Democrats were projected to take control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate. Nigerian-Americans are one of the most highly educated immigrant groups in the United States, and a growing number have entered politics at local, state and national level in recent years.

How people reacted

Many Nigerians at home and abroad celebrated the results online, describing them as a proud moment for the diaspora.

  • Will Jawando won the Montgomery County executive race in Maryland.
  • Onitsha-born Junior Ezeonu won a Texas House seat.
  • Oye Owolewa won an at-large seat on the DC Council.

Ukraine strikes Crimean bridge, part of crossing collapses

18 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Ukraine has struck the bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea, collapsing part of the road-and-rail crossing and halting road traffic. Russia also hit two major bridges in Kyiv.

Read more

Ukraine has struck the bridge linking Russia to the annexed Crimean peninsula, destroying part of the road-and-rail crossing and halting road traffic, according to reports on Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

The strike

Ukraine's SBU security service said one of the bridge's metal arches and the road span beneath it had been completely destroyed. The defence ministry said the central arch and spans had collapsed and the rail section had suffered critical damage. Images released by the SBU showed rubble and a gap several dozen metres wide between sections of the bridge.

Zelensky's message

President Volodymyr Zelensky described the strike as unprecedented and said it was a response to Russian attacks on Ukraine's infrastructure. He said Ukraine had repeatedly offered steps towards de-escalation but had been rebuffed.

Russia's attacks

Russia also attacked two major bridges in Kyiv, disrupting travel and damaging infrastructure in the Ukrainian capital. Road traffic on the Crimean bridge was suspended, and Russian-installed officials in Crimea urged residents not to panic.

Lagos-bound British Airways plane veers off taxiway at Heathrow

A British Airways Boeing 787 bound for Lagos reportedly veered off a taxiway at Heathrow, leaving its nose wheel stuck in the grass. The airline apologised to passengers for the disruption.

A British Airways Boeing 787 Dreamliner heading to Lagos reportedly veered off a taxiway at London's Heathrow Airport on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, leaving its nose wheel stuck in the grass.

What happened

The incident happened at about 10:10am as the aircraft was preparing for departure. Photos from the scene showed the plane's front wheel lodged in the grass beside the paved surface. No injuries were reported.

The airline's response

The incident disrupted the flight, and the airline had to make alternative arrangements for passengers. A British Airways spokesperson apologised to customers and said its teams were working to get them on their way as quickly as possible and were in touch with them.

What happens next

It was not immediately clear what caused the aircraft to leave the taxiway.

Cyberattack hits 178 major websites, targeting crypto and older iPhones

18 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Hackers reportedly exploited a flaw in website platform Optimizely to plant malicious code on sites belonging to 178 organisations. The code tried to steal cryptocurrency and personal data.

Read more

A cyberattack on the website optimisation platform Optimizely reportedly exposed visitors to websites run by 178 organisations to malicious code designed to steal cryptocurrency and personal information.

What happened

Security researcher Scott Helme said he identified the attack on Monday, 16 November 2026. The attackers are said to have exploited a weakness in Optimizely, a service businesses use to test and improve their websites, to insert harmful JavaScript into customer projects. The code could then reach visitors without them clicking any suspicious link.

Helme reportedly found 188 infected scripts linked to 178 organisations. Websites linked to well-known names including CNN, the BBC, Zoom, HSBC, IKEA, Salesforce and Equifax were among those said to be affected.

What the code did

Researchers said the code tried to swap cryptocurrency payment addresses for wallets controlled by the attackers, capture card details, steal browser cookies, take screenshots and target security weaknesses in older Apple devices.

US loses measles elimination status after more than a year of spread

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Pan American Health Organization says measles is no longer eliminated in the United States, after the virus spread continuously for over a year. More than 5,400 US cases have been recorded this year.

Read more

The United States has lost the measles elimination status it held since 2000, after a commission of the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) found that the virus had been spreading continuously in the country for more than a year.

The decision

PAHO announced the decision on Tuesday, 17 November 2026. The commission also found that measles was no longer eliminated in Mexico and Bolivia. PAHO's director, Dr Jarbas Barbosa, said it means the virus has circulated for a long time, putting families at risk of a preventable illness, hospitalisation and death.

The numbers

More than 5,400 measles cases have been recorded in the US this year, more than twice last year's total and the highest since 1991. State health departments have reported 10 deaths, including at least four babies too young for routine vaccination.

Reaction

A US public health leader, Dr Richard Besser, described the loss of elimination status as a distressing setback.
