Philanthropist Okwuluora vows to personally fund woman's medical treatment, says no fundraising needed

24 Oct 2026, 22:27 BST

Popular philanthropist Okwuluora says he will take full responsibility for the medical treatment of a woman whose condition has drawn wide public attention, paying all costs from his own pocket until she recovers. He plans to send her photograph to doctors at Parklane Hospital and the University of Nigeria Teaching Hospital (UNTH) in Enugu for assessment, then bring her and her family in for treatment once they approve. He said he first stepped back because some people around her appeared more interested in the money that could be raised than in her wellbeing, but he has now decided to go ahead without any public fundraising. Once she has recovered, he said, an account could be opened for well-wishers who want to support her.

  • Okwuluora said concerns about her safety made him reconsider his involvement before he decided to proceed.
  • Her case has also sparked a debate online, with some Nigerians asking why pastors who claim to perform healing miracles have not stepped forward to help her.
  • Others are asking why cases like hers so often depend on expensive treatment and public donations.

Court remands journalist Daniel Ojukwu in Abuja prison over cybercrime charges

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu has been remanded in prison in Abuja on a 12-count cybercrime charge linked to his reports about a presidential aide, pending a bail hearing. The decision has renewed concerns about press freedom.

Read more

A court in Abuja has ordered that investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu be held in prison pending a hearing on his bail application.

The charges

Ojukwu, who works for the Foundation for Investigative Journalism, was arraigned on a 12-count charge of allegedly breaching Nigeria's Cybercrimes Act, and the court ordered his remand on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The charges relate to reports he published about Adejoke Orelope-Adefulire, who was senior special assistant on the Sustainable Development Goals under former President Muhammadu Buhari and now holds the same role under President Bola Tinubu. The charges have not been tested at trial.

Press freedom concerns

The decision drew widespread criticism from media freedom advocates, who have long argued that the cybercrimes law is used to silence journalists. According to the Committee to Protect Journalists, at least 29 journalists have been prosecuted under the law since it was enacted in 2015. Nigeria is ranked 112th out of 180 countries in the 2026 World Press Freedom Index compiled by Reporters Without Borders.

Background

Ojukwu was previously detained by police for several days in 2024 over a report on the same official, a case that also drew national and international criticism.

FG reopens free CAC business registration for 250,000 small businesses

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The federal government has reopened its free business name registration programme through SMEDAN, targeting 250,000 nano, micro and small businesses, after earlier problems with the application portal.

Read more

The federal government has reopened its programme offering free registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) to small business owners.

The programme

The scheme, run through the Small and Medium Enterprises Development Agency of Nigeria (SMEDAN), targets 250,000 nano, micro and small enterprises across the country, according to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026. It covers the cost of business name registration, which is one of the barriers that keeps many small businesses informal.

Who can apply

The programme is aimed at owners of small businesses not yet registered with the CAC, including traders, artisans, retailers and service providers. Business owners already registered with SMEDAN but not with the CAC may also qualify, subject to the agency's requirements.

Why it reopened

The portal was reopened after applicants earlier reported difficulties accessing it, which disrupted the application process.

Background

The reopening comes as the government also rolls out a ₦50,000 cash grant to vulnerable households.

Tinubu launches ₦52,500 minimum monthly pension for 173,209 retirees

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

President Tinubu has activated a guaranteed minimum pension of ₦52,500 a month for 173,209 retirees under the Contributory Pension Scheme, a provision that has been in law since 2004 but was never enforced. He also launched free basic healthcare for low-income pensioners.

Read more

Retirees under Nigeria's Contributory Pension Scheme will now receive at least ₦52,500 a month, after President Bola Tinubu activated a minimum pension guarantee in Abuja.

What was announced

At the Presidential Pension Security Initiatives ceremony at the State House on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the President, represented by Senate President Godswill Akpabio, declared the guarantee active for 173,209 qualifying retirees. He described it as a floor beneath Nigerian retirees and urged future governments to raise it but never remove it. The Director-General of the National Pension Commission (PenCom), Omolola Oloworaran, confirmed the figure and said more than 65 per cent of private-sector retirees currently earn less than the new minimum.

Who benefits

According to PenCom, 54 per cent of the qualifying retirees come from the private sector, 34 per cent from federal service and 12 per cent from state employment. More than ₦400bn in the Pension Protection Fund will finance the guarantee.

Healthcare and arrears

The President also launched PenCare, a healthcare plan that will give low-income pensioners free access to essential health services, with access cards to be distributed in the coming months. He said the government had issued a ₦758bn bond to clear pension arrears and approved a ₦32,000 monthly adjustment for affected federal pensioners.

Background

The minimum pension was first written into the Pension Reform Act of 2004 and kept in the 2014 law, but no government had put it into effect until now.
