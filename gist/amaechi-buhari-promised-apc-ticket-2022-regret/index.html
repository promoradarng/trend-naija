"Buhari promised me APC presidential ticket in 2022, I regret listening to him": Rotimi Amaechi

8 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

Former Rivers State Governor and ex-Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi has reportedly expressed regret over his decision to trust former President Muhammadu Buhari during the race for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential ticket in 2022.

The promise

According to the statement attributed to Amaechi, Buhari had promised him the party's presidential ticket, a promise he now regrets believing.

The claim raises fresh questions about the political arrangements and behind-the-scenes discussions that took place before the APC primary that eventually produced Bola Ahmed Tinubu as the party's candidate.

Buhari died in July 2025, so he is no longer able to respond to Amaechi's account.

How the 2022 race played out

Amaechi was among the prominent politicians who contested the ticket in 2022. Despite being a serving minister in Buhari's government and a close associate of the President, he lost the primary to Tinubu.

As the sitting President and leader of the ruling party at the time, Buhari's influence was considerable, although the final decision rested with the party's delegates.

Loyalty without reward

For Amaechi, the disappointment appears to go beyond losing an election. His comments suggest he believed he had received an assurance from someone whose word carried enormous political weight.

That points to one of the oldest lessons in Nigerian politics: loyalty does not always guarantee reward.

Amaechi served under Buhari for nearly seven years as Minister of Transportation, and he was one of the key figures who helped build the coalition that brought the APC to power in 2015. Yet when his chance came to seek the presidency, things did not turn out as he had apparently expected.

The questions that remain

Did Buhari actually make such a commitment, and if he did, what happened between that promise and the primary?

Was Amaechi politically outplayed, or did he place too much confidence in a promise that was never guaranteed?

Whatever the answer, his regret offers another glimpse into the complicated world of Nigerian party politics, where today's allies can have very different interests when power is at stake.

Former Kaduna State Governor Nasir El-Rufai has reportedly told a Federal High Court in Abuja that he never intercepted the telephone conversations of National Security Adviser Nuhu Ribadu, and never asked anyone to do so for him.

In court

El-Rufai opened his defence on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, before Justice Joyce Abdulmalik, after his no-case submission was dismissed in October. He also denied having any access to the NSA's communication networks or personal devices.

How the case began

The case arises from remarks El-Rufai made in a television interview on 13 February 2026. He denies the charges.

What happens next

The trial continues as El-Rufai presents his defence.

Report records 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in four months

26 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

A civic group says it has documented 124 incidents of election-related violence and 22 deaths across Nigeria since July. It warned the trend could worsen as the January 2027 elections approach.

Read more

A non-partisan civic organisation has warned of rising political violence in Nigeria, saying it recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in the past four months.

The figures

In a statement in Abuja on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, the Kimpact Development Initiative (KDI) said the incidents were documented between 1 July and 23 November 2026 through its election violence monitoring project. Incidents rose from three in July to 48 in October, with another 40 recorded in the first half of November alone.

The warning

The group warned that the trend could worsen as the 16 January 2027 elections draw closer unless political parties, security agencies and other stakeholders take urgent action. It pointed to growing political intolerance and inflammatory rhetoric among politicians and their supporters as key triggers.

KDI stressed that the problem cuts across parties and should not be blamed on any single group, and urged candidates to restrain their supporters.

Background

The report comes days after deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states, and after INEC warned that the popular 'rig and die' slogan could endanger polling staff and voters. The House of Representatives has also warned that a state of emergency may be needed in Delta if violence continues.
