"Buhari promised me APC presidential ticket in 2022, I regret listening to him": Rotimi Amaechi

8 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

Former Rivers State Governor and ex-Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi has reportedly expressed regret over his decision to trust former President Muhammadu Buhari during the race for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential ticket in 2022.

The promise

According to the statement attributed to Amaechi, Buhari had promised him the party's presidential ticket, a promise he now regrets believing.

The claim raises fresh questions about the political arrangements and behind-the-scenes discussions that took place before the APC primary that eventually produced Bola Ahmed Tinubu as the party's candidate.

Buhari died in July 2025, so he is no longer able to respond to Amaechi's account.

How the 2022 race played out

Amaechi was among the prominent politicians who contested the ticket in 2022. Despite being a serving minister in Buhari's government and a close associate of the President, he lost the primary to Tinubu.

As the sitting President and leader of the ruling party at the time, Buhari's influence was considerable, although the final decision rested with the party's delegates.

Loyalty without reward

For Amaechi, the disappointment appears to go beyond losing an election. His comments suggest he believed he had received an assurance from someone whose word carried enormous political weight.

That points to one of the oldest lessons in Nigerian politics: loyalty does not always guarantee reward.

Amaechi served under Buhari for nearly seven years as Minister of Transportation, and he was one of the key figures who helped build the coalition that brought the APC to power in 2015. Yet when his chance came to seek the presidency, things did not turn out as he had apparently expected.

The questions that remain

Did Buhari actually make such a commitment, and if he did, what happened between that promise and the primary?

Was Amaechi politically outplayed, or did he place too much confidence in a promise that was never guaranteed?

Whatever the answer, his regret offers another glimpse into the complicated world of Nigerian party politics, where today's allies can have very different interests when power is at stake.

Court freezes $100m linked to Governors' Forum in Paris Club fees dispute

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

An Abuja High Court has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum, pending a dispute over legal and consultancy fees tied to Paris Club refunds. The order does not decide who owns the money.

Read more

A High Court of the Federal Capital Territory in Abuja has ordered the preservation of up to $100 million allegedly held by the Nigeria Governors' Forum (NGF), in a dispute over unpaid legal and consultancy fees connected to the Paris Club refunds.

The order

Justice O.C. Agbaza granted the interim freezing order on 19 November 2026, following an application by a lawyer against the NGF's incorporated trustees and 29 commercial banks. The order covers a single total sum of $100 million, about ₦135.6 billion, across the affected accounts.

What it means

The order preserves the disputed money until the court hears an application for an injunction or makes a further order. It does not decide who owns the funds.

The claim

The lawyer is seeking payment under an indemnity he says the NGF issued in December 2018, after the federal government paid $100 million to the forum to hold pending a decision on liability for professional fees. No response from the NGF was included in the reports.

Tinubu asks Sunday Igboho to deploy his security network in the South-West

23 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

President Tinubu reportedly asked activist Sunday Igboho to use his Iru Ekun Security Network to help protect lives and property in the South-West. The two met briefly in the president's car after the Ibadan rally.

President Bola Tinubu has reportedly asked Yoruba nation activist Sunday Adeyemo, popularly known as Sunday Igboho, to deploy his Iru Ekun Security Network to help protect lives and property across South-West Nigeria.

The meeting

According to a statement released on Monday, 23 November 2026, by Igboho's media office in Ibadan, the two met briefly inside the president's official car after the APC presidential campaign rally at Mapo Hall, Ibadan, on Saturday.

What was discussed

The meeting is said to have focused on the work of Iru Ekun, a community security outfit founded by Igboho, and how its members could help reduce insecurity in rural and urban areas of the region.

Background

Igboho is a well-known campaigner for Yoruba self-determination. The meeting took place on the day of Tinubu's campaign rally in Ibadan, where the president also met traditional rulers.

Nigeria's High Commissioner to South Africa, Femi Fani-Kayode, has dismissed reports on social media that the federal government had recalled or reprimanded him, describing them as completely unfounded.

What he said

Fani-Kayode made the clarification during a visit to Abuja, where he said he had been meeting officials at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs as part of routine diplomatic consultations. Nollywood legend Pete Edochie dies at 79

25 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Veteran actor Pete Edochie, famous for playing Okonkwo in Things Fall Apart, has died at the age of 79. His family says he died in Enugu early on Wednesday after a long battle with prostate cancer.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actor Pete Edochie has died at the age of 79, his family has announced.

The announcement

In a statement shared on Instagram by his son, Leo Pete Edochie, on behalf of the family, the family said the actor died at 12:30am on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, in Enugu, after a prolonged battle with prostate cancer. The statement, titled 'An Iroko Has Fallen', described him as a beloved husband, father, brother and kinsman.

He is survived by his wife, Josephine Edochie, his children, including actors Yul and Linc Edochie, his siblings and other relatives. The family said funeral arrangements would be announced in due course.

His life and career

Pete Edochie hailed from Akamnato, Ifite Nteje, in Anambra State. He began his career in broadcasting in 1967 before becoming one of Nigeria's best-known actors. His major breakthrough came in 1987, when he played Okonkwo in the Nigerian Television Authority's adaptation of Chinua Achebe's Things Fall Apart, a role that brought him recognition far beyond Nigeria.

Known for his commanding voice and powerful screen presence, he became famous for portraying traditional patriarchs and kings in countless Nollywood films. He was honoured with the national award of Member of the Order of the Niger (MON).

His final weeks

In October, during a visit to Ghana, he spoke openly about his declining health, saying walking had become difficult, and prayed that he would not become a burden to those around him. Just this week, actress Toyin Abraham and her crew knelt to welcome him on her film set, and he had spoken publicly about his son Yul days earlier.

Tributes

News of his death has drawn an outpouring of grief from fans, many of whom described it as the end of an era for Nigerian film.
