"I am not a drug addict, I don't smoke, drink or even party" – Blessing CEO breaks silence on drug claims

26 Oct 2026, 18:24 GMT

Popular Nigerian relationship therapist and social media personality Blessing CEO has strongly denied allegations of drug use, insisting that she has never been involved in smoking, drinking alcohol or taking illegal substances.

Blessing made the clarification during a recent interview following her release from custody, where she addressed several rumours and allegations that circulated on social media while she was detained.

During the interview, she was confronted with claims suggesting that she had been struggling with substance use and needed rehabilitation or professional help rather than business opportunities.

However, the controversial relationship therapist dismissed the allegations, describing them as false and misleading.

"I am not a drug addict. I do not smoke, drink, and I do not even party," she stated.

According to Blessing, many of the stories circulated about her on social media have little or no connection to her actual lifestyle.

She expressed frustration over what she described as the growing habit of internet users creating and spreading damaging narratives about public figures without verifying their claims.

Blessing maintained that being controversial or frequently discussed online should not give people the freedom to make unfounded accusations about her personal life.

Her remarks have once again drawn attention to the challenges celebrities and internet personalities face when rumours about their private lives spread across social media.

While Blessing has firmly rejected the allegations, the claims remain unsubstantiated, and no verified evidence of illegal drug use was presented in the account of the interview.

VeryDarkMan brings out cash counting machine after claiming record ₦90 million sales in one day

1 Nov 2026, 16:09 GMT

VeryDarkMan has brought out a cash counting machine to count money reportedly received from sales of his RATEL products, after saying the business made ₦90 million in a single day. He has also promised to make his bank statement public to clear any doubts.

Read more

Social media activist and businessman VeryDarkMan (VDM) has once again got people talking, this time after bringing out a cash counting machine to count money reportedly received from the sale of his RATEL products.

Photos show him and a colleague surrounded by bundles of naira notes, with an open suitcase full of cash, stacks of money on cartons of RATEL power stations and a counting machine beside them.

A record day

According to VDM, the business recorded about ₦90 million in sales in one day, the biggest single-day figure since the products were launched.

Ready to show proof

VDM knows how Nigerians think. Once you mention ₦90 million in one day, some people will immediately start asking questions, while others will simply refuse to believe it.

To clear the doubts, he has promised to go a step further and make his bank account statement public so people can see the transactions for themselves. He appeared confident about the figures and made it clear he was ready to back up his claims with evidence.

A fast-growing brand

What makes this development interesting is how quickly the RATEL business appears to be gaining attention. From importing products from China to demonstrating how they perform in public, VDM has continued to attract customers and supporters.

However, the ₦90 million figure has yet to be independently verified, and sales revenue should not be confused with profit.

Social media clout without financial growth is a wasted opportunity: what Daniel Regha can learn from VDM's RATEL

1 Nov 2026, 09:07 GMT

Daniel Regha has questioned VeryDarkMan's move from activism into business with his RATEL brand. But VDM's shift raises a bigger question for every Nigerian influencer: what are you building with all that attention?

Read more

Daniel Regha has weighed in on VeryDarkMan's growing RATEL business, writing on X that VDM had gone "from activist, to NGO business, to CEO" and "from criticizing business owners to joining the same businesses to make profit."

His post drew mixed reactions. One user replied that anyone who has not yet understood VDM is to be pitied, while another asked what VDM is supposed to do with the money he makes from social media: give it all away and own nothing because he is an activist?

Influence without a business

Sometimes one wonders whether Daniel Regha sees the business opportunities sitting right in front of him. For someone who commands so much attention on X, sparks conversations almost daily and has built a considerable following, you would expect him to have turned that influence into something financially rewarding by now.

Perhaps the fear of what people will say is holding him back.

You are only a tenant

Many influencers forget that they do not own these platforms. They are simply tenants. Facebook, X, TikTok and Instagram can suspend an account, restrict its reach or change their policies overnight, and years of gathering followers could suddenly amount to very little.

What VDM seems to understand

VeryDarkMan appears to understand something many influencers have yet to figure out: popularity has an expiry date. The same people cheering you today may move on to another personality tomorrow.

Instead of depending entirely on public attention, he is gradually converting his influence into a business. He is introducing products, building a customer base and creating something that could keep generating income long after the social media excitement fades. That is smart thinking.

People will always talk

Imagine spending years building an audience of hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of people, only to have nothing financially sustainable to show for it.

If you have the audience, build a business. Sell products, offer services, create something valuable and earn legitimate money from the influence you have worked hard to establish. If you sell something, people will criticise you. If you remain broke, those same people will still have something to say. The people whose opinions you fear will not come together to pay your rent, feed your family or settle your bills.

The real question

The advice to Daniel Regha and other social media personalities is simple: stop treating online popularity as a lifetime achievement, and find a way to turn that attention into lasting value. VDM may have his critics, but his decision to venture into business deserves recognition.

Because when the followers stop cheering and the cameras stop rolling, the real question will be: what did you build with all that influence?

What do you think? Should influencers concentrate on entertaining their followers, or should they use their popularity to build businesses?

VeryDarkMan's RATEL brand reportedly records ₦90 million in sales in one day

31 Oct 2026, 23:17 GMT

VeryDarkMan and his RATEL brand have reportedly made about ₦90 million in sales in a single day, after he shared bank alerts showing a stream of customer payments. If the figure holds, it would be one of the strongest signs yet that the social media critic is turning his influence into a serious business.

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) and his RATEL brand have reportedly recorded about ₦90 million in sales in just one day, a figure that has set social media talking about how far the activist-turned-businessman could go.

The claim followed a post showing VDM in RATEL Power branded safety gear, holding up his phone, alongside a screenshot of bank alerts. The alerts, all dated 31 October 2026, show several customer payments for RATEL products, including a 600-watt power station, with the account balance climbing above ₦83 million.

From critic to businessman

VDM first became widely known for his social media videos and for publicly challenging powerful figures. Over time he has moved into importing products from China, building his own brand and steadily establishing himself in business. RATEL now sells items ranging from power stations to sensor lights.

A marketing style built on openness

What stands out most is how he markets the brand. Rather than relying on traditional adverts, he brings his audience along with him. He shows them the products, explains how they work and tests them publicly, so followers are watching the business grow in real time.

Whether people like him or not, few can deny the attention he has drawn to RATEL.

Sales are not profit

It is worth remembering that ₦90 million in sales is not ₦90 million in profit. Running a business comes with stock costs, shipping, staff, risk and plenty of other challenges, and the figure itself has not been independently confirmed.

Still, if RATEL keeps up this momentum, many observers believe it could grow into a multibillion-naira business group. Ten years from now, people may look back at these early videos and remember exactly how it all started.

Why it matters

Nigeria needs more young people who can turn online influence into productive businesses that create jobs and last. VDM's journey, with its struggles, criticism, mistakes and determination, is playing out in full public view.

So the question is simple: do you believe RATEL can become one of Nigeria's biggest indigenous brands in the next five years?

President Tinubu seen kissing the sacred Black Stone during Umrah in Makkah

2 Nov 2026, 20:39 GMT

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has been seen kissing the Black Stone, known as Al-Hajar Al-Aswad, while performing Umrah in Makkah, Saudi Arabia. Photos show him at the eastern corner of the Kaaba, surrounded by security officials.

Read more

President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has been seen kissing the sacred Black Stone, known as Al-Hajar Al-Aswad, while performing Umrah in the holy city of Makkah, Saudi Arabia.

Photos from the visit show the President, dressed in white, leaning into the silver frame that holds the stone as Saudi security officers stand by. Another photo shows him with members of his delegation in the courtyard of the Grand Mosque.

About the Black Stone

The Black Stone sits at the eastern corner of the Kaaba, inside the Grand Mosque, and holds deep religious significance for Muslims around the world.

Kissing the stone is a practice associated with Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), and many pilgrims seek the chance to do so whenever they visit the holy site.

A spiritual journey

For Muslims, Umrah is a spiritual journey marked by prayer, worship and personal reflection.

The moment has also drawn attention to the religious side of Nigeria's leader as he performs one of Islam's important acts of worship.
