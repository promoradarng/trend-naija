"I am not a drug addict, I don't smoke, drink or even party" – Blessing CEO breaks silence on drug claims

26 Oct 2026, 18:24 GMT

Popular Nigerian relationship therapist and social media personality Blessing CEO has strongly denied allegations of drug use, insisting that she has never been involved in smoking, drinking alcohol or taking illegal substances.

Blessing made the clarification during a recent interview following her release from custody, where she addressed several rumours and allegations that circulated on social media while she was detained.

During the interview, she was confronted with claims suggesting that she had been struggling with substance use and needed rehabilitation or professional help rather than business opportunities.

However, the controversial relationship therapist dismissed the allegations, describing them as false and misleading.

"I am not a drug addict. I do not smoke, drink, and I do not even party," she stated.

According to Blessing, many of the stories circulated about her on social media have little or no connection to her actual lifestyle.

She expressed frustration over what she described as the growing habit of internet users creating and spreading damaging narratives about public figures without verifying their claims.

Blessing maintained that being controversial or frequently discussed online should not give people the freedom to make unfounded accusations about her personal life.

Her remarks have once again drawn attention to the challenges celebrities and internet personalities face when rumours about their private lives spread across social media.

While Blessing has firmly rejected the allegations, the claims remain unsubstantiated, and no verified evidence of illegal drug use was presented in the account of the interview.

Report records 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in four months

26 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

A civic group says it has documented 124 incidents of election-related violence and 22 deaths across Nigeria since July. It warned the trend could worsen as the January 2027 elections approach.

Read more

A non-partisan civic organisation has warned of rising political violence in Nigeria, saying it recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in the past four months.

The figures

In a statement in Abuja on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, the Kimpact Development Initiative (KDI) said the incidents were documented between 1 July and 23 November 2026 through its election violence monitoring project. Incidents rose from three in July to 48 in October, with another 40 recorded in the first half of November alone.

The warning

The group warned that the trend could worsen as the 16 January 2027 elections draw closer unless political parties, security agencies and other stakeholders take urgent action. It pointed to growing political intolerance and inflammatory rhetoric among politicians and their supporters as key triggers.

KDI stressed that the problem cuts across parties and should not be blamed on any single group, and urged candidates to restrain their supporters.

Background

The report comes days after deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states, and after INEC warned that the popular 'rig and die' slogan could endanger polling staff and voters. The House of Representatives has also warned that a state of emergency may be needed in Delta if violence continues.

Spectranet to shut down services in Ibadan and Port Harcourt

26 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

Internet provider Spectranet says it will end its broadband services in Ibadan and Port Harcourt after a six-month notice period, citing rising operating costs. It will focus on Lagos and Abuja.

Read more

Internet service provider Spectranet Limited has announced that it will discontinue its broadband services in Ibadan and Port Harcourt after a six-month notice period.

What it means for users

The company said it would keep its network running and continue to provide customer support in both cities until the notice period ends, giving subscribers time to arrange alternative connections.

Why

Spectranet said higher operating costs had made it increasingly difficult to maintain its service standards in the two cities. It plans to concentrate its resources in Lagos and Abuja, where it says it will work on improving network performance.
