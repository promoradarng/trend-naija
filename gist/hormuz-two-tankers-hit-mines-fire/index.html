Iran says two oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines in Strait of Hormuz

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps says two oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

What Iran said

In a statement carried by Iran's state news agency on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the Guards said one tanker struck a mine in the eastern part of the strait and the other elsewhere in the waterway, while trying to cross using routes not authorised by Tehran. The statement did not name the vessels or give their nationalities, and accused them of violations including switching off their navigation and tracking systems. There was no independent confirmation of the incidents or of any casualties.

Background

Iran has sharply restricted shipping through the strait since a war began in late February, requiring ships to follow routes it has approved. The United States responded with a naval blockade of Iranian ports and has helped tankers pass through the waterway, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil. Iran has reported several ships catching fire in the strait in recent weeks. The incident comes days after US forces struck an Iranian air defence site on Qeshm Island, an operation President Donald Trump described as minor.

Why it matters

Disruption in the Strait of Hormuz affects global oil supply and prices, which in turn affect Nigeria's oil revenue and the cost of imported fuel.

  • Two tankers reportedly hit mines in the strait.
  • Iran says they used unauthorised routes.
  • The vessels have not been named.

Pope Leo appoints Nigerian-born priest Benedict Koledoye as bishop in Wales

28 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Pope Leo XIV has reportedly appointed Nigerian-born Canon Benedict Oladipo Koledoye as auxiliary bishop of Cardiff-Menevia in Wales. He is said to be the first black Latin Rite priest to be made a bishop in England and Wales.

Read more

Pope Leo XIV has reportedly appointed Nigerian-born Canon Benedict Oladipo Koledoye as Auxiliary Bishop of the Archdiocese of Cardiff-Menevia in Wales.

A historic first

The appointment, announced on Saturday, 28 November 2026, by the Catholic Bishops' Conference of England and Wales, reportedly makes him the first black Latin Rite priest to be ordained a bishop in England and Wales.

Who he is

Koledoye, 55, was born on 21 April 1971 in Ado-Ekiti, Ekiti State, and has family roots in the Ilaje-Ese Odo area of Ondo State. He has served as a priest in South Wales for about 15 years.

His reaction

Koledoye said the news, which was delivered to him by the Apostolic Nuncio, took him by surprise. He thanked the Pope, acknowledged the weight of the role and said he looked forward to working with the Archbishop of Cardiff-Menevia, Mark O'Toole, who described the appointment as a moment of joy.

How Nigerians reacted

Video of the announcement has been trending among Nigerians online, with many celebrating the appointment as a proud moment.

Farage pledges to scrap UK graduate visa used by many Nigerian students

27 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage has said his party would abolish the UK's post-study Graduate visa if it wins power. The route lets international graduates stay for two years after their degree.

Read more

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage has pledged to abolish the UK's post-study Graduate visa route if his party wins power, a move that would affect many Nigerian students who hope to work in Britain after their degrees.

What he said

In a post on X, Farage argued that the route makes it harder for young Britons to find jobs, because it allows international graduates to stay for two years without a job offer and compete for entry-level roles. He said the Conservatives created the route and Labour kept it, and that Reform would end it.

About the visa

The Graduate visa, introduced in July 2021, lets international students who complete an eligible UK degree stay to work or look for work without employer sponsorship or minimum salary requirements.

Netflix removes Tyson Fury schizophrenia claim from documentary

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Netflix has removed references to schizophrenia from its Tyson Fury documentary after it emerged the boxer has not been medically diagnosed with the condition. The claim had come from his wife, Paris.

Read more

Netflix has removed references to schizophrenia from its upcoming documentary about boxer Tyson Fury, after it emerged that he has not been medically diagnosed with the condition.

What changed

In the original version of the 90-minute film, Fury's wife, Paris, said doctors had regarded him as a mild schizophrenic in the year after he beat Wladimir Klitschko in November 2015. Those references have now been taken out. The documentary premieres on Netflix on Friday, 27 November 2026.

Boxing board concerns

The British Boxing Board of Control, which is sanctioning Fury's fight with Anthony Joshua on 11 December, had raised concerns about his mental health after the original statements. Its rules bar boxers from fighting if they have certain psychological disorders.

Background

Fury struggled with depression, drug and alcohol problems after the Klitschko win and did not return to the ring for nearly three years. He has spoken openly about his mental health, including bipolar disorder. An earlier story on this site said he had been diagnosed with schizophrenia; that report has been corrected.

Cleared to fight

The British Boxing Board of Control has since cleared Fury to face Anthony Joshua, saying he passed all up-to-date medical checks. His wife, Paris, has retracted her claim, saying he was never diagnosed by any doctor.
