Iran says two oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines in Strait of Hormuz

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps says two oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

What Iran said

In a statement carried by Iran's state news agency on Sunday, 8 November 2026, the Guards said one tanker struck a mine in the eastern part of the strait and the other elsewhere in the waterway, while trying to cross using routes not authorised by Tehran. The statement did not name the vessels or give their nationalities, and accused them of violations including switching off their navigation and tracking systems. There was no independent confirmation of the incidents or of any casualties.

Background

Iran has sharply restricted shipping through the strait since a war began in late February, requiring ships to follow routes it has approved. The United States responded with a naval blockade of Iranian ports and has helped tankers pass through the waterway, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil. Iran has reported several ships catching fire in the strait in recent weeks. The incident comes days after US forces struck an Iranian air defence site on Qeshm Island, an operation President Donald Trump described as minor.

Why it matters

Disruption in the Strait of Hormuz affects global oil supply and prices, which in turn affect Nigeria's oil revenue and the cost of imported fuel.

  • Two tankers reportedly hit mines in the strait.
  • Iran says they used unauthorised routes.
  • The vessels have not been named.

Trump admits immigration crackdown was 'too tough' after midterm defeat

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

President Donald Trump has said his administration's immigration crackdown was too tough and hurt Republicans in the midterm elections, particularly the deportation of people with stable jobs whose only offence was breaking immigration rules.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has acknowledged that his administration's immigration crackdown went too far, in a notable change of tone after his party's heavy losses in the midterm elections.

What he said

In an interview broadcast by the CBS programme '60 Minutes' on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Trump defended his record on border security and on removing dangerous criminals, but said he thought the crackdown had been too tough. He said the administration had suffered politically by deporting people who had stable jobs and had only committed immigration violations, and that this had hurt Republican candidates at the ballot box.

The election

Exit polls showed immigration was the third most important issue for voters, after the economy and the state of democracy. In traditionally conservative states such as Texas, a shift among Latino voters towards the Democrats contributed to their gains in Congress, where they are projected to control both the House of Representatives and the Senate.

Background

Mass deportations became a defining policy of Trump's second term, drawing protests and legal challenges. Only days ago he promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of the deportation programme, to White House chief of staff, so it is unclear how far policy will change in practice. Groups of Nigerians have been among those deported, including six who arrived in Lagos last week.

Another Ghanaian found dead in USA as body of 24-year-old church musician Joseph Owusu is discovered dumped near trash can in New York

9 Nov 2026, 06:11 GMT

The body of Joseph Owusu, a 24-year-old Ghanaian church organist from the Bronx, was found in a large black bag left near a trash can on a Brooklyn sidewalk. CCTV footage shows two people taking the bag from a white car. Ghana's government has demanded justice.

Read more

The Ghanaian community in the United States has been thrown into mourning again following the disturbing discovery of the body of 24-year-old Joseph Owusu, a talented church musician who lived in the Bronx, New York.

Found in a bag on the sidewalk

Owusu's body was discovered inside a large black bag left near a trash can on a sidewalk in Brooklyn on 30 October 2026.

CCTV footage shows two unidentified people taking the bag out of a white car, leaving it by the roadside and driving away.

Who was Joseph Owusu?

The young man was the organist at the Ghana United Methodist Church in the Bronx. He was also studying computer science and worked as a caregiver for people living with disabilities.

A family demands answers

His devastated family is demanding justice as investigators work to establish how he died and who abandoned his body. Ghana's embassy in the United States has confirmed his death, and the Ghanaian government has called on US authorities to ensure those responsible are found and brought to justice.

Two young lives in days

The tragedy came just a day after another young Ghanaian, 15-year-old Javits Frimpong, was shot dead while walking to school in the Bronx.

Two young Ghanaians, two heartbreaking deaths in New York within days. Both families are now waiting for answers.

Suspected thieves face public humiliation in South Africa as viral video sparks reactions

9 Nov 2026, 00:56 GMT

A group of suspected thieves in South Africa has become the talk of social media after a video showed them lying face down in a line by the roadside, each with his face pressed against the backside of the man in front. Reactions online are split between laughter and concern.

Read more

A group of suspected thieves in South Africa has become the talk of social media after they were reportedly caught and subjected to an unusual form of punishment.

The viral video

The suspects were made to lie face down on the ground in a single line by the side of a road, with each person's face pressed against the backside of the man in front of him. Their hands appear to be tied behind them.

Reports say they were caught by locals, although a uniformed officer can also be seen standing beside the men in the footage, so it is not clear whether the police or members of the public arranged them that way.

Laughter and concern

The incident has sparked mixed reactions online. Many people found the punishment hilarious, joking that South Africans seem to have invented a brand-new style of discipline.

Others were less amused, questioning whether suspects should be humiliated in public before any court has found them guilty, and whether members of the public should ever take the law into their own hands.

What we don't know

The circumstances of the incident, including where and when it happened and what the men are accused of stealing, have not been independently verified.

"Pharaoh, let my people go!": Cubana Chief Priest replies Umahi, accuses him of giving Tinubu fake promises on South-East votes

9 Nov 2026, 11:14 GMT

Cubana Chief Priest has fired back at Works Minister David Umahi, nicknaming him "Pharaoh" and accusing him of making empty promises to President Tinubu about South-East votes. He also claimed Umahi is preparing his son to become Ebonyi governor, and mocked him with his own "money na water" catchphrase.

Read more

Socialite and businessman Cubana Chief Priest has replied Minister of Works David Umahi, a day after the minister mocked his famous "money na water" catchphrase and asked how many people he has given scholarships.

"Pharaoh, let my people go"

Borrowing from the Bible story of Moses and Pharaoh, Cubana Chief Priest gave the minister a new nickname, and accused him of making promises to President Bola Tinubu that he cannot deliver.

"Pharaoh, let my people go. Stop giving Tinubu fake promises on votes. What project are you using to convince South-East people to vote for him?" he said.

The governorship claim

He went further, claiming that Umahi is preparing his son to become governor of Ebonyi State after the current governor, Francis Nwifuru.

Speaking partly in Pidgin, he said, in effect: "You're busy preparing your son to be governor after Nwifuru. God won't let that happen. You want to use Ebonyi State to settle your child. Let the young man come and meet us on the street so we can teach him how to make 'money na water'."

This is Cubana Chief Priest's claim, and Umahi has not yet responded to this latest attack.

How it started

The exchange began weeks ago when Cubana Chief Priest described Umahi as the second-worst minister from the South-East. Umahi hit back by telling him he was "becoming very noisy", comparing him unfavourably with Dangote and Otedola, and asking, "How many people have you given scholarships?"

Now the socialite has turned the argument back to politics, questioning what Umahi has done to win South-East votes for the President.
