"I would have been worried if Amaechi stood a chance in Rivers State": Wike fires back at political rivals

6 Nov 2026, 00:05 GMT

The Minister of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), Nyesom Wike, has once again spoken boldly about his political influence in Rivers State, dismissing the chances of former Minister of Transportation Rotimi Amaechi and insisting he remains a major force in the state.

"You cannot wish me away"

According to Wike, he would only have reason to worry if Amaechi stood a realistic chance of a significant political comeback in Rivers State.

The former Rivers governor made it clear that leaving Government House in Port Harcourt has not reduced his influence or weakened the structures he built over the years.

"I am not a governor, I am a minister, but you cannot wish me away in Rivers State, because the people know what I stand for and because of the people I have raised," Wike said.

His statement appears to be a direct message to those who believe his appointment as FCT Minister has taken him away from the centre of Rivers politics.

Claims about the APC

Wike also claimed that the All Progressives Congress (APC) had little political strength in Rivers State before he decided to support the party.

"Before I decided to support the APC, the party did not exist in Rivers State," he said.

That is a bold claim, given the long rivalry between Wike and Amaechi, who both served as governors of Rivers State at different times.

"I have killed the Obi wave"

Wike did not limit his remarks to Rivers. He also turned his attention to the movement around NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, claiming he had already weakened its influence in the FCT.

"As for the NDC, I have killed the Obi wave in the FCT," he said.

The remarks reflect Wike's confidence in his political strength, although his claims about the APC and about Obi's support remain his own assessment.

The road to 2027

With the 2027 general elections approaching, the political battle in Rivers State is expected to draw even more attention. Wike, Amaechi and other influential figures may once again find themselves on opposing sides as parties prepare for another contest.

For now, Wike is making one thing clear to his opponents: he may no longer be governor, but he has no intention of surrendering his influence in Rivers State.

Orji Kalu says he once sold arms to anyone willing to pay, until his parents stopped him

10 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Former Abia governor Orji Uzor Kalu has said in a television interview that he previously sold weapons to individuals and countries that could pay, and stopped only after his parents told him to.

Read more

Former Abia State governor and senator Orji Uzor Kalu has said he once sold weapons to individuals and countries.

What he said

Speaking in a television interview reported on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, Kalu said he sold arms to anyone willing to pay, and that he was interested only in making money at the time, not in how the weapons would be used. He said he stopped after his mother and father told him to. Asked whether he had supplied the African National Congress during the struggle against apartheid in South Africa, he said the ANC was not a specific customer and that he sold to anybody who wanted to buy. He did not say when the business took place or name any buyers.

Background

Kalu, a businessman and politician, governed Abia State from 1999 to 2007 and later served as senator for Abia North. He was convicted of fraud in 2019, but the Supreme Court later set the conviction aside on technical grounds. He has recently been in the news over comments on the Senate presidency and predictions about the 2027 election.

Court orders INEC chairman to produce Tinubu's 1999 and 2023 nomination forms in Sowore trial

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has subpoenaed INEC chairman Joash Amupitan to produce President Tinubu's nomination forms from 1999 and 2023 in the criminal trial of activist Omoyele Sowore, who is charged over social media posts about the President.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has ordered the chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Amupitan, to produce President Bola Tinubu's nomination documents in the trial of activist Omoyele Sowore.

The order

The subpoena, signed by Justice Mohammed Umar, directs the INEC chairman to present Tinubu's Form CF001 from his 1999 Lagos governorship campaign, which includes an affidavit relating to lost certificates, as well as the equivalent form submitted before the 2023 presidential election. It followed a ruling on Monday, 9 November 2026, approving a defence request to call Amupitan and Joe Igbokwe, a former publicity secretary of the APC in Lagos, as witnesses.

The case

Sowore, the publisher of Sahara Reporters and a former presidential candidate, is being tried over social media posts in which he described the President as a criminal. His lawyers say they intend to call both men to testify. The trial is due to resume on 11 November.

Background

Questions about Tinubu's academic records and early life have been raised by critics for years, and were the subject of legal challenges after the 2023 election, which the Supreme Court dismissed. The President and his supporters have consistently rejected claims of wrongdoing. Sowore has also faced other legal cases, including a separate cyberbullying trial in which a court recently rejected his bid to subpoena a DSS lawyer.

Jigawa declares holiday as Tinubu visits for rally and project launches

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Jigawa State declared Tuesday a public holiday for President Tinubu's first official visit since 2023. He is due to hold an APC rally and commission projects including an agricultural mechanisation programme with 400 tractors.

Read more

Jigawa State has declared Tuesday, 10 November 2026, a public holiday for a visit by President Bola Tinubu.

The visit

Governor Umar Namadi told journalists in Dutse on Monday that the President would hold an All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally and inaugurate four projects. He said it would be Tinubu's first official visit to the state since he took office in May 2023. The projects include a technical services centre, a skills acquisition centre named after the President, an agricultural mechanisation programme and a dualised road in Dutse.

The farm equipment

The agricultural package includes 400 tractors, 60 combine harvesters, 150 boom sprayers and 150 seeders, along with other equipment intended to boost farm output in the state.

Background

The Jigawa visit continues the President's campaign tour, which in the past week has taken him to Akwa Ibom, Rivers and Ebonyi states. Namadi, elected on the APC platform in 2023, is a strong supporter of the President's re-election. The North-West, which includes Jigawa, has one of the largest voting populations in Nigeria.

Tinubu mourns former ACN deputy national secretary Eteyen Amba

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

President Tinubu has paid tribute to Chief Unanawao Eteyen Amba, a former deputy national secretary of the defunct Action Congress of Nigeria, describing him as a committed progressive who helped build the party.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has mourned the death of Chief Unanawao Eteyen Amba, popularly known as Utrikang Oro, a former deputy national secretary of the defunct Action Congress of Nigeria (ACN).

The tribute

In a condolence statement on Monday, 9 November 2026, the President described Amba as a committed progressive whose contributions to party building and Nigeria's democracy would not be forgotten. He recalled Amba's loyalty during the ACN's formative years and credited him with helping to shape the party's structures and political ideas, as well as offering wise counsel.

Background

Tinubu was a leading figure in the ACN, which was one of the main opposition parties before it merged with other groups in 2013 to form the All Progressives Congress (APC). Details of the circumstances of Amba's death were not given in the statement.
