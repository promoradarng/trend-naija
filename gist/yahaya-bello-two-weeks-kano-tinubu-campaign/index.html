Yahaya Bello vows to spend two weeks in Kano campaigning for Tinubu

1 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former Kogi State Governor Yahaya Bello has promised to personally campaign in Kano for President Bola Tinubu's re-election.

What he said

Speaking at a political mobilisation and empowerment programme for Yoruba and other non-indigenous communities in Kano, organised by the foundation of Rep. Leke Abejide and reported on Sunday, 1 November 2026, Bello said he would move to Kano for two weeks to campaign for the President and for Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf. He urged non-indigenous communities in the state to beat the nearly 500,000 votes they reportedly gave Tinubu in 2023, and said Abejide's network could deliver at least two million votes. He also pointed to work on the Kano–Abuja highway as evidence of the President's commitment to the North.

Support for communities

Abejide, who represents Yagba Federal Constituency in Kogi State and holds a Yoruba community title in Kano, announced ₦500 million in interest-free loans for women's cooperatives, along with support for widows. He said programmes for young people and men are being planned.

Background

Kano has the second-highest number of registered voters in Nigeria after Lagos, and the APC is investing heavily there after Governor Yusuf moved from the NNPP to the APC. The state is also the home of Rabiu Kwankwaso, Peter Obi's running mate on the NDC ticket. Bello, who governed Kogi from 2016 to 2024, is facing a money-laundering case brought by the EFCC, which he denies. Deputy Senate President Barau Jibrin also predicted a Tinubu victory at an APC event in Kano on Sunday.

What happens next

The campaign in Kano is expected to intensify as the APC and NDC compete for the state's large vote.

  • Bello says he will spend two weeks campaigning in Kano.
  • He wants non-indigenous communities to deliver more votes than in 2023.
  • Rep. Leke Abejide announced ₦500m in loans for women's cooperatives.

The Coalition for Democracy has confirmed that the second summit of Nigeria's opposition political parties will hold on Monday, 30 November 2026, at the NAF Conference Centre in Abuja.

The details

Proceedings will begin at 10am, with accreditation opening at 9am. The confirmation followed the seventh meeting of the coalition's central leadership committee on Wednesday, 26 November. The statement was signed on behalf of the coalition parties by Salihu Lukman.

Report records 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in four months

26 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

A civic group says it has documented 124 incidents of election-related violence and 22 deaths across Nigeria since July. It warned the trend could worsen as the January 2027 elections approach.

Read more

A non-partisan civic organisation has warned of rising political violence in Nigeria, saying it recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in the past four months.

The figures

In a statement in Abuja on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, the Kimpact Development Initiative (KDI) said the incidents were documented between 1 July and 23 November 2026 through its election violence monitoring project. Incidents rose from three in July to 48 in October, with another 40 recorded in the first half of November alone.

The warning

The group warned that the trend could worsen as the 16 January 2027 elections draw closer unless political parties, security agencies and other stakeholders take urgent action. It pointed to growing political intolerance and inflammatory rhetoric among politicians and their supporters as key triggers.

KDI stressed that the problem cuts across parties and should not be blamed on any single group, and urged candidates to restrain their supporters.

Background

The report comes days after deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states, and after INEC warned that the popular 'rig and die' slogan could endanger polling staff and voters. The House of Representatives has also warned that a state of emergency may be needed in Delta if violence continues.
