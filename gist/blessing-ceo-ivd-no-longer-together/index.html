"IVD and I are no longer together. I've left him to pick up the pieces of my life": Blessing CEO reveals

5 Nov 2026, 10:05 GMT

Controversial Nigerian relationship therapist and social media personality Blessing CEO has got people talking again after saying her relationship with businessman IVD has come to an end.

"We are no longer together"

According to Blessing, she and IVD are no longer together, and she has decided to walk away from the relationship to concentrate on rebuilding her life.

"IVD and I are no longer together. I've left him to pick up the pieces of my life," she said.

A difficult period

The revelation comes during a difficult time in Blessing CEO's personal life. Only days ago she shared an emotional apology, describing herself as broken and comparing her journey to the biblical story of the prodigal son.

For someone who has spent years advising people about love, relationships and marriage, her own romantic life has often drawn public attention. With this latest news, many people are wondering what went wrong between her and IVD.

Their relationship had already generated considerable discussion on social media, with supporters and critics holding very different opinions about it.

What we don't know

Blessing has not given a detailed explanation of what led to the decision, and IVD has yet to respond publicly to her statement.

What seems clear from her words is that she wants to focus on herself and find a way forward after everything she says she has been through.

Blessing CEO has built much of her public identity on relationship advice, sometimes making controversial statements about how men and women should handle love. Now that her own relationship has ended, social media users are likely to revisit some of those statements.

Relationships can end for many reasons, and the public may never know the full story behind this one.

Nkem Owoh denies saying Yoruba founded Nollywood, credits them with pioneering films

8 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Veteran actor Nkem Owoh, known as Osuofia, has said on a popular interview show that he never claimed the Yoruba founded Nollywood, pointing out that the word did not exist when he started acting, but credited Yoruba filmmakers such as Hubert Ogunde with pioneering the tradition.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actor Nkem Owoh, popularly known as Osuofia, has addressed comments attributed to him about who founded Nigeria's film industry.

What he said

Speaking on the YouTube interview show 'Curiosity Made Me Ask', hosted by content creator Isbae U, in an episode uploaded on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Owoh rejected reports that he had said the Yoruba people started Nollywood rather than the Igbo. He explained that the word 'Nollywood' did not even exist when he and his colleagues first began making films. However, he said Yoruba filmmakers had indeed pioneered the home-video tradition in Nigeria, giving particular credit to the late theatre and film pioneer Hubert Ogunde. When the host jokingly suggested Ogunde might have been Igbo, Owoh insisted that he was Yoruba.

The wider debate

The question of who built Nollywood has been debated for years. In August 2026, the late actor Francis Duru argued that Igbo businesspeople were the real architects of the home-video boom of the 1990s and early 2000s, while accepting that the Yoruba film community had become more dominant through stronger organisation. Owoh was reported at the time to have pointed out that Yoruba filmmakers were active in theatre and film long before the video boom. Duru died in October 2026.

Background

Owoh is one of Nollywood's best-loved comic actors, famous for the 'Osuofia in London' films and the song 'I Go Chop Your Dollar'. 'Curiosity Made Me Ask' is one of Nigeria's most popular celebrity interview shows.

Ghanaian actor Van Vicker has dismissed viral images appearing to show him with Nollywood actress Regina Daniels, saying they were generated using artificial intelligence.

The photos

According to reports on Sunday, 8 November 2026, one of the images showed Van Vicker carrying a much younger Regina Daniels, while another placed him beside her during what looked like her recent birthday photoshoot. The pictures spread widely online and caused a stir among fans of both actors.

What he said

Van Vicker said neither image was real, and that he and Regina Daniels had not been in contact for well over a decade. He spoke out quickly to stop the rumours from spreading further.

Background

Van Vicker is one of the best-known Ghanaian actors in Nollywood and has starred in many Nigerian films since the mid-2000s. Regina Daniels, who began acting as a child, has been in the news recently, including a public clash with journalist Kemi Olunloyo, and new videos from the mansion of her husband, Senator Ned Nwoko, have also drawn attention online. The case is the latest example of AI-generated images of celebrities spreading on social media and being mistaken for real photos.

Crossdresser and socialite Bobrisky says relocating from Nigeria two years ago was one of the best decisions ever made, describing the country as toxic and full of daily drama, and saying life abroad is peaceful.

Socialite Idris Okuneye, known as Bobrisky, says leaving Nigeria was one of the best decisions of their life.

What Bobrisky said

In an Instagram post on Saturday, 7 November 2026, Bobrisky described Nigeria as a toxic environment where a new drama, piece of gossip or fight springs up every day. The socialite said life since relocating had been peaceful and that they would not give up that peace.

Background

Bobrisky left Nigeria in November 2024 after a public row with the federal government over claims about the conditions under which a six-month jail sentence for abusing the naira had been served. The sentence was handed down in April 2024 after Bobrisky pleaded guilty to spraying and mutilating naira notes. Allegations that the socialite had served part of the sentence outside prison led to a government investigation and a dispute with activist VeryDarkMan, who first raised the claims. Bobrisky was stopped at the Seme border while trying to leave the country in October 2024 before eventually relocating.
