2027 Senate presidency: Orji Uzor Kalu says he cannot reject Tinubu's choice as political battle begins

10 Nov 2026, 01:13 GMT

Senator Orji Uzor Kalu has openly said he would be willing to become Nigeria's next Senate President, and would have no reason to turn down the position if President Bola Ahmed Tinubu backed him for it.

"I can't say no"

The former Abia State governor, who represents Abia North Senatorial District, made the statement in a television interview, as talk about the leadership of the next National Assembly begins to gather pace ahead of the 2027 general elections.

"If Tinubu chooses me as the next Senate President, I can't say no," Kalu said.

Brief as it was, the statement has added another twist to the political calculations already taking shape within the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC).

Zoning to the South-East?

For some time, there has been talk of zoning the next Senate presidency to the South-East. Nothing has been officially agreed, but that has not stopped political figures from being linked to the position.

One of the names being mentioned is Imo State Governor Hope Uzodimma, who served as senator for Imo West before becoming governor. His reported interest has sparked political conversation, given his influence within the APC and his relationship with the party's national leadership.

There have also been reports of reactions involving FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, although the extent of any disagreement over the position remains unclear.

Kalu versus Uzodimma

Both Kalu and Uzodimma are experienced politicians from the South-East. Both have served in the Senate, and both have strong relationships within the ruling party.

The big question is whether the APC will actually zone the Senate presidency to the South-East in 2027, and if so, who will win the party's backing. For now, there is no official confirmation that the position has been reserved for the region.

The senators decide

It is worth remembering that the President has no constitutional power to appoint the Senate President. Senators elect their presiding officer themselves when a new National Assembly is inaugurated.

Even so, presidential support and internal party negotiations often play a major role in deciding who eventually emerges.

Whether Kalu's remarks signal serious ambition or simply a willingness to serve if called upon remains to be seen. What is clear is that the race for the leadership of the next National Assembly is already attracting attention, even before Nigerians go to the polls.

ECOWAS has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Akufo-Addo to head its election observation mission for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections on 16 January 2027.

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo to lead its election observation mission to Nigeria.

The appointment

The appointment was announced in a statement from Akufo-Addo's office on Thursday, 12 November 2026. He will oversee the deployment of ECOWAS observers for Nigeria's presidential and National Assembly elections, scheduled for 16 January 2027. The mission will monitor the electoral process and report on how the polls are conducted.

Who is Akufo-Addo

Akufo-Addo served two terms as Ghana's president, from 2017 to January 2025.

Background

International attention on Nigeria's election is growing. The European Union has raised concerns about violence and hate speech, and a senior US State Department official told Congress this week that the vote could spur violence. INEC says preparations are on track, with non-sensitive materials already being moved to the states.

Makinde says talks with Atiku and Obi are still open ahead of 2027

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Oyo governor and APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde says discussions with Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi are ongoing and could lead to different political arrangements before the election, but that any choice of partner must be based on evidence and public opinion.

Read more

Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde, the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM), says he is still open to working with Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi before the 2027 elections.

What he said

In a television interview aired on Friday, 13 November 2026, Makinde said discussions with both men were still active and that no option had been ruled out. He said the talks could lead to a range of political arrangements before the election, and that any decision would be guided by the goal of changing the country's direction. Asked whether he preferred Atiku or Obi as a partner, he said the choice should be based on evidence and public opinion rather than sentiment. He has also said he is willing to work with any Nigerian except President Bola Tinubu.

Background

Atiku is the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and Obi of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC). Efforts to agree a single opposition candidate have repeatedly stalled, with each insisting on running. Earlier this week, Makinde gave four reasons for challenging Tinubu, whom he supported in 2023, and PRP candidate Donald Duke said he was willing to step down for a consensus candidate. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

"Peter Obi is one in a million. Just imagine the kind of travelling we do together": Dr. Mo

13 Nov 2026, 12:29 GMT

Dr. Mo has described Peter Obi as "one in a million", marvelling at the NDC presidential candidate's punishing travel schedule, which he says can take them from Lagos to Abuja, London, several US cities and back again in one trip.

Read more

Dr. Mo has expressed admiration for former Anambra State Governor Peter Obi, now the NDC presidential candidate, describing his travel schedule as one that is very hard to keep up with.

"Just visualise what I'm saying"

According to Dr. Mo, travelling with Obi means moving from one city to another, sometimes across continents, with little time to settle before the next journey.

"We move from Lagos to Abuja, then take off to London. From there, we spend eight hours flying to Houston, hold a meeting and move to Phoenix, then California. We return to Texas, go to Dallas, fly back to the UK and eventually return to Nigeria," he said.

"Just imagine it. Just visualise what I'm saying."

"One in a million"

Dr. Mo described Obi as "one in a million", expressing admiration for the amount of travelling and the number of engagements he handles.

For anyone who has done long-distance travel, moving through several cities, attending meetings and catching yet another flight can be exhausting. Doing it repeatedly, while running a national campaign, is another level entirely.
