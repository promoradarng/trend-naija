2027 Senate presidency: Orji Uzor Kalu says he cannot reject Tinubu's choice as political battle begins

10 Nov 2026, 01:13 GMT

Senator Orji Uzor Kalu has openly said he would be willing to become Nigeria's next Senate President, and would have no reason to turn down the position if President Bola Ahmed Tinubu backed him for it.

"I can't say no"

The former Abia State governor, who represents Abia North Senatorial District, made the statement in a television interview, as talk about the leadership of the next National Assembly begins to gather pace ahead of the 2027 general elections.

"If Tinubu chooses me as the next Senate President, I can't say no," Kalu said.

Brief as it was, the statement has added another twist to the political calculations already taking shape within the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC).

Zoning to the South-East?

For some time, there has been talk of zoning the next Senate presidency to the South-East. Nothing has been officially agreed, but that has not stopped political figures from being linked to the position.

One of the names being mentioned is Imo State Governor Hope Uzodimma, who served as senator for Imo West before becoming governor. His reported interest has sparked political conversation, given his influence within the APC and his relationship with the party's national leadership.

There have also been reports of reactions involving FCT Minister Nyesom Wike, although the extent of any disagreement over the position remains unclear.

Kalu versus Uzodimma

Both Kalu and Uzodimma are experienced politicians from the South-East. Both have served in the Senate, and both have strong relationships within the ruling party.

The big question is whether the APC will actually zone the Senate presidency to the South-East in 2027, and if so, who will win the party's backing. For now, there is no official confirmation that the position has been reserved for the region.

The senators decide

It is worth remembering that the President has no constitutional power to appoint the Senate President. Senators elect their presiding officer themselves when a new National Assembly is inaugurated.

Even so, presidential support and internal party negotiations often play a major role in deciding who eventually emerges.

Whether Kalu's remarks signal serious ambition or simply a willingness to serve if called upon remains to be seen. What is clear is that the race for the leadership of the next National Assembly is already attracting attention, even before Nigerians go to the polls.

Reps order probe into attacks on political rallies ahead of 2027 polls

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The House of Representatives has ordered an investigation into violence at political rallies and meetings across the country, citing deaths and injuries in Delta, Nasarawa, Katsina and Enugu. Committees are to report back within three weeks.

Read more

The House of Representatives has resolved to investigate violence at political rallies, meetings and processions ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The motion

On Thursday, 12 November 2026, the House adopted a motion of urgent public importance sponsored by Minority Leader Frederick Agbedi and 16 other lawmakers. It directed its committees on Police Affairs and on National Security and Intelligence to examine the causes of the violence and recommend ways to prevent further attacks. A principal officer of the House will lead the inquiry, and the committees are to report within three weeks.

Incidents cited

Presenting the motion, Agbedi pointed to an attack during a political procession in Asaba, Delta State, on 7 November, which police said left one person dead and five injured; violence at an APC rally in Lafia, Nasarawa State, on 4 November; an attack on APC supporters travelling to a rally in Katsina State on 28 October, after which police reportedly arrested 14 suspects; and the disruption of political meetings in Enugu State in September.

Background

The motion came on the same day suspected thugs stormed a Nigeria Democratic Congress town hall in Lagos. The APC presidential campaign council condemned that attack and other recent attacks on supporters across party lines, and urged security agencies to arrest those responsible. The Inspector-General of Police and the European Union have also raised concerns about rising political violence. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Protesters heckle INEC chairman Amupitan in London; he denies running pro-APC X account

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Diaspora protesters surrounded INEC chairman Joash Amupitan's car outside Chatham House in London, accusing the commission of bias. Amupitan, who pledged to refuse any political appointment after his tenure, has again denied operating an X account that once backed the APC.

Read more

Protesters confronted the chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Amupitan, in London on Thursday, 12 November 2026.

What happened

Amupitan was in London to speak at a panel on Nigeria's preparations for the 2027 elections, hosted by Chatham House and the Kimpact Development Initiative. Nigerian diaspora activists who had mobilised on social media gathered outside the venue and reportedly surrounded his vehicle, accusing the commission of favouring President Bola Tinubu and the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) and demanding his resignation. They cited duplicate entries and AI-generated images in the preliminary voters' register and alleged fake polling units.

Amupitan's response

At the panel, Amupitan pledged to turn down any political or diplomatic appointment after leaving INEC, saying he wanted to demonstrate his impartiality, and said the commission was introducing technical safeguards. INEC has said it removed 1.6 million invalid records from the register, including entries with manipulated images, and flagged officials in 10 states. Amupitan also again denied operating an X account that had allegedly posted support for the APC during the 2023 elections, saying he had never had an X account and that it was never linked to his email. INEC said in April that the claims were fabricated and that fake accounts had been created in his name.

Background

At the same event, Amupitan said INEC would obey the final court ruling on Atiku Abubakar's candidacy. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

INEC says it will obey final court ruling on Atiku, begins moving election materials

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

INEC chairman Joash Amupitan says the commission will abide by the final court decision on Atiku Abubakar's candidacy. The commission has also started moving non-sensitive election materials to states and says PVC production is complete.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) says it will be bound by the final court decision on whether Atiku Abubakar can contest the 2027 presidential election.

What INEC said about Atiku

Speaking in London on Thursday, 12 November 2026, where he was briefing an audience at Chatham House on election preparations, INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan said the commission would follow whatever final ruling the courts give in the dispute over Atiku's nomination. A Federal High Court in Abuja on 5 November nullified the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) and reportedly voided the primary that produced Atiku as its candidate. The ADC has appealed, and its lawyer has argued that the judgment contained no express order invalidating Atiku's candidacy.

Materials on the move

Separately, INEC National Commissioner Professor Rhoda Gumus said on Thursday that the commission had begun moving non-sensitive election materials to some geopolitical zones. Speaking while receiving a delegation from Kenya's electoral commission, she said INEC had completed nine of the 14 activities on its election timetable, finished producing Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs) and sent the first two batches to most states. She urged registered voters to collect their cards and said staff training was under way.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027. Amupitan recently completed a tour of the six geopolitical zones to assess readiness. Separately, a Federal High Court in Kaduna on Thursday nullified an ADC primary in the state, adding to the party's legal troubles.

Obi pledges 10,000MW of electricity in four years at Lagos town hall

12 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has promised to generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000 megawatts of electricity within four years if elected, and to declare emergencies on security, education and power. He spoke at the Lagos town hall that was attacked by thugs earlier in the day.

Read more

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has promised to deliver at least 10,000 megawatts of electricity within four years if he wins the 2027 election.

What he said

Speaking at the party's town hall meeting in Oregun, Lagos, on Thursday, 12 November 2026, which went ahead despite an attack on the venue by suspected thugs that morning, Obi said unreliable electricity was one of the biggest obstacles to economic growth and industrialisation. He said his government would declare an immediate state of emergency on security, education and electricity, and would generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000MW. He said Nigeria currently generates and distributes roughly 4,000MW, and compared the country's power supply unfavourably with South Africa's.

Background

Power supply has long been one of Nigeria's most persistent problems. President Bola Tinubu's government has begun a ₦30bn programme to distribute free prepaid meters. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
