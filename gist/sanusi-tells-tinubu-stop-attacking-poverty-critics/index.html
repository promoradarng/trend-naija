Sanusi urges Tinubu government to stop treating talk of poverty as an attack

3 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

The Emir of Kano and former Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) governor, Muhammadu Sanusi II, has urged President Bola Tinubu's government to listen to concerns about poverty rather than treating critics as enemies.

What he said

Speaking at the Oxford Global Think Tank conference convened by former World Bank vice-president Arunma Oteh, in comments reported on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Sanusi reportedly said the government's habit of issuing statements from the Presidential Villa to condemn people who raise economic concerns was counterproductive. He argued that citizens who talk about poverty are not attacking the President personally, and that the government should distinguish between criticism and hostility.

On the economy

Sanusi also said Nigeria was dangerously close to economic collapse in 2023 and on the verge of hyperinflation, a view he said he had held and expressed at the time. His comments suggest he sees the need for reform, while insisting that the hardship many people feel should be acknowledged.

Background

Sanusi was CBN governor from 2009 to 2014 and is a well-known commentator on the economy. He was reinstated as Emir of Kano in 2024. The Tinubu government removed the petrol subsidy and unified exchange rates in 2023, and says these moves rescued the economy, pointing to falling inflation and rising reserves. Critics, including investment banker Atedo Peterside this week, say the poor have become poorer. Presidency officials have often responded sharply to such criticism, accusing some critics of playing politics ahead of 2027.

What happens next

The Presidency had not responded to Sanusi's comments at the time of reporting.

  • Sanusi says critics of hardship are not enemies of the President.
  • He says Nigeria was close to hyperinflation in 2023.
  • The Presidency has not responded.

INEC asks court to cancel subpoena for Tinubu's 1999 and 2023 election records

11 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

INEC has asked the Federal High Court in Abuja to set aside a subpoena ordering its chairman to appear and produce President Tinubu's 1999 and 2023 nomination documents in the trial of activist Omoyele Sowore.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has asked the Federal High Court in Abuja to set aside a subpoena requiring its chairman to produce President Bola Tinubu's election documents.

The application

INEC's lawyer, Alex Izinyon, a Senior Advocate of Nigeria, filed the motion on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, the day before INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan was due to appear as a defence witness in the criminal trial of activist Omoyele Sowore. The subpoena, issued by Justice Mohammed Umar, had directed the INEC chairman to produce Tinubu's nomination forms from the 1999 Lagos governorship election, including an affidavit relating to lost certificates, and from the 2023 presidential election.

The case

Sowore is being prosecuted by the State Security Service over social media posts in which he described the President as a criminal. His defence team asked the court to call the INEC chairman and Joe Igbokwe, a former APC publicity secretary in Lagos, as witnesses.

Background

Questions about the President's academic records have been raised by critics for years and were the subject of legal challenges after the 2023 election, which the Supreme Court dismissed. The President and his supporters have consistently rejected claims of wrongdoing.

Jigawa declares holiday as Tinubu visits for rally and project launches

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Jigawa State declared Tuesday a public holiday for President Tinubu's first official visit since 2023. He is due to hold an APC rally and commission projects including an agricultural mechanisation programme with 400 tractors.

Read more

Jigawa State has declared Tuesday, 10 November 2026, a public holiday for a visit by President Bola Tinubu.

The visit

Governor Umar Namadi told journalists in Dutse on Monday that the President would hold an All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign rally and inaugurate four projects. He said it would be Tinubu's first official visit to the state since he took office in May 2023. The projects include a technical services centre, a skills acquisition centre named after the President, an agricultural mechanisation programme and a dualised road in Dutse.

The farm equipment

The agricultural package includes 400 tractors, 60 combine harvesters, 150 boom sprayers and 150 seeders, along with other equipment intended to boost farm output in the state.

Power pledge at the rally

At the APC rally in Jigawa, Tinubu promised to lower the cost of living and provide reliable electricity, saying the old privatisation model was not working. During his 2023 campaign he said Nigerians should not re-elect him if he failed to provide stable power in his first term.

Background

The Jigawa visit continues the President's campaign tour, which in the past week has taken him to Akwa Ibom, Rivers and Ebonyi states. Namadi, elected on the APC platform in 2023, is a strong supporter of the President's re-election. The North-West, which includes Jigawa, has one of the largest voting populations in Nigeria.

INEC says its staff in about 10 states uploaded AI photos to voters' register

11 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

INEC says it has identified its own officials in about 10 states who uploaded AI-generated photographs to the voters' register. The images have been removed and the commission says the staff involved will face disciplinary action.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) says it has identified officials in about 10 states who uploaded photographs generated by artificial intelligence to Nigeria's voters' register.

What INEC said

Speaking on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, at a webinar on AI, misinformation and the 2027 elections, INEC National Commissioner Mohammed Haruna, who chairs the commission's Information and Voter Education Committee, said a large number of AI-generated pictures had been posted onto the register and had since been taken down. He said the commission had the names of the staff involved across about 10 states and would take disciplinary action against them.

Limits on what INEC can do

Haruna said it could be difficult for the commission to stop members of the public from digitally altering their own photographs before submitting them, but that INEC could act directly where its own staff were responsible.

Background

The issue of AI-generated images on the register had already drawn public attention, raising concerns about the accuracy of the voter roll ahead of the general elections. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

First Lady Remi Tinubu receives U-17 World Cup champions Flamingos at the Villa

11 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

First Lady Oluremi Tinubu, standing in for President Tinubu, received the Flamingos at the Presidential Villa in Abuja after their historic FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup win, the first by an African team.

Read more

The Flamingos, Nigeria's under-17 women's football team, have been welcomed at the Presidential Villa in Abuja after winning the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup.

The reception

First Lady Oluremi Tinubu received the players and officials on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, on behalf of President Bola Tinubu, in what was described as a celebratory homecoming visit and the federal government's formal recognition of the team's achievement.

The win

The Flamingos beat Japan 2–1 in the final in Morocco on Saturday, 7 November, with goals from Awawu Bashiru and Oluebube Umejiaku, becoming the first African side to win the tournament. After losing their opening game to Poland, they won five matches in a row, including victories over defending champions North Korea, Mexico, Venezuela and Brazil. The win means Nigeria has now won the FIFA U-17 World Cup in both the men's and women's competitions.

Rewards

The Senate has pledged ₦100 million to the team. The players had earlier received $50,000 in cash.
