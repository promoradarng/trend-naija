Pastor David Ibiyeomie has reportedly advised parents to prioritise paying their tithes before spending money on Christmas clothes for their children.

But this raises a serious question about the priorities being preached in some churches today.

Imagine a struggling father or mother who has worked tirelessly throughout the year, trying to put food on the table, pay school fees and provide a little Christmas happiness for their children. Should such a parent feel guilty for buying their children new clothes because they have not paid their tithe?

Tithing is an important religious practice for many Christians, but should it come before the basic needs and happiness of one's children?

Critics are asking: since when did buying Christmas clothes for your own children become something you must first seek financial clearance from the church to do?
