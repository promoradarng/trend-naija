Petrol depots cut prices below Dangote's ₦1,300 gantry rate

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Petrol marketers have started selling below the Dangote refinery's new ₦1,300 per litre gantry price, raising hopes that fuel could become a little cheaper for Nigerians.

The new prices

Price checks in Lagos and Port Harcourt on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, reportedly showed several depots selling petrol up to ₦17 per litre below the refinery's official rate. A depot in Port Harcourt listed the lowest price at ₦1,283 per litre. The Dangote refinery recently raised its gantry price to a fixed ₦1,300 per litre, saying it wanted to bring more stability to petrol pricing.

What it means for motorists

Depot prices are what marketers pay before transporting fuel to filling stations, so lower depot prices do not immediately mean cheaper fuel at the pump. Transport costs and stock bought at higher prices may delay any reduction for consumers, but analysts say competition between depots could gradually push pump prices down.

Background

Since the removal of the petrol subsidy in 2023, prices have been set by the market, and the Dangote refinery has become the biggest player in local supply. Its pricing decisions now strongly influence what other marketers charge. Days ago it had cut its gantry price to ₦1,210, before moving to the new fixed rate of ₦1,300, and depot owners have responded with their own changes. Petrol prices remain far higher than before the subsidy was removed, adding to the cost of transport and food. Meanwhile, cooking gas prices rose by up to 16 per cent at Lagos depots in recent days.

What happens next

Marketers say prices will depend on supply, exchange rates and global oil prices, which rose slightly this week after US strikes on Iran.

  • Dangote's fixed gantry price is ₦1,300.

Christmas airfares double as return tickets hit ₦822,000

24 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Domestic airfares have reportedly risen by as much as 100% in three weeks, with some return tickets now above ₦800,000 ahead of Christmas. Travellers heading to the South-East and South-South are hit hardest.

Nigerians planning to fly home for Christmas are facing steep price rises, with domestic airfares reportedly up by as much as 100% in just three weeks.

The prices

Return tickets on some routes now cost more than ₦800,000, with fares as high as ₦822,000 recorded on some airline booking platforms. Airlines including Air Peace and United Nigeria are among those whose prices have gone up.

Airlines including Air Peace and United Nigeria are among those that have raised fares.

The increases are raising the cost of travel for millions of Nigerians, especially those heading to the South-East and South-South for the festive season.

Why prices are rising

Airlines are said to be struggling to meet the seasonal surge in demand with limited aircraft, while also dealing with higher operating costs. Security worries on major highways are also pushing more travellers towards flying.

Several carriers are reportedly adding extra flights on domestic and international routes between late November and mid-December to cope with the expected rush, but fares have continued to climb.

Bandits kill two teachers and abduct 13 people in Zamfara

24 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Suspected bandits have killed two teachers and abducted 13 people in separate attacks in Maru Local Government Area of Zamfara State, police say. Security teams are pursuing the attackers.

Suspected bandits have killed two teachers and abducted 13 people in separate attacks on communities in Zamfara State, according to the police.

The attacks

The attacks took place in Jabaka and Tungar Janbuzu villages in Maru Local Government Area. In Jabaka, the gunmen reportedly invaded in large numbers and abducted 13 residents, including children. In Tungar Janbuzu, two teachers, both aged 35, were ambushed and killed while working on their farms.

Police response

Force spokesman CSP Ani Iniedu, who disclosed the attacks on Monday, 23 November 2026, said police tactical teams, soldiers, local vigilantes and hunters had been deployed and were on the trail of the bandits to rescue the victims and arrest those responsible.

Background

Zamfara is one of the North-West states worst hit by banditry and kidnapping. Maru is also the area where an APC campaign rally was called off this week after supporters died in a road crash.

15 APC supporters killed in road crash on way to Zamfara campaign flag-off

24 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Fifteen APC supporters died in a road crash while travelling from Gummi to the party's campaign flag-off in Talata-Mafara, Zamfara State. The party has called off a rally planned for Maru.

Read more

More than ten members of the All Progressives Congress (APC) have reportedly died in a road crash in Zamfara State, shortly after attending the party's governorship campaign flag-off in Talata-Mafara.

What happened

The victims were travelling back to Gummi Local Government Area after the rally when the accident occurred. Several other people were injured.

Government response

Zamfara's Commissioner for Information and Culture confirmed the deaths in a statement on Monday, 23 November 2026, and said the state government had sent condolences to the victims' families and the wider APC family. As a mark of respect, the government announced the suspension of a campaign rally.

Background

The tragedy came on the same day that four people were killed in an attack on an NDC campaign event in Delta State.

Toll rises to 15

The APC's Zamfara campaign council later put the death toll at 15, saying the victims were travelling from Gummi to Talata-Mafara for the flag-off when the crash happened. It called off a rally planned for Maru Local Government Area on Tuesday as a mark of respect, and pledged support for the injured and the bereaved families.
