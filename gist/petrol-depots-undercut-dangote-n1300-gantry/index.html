Petrol depots cut prices below Dangote's ₦1,300 gantry rate

4 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Petrol marketers have started selling below the Dangote refinery's new ₦1,300 per litre gantry price, raising hopes that fuel could become a little cheaper for Nigerians.

The new prices

Price checks in Lagos and Port Harcourt on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, reportedly showed several depots selling petrol up to ₦17 per litre below the refinery's official rate. A depot in Port Harcourt listed the lowest price at ₦1,283 per litre. The Dangote refinery recently raised its gantry price to a fixed ₦1,300 per litre, saying it wanted to bring more stability to petrol pricing.

What it means for motorists

Depot prices are what marketers pay before transporting fuel to filling stations, so lower depot prices do not immediately mean cheaper fuel at the pump. Transport costs and stock bought at higher prices may delay any reduction for consumers, but analysts say competition between depots could gradually push pump prices down.

Background

Since the removal of the petrol subsidy in 2023, prices have been set by the market, and the Dangote refinery has become the biggest player in local supply. Its pricing decisions now strongly influence what other marketers charge. Days ago it had cut its gantry price to ₦1,210, before moving to the new fixed rate of ₦1,300, and depot owners have responded with their own changes. Petrol prices remain far higher than before the subsidy was removed, adding to the cost of transport and food. Meanwhile, cooking gas prices rose by up to 16 per cent at Lagos depots in recent days.

What happens next

Marketers say prices will depend on supply, exchange rates and global oil prices, which rose slightly this week after US strikes on Iran.

  • Dangote's fixed gantry price is ₦1,300.
  • Pump prices may take time to fall.

FG seeks another $500 million World Bank loan to expand renewable energy programme

16 Nov 2026, 16:20 GMT

The Federal Government is seeking an additional $500 million loan from the World Bank for its renewable energy programme, which would raise the World Bank's total financing for the project to $1.25 billion. Many Nigerians are asking where the results of earlier spending can be seen.

Read more

The Federal Government is seeking an additional $500 million loan from the World Bank to expand its renewable energy programme.

What the money is for

The programme is meant to expand access to electricity through renewable sources such as solar mini-grids and stand-alone solar systems, particularly for homes, businesses, schools and health centres in areas the national grid does not reach.

In 2023, the World Bank approved $750 million for Nigeria's Distributed Access through Renewable Energy Scale-up (DARES) project, which aims to bring clean electricity to millions of Nigerians. The new $500 million would raise the World Bank's total financing for the project to $1.25 billion.

"Where is the renewable energy?"

The news has been met with frustration by many Nigerians, who say they are yet to see the impact of the money already borrowed.

Millions of households and small businesses still depend on generators, while frequent grid collapses and rising electricity tariffs continue to bite. For many people, the question is simple: where is the renewable energy?

The debt question

The request also comes amid growing concern about Nigeria's rising debt. Critics argue that new loans should only be taken when there is clear evidence that earlier funds have been well spent and are delivering results.

Supporters of the programme argue that renewable energy is essential for closing Nigeria's huge electricity gap, and that such projects take time to deliver.

What happened

According to security sources, the attack happened on Saturday, 14 November 2026, at Ruga village in Alkaleri Local Government Area. The Chinese national, who was reportedly working on core drilling in the area, was travelling in a company vehicle when it was ambushed. A police inspector from a mobile police unit, who was deployed as his escort, was also killed. A local source said the attackers killed another man in the village.

Response

Troops of the Nigerian Army's 3 Division were informed of the attack late on Saturday night, and security sources said efforts were under way to track down the attackers and establish the circumstances of the ambush.

Background

Court warns AGF Fagbemi of jail over detained Okuama community leaders

A Federal High Court in Warri has warned Attorney-General Lateef Fagbemi that he faces imprisonment if he keeps defying an order to release detained leaders of the Okuama community in Delta State.

A Federal High Court in Warri, Delta State, has warned the Attorney-General of the Federation, Lateef Fagbemi, that he could be jailed for contempt over the continued detention of leaders of the Okuama community.

The notice

The warning was contained in a formal notice of consequences of disobedience to a court order, issued on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, and reported on Sunday. It relates to an order made on 10 June 2026 by Justice Hyeladzira Nganjiwa directing the immediate release of the applicants, six community leaders who brought a fundamental rights case. The respondents include the federal government, the AGF and the armed forces.

Background

Okuama, in Ughelli South Local Government Area of Delta State, was the scene of the killing of 17 soldiers in March 2024, after which the military carried out operations in the area and detained several community members. The AGF's office had not responded to the court notice at the time of writing.

Dangote Refinery cuts petrol gantry price by ₦51 to ₦1,159

16 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its petrol gantry price by ₦51 to ₦1,159 per litre, its latest reduction, as depot owners also lower prices.

Read more

Dangote Refinery has reduced the price at which it sells petrol at its gantry by ₦51 per litre, according to reports on Monday, 16 November 2026.

The new price

The new rate is ₦1,159 per litre, down from ₦1,210, a cut of about 4.2 per cent. The reduction gives marketers and filling station owners room to lower pump prices, although retail prices also depend on transport, distribution costs and each retailer's margins.

Depot prices

Depot owners have also been cutting their prices. Market data from Friday showed several Lagos depots selling at about ₦1,185 per litre, with one of the larger operators cutting its price by ₦35.

Background

Petrol prices have fallen steadily in recent days after the refinery moved to pricing linked to international petrol prices. NNPC cut its pump prices in Lagos to ₦1,289 per litre at the weekend, and MRS stations in Abuja reduced theirs to ₦1,399. Marketers have warned, however, that renewed tension between Iran and the United States could push global prices up again.
