BBNaija's Faith builds filter game for Funke Akindele's new film The Four

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Big Brother Naija star Dr Faith Adewale has won praise online after creating a game to promote Funke Akindele's upcoming film, The Four.

The game

In a video shared on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, Faith showed how the Snapchat filter works. Players are paired with three of their real friends and must complete quick physical challenges, such as tapping the screen rapidly or raising their eyebrows, to 'rescue' each one. When all three are saved, the group of four is reunited, echoing the theme of the film. Faith set a time of 15.4 seconds and challenged her followers to beat it. The filter also includes a button that links to a cinema website where users can buy tickets.

Funke reacts

Funke Akindele, who directs and stars in the film, spotted the post and responded with delight, and the cinema chain also celebrated the idea online.

Background

The Four is due in Nigerian cinemas on 11 December 2026, in time for the festive season. Funke Akindele is one of Nollywood's most commercially successful filmmakers, with several of her previous December releases breaking box office records.

State police bill moves to Tinubu's table after all 36 state assemblies approve it

18 Nov 2026, 14:51 GMT

The Senate has authorised its Clerk to send the constitutional amendment creating state police to President Bola Tinubu for assent, after all 36 state Houses of Assembly approved it. But state police will not start immediately: a separate National Policing Bill is still needed.

Read more

Nigeria has moved a step closer to state police. The Senate has authorised its Clerk to transmit the constitutional amendment establishing state police to President Bola Tinubu for his assent.

All 36 states on board

All 36 state Houses of Assembly have approved the proposal, completing the state-legislature stage of the constitutional amendment process.

The bill was originally sent to the National Assembly by President Tinubu himself, as part of efforts to tackle the country's worsening insecurity, so his assent is widely expected.

Not an overnight change

However, state police will not begin operating immediately once the President signs the amendment.

According to the Senate, a separate National Policing Bill is still needed to settle the key details: how state police will be funded, how officers will be recruited, who will command them and who will oversee them.

Why it matters

For years, many Nigerians, especially in states hit hard by banditry, kidnapping and communal violence, have argued that a single, centrally controlled police force is too stretched to protect every community.

Supporters say state police will bring security closer to the people, with officers who know the local terrain, language and culture.

Critics worry that governors could use state police against political opponents, journalists and critics, particularly at election time, and that poorer states may struggle to fund them properly.

The real test

Nigeria is now closer to decentralised policing, but the most important safeguards, the rules that will decide whether state police protect citizens or serve politicians, are still to be written.

Thousands march in Haiti to praise El Salvador's President Bukele as his troops take on gangs

18 Nov 2026, 14:49 GMT

Thousands of Haitians took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other cities on Tuesday to praise El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his soldiers, who are leading a tough new offensive against the gangs that have terrorised Haiti for years.

Read more

Thousands of people took to the streets of Port-au-Prince and other parts of Haiti on Tuesday, praising El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele and his troops as they take on the country's powerful gangs.

"Viv Bukele"

Photos from the marches show huge crowds filling the streets, waving Haitian flags and holding up posters of the Salvadoran president. Many carried banners reading "Viv Bukele", Haitian Creole for "Long live Bukele".

Why Haitians are cheering

For years, armed gangs have controlled large parts of Port-au-Prince and beyond, killing, kidnapping and forcing hundreds of thousands of people from their homes.

Salvadoran troops have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an international mission, and recently launched their first major offensive against the gangs, with reports of heavy fighting and deaths.

Many marchers said Bukele is doing what the United Nations and the United States failed to do for years, while ordinary Haitians continued to suffer.

The Bukele model

Bukele became popular at home by cracking down hard on El Salvador's gangs, sharply reducing violence in a country once known as one of the most dangerous in the world. Many Haitians hope his forces can bring the same results.

His methods have also faced criticism from human rights groups, who say the crackdown in El Salvador involved mass arrests and abuses. But for Haitians who have lived in fear for years, many say any hope of relief is welcome.
