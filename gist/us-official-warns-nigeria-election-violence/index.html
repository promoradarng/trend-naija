US official tells Congress Nigeria's January election 'could spur violence'

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

The United States says it will keep a close watch on Nigeria's 2027 presidential election, with a senior official warning that it could trigger violence.

What was said

Frank Garcia, the Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs, made the remarks on Thursday, 12 November 2026, in prepared testimony before the House Foreign Affairs Subcommittee on Africa during a hearing on violence in Nigeria. He said he was realistic about the challenges ahead and that the January presidential election could spur violence. He also pointed to an overstretched security system, limited reach of the courts in the Middle Belt and a lack of resources as obstacles to tackling insecurity.

Congress weighs in

Congressman Riley Moore used the hearing to call for close American attention to how the election unfolds.

Background

ECOWAS has appointed former Ghanaian President Nana Akufo-Addo to lead its observer mission for the polls.

Religious freedom concerns

The hearing, chaired by Congressman Chris Smith, also examined Nigeria's response to attacks on communities a year after the US designated it a country of particular concern over religious freedom. State Department official Riley Barnes said Nigeria had taken steps to curb the violence but that safety for Christian communities in the Middle Belt had not yet improved, describing the US approach as trust but verify. Congressman Riley Moore called for the designation to be kept.

Report records 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in four months

26 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

A civic group says it has documented 124 incidents of election-related violence and 22 deaths across Nigeria since July. It warned the trend could worsen as the January 2027 elections approach.

Read more

A non-partisan civic organisation has warned of rising political violence in Nigeria, saying it recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths in the past four months.

The figures

In a statement in Abuja on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, the Kimpact Development Initiative (KDI) said the incidents were documented between 1 July and 23 November 2026 through its election violence monitoring project. Incidents rose from three in July to 48 in October, with another 40 recorded in the first half of November alone.

The warning

The group warned that the trend could worsen as the 16 January 2027 elections draw closer unless political parties, security agencies and other stakeholders take urgent action. It pointed to growing political intolerance and inflammatory rhetoric among politicians and their supporters as key triggers.

KDI stressed that the problem cuts across parties and should not be blamed on any single group, and urged candidates to restrain their supporters.

Background

The report comes days after deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states, and after INEC warned that the popular 'rig and die' slogan could endanger polling staff and voters. The House of Representatives has also warned that a state of emergency may be needed in Delta if violence continues.
