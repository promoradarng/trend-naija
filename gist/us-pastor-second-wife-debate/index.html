Pastor Jimmy Odukoya, the Senior Pastor of The Fountain of Life Church, has reportedly introduced his new wife to the congregation, bringing fresh attention to his personal life and to the church's leadership.

Photos from the occasion show the couple dressed in matching red outfits, smiling together on the church stage.

Background

For those who may not know, Pastor Jimmy was previously married to Kemi Odukoya, and the marriage produced two children. That marriage reportedly ended some years ago.

His sister, who also serves as an associate pastor in the church, has spoken publicly about the breakdown of her own marriage, including her personal experiences and how her faith in God carried her through that difficult period.

The Fountain of Life Church was founded by their late parents, Pastors Taiwo and Bimbo Odukoya, and the children have continued the family's ministry.

A question about double standards

The news raises a question about how marriage and divorce are discussed in some Nigerian churches.

Many religious institutions regularly remind ordinary members that marriage is sacred and that the Bible says God hates divorce. Some believers are even made to feel guilty for leaving marriages that have become unbearable.

Yet when divorce happens within prominent pastoral families, the conversation often becomes more understanding. Suddenly, people are encouraged to consider personal circumstances, healing, forgiveness and new beginnings.

Perhaps they should. Pastors are human beings too. But shouldn't ordinary church members receive that same understanding?

Finding love again

There is nothing wrong with anyone finding love again after a failed marriage. The concern is the apparent gap between what some churches preach to their members and how they respond when their own leaders face similar situations.

So the question is this: if divorce can be understood and accepted when it involves church leaders, why should ordinary Christians sometimes be made to feel condemned for the same decision?

Federal government's CREDICORP unveils SPARK loans for people without smartphones

1 Nov 2026, 11:44 GMT

The Nigerian Consumer Credit Corporation has unveiled SPARK, a credit product that lets people borrow through USSD on basic mobile phones, without internet access. It is aimed at first-time and low-income borrowers and is expected to be widely available before the end of 2026.

Read more

Nigerians without smartphones or internet access may soon be able to borrow money through a new government-backed scheme.

What was unveiled

The Nigerian Consumer Credit Corporation (CREDICORP) launched SPARK at a 'CREDICORP for All' event in Aba, Abia State, attended by more than 1,000 residents, according to reports on Sunday, 1 November 2026. The product works on USSD, the basic mobile service available on simple feature phones, allowing eligible users to start credit transactions without a data connection.

Who it is for

SPARK is aimed at first-time borrowers, low-income earners and others who are poorly served by traditional banks. CREDICORP says it will also help users build formal credit histories by borrowing and repaying responsibly. Loan sizes, interest rates and eligibility rules were not disclosed, and the USSD code has not yet been published. CREDICORP expects the product to be widely available before the end of 2026.

Who spoke

CREDICORP's chief executive, Uzoma Nwagba, spoke at the event, along with a representative of Abia Governor Alex Otti and an official of the Ministry of Finance Incorporated.

Background

CREDICORP was set up in 2024 to expand access to consumer credit, so that Nigerians can buy goods such as phones, solar systems and household items and pay over time. Many Nigerians rely on online loan apps, some of which have been criticised for high interest rates and harsh debt-recovery methods.

A warning

Officials urged people to wait for official announcements and to avoid unverified platforms claiming to register people for SPARK loans.
