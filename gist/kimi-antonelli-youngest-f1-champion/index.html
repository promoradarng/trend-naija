Kimi Antonelli, 20, becomes youngest Formula 1 world champion

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

Italian driver Kimi Antonelli has become the youngest world champion in the history of Formula 1, according to reports, after sealing the title at the Mexico City Grand Prix on Sunday, 1 November 2026.

What happened

Antonelli, who drives for Mercedes, needed only a ninth-place finish to make sure of the championship. He finished third, behind race winner Max Verstappen of Red Bull and Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, which put the title out of reach of his rivals with four races of the season still left. Hamilton and his Mercedes team-mate George Russell had been his closest challengers.

Records broken

At 20 years and 68 days, Antonelli breaks the record set by Sebastian Vettel, who was 23 years and 134 days old when he won his first title with Red Bull in 2010. He is also the first Italian to win the drivers' championship since Alberto Ascari in 1953, ending a wait of 73 years for the country. Remarkably, he is only in his second season in Formula 1.

What he said

Celebrating with his family and the Mercedes team, Antonelli reportedly thanked both, saying he would not be there without them. Verstappen was among the first to congratulate him and said he was very proud of the young driver, adding that it must be an emotional day for his family.

Background

Antonelli was picked by Mercedes as a teenager to replace Hamilton, who moved to Ferrari in 2025. Many questioned whether he was ready for such a big seat, but he has now delivered the team's first drivers' title since Hamilton's last championship in 2020.

What happens next

The remaining four races will decide the other places in the drivers' standings and the constructors' championship.

  • Antonelli won the title at 20 years and 68 days.
  • He beats Sebastian Vettel's 2010 record.
  • He is Italy's first F1 champion since 1953.

Marathon world champion Peres Jepchirchir banned for three years for doping

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Kenya's Peres Jepchirchir, the reigning marathon world champion and Tokyo Olympic gold medallist, has been banned for three years after testing positive for EPO. Two other Kenyan runners were also banned.

Read more

Reigning marathon world champion Peres Jepchirchir of Kenya has been banned for three years for doping, the Athletics Integrity Unit (AIU) announced on Thursday, 19 November 2026.

The ban

The 33-year-old, who also won Olympic gold at the Tokyo Games in 2021, was suspended after an out-of-competition sample showed traces of the banned blood booster EPO. Her ban runs from 20 October.

Others banned

Two other Kenyan runners were also sanctioned. Veronica Loleo, a half marathon silver medallist at this year's World Road Running Championships, received three years for the same offence, while Ronald Korir, fourth at the 2023 Berlin Marathon, was banned for six years over abnormalities in his biological passport.

A wider problem

They join more than 140 Kenyan athletes, including women's marathon world record-holder Ruth Chepngetich, who have been suspended or banned by the AIU since 2017. The AIU acknowledged that Kenyan authorities have made progress against doping but said these cases show there is still a long way to go.

FIFA appoints normalisation committee to run NFF, names Amokachi and Enakhena

19 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

FIFA has named lawyer Sampson Ebomhe to chair a normalisation committee for the Nigeria Football Federation ahead of its 2027 elections. Daniel Amokachi, Godwin Enakhena, Amina Yerima and Joe Abah are also members.

Read more

FIFA has set up a normalisation committee to run the Nigeria Football Federation (NFF), appointing lawyer Sampson Ebomhe as chairman with immediate effect.

The members

The world governing body named four other members: sports journalist and administrator Godwin Enakhena, former Super Eagles captain Daniel Amokachi, Amina Yerima and Dr Joe Abah, former Director-General of the Bureau of Public Service Reforms.

FIFA's Chief Member Associations Officer, Elkhan Mammadov, confirmed the appointments in a letter, noting that all members are still subject to an eligibility check by the FIFA Review Committee before they are formally confirmed.

Background

Amokachi, nicknamed The Bull, played at two World Cups for Nigeria and is one of the country's best-known football figures.

Nigeria's Super Eagles have moved up one place to 29th in the latest FIFA world rankings, ending their 2026 international calendar with a small boost.

The ranking

The Super Eagles climbed from 30th to 29th in the world and moved back into Africa's top five, after slipping out of that group earlier in the year.

The results behind it

Nigeria beat Tanzania 3-1 in Uyo before playing out a goalless draw in the return leg in Zanzibar. The draw left them on seven points, three behind group leaders Guinea-Bissau, making their path to AFCON 2027 more complicated.
