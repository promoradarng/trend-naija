Kimi Antonelli, 20, becomes youngest Formula 1 world champion

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

Italian driver Kimi Antonelli has become the youngest world champion in the history of Formula 1, according to reports, after sealing the title at the Mexico City Grand Prix on Sunday, 1 November 2026.

What happened

Antonelli, who drives for Mercedes, needed only a ninth-place finish to make sure of the championship. He finished third, behind race winner Max Verstappen of Red Bull and Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, which put the title out of reach of his rivals with four races of the season still left. Hamilton and his Mercedes team-mate George Russell had been his closest challengers.

Records broken

At 20 years and 68 days, Antonelli breaks the record set by Sebastian Vettel, who was 23 years and 134 days old when he won his first title with Red Bull in 2010. He is also the first Italian to win the drivers' championship since Alberto Ascari in 1953, ending a wait of 73 years for the country. Remarkably, he is only in his second season in Formula 1.

What he said

Celebrating with his family and the Mercedes team, Antonelli reportedly thanked both, saying he would not be there without them. Verstappen was among the first to congratulate him and said he was very proud of the young driver, adding that it must be an emotional day for his family.

Background

Antonelli was picked by Mercedes as a teenager to replace Hamilton, who moved to Ferrari in 2025. Many questioned whether he was ready for such a big seat, but he has now delivered the team's first drivers' title since Hamilton's last championship in 2020.

What happens next

The remaining four races will decide the other places in the drivers' standings and the constructors' championship.

  • Antonelli won the title at 20 years and 68 days.
  • He beats Sebastian Vettel's 2010 record.
  • He is Italy's first F1 champion since 1953.

Manchester United are increasingly expected to sack head coach Michael Carrick after a poor start to the 2026/27 season.

What is reported

According to reports on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox are expected to recommend to minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazer family that the 45-year-old be dismissed. No final decision has been made.

Poor results

Flamingos get $50,000 reward, but no FIFA prize money for World Cup win

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Nigeria's U-17 world champions, the Flamingos, have received a $50,000 cash gift from a sponsor of the National Sports Commission at a reception in Rabat. FIFA does not pay prize money for the tournament because it is classed as a developmental competition.

Read more

Nigeria's Flamingos have received a $50,000 cash reward after winning the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup, but the team will not receive any official prize money from FIFA.

The reward

The gift was presented by Alhaji Saleh Ahmadu, a sponsor of the National Sports Commission (NSC), at a reception held in the team's honour in Rabat, Morocco, on Saturday night, 7 November 2026, according to reports. The event was hosted by NSC chairman Shehu Dikko and director-general Bukola Olopade, and was also attended by the Comptroller-General of the Nigeria Customs Service, Bashir Adewale Adeniyi. The sponsor said the gift recognised the players' outstanding performance, commitment and determination.

No FIFA prize money

FIFA classifies the U-17 Women's World Cup as a developmental tournament and does not attach official prize money to it, unlike the senior World Cups. That means the champions receive the trophy and medals but no payment from world football's governing body.

How the final was won

The Flamingos beat Japan 2-1 in the final on Saturday. Awawu Bashiru opened the scoring when her looping effort slipped through the hands of Japan's goalkeeper, and Oluebube Umejiaku doubled the lead with a lob after a through ball from Esther Stephen. Japan's Fuki Ito pulled one back with ten minutes left, but Nigeria held on to become the first African winners of the tournament.

US Congress to hold hearing on Nigeria's protection of Christians and the Middle Belt

10 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

A US House subcommittee chaired by Congressman Chris Smith will hold a hearing on Thursday, 12 November, to assess Nigeria's efforts to protect Christians and curb violence in the Middle Belt, with State Department officials due to testify.

Read more

A United States congressional committee will hold a hearing this week to review Nigeria's record on protecting Christians and tackling violence in the Middle Belt.

The hearing

The House Foreign Affairs Africa Subcommittee will hold the session on Thursday, 12 November 2026, according to a notice from the office of its chairman, Republican Congressman Chris Smith of New Jersey, issued on Monday. The hearing is titled 'One Year Later: Assessing Nigeria's Efforts to Protect Christians and Secure the Middle Belt'.

What will be discussed

The hearing will examine religious violence by extremist groups, including Boko Haram, and how effectively the Nigerian government has responded to attacks on vulnerable communities. It will also consider what measures the United States could press for to make sure perpetrators are held accountable and to prevent future attacks on Christians and non-radical Muslims. The first panel will hear from two senior State Department officials, Frank Garcia, assistant secretary of state for African affairs, and Riley Barnes, assistant secretary of state for democracy, human rights and labour, followed by outside experts.

Background

The title refers to developments around a year ago, when the US designated Nigeria a 'Country of Particular Concern' over religious freedom. The Nigerian government has rejected claims of a targeted genocide against Christians, saying insecurity affects people of all faiths, and has pointed to military operations against armed groups. Attacks have continued in states such as Plateau and Benue, including a deadly attack in Barkin Ladi at the weekend.

Republican congressman files articles of impeachment against Pete Hegseth over Iran war

10 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Republican Congressman Thomas Massie has introduced articles of impeachment against US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, accusing him of waging war against Iran without congressional authorisation, as criticism of the conflict grows after the midterms.

Read more

Articles of impeachment have been introduced in the US House of Representatives against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

What happened

Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky brought the resolution on Monday, 9 November 2026, as Congress returned for a 'lame-duck' session after the midterm elections. Massie accused Hegseth of high crimes and misdemeanours and abuse of power, alleging that he violated the War Powers Resolution by carrying out military operations against Iran and other countries without a declaration of war or specific authorisation from Congress.

Background

The United States has been in a conflict with Iran since late February, with US forces carrying out strikes on Iranian targets as recently as last week. President Donald Trump has insisted the US is not at war, but has acknowledged that the conflict contributed to his party's heavy losses in the midterms, partly by pushing up petrol prices.

What happens next

Impeachment would require a majority vote in the House, followed by a trial in the Senate.
