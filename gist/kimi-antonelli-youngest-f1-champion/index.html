Kimi Antonelli, 20, becomes youngest Formula 1 world champion

2 Nov 2026, 14:56 GMT

Italian driver Kimi Antonelli has become the youngest world champion in the history of Formula 1, according to reports, after sealing the title at the Mexico City Grand Prix on Sunday, 1 November 2026.

What happened

Antonelli, who drives for Mercedes, needed only a ninth-place finish to make sure of the championship. He finished third, behind race winner Max Verstappen of Red Bull and Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, which put the title out of reach of his rivals with four races of the season still left. Hamilton and his Mercedes team-mate George Russell had been his closest challengers.

Records broken

At 20 years and 68 days, Antonelli breaks the record set by Sebastian Vettel, who was 23 years and 134 days old when he won his first title with Red Bull in 2010. He is also the first Italian to win the drivers' championship since Alberto Ascari in 1953, ending a wait of 73 years for the country. Remarkably, he is only in his second season in Formula 1.

What he said

Celebrating with his family and the Mercedes team, Antonelli reportedly thanked both, saying he would not be there without them. Verstappen was among the first to congratulate him and said he was very proud of the young driver, adding that it must be an emotional day for his family.

Background

Antonelli was picked by Mercedes as a teenager to replace Hamilton, who moved to Ferrari in 2025. Many questioned whether he was ready for such a big seat, but he has now delivered the team's first drivers' title since Hamilton's last championship in 2020.

What happens next

The remaining four races will decide the other places in the drivers' standings and the constructors' championship.

  • Antonelli won the title at 20 years and 68 days.
  • He beats Sebastian Vettel's 2010 record.
  • He is Italy's first F1 champion since 1953.

Super Eagles forward Ademola Lookman has come under fire from Nigerian fans after a video reportedly showed him socialising with Burna Boy in Lagos on the same evening the national team drew 0-0 with Tanzania.

Why fans are angry

Lookman had been excused from the squad before the second leg in Zanzibar, with the team citing a knee injury he picked up in the first leg in Uyo on 13 November. He was said to have been sent back to his club, Atletico Madrid, so its medical staff could assess the problem.

No response yet

Juan Mata retires from football at 38

18 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Former Chelsea and Manchester United midfielder Juan Mata has announced his retirement at the age of 38. He won the World Cup and Euro 2012 with Spain and the Champions League with Chelsea.

Read more

Former Spain, Chelsea and Manchester United midfielder Juan Mata has announced his retirement from professional football at the age of 38.

The announcement

Mata confirmed the decision on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, with a farewell video on X in which he symbolically hung up his boots. He thanked the game, saying that sometimes loving something means letting it go.

A decorated career

Mata was part of the Spain squads that won the 2010 World Cup and Euro 2012. After coming through Real Madrid's youth system and breaking through at Valencia, he joined Chelsea in 2011, where he won the Champions League and FA Cup in 2012 and the Europa League in 2013.

He moved to Manchester United in 2014 and made 285 appearances over eight seasons, winning the FA Cup in 2016 and the League Cup and Europa League in 2017.

Later years

After leaving United in 2022, he won domestic titles with Galatasaray in Turkey and Vissel Kobe in Japan, before finishing his career in Australia with Western Sydney Wanderers and Melbourne Victory, where he also took an ownership stake in the club earlier this year.

Flamingos coach Busari nominated for CAF Women's Coach of the Year

18 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Akeem Busari, who led the Flamingos to the FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup title, has been nominated for CAF Women's Coach of the Year. The awards take place on 2 December.

Read more

Flamingos head coach Akeem Busari has been nominated for the CAF Women's Coach of the Year award after leading Nigeria to victory at the 2026 FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup in Morocco.

The shortlist

Busari is one of 10 coaches on the shortlist. Others include Algeria's Farid Benstiti, Cameroon's Valentine Nguele, Ghana's Kim Lars Bjorkegren, Morocco's Jorge Vilda, South Africa's Desiree Ellis and Tanzania's Bakari Nyundo Shime, as well as Silveria Nedio of Cabo Verde, Lovemore Fazili of Malawi and Hicham Drissi of Moroccan club AS FAR.

His achievement

Busari guided the Flamingos to Nigeria's first U-17 Women's World Cup title, making them the first African team to win the competition after beating Japan 2-1 in the final.

When the winners are announced

The 2026 CAF Awards will take place on Wednesday, 2 December, at the Professor Wole Soyinka Centre for Culture and the Arts.
