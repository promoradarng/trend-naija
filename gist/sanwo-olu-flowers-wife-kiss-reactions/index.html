Woman sues MTN after father is arrested twice over recycled phone number

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A woman has reportedly taken MTN to court after her father was arrested twice because the phone number he was given had previously been used by someone linked to crimes. The case has raised concerns about recycled numbers.

Read more

A Nigerian woman has reportedly sued telecommunications company MTN after her father was arrested twice over a phone number he received.

What happened

According to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026, MTN reassigned a recycled phone number to her father. Unknown to him, the number had allegedly been used by a previous owner for criminal activity. As a result, he was reportedly arrested on two separate occasions over matters he had no knowledge of. His daughter has now taken legal action, holding MTN responsible for the distress the family suffered. MTN has not publicly commented on the case.

Why it matters

Telecom operators in Nigeria, as in many countries, recycle numbers that have been inactive for a long period and reassign them to new customers. The case has raised questions about whether operators should check that numbers are not linked to criminal investigations before giving them out, and about how security agencies verify that the current holder of a number is the person they are looking for.

Background

Nigeria requires all SIM cards to be linked to the National Identification Number, a policy meant to make it easier to trace criminals. However, problems with outdated records and recycled numbers have led to complaints from people who receive calls, messages or even police visits meant for previous owners.

How people reacted

Many social media users shared similar experiences with recycled numbers and said they hoped the case would force telecom companies to change their practices.

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

The mother's alleged role

What makes the story even more disturbing is the allegation that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allegedly allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth. The account further claims that she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal.

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

If these allegations are established, serious questions must be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Kano court orders TikToker to clean traffic agency offices for two weeks over defamation

4 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

A Kano magistrate court has reportedly ordered a TikTok user to clean the headquarters of the state traffic agency, KAROTA, for two weeks and write an apology, after finding that his videos defamed the agency's officials.

Read more

A magistrate court in Kano has handed an unusual sentence to a TikTok user over videos that ridiculed officials of the state's traffic agency.

The sentence

According to the Kano State Road Traffic Agency (KAROTA), the court found that the TikTok user published videos containing statements that damaged the image of its personnel and exposed them to public ridicule. Magistrate Halima Wali ordered him to clean the agency's headquarters on Club Road, Kano, for two weeks and to carry out any other task the agency considers appropriate. He was also ordered to write a formal letter of apology to KAROTA's management and promise not to repeat the conduct. The agency's spokesman, Abubakar Ibrahim Sharada, confirmed the sentence.

Background

Non-custodial sentences such as community service are increasingly used by Nigerian courts for minor offences, partly to reduce overcrowding in prisons. Content creators have faced a rising number of cases over online posts in recent years, often under defamation or cybercrime laws. Rights groups have warned that such laws can be used to silence critics, while authorities say people must be held responsible for false or damaging content.

How people reacted

Many social media users found the sentence amusing and said it was a fair alternative to jail, while others said it showed the risks content creators face when they criticise officials.

He recalled that he became heavily involved in demanding answers after Mohbad's death in September 2023, when questions about the circumstances of the death and the singer's earlier complaints about his former record label drew huge public attention. VDM said Davido followed him on social media in early 2024, which excited him, and that they became friends, with VDM supporting and promoting some of the singer's music. He said their relationship later changed, with the Mohbad matter at the heart of the fallout.

The current feud

The explanation comes amid an escalating public feud. This week VDM dared Davido's camp to show proof that the singer ever helped him financially, warned Zlatan Ibile after Davido was filmed singing along to Zlatan's song 'Inferno', and released a preview of his own diss track aimed at Davido. Several celebrities, including Oyemykke and Roskey, have weighed in.

How people reacted

Fans were divided, with some saying VDM's account explained the bitterness, and others urging both men to end the public quarrel.
