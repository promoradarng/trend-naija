Rapper Odumodublvck has spoken about a court case brought against him by a man he says repeatedly filmed him without permission.

His account

According to reports on Monday, 9 November 2026, the rapper said the man gained access to his home through a mutual friend and began filming him without his consent, continuing even after warnings from him and his bouncer. He said the disagreement continued at a party when the man brought out his camera again, and that he confronted him on stage, but that they later settled their differences, with the man even dancing with him while still filming. Odumodublvck said he was surprised when the man later took him to court, accusing him of assault.

Refusing to settle

The rapper said his lawyers advised him to settle the matter out of court, but he refused. The man's side of the story has not been reported, and the allegations have not been tested in court.

Background

Odumodublvck, is an Abuja-based rapper known for hits such as 'Declan Rice'. He went viral last week after a brief encounter with Peter Obi at Abuja airport.

At least 20 people have reportedly been killed in an operation involving Salvadoran troops targeting suspected gang members in Haiti.

A country held hostage

The reported incident comes as Haiti continues to struggle with widespread gang violence, kidnappings and insecurity, particularly in areas where armed groups have taken control of communities and forced thousands of residents to flee.

For years, ordinary Haitians have lived under the constant threat of armed gangs, with many families unable to go about their daily lives without fear.

Foreign forces on the ground

Soldiers from El Salvador have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an internationally backed mission to help the Haitian police fight the gangs, alongside forces from other countries.

The reported operation has raised fresh questions about the growing role of foreign security forces in efforts to restore order in the troubled Caribbean nation.

What we don't know

The circumstances of the reported deaths, including the identities of those killed and the exact role played by the Salvadoran troops, have not been independently confirmed.

Tinubu launches ₦52,500 minimum monthly pension for 173,209 retirees

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

President Tinubu has activated a guaranteed minimum pension of ₦52,500 a month for 173,209 retirees under the Contributory Pension Scheme, a provision that has been in law since 2004 but was never enforced. He also launched free basic healthcare for low-income pensioners.

Read more

Retirees under Nigeria's Contributory Pension Scheme will now receive at least ₦52,500 a month, after President Bola Tinubu activated a minimum pension guarantee in Abuja.

What was announced

At the Presidential Pension Security Initiatives ceremony at the State House on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the President, represented by Senate President Godswill Akpabio, declared the guarantee active for 173,209 qualifying retirees. He described it as a floor beneath Nigerian retirees and urged future governments to raise it but never remove it. The Director-General of the National Pension Commission (PenCom), Omolola Oloworaran, confirmed the figure and said more than 65 per cent of private-sector retirees currently earn less than the new minimum.

Who benefits

According to PenCom, 54 per cent of the qualifying retirees come from the private sector, 34 per cent from federal service and 12 per cent from state employment. More than ₦400bn in the Pension Protection Fund will finance the guarantee.

Healthcare and arrears

The President also launched PenCare, a healthcare plan that will give low-income pensioners free access to essential health services, with access cards to be distributed in the coming months. He said the government had issued a ₦758bn bond to clear pension arrears and approved a ₦32,000 monthly adjustment for affected federal pensioners.

Background

The minimum pension was first written into the Pension Reform Act of 2004 and kept in the 2014 law, but no government had put it into effect until now.

Police open probe after video shows officers escorting Blord

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Nigeria Police Force says it has opened an investigation after a video showed police officers escorting businessman and influencer Blord, describing the arrangement as unlawful. Some social media users have defended him.

Read more

The Nigeria Police Force has launched an investigation into officers seen in a video providing an escort for businessman and social media personality Linus Williams, popularly known as Blord.

What the police said

In a post on X on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the Force Public Relations Officer described the situation as unlawful and illegal, added the hashtag #NoToImpunity, and confirmed that a probe had begun. The statement did not make clear exactly which part of the incident is being investigated.

Background

The video drew attention after social media users pointed out that Blord had recently been seen in caps associated with Rabiu Kwankwaso, running mate to Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), fuelling speculation about his political leanings. Blord was recently released from detention and publicly thanked activist Omoyele Sowore, now a presidential candidate himself, for his intervention. In late 2025, President Bola Tinubu ordered the withdrawal of police officers attached to VIPs.

How people reacted

The police announcement drew strong reactions online, with some users defending Blord. Blord had not publicly responded at the time of writing.
