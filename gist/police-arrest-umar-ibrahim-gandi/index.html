BREAKING: Police arrest Umar Ibrahim Gandi over viral video calling for AK-47s to attack worshippers in Enugu and Jos

27 Oct 2026, 17:21 GMT

The Zamfara State Police Command has arrested Umar Ibrahim Gandi, the man seen in a viral video calling for AK-47 rifles to carry out attacks against Christians in Enugu State and Jos, Plateau State.

Police identified the suspect as a 22-year-old native of Maradun Local Government Area of Zamfara State. They say the video was recorded on Friday, October 23, 2026, and that he faces allegations of incitement to violence, disturbance of public peace and criminal intimidation. The Commissioner of Police said an investigation is under way and that the suspect will be charged before a court.

In the video, the suspect, wearing the uniform of the JIBWIS (Izala) first-aid group, is heard chanting for rifles to kill people he calls "infidels" during Sunday worship. According to the police, JIBWIS has disassociated itself from his alleged conduct.

His statements raised serious concerns about religious extremism, hate speech and threats to innocent lives, and the video attracted widespread condemnation, with many Nigerians calling on the DSS and the police to identify and arrest him.

Particularly troubling was the presence of young children during the recording, raising questions about the exposure of minors to violent extremist messages.

Nigerians will now expect a thorough investigation and appropriate legal action based on the evidence. Like any suspect, he is presumed innocent until proven guilty in court.

Nigeria cannot afford to treat public threats of violence against religious communities as ordinary social media content. Nobody should be threatened with death simply because of where they come from or how they worship God.

Lagos police detain officer after two-year-old girl is shot dead in Ajegunle

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Lagos police have detained an assistant superintendent after a shooting in Ajegunle killed a two-year-old girl and injured another girl. Police say he fired his weapon while responding to a clash between rival groups.

Read more

The Lagos State Police Command has detained an officer after a shooting in Ajegunle left a two-year-old girl dead and another girl injured.

What happened

The shooting happened between 9.30pm and 10pm on Thursday, 5 November 2026, in the Nosamu area of Ajeromi-Ifelodun Local Government Area. In a statement on Saturday, police spokesperson SP Abimbola Adebisi said the officer, an assistant superintendent attached to Ajeromi Police Station, had been disarmed and detained, and that the State Criminal Investigation Department had taken over the case.

Police account

According to the police, preliminary findings showed that the officer and his team were responding to a distress call about a violent clash between two groups said to be fighting over toll collection and armed with cutlasses and other weapons. The officer discharged his firearm during the operation, and the bullet struck both girls. Police said the injured girl was receiving treatment and was in a stable condition. Initial reports suggested the officer may have been drunk, but the police statement did not confirm this.

Family's grief

The mother of the girl who died has publicly mourned her daughter in a video shared online.

Background

Killings of civilians by police officers, often involving the misuse of firearms, have long caused public anger in Nigeria and were among the issues behind the 2020 #EndSARS protests. This week, the Police Service Commission dismissed three senior officers and demoted 16 others over misconduct.

What happens next

The police said the investigation would determine further action against the officer.

Germany to add visa counters and appointment slots in Nigeria

7 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Germany's consulate in Lagos says it will add visa counters before the end of the year, increase appointment slots and set up a document verification centre for students, describing Nigeria as a priority partner.

Read more

Germany says it will expand visa processing in Nigeria to make it easier for Nigerians to travel to the country.

What was announced

Speaking at the German National Day reception in Lagos, reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, Consul General Daniel Krull said the consulate would add more visa counters before the end of the year and work with external service providers to increase the number of appointment slots. He said Germany was also moving more applications to a dedicated processing centre in Germany to reduce delays.

Help for students

Krull said a document verification centre would be set up in Lagos to check the documents of Nigerians applying to German universities and research institutions. He noted that German public universities generally charge little or no tuition fees, making them an affordable option for international students.

A priority partner

Krull described Nigeria as Germany's most important trading partner in West Africa and a priority partner in the region, pointing to recent visits by German officials and business delegations.

Background

Long waits for visa appointments have been a major complaint among Nigerians applying to European countries, and Germany has become an increasingly popular destination for Nigerian students and skilled workers, partly because of low tuition fees and labour shortages in sectors such as health care. The announcement contrasts with some other countries that have recently tightened rules for Nigerian travellers.

What happens next

No dates were given for when the new centre will open.

Two Mexicans and four Nigerians remanded over alleged meth lab in Oyo forest

7 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Ibadan has remanded two Mexican nationals and four Nigerians charged over an alleged methamphetamine laboratory in Tapa village, Oyo State. All six pleaded not guilty.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Ibadan has ordered that two Mexican nationals and four Nigerians be held in custody over an alleged methamphetamine laboratory in Oyo State.

The charges

The National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) said in a statement on Friday, 6 November 2026, that the six were arraigned on four counts relating to the alleged unlawful transportation and distribution of chemicals meant for making methamphetamine. The laboratory was allegedly located in a forest in Tapa village, Ibarapa North Local Government Area. All six pleaded not guilty, and the judge ordered that they be remanded at a correctional centre in Ibadan.

The arrests

According to the NDLEA, one of the Mexicans and the four Nigerians were arrested on 17 June 2026, while the second Mexican was arrested on 19 August at the Lagos airport, allegedly while trying to leave the country. The agency had earlier alleged that one of the Mexicans was brought into Nigeria to provide technical expertise for large-scale production, while the Nigerian suspects handled logistics and local operations. The allegations are yet to be tested at trial.

Background

Methamphetamine, popularly known in Nigeria as 'mkpuru mmiri' or 'ice', has become a serious public health concern, especially in the South-East. The NDLEA has in recent years uncovered several laboratories it says were set up with the help of foreign experts. In September, the agency said it had discovered an industrial-scale methamphetamine laboratory in Ebonyi State.

Enugu governor's wife sends representative to Ukehe woman with financial support, promises new house and business

6 Nov 2026, 23:49 GMT

Mrs Nkechinyere Mbah, wife of the Enugu State governor, has sent a representative to a woman in Ukehe with an envelope of cash, and promised to build her a house and help her set up a business so she can support herself.

Read more

A woman from Ukehe in Enugu State has received financial assistance from the wife of the state governor, Mrs Nkechinyere Mbah, who sent a representative to visit her with promises that could significantly improve her living conditions.

Cash, a house and a business

During the visit, the representative handed the woman an envelope containing cash on behalf of the governor's wife. But the help did not end there.

Mrs Mbah also promised to build a house for the woman and to help her set up a business that would give her a steady income, so she can take care of herself without depending on others to survive.

Photos from the visit show women celebrating with raised hands outside a building in Ukehe, and an envelope being handed over as a crowd gathers around.

Support that lasts

For someone facing difficult living conditions, a decent roof and a business that can put food on the table could make a lasting difference.

What makes the gesture stand out is the promise of long-term support. Money solves an immediate problem, but helping someone build a business can give them a livelihood for years to come.

Remembering the vulnerable

The visit has also drawn attention to the importance of identifying vulnerable people in our communities and offering help that goes beyond temporary relief.

Many Nigerians are struggling quietly, especially elderly women, widows and people without a reliable income. Sometimes what they need most is a chance to become financially independent.

For the Ukehe woman, the hope now is that the promises are fulfilled and that she soon has a comfortable home and a business of her own.
