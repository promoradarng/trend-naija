FCT Minister Nyesom Wike and his Rainbow Coalition campaign have got people talking again, this time after he appeared visibly angry at the presidential campaign ground in Rivers State.

One can only feel sorry for any aide who collected mobilisation money and failed to deliver the expected crowd.

The turnout question

The expectation may have been a stadium filled to capacity, but the turnout has now become a talking point on social media.

That brings us to Peter Obi, whose supporters often describe his campaign crowds as organic, with people attending out of personal conviction rather than financial inducement.

Can anyone fill a stadium for free?

Crowd mobilisation, from buses to refreshments to small payments, has long been a feature of Nigerian political rallies across parties. Large crowds make for impressive pictures, but they do not always reflect how people will vote.

So one question remains: can any Nigerian politician genuinely fill a stadium without spending money on crowd mobilisation?

Peter Obi corrects claims that his Washington visit was an endorsement from the US Congress

18 Nov 2026, 16:43 GMT

Peter Obi says his recent trip to Washington involved talks on Nigeria's development, citizens' welfare and democracy, and that he neither sought nor announced any endorsement, after social media posts claimed the US Congress had backed him.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has corrected claims that his recent visit to Washington amounted to an endorsement from the United States Congress.

What Obi said

According to Obi, the trip involved discussions about Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and the state of democracy. He said he neither sought nor announced any endorsement.

He said he met serving and former US lawmakers and other stakeholders during the visit.

How the confusion started

The confusion followed a viral video in which retired US Army Colonel Albert Short praised Obi.

Some social media posts presented those personal remarks as if they were official backing from the US Congress, and the claim spread quickly online.

What the facts show

There is no public evidence that the US Congress as an institution has endorsed Obi's 2027 presidential bid. Congress does not endorse candidates in other countries' elections.

A meeting with politicians, or kind words from an individual, is not the same as an endorsement from their institution.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections drawing closer, claims about foreign support for candidates are likely to keep spreading on social media. Obi's correction is a reminder for supporters on all sides to check claims before sharing them.

Court rejects Atiku's bid to add new evidence to suit against Tinubu's eligibility

20 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has dismissed an application by Atiku Abubakar to introduce fresh evidence into his suit challenging President Tinubu's eligibility for 2027. The judge said the constitutional 14-day window had passed.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has thrown out an application by ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar to bring new evidence into his suit challenging President Bola Tinubu's eligibility to contest the 2027 election.

The ruling

Justice Inyang Ekwo held that Section 285(9) of the Constitution allows only 14 days for evidence to be filed in a pre-election case. He found that Atiku's request to amend his originating summons would effectively bring in fresh evidence after that period had expired, which would breach the Constitution.

What it means

The ruling is a setback for Atiku's legal challenge, although the main suit itself was not decided in the reports. Atiku's camp had not responded publicly to the ruling in the reports.

Background

Atiku is also involved in a separate legal battle over his ADC candidacy, after a court on 5 November voided the process that produced him as the party's candidate. INEC has said he will remain on its list until that dispute is settled.
