VeryDarkMan sets an example for Nigerian entrepreneurs with public demonstration of Ratel products

29 Oct 2026, 14:31 GMT

VeryDarkMan (VDM) deserves recognition for taking a bold approach to marketing his Ratel-branded products, particularly his newly introduced power stations.

At a time when many businesses rely heavily on advertisements, celebrity endorsements and social media publicity to attract customers, VDM has chosen to demonstrate what his products can actually do.

Rather than simply telling Nigerians how powerful and reliable his gadgets are, he has taken the initiative to publicly test their capabilities, showing potential buyers how they work and what they can expect from them.

This is a commendable business approach that other Nigerian entrepreneurs should consider adopting.

One of the biggest challenges facing consumers in Nigeria today is the difficulty of telling quality products apart from those that fail to meet expectations. Many people have spent their hard-earned money on gadgets that promised excellent performance but delivered disappointment.

That is why public product demonstrations matter. By putting his Ratel power stations to the test in front of his audience, VeryDarkMan is giving prospective customers a chance to assess their performance before buying.

Of course, a demonstration alone does not guarantee long-term durability or reliability. However, it provides a useful starting point for customers who want evidence beyond advertising claims.

There is also an important business lesson here: a company that is confident in its products should be willing to show customers what those products can do.

VDM appears to understand that building a successful brand requires more than having thousands of followers or generating excitement on social media. Customers must have reasons to trust what they are buying.

His approach could also encourage healthy competition among Nigerian businesses, particularly those selling electronic gadgets, power solutions and other consumer products. If more entrepreneurs publicly demonstrated their products under realistic conditions, buyers would be better equipped to compare options and make informed decisions.

VeryDarkMan may be known mainly for his social media activism and outspoken personality, but his latest business activities show another side of him as an entrepreneur seeking a place in Nigeria's competitive consumer market.

Whether you support VDM or disagree with his activities, one thing deserves acknowledgement: his willingness to demonstrate his products publicly is a positive example of consumer-focused marketing.

The real test will be whether the products continue to meet customers' expectations after purchase. For now, credit should be given where it is due.

Big kudos to VeryDarkMan! Nigerian entrepreneurs can learn something from this approach.

What do you think? Should companies be required to demonstrate the actual performance of their products before selling them to the public?

Nollywood actor Aremu Afolayan has become a talking point online after his appearance on comedian Isbae U's interview show, Curiosity Made Me Ask, reportedly ended in a heated exchange.

What happened

Isbae U, known for his sarcastic interview style, is said to have questioned whether Aremu was still active in Nollywood and made jokes about his body and how he earns a living given his few recent film roles.

Aremu reportedly explained that he has stayed away from film sets because of insecurity, saying travelling for shoots had become dangerous and he was not willing to risk his life for a role paying ₦2 million or ₦3 million.

Where it went wrong

The mood reportedly changed when Isbae U brought up his own late father, actor Sir K. Kamoru, and compared him with Aremu's late father, the veteran filmmaker Ade Love Afolayan. Aremu is said to have taken offence and warned the host against such comparisons, insisting his father's contribution to the film industry was not up for debate. He also reportedly questioned Isbae U's own achievements as an actor.

Tinubu announces home loans of up to ₦100m at 9.75% interest over 20 years

17 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Tinubu says eligible Nigerians can get home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed 9.75% interest rate, repayable over 20 years. Buyers must pay at least 10% of the property's value upfront.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has announced that eligible Nigerians can now access home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed interest rate of 9.75%, repayable over 20 years.

How it works

In a post on his X account on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the President reportedly said the loans are available through the ₦1 trillion MOFI Real Estate Investment Fund (MREIF). Applicants must pay a deposit of at least 10% of the property's value to qualify.

Using a ₦10 million home as an example, he said the buyer would pay ₦1 million upfront and then about ₦85,400 a month over the 20 years.

Extra options

He added that workers in the Contributory Pension Scheme can use up to 25% of their retirement savings towards the deposit, while married couples can apply jointly to qualify for bigger loans.

What happens next

Full eligibility rules and the application process were not detailed in the reports.

Nigeria's Minister of Defence, Christopher Musa, has reportedly said the United States is providing the Nigerian military with satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help locate militant leaders ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What the minister said

Speaking to journalists on Monday, 16 November 2026, Musa said troops had killed a number of bandit and terrorist commanders in overnight operations across the north-west and north-east. He described the operations as happening almost daily and said they were backed by much better intelligence.

He said the partnership with Washington had improved the military's ability to find and strike targets.

More equipment

The minister also said Nigeria is expecting more helicopters from the United States and Italy to support operations against insurgents in the north-east and armed bandits in the north-west.
