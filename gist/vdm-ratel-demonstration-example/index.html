VeryDarkMan sets an example for Nigerian entrepreneurs with public demonstration of Ratel products

29 Oct 2026, 14:31 GMT

VeryDarkMan (VDM) deserves recognition for taking a bold approach to marketing his Ratel-branded products, particularly his newly introduced power stations.

At a time when many businesses rely heavily on advertisements, celebrity endorsements and social media publicity to attract customers, VDM has chosen to demonstrate what his products can actually do.

Rather than simply telling Nigerians how powerful and reliable his gadgets are, he has taken the initiative to publicly test their capabilities, showing potential buyers how they work and what they can expect from them.

This is a commendable business approach that other Nigerian entrepreneurs should consider adopting.

One of the biggest challenges facing consumers in Nigeria today is the difficulty of telling quality products apart from those that fail to meet expectations. Many people have spent their hard-earned money on gadgets that promised excellent performance but delivered disappointment.

That is why public product demonstrations matter. By putting his Ratel power stations to the test in front of his audience, VeryDarkMan is giving prospective customers a chance to assess their performance before buying.

Of course, a demonstration alone does not guarantee long-term durability or reliability. However, it provides a useful starting point for customers who want evidence beyond advertising claims.

There is also an important business lesson here: a company that is confident in its products should be willing to show customers what those products can do.

VDM appears to understand that building a successful brand requires more than having thousands of followers or generating excitement on social media. Customers must have reasons to trust what they are buying.

His approach could also encourage healthy competition among Nigerian businesses, particularly those selling electronic gadgets, power solutions and other consumer products. If more entrepreneurs publicly demonstrated their products under realistic conditions, buyers would be better equipped to compare options and make informed decisions.

VeryDarkMan may be known mainly for his social media activism and outspoken personality, but his latest business activities show another side of him as an entrepreneur seeking a place in Nigeria's competitive consumer market.

Whether you support VDM or disagree with his activities, one thing deserves acknowledgement: his willingness to demonstrate his products publicly is a positive example of consumer-focused marketing.

The real test will be whether the products continue to meet customers' expectations after purchase. For now, credit should be given where it is due.

Big kudos to VeryDarkMan! Nigerian entrepreneurs can learn something from this approach.

What do you think? Should companies be required to demonstrate the actual performance of their products before selling them to the public?

INEC names nine states as hotspots for campaign violence

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

INEC Chairman Joash Amupitan has listed Lagos, Edo, Ekiti, Enugu, Ebonyi, Delta, Cross River, Oyo and Benue as states hit by campaign violence and political intolerance. He warned that the unrest could threaten preparations for the 2027 elections.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence and political intolerance ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The states

INEC Chairman Professor Joash Amupitan listed Lagos, Edo, Ekiti, Enugu, Ebonyi, Delta, Cross River, Oyo and Benue. He spoke on Wednesday in Abuja at a strategic election security conference organised with the Office of the National Security Adviser and attended by the heads of security agencies.

What he said

Amupitan said campaign convoys, town hall meetings, party offices and supporters had all come under attack in the listed states. He warned that the growing unrest could jeopardise preparations for the elections, and also raised concern about harassment, intimidation and assault of INEC officials and staff.

Background

The warning follows a series of violent incidents this month, including deadly attacks on NDC campaign events in Delta and Edo states. A civic group this week recorded 124 cases of electoral violence and 22 deaths since July, and INEC has also cautioned against the 'rig and die' slogan.

Dangote Refinery has reportedly cut its cooking gas gantry price to ₦1,000 per kg, with its marketers selling at ₦1,100. Consumers, however, are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kg at retail outlets.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery has reportedly reduced the wholesale price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), commonly known as cooking gas, to ₦1,000 per kilogram.

The new prices

Marketers linked to the refinery have lowered their selling price to ₦1,100 per kilogram, according to a market survey.

The retail gap

Despite the cuts, consumers are still paying between ₦1,600 and ₦1,800 per kilogram at retail outlets, raising concerns about the gap between wholesale and retail prices. Earlier this week, cooking gas was reported to be selling for up to ₦2,000 per kg in parts of Abuja.

Background

The refinery has also cut its petrol and diesel prices in recent weeks, most recently reducing diesel by ₦125 to ₦1,595 per litre.

NNPC cuts petrol price by ₦60 to ₦1,240 per litre in Abuja

27 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

NNPC has reportedly reduced its petrol pump price from ₦1,300 to ₦1,240 per litre at outlets in Abuja, undercutting rivals. The cut comes amid intense competition among marketers.

Read more

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPCL) has reduced its petrol pump price again, as competition among marketers intensifies.

The new price

A market survey on Thursday, 26 November 2026, showed that NNPC cut its price from ₦1,300 to ₦1,240 per litre, a reduction of ₦60. The new price was seen at several NNPC stations across the Federal Capital Territory, including Gwarinpa, the Kubwa Expressway, Airport Junction and Wuse Zone 6.

Competition

The cut undercuts other major marketers such as MRS and AA Rano, which have also reduced prices in recent days. Wholesale prices have fallen sharply, with some Lagos depots selling at about ₦1,055 per litre.

Background

Prices have been falling for weeks amid competition between Dangote Refinery and importers.

Vice President Kashim Shettima has reportedly taken a swipe at NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi, questioning why his hometown, Agulu, was not transformed during his eight years as governor of Anambra State.

What he said

Shettima made the remarks at a town hall meeting with traditional rulers in Isuofia, Anambra State, where he praised Governor Chukwuma Soludo for infrastructure projects. He credited Soludo, who is from Isuofia, with dualising the road linking Agulu to Ekwulobia, saying it took another governor to transform the road to Obi's hometown.

Background

Shettima has been campaigning in Anambra for President Tinubu's re-election. He recently said he would keep firing back at Atiku Abubakar as long as he attacks the president.

The other side

No response from Obi or the NDC was included in the reports.
