Osimhen to have knee surgery, ruled out of Super Eagles' Tanzania qualifiers

7 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Super Eagles striker Victor Osimhen is set to undergo knee surgery, ruling him out of Nigeria's upcoming Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Tanzania.

What happened

Galatasaray announced on Friday, 6 November 2026, that the 27-year-old needs arthroscopic surgery after medical checks found a loose body in his left knee. The problem was discovered after he played the full 90 minutes of the Turkish club's 2-0 Champions League win over VfB Stuttgart on Tuesday. The club said its doctor, Yener İnce, would carry out the operation at Maslak Acıbadem Hospital in Istanbul on Saturday, 7 November.

How long he could be out

Arthroscopic surgery is a minimally invasive procedure that uses small cuts and a camera to treat problems inside a joint. According to reports, Osimhen is expected to be out for two to four weeks, which could mean a return in early to mid-December if there are no setbacks. Galatasaray have not given an official timeline and say they will assess his recovery in stages.

Blow for the Super Eagles

The injury is a major blow for Nigeria, who face Tanzania twice in AFCON qualifying, including the match in Uyo on 13 November. Osimhen is the Super Eagles' main striker and one of Africa's most prolific forwards and was African Player of the Year in 2023. He was recently left off the shortlist for this year's CAF Player of the Year award.

Squad named

On Saturday, Galatasaray said the operation had been successful and that Osimhen would be out for at least three weeks. Super Eagles coach Eric Chelle then named a 24-man squad for the two Tanzania matches without Osimhen. Goalkeeper Maduka Okoye was also left out by the coach's choice, with Stanley Nwabali, Arthur Okonkwo and the recalled Francis Uzoho named in goal. Midfielder Frank Onyeka was dropped, while Fisayo Dele-Bashiru, Tochukwu Nnadi and Yusuf Alhassan return. Wilfred Ndidi, Alex Iwobi, Ola Aina and Calvin Bassey are among those included.

Background

Osimhen joined Galatasaray from Napoli and has been in strong form, with reports this week suggesting that a top Italian club is interested in signing him. His absence means the Super Eagles coaching staff will have to rely on other forwards for the Tanzania double-header.

Chelle's contract

Eric Chelle was appointed in January 2025 on a two-year deal that expires in January 2027. He had reportedly agreed in principle on an extension to August 2028, but the deal was never formally signed after NFF President Ibrahim Gusau and his board resigned and federation elections were postponed.

Limited powers

Qualifying pressure

The uncertainty comes as Nigeria sit in a difficult position in their 2027 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying group.

FIFA confirms US-led bid for 2031 Women's World Cup, UK to host 2035

24 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

FIFA has confirmed the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica as hosts of the 2031 Women's World Cup, the first with 48 teams. The four UK nations will host the 2035 edition.

Read more

FIFA has officially named the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica as joint hosts of the 2031 Women's World Cup, while England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland will host the 2035 tournament.

The decision

The hosts were confirmed on Monday, 23 November 2026, at an Extraordinary FIFA Congress held virtually with representatives of its 211 member associations. Both joint bids were approved by acclamation, having been the only candidates.

A bigger tournament

The 2031 edition will be the first Women's World Cup with 48 teams and 104 matches, bringing it in line with the men's tournament, and the first to be hosted across four countries.

It will be the third time the US has hosted the Women's World Cup, after 1999 and 2003, and the first time for Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica.

Infantino says Nigeria is not ready to host a World Cup yet

23 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

FIFA President Gianni Infantino has reportedly said Nigeria cannot yet host the World Cup because of gaps in stadiums, airports and transport. He added that the country has the building blocks to get there.

Read more

FIFA President Gianni Infantino has reportedly said Nigeria is not currently ready to host the FIFA World Cup.

What he said

Speaking during his visit to Nigeria, Infantino was asked whether the country could stage a tournament of that size. He is said to have pointed to serious shortfalls in stadiums, airports and transport networks as the main obstacles, and explained the kind of infrastructure any host nation must have in place.

Hope for the future

However, he reportedly did not rule out Nigeria's long-term chances, saying the country has the building blocks needed to eventually meet the standard. He also described the national training facilities he visited in Abuja and Warri as impressive.

Background

South Africa remains the only sub-Saharan African country to have hosted the World Cup, in 2010, while Morocco will co-host the 2030 edition with Spain and Portugal.

How fans reacted

His comments drew mixed reactions from Nigerian football fans online.

CAF adopts Kiswahili as its fifth official language

23 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

African football's governing body has added Kiswahili to its official languages alongside English, French, Arabic and Portuguese. The decision was taken at CAF's General Assembly in Abuja.

Read more

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) has adopted Kiswahili as its fifth official language, alongside English, French, Arabic and Portuguese.

The decision

The change was approved at CAF's 48th Ordinary General Assembly, held in Abuja on Saturday, 21 November 2026. CAF described it as a historic decision that reflects its commitment to Africa's linguistic and cultural diversity, and said it would strengthen the place of African languages in football administration.

Who pushed for it

The proposal was championed by the Tanzania Football Federation, with backing from the football bodies of Kenya and Uganda. CAF President Patrice Motsepe congratulated the three East African countries.

About Kiswahili

Kiswahili is spoken by more than 200 million people, mainly in Tanzania, Kenya and Uganda, and is also widely used in other parts of East, Central and Southern Africa.
