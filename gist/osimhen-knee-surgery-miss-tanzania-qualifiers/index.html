Osimhen to have knee surgery, ruled out of Super Eagles' Tanzania qualifiers

7 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Super Eagles striker Victor Osimhen is set to undergo knee surgery, ruling him out of Nigeria's upcoming Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Tanzania.

What happened

Galatasaray announced on Friday, 6 November 2026, that the 27-year-old needs arthroscopic surgery after medical checks found a loose body in his left knee. The problem was discovered after he played the full 90 minutes of the Turkish club's 2-0 Champions League win over VfB Stuttgart on Tuesday. The club said its doctor, Yener İnce, would carry out the operation at Maslak Acıbadem Hospital in Istanbul on Saturday, 7 November.

How long he could be out

Arthroscopic surgery is a minimally invasive procedure that uses small cuts and a camera to treat problems inside a joint. According to reports, Osimhen is expected to be out for two to four weeks, which could mean a return in early to mid-December if there are no setbacks. Galatasaray have not given an official timeline and say they will assess his recovery in stages.

Blow for the Super Eagles

The injury is a major blow for Nigeria, who face Tanzania twice in AFCON qualifying, including the match in Uyo on 13 November. Osimhen is the Super Eagles' main striker and one of Africa's most prolific forwards and was African Player of the Year in 2023. He was recently left off the shortlist for this year's CAF Player of the Year award.

Squad named

On Saturday, Galatasaray said the operation had been successful and that Osimhen would be out for at least three weeks. Super Eagles coach Eric Chelle then named a 24-man squad for the two Tanzania matches without Osimhen. Goalkeeper Maduka Okoye was also left out by the coach's choice, with Stanley Nwabali, Arthur Okonkwo and the recalled Francis Uzoho named in goal. Midfielder Frank Onyeka was dropped, while Fisayo Dele-Bashiru, Tochukwu Nnadi and Yusuf Alhassan return. Wilfred Ndidi, Alex Iwobi, Ola Aina and Calvin Bassey are among those included.

Background

Osimhen joined Galatasaray from Napoli and has been in strong form, with reports this week suggesting that a top Italian club is interested in signing him. His absence means the Super Eagles coaching staff will have to rely on other forwards for the Tanzania double-header.

Opposition parties to sign unity pact in Abuja on 23 November

16 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Nigeria's main opposition parties will sign a joint commitment to democratic renewal at a summit in Abuja on 23 November, adopting a national covenant, a framework for opposition unity and a reform programme.

Read more

Nigeria's main opposition parties are set to sign a joint pact at a summit in Abuja on Monday, 23 November 2026.

The plan

In a statement on Monday, 16 November 2026, signed by Salihu Lukman, the Coalition for Democracy said the Second Summit of Nigeria's Opposition Political Parties would take place at the NAF Conference Centre. Participating parties are expected to adopt three documents prepared by working groups over several months: a National Covenant for Democratic Renewal, a Framework for Opposition Unity and a National Reform Programme. Party leaders will then sign them. The coalition described the summit as a turning point in how the opposition coordinates ahead of the 2027 elections.

Background

The event follows the first opposition leaders' summit, held at the Yar'Adua Centre in Abuja on 31 August 2026, convened by the G100 group with the House of Representatives minority caucus. Efforts to field a single opposition candidate against President Bola Tinubu have so far failed, with Atiku Abubakar, Peter Obi and Seyi Makinde all running, though the Atiku and Makinde camps are reportedly in talks. It is not clear whether the pact will include any agreement on candidates. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Atiku says Tinubu only came to Plateau when he needed votes

16 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has criticised President Tinubu's visit to Plateau State, saying residents needed their president during killings and kidnappings, not at an election rally.

Read more

Former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar has criticised President Bola Tinubu over his recent campaign visit to Plateau State.

What he said

In a statement issued from Potiskum, Yobe State, on Monday, 16 November 2026, by his campaign's director of strategic communication, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku said no campaign promise or appeal for forgiveness could make up for the President's absence when communities in Plateau were under attack. He said residents had needed the President at the height of killings and kidnappings, and accused him of using people's grief as a political tool by returning to seek votes.

Atiku's campaign

Atiku, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), was in Yobe State to continue his 'affordable life' campaign, after launching the North-East leg in Bauchi on Saturday.

Background

Tinubu's campaign says the government has launched a five-year defence plan and approved the recruitment of 28,000 soldiers and 50,000 police officers. Former Plateau governors Simon Lalong and Joshua Dariye recently endorsed the President. The Presidency had not responded to Atiku's statement at the time of writing.

Court warns AGF Fagbemi of jail over detained Okuama community leaders

16 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

A Federal High Court in Warri has warned Attorney-General Lateef Fagbemi that he faces imprisonment if he keeps defying an order to release detained leaders of the Okuama community in Delta State.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Warri, Delta State, has warned the Attorney-General of the Federation, Lateef Fagbemi, that he could be jailed for contempt over the continued detention of leaders of the Okuama community.

The notice

The warning was contained in a formal notice of consequences of disobedience to a court order, issued on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, and reported on Sunday. It relates to an order made on 10 June 2026 by Justice Hyeladzira Nganjiwa directing the immediate release of the applicants, six community leaders who brought a fundamental rights case. The respondents include the federal government, the AGF and the armed forces.

Background

Okuama, in Ughelli South Local Government Area of Delta State, was the scene of the killing of 17 soldiers in March 2024, after which the military carried out operations in the area and detained several community members. The AGF's office had not responded to the court notice at the time of writing.
