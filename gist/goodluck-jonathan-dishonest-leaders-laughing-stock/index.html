Goodluck Jonathan says dishonest leaders have made Nigeria a laughing stock

22 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has reportedly criticised Nigeria's political class, saying dishonest leadership and unkept campaign promises have hurt the country's standing in the world.

Where he spoke

Jonathan gave the keynote address on Saturday, 21 November 2026, at the third Paul Alaje Colloquium, themed The Brand: Nigeria, at the NAF Conference Centre in Kado, Abuja.

What he said

He is said to have lamented that Nigeria has lost much of the respect it once enjoyed internationally, and accused office holders of misleading citizens and acting against their public promises. He warned that continued dishonesty in public life would hold back the country's progress.

Jonathan reportedly called for reforms to how leaders are chosen, credible elections and real democracy within parties. He said the country needs a political culture that rewards ideas, character and competence rather than money, patronage and sectional interests.

Background

Jonathan was president from 2010 to 2015. His remarks come as campaigns for the January 2027 elections get under way.

  • He spoke at a colloquium in Abuja on Saturday.
  • He called for credible elections.
  • He urged leaders to reward competence over money.

Osun Governor Ademola Adeleke was sworn in for a second term on Friday in Osogbo. His nephew Davido and other family members drew attention with a coordinated appearance at the ceremony.

Osun State Governor Ademola Adeleke was inaugurated for a second term on Friday, 27 November 2026, at the Osogbo Township Stadium.

Family affair

Afrobeats star Davido, the governor's nephew, drew almost as much attention as the ceremony itself. He arrived with his brother Adewale Adeleke and cousins Shina Adeleke, known as Shina Rambo, and Adebayo Adeleke, known as B-Red. NAFDAC shuts Abuja's Zuba International Market over fake products worth ₦300m

27 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

NAFDAC has shut Zuba International Market in Abuja after a joint operation with police and the DSS reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome goods worth more than ₦300 million. Over 160 shops were inspected.

Read more

The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) has shut down Zuba International Market in the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.

The operation

The closure followed a joint enforcement operation with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services, which reportedly uncovered counterfeit and unwholesome products worth more than ₦300 million. The exercise covered more than 160 shops in the market.

Why now

NAFDAC said the closure was part of a wider crackdown on substandard and fake goods ahead of the festive season.
