Peller apologises after calling himself Jesus Christ on livestream

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

Popular streamer Habeeb Adelaja, known as Peller, has apologised after a clip from his livestream in which he referred to himself as Jesus Christ drew widespread criticism.

What happened

In the clip, which spread online on Sunday, 1 November 2026, Peller appeared in a white and red costume with a wig and greeted his guest as Jesus Christ. The guest refused to go along with the joke and told him not to disrespect any religion, pointing out that someone with about 17 million followers had a responsibility to the people watching. Peller argued that he was only wearing a costume and that nobody alive knows what Jesus looked like. During the exchange he also said he was a traditionalist and joked about dressing as an Imam the next day, which the people with him warned was only making things worse.

The apology

In a follow-up video posted the same day, Peller said he had not expected the outfit to cause such a reaction, describing it as just a white cloth. He described himself as both a Christian and a Muslim, apparently to show that he meant no offence to either faith, and asked his fans to forgive him.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the stunt, saying religion should not be used for content. Some said the apology fell short because he did not clearly admit he was wrong, while others defended him as a young entertainer who made a mistake.

Background

Peller, one of Nigeria's best-known TikTok streamers, is often in the news for his livestreams. Last week, rapper YCee used his name as an example of what he called brain-rot content and refused to apologise for it. Peller and fellow content creator Jarvis held a white wedding in Lekki, Lagos, in August.

Dangote Refinery considers bigger IPO after ₦6 trillion in subscriptions

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering enlarging its share sale by up to 30% after investors put in nearly ₦6 trillion. That could take the offer to about $2.1 billion, one of Africa's largest.

Read more

Dangote Petroleum Refinery is reportedly considering expanding its initial public offering (IPO) by as much as 30%, after strong investor demand pushed subscriptions to nearly ₦6 trillion, or about $4.5 billion.

The numbers

The refinery, owned by billionaire Aliko Dangote, initially planned to sell 4.1 billion shares. Demand is said to have exceeded the shares on offer by nearly three times, prompting talks about increasing the allocation.

If the expansion goes ahead, the value of the share sale could rise to about $2.1 billion, potentially making it one of the biggest stock market offerings on the continent.

Why it matters

The refinery is the largest in Africa, and its pricing decisions have recently driven changes in pump prices across Nigeria.

Air Peace offers ₦80,000 domestic fares to serving and retired military personnel

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Air Peace chairman Allen Onyema has announced a special ₦80,000 one-way domestic fare for serving and retired members of the armed forces. He said it recognises their sacrifices for the country.

Read more

Air Peace has announced a special one-way domestic fare of ₦80,000 for serving and retired military personnel.

The announcement

The airline's chairman, Allen Onyema, disclosed the initiative on Monday, 16 November 2026, while delivering a lecture at the Chief of Defence Intelligence Annual Conference in Abuja.

He said the discounted fare would apply across all of Air Peace's domestic routes, and would cover serving officers and veterans who have served Nigeria since independence in 1960.

Why the airline is doing it

Onyema said the initiative was meant to recognise the sacrifices made by members of the armed forces in protecting the country.

Atiku asks Tinubu to release full Villa interview, raises questions over $4.5m lobbying deal

18 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar has challenged President Tinubu to release the unedited recording of a recent Villa interview. His campaign questioned whether it was linked to a $4.5 million US lobbying contract.

Read more

ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has challenged President Bola Tinubu to release the full, unedited recording of a recent interview at the Presidential Villa.

The claims

In a statement issued on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, through his campaign's director of strategic communication, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku raised questions about whether the interview was funded through a $4.5 million foreign lobbying contract paid on Nigeria's behalf.

He pointed to US filings that reportedly list a consultant working with the lobbying firm DCI Group on work for the Republic of Nigeria. The filings are said to record a $4.5 million retainer paid to the firm, but do not identify the interview as one of the services covered.

What Atiku wants

The former vice president called for the unedited recording to be made public and for the government to explain the lobbying arrangement.

The other side

No response from the Presidency or the APC campaign was included in the reports.
