Peller apologises after calling himself Jesus Christ on livestream

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

Popular streamer Habeeb Adelaja, known as Peller, has apologised after a clip from his livestream in which he referred to himself as Jesus Christ drew widespread criticism.

What happened

In the clip, which spread online on Sunday, 1 November 2026, Peller appeared in a white and red costume with a wig and greeted his guest as Jesus Christ. The guest refused to go along with the joke and told him not to disrespect any religion, pointing out that someone with about 17 million followers had a responsibility to the people watching. Peller argued that he was only wearing a costume and that nobody alive knows what Jesus looked like. During the exchange he also said he was a traditionalist and joked about dressing as an Imam the next day, which the people with him warned was only making things worse.

The apology

In a follow-up video posted the same day, Peller said he had not expected the outfit to cause such a reaction, describing it as just a white cloth. He described himself as both a Christian and a Muslim, apparently to show that he meant no offence to either faith, and asked his fans to forgive him.

How people reacted

Many social media users criticised the stunt, saying religion should not be used for content. Some said the apology fell short because he did not clearly admit he was wrong, while others defended him as a young entertainer who made a mistake.

Background

Peller, one of Nigeria's best-known TikTok streamers, is often in the news for his livestreams. Last week, rapper YCee used his name as an example of what he called brain-rot content and refused to apologise for it. Peller and fellow content creator Jarvis held a white wedding in Lekki, Lagos, in August.

"My hair costs more than my clothes": Davido's elder sister, Sharon Adeleke, says she doesn't believe in spending big on outfits

10 Nov 2026, 01:10 GMT

Davido's elder sister, Sharon Adeleke, says she would rather spend good money on her hair than on expensive designer clothes. She also revealed that her own wedding dress cost just $2,000, and that her outfit for Davido and Chioma's white wedding was a last-minute rescue.

Read more

Davido's elder sister, Sharon Adeleke, has opened up about her fashion choices, revealing that she would rather spend good money on her hair than buy expensive designer clothes.

"My hair costs more than my clothes"

Speaking on a podcast, Sharon said she has never really believed in spending a fortune on clothing, no matter how much money a person has.

She enjoys looking good, especially when it comes to her hair, but she does not see the need to attach an expensive price tag to every outfit.

The wedding outfit drama

Sharon also spoke about her appearance at Davido and Chioma's white wedding, revealing that things did not go as smoothly as she had planned.

She said she had hired a stylist and a fashion designer to handle her outfit, but they failed to deliver as expected. Everything ended up as a last-minute arrangement, leaving her with little choice but to hope it worked out. At some point, she said, it was simply a case of "God abeg!"

A $2,000 wedding dress

She also shared an interesting detail about her own wedding. Despite coming from one of Nigeria's wealthiest and best-known families, she said her husband spent just $2,000 on her wedding dress.

She added that she personally paid for the dresses worn by all 13 of her bridesmaids.

Not what people expect

Her revelations are interesting because many people assume wealthy families spend outrageous amounts on everything they wear.

Now imagine Davido's own elder sister saying she doesn't believe in buying expensive clothes, while some people borrow money just to wear designer labels and impress strangers.

Nollywood filmmaker Funke Akindele has revealed that a social media influencer turned down her paid offer to promote her blockbuster film 'Everybody Loves Jenifa'.

What she said

Speaking at a panel session at the Creative Africa Nexus Weekend, in a video shared online on 7 November 2026, Akindele said she had been reluctant to approach influencers but eventually decided to try. She proposed that the influencer post about the film several times a week during the promotional campaign, and offered to pay. However, the influencer's management turned her down, and when she followed up to make clear she was willing to pay, her message was ignored. She did not name the influencer.

Background

'Everybody Loves Jenifa' was one of the biggest Nollywood box-office hits. Akindele, one of the industry's most successful filmmakers, is now promoting her new film 'The Four', which recently sold out its London premiere. Influencer marketing has become a major part of how Nigerian films are promoted, with content creators often paid to make videos about new releases.

APC manifesto promises 50,000 police recruits, 200,000 homes and 10,000MW of power

10 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

The APC has released President Tinubu's 109-page 2027 manifesto, 'From Reform to Prosperity Agenda 2027–2031', pledging 50,000 new police officers, 28,000 military recruits, 6% annual growth, 200,000 affordable homes and power generation approaching 10,000 megawatts.

Read more

The All Progressives Congress (APC) has released President Bola Tinubu's manifesto for the 2027 presidential election, setting out his plans for a second term.

The document

The 109-page manifesto, titled 'From Reform to Prosperity Agenda 2027–2031', was due to be formally unveiled at the Shehu Musa Yar'Adua Centre in Abuja on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. It covers security, the economy, housing, health and education, and says a second term would focus on turning the economic foundations of the first term into concrete gains for Nigerians.

Security

The manifesto pledges to train and deploy 50,000 new police officers and work towards one officer for every 400 citizens, recruit 28,000 more military personnel, and complete four new Army divisions in Makurdi, Ilorin, Jalingo and Benin City. It proposes that the civil defence corps take over static guard duties from the police, the rollout of body cameras and forensic facilities, wider electronic surveillance of borders, and the deployment of forest guards, agro-rangers and mining marshals.

Economy, housing and power

It targets annual economic growth of 6 per cent by 2031, halving youth unemployment, and extending credit to half of economically active Nigerians. It promises 200,000 affordable homes under a mass housing scheme and aims to raise available power generation towards 10,000 megawatts.

Health and education

The plan aims to raise the share of functional primary health centres from 53 to 90 per cent, extend health insurance to 40 per cent of Nigerians, cut out-of-pocket health spending from 71 per cent to below 50 per cent, recruit 20,000 more teachers, halve the number of out-of-school children and expand the student loan scheme.

Background

Tinubu faces 17 other presidential candidates, including Atiku Abubakar of the ADC, Peter Obi of the NDC and Seyi Makinde of the APM, in the election on 16 January 2027. Makinde launched his 'Reset Agenda' last week. Opposition candidates have criticised the government's record on the economy and insecurity, while the President says his reforms saved the economy from collapse.
