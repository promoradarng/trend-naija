Tinubu to inaugurate sections of Kaduna–Kano and Kano–Maradi railways on 3 December

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has fixed 3 December 2026 for the inauguration of completed sections of two major railway projects in northern Nigeria.

The projects

According to a statement on Sunday, 8 November 2026, by Dele Alake, spokesman of the APC Presidential Campaign Council, the 203-kilometre Kaduna–Kano Standard Gauge Railway is about 90 per cent complete, while the 400-kilometre Kano–Katsina–Maradi line, which will link Nigeria with the Republic of Niger, is about 70 per cent complete. The 190-kilometre Jaji-to-Kano segment of the Kaduna–Kano line and the 250-kilometre Dawanu–Kano–Kazaure–Daura–Katsina stretch of the Kano–Maradi line are due to be inaugurated on 3 December.

Progress claims

The statement said that when Tinubu took office in 2023, the Kano–Maradi project was barely 5 per cent complete and the Kaduna–Kano line under 30 per cent. It said a federal inspection team led by the Permanent Secretary of the Federal Ministry of Transportation, Funso Adebiyi, recently assessed both corridors and carried out a test run. The figures come from the President's campaign and have not been independently verified.

Background

The Kaduna–Kano line is part of the planned Lagos–Kano standard gauge railway, which already runs from Lagos to Ibadan and from Abuja to Kaduna. The Kano–Maradi project was started under former President Muhammadu Buhari.

Why it matters

The inauguration will come just weeks before the 16 January 2027 presidential election, and infrastructure is a key part of the President's re-election campaign.

Cooking gas prices rise; refilling 12.5kg now costs up to ₦22,500

19 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Cooking gas has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kg to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800 depending on location. Some households are cutting back or switching to other fuels ahead of Christmas.

Read more

Nigerian households are facing fresh pressure as cooking gas prices rise across major cities ahead of the Christmas season.

The new prices

The price of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) has reportedly risen from about ₦1,400 per kilogram to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,800, depending on location. That means refilling a 12.5kg cylinder can now cost as much as ₦22,500, up from about ₦17,500.

How households are coping

Market findings suggest some Nigerians are now buying smaller quantities, cutting down on how much gas they use or switching to other cooking fuels.

The federal government has announced plans to release stored rice, maize and other grains into the market in a bid to bring down food prices.

What was announced

Minister of Agriculture and Food Security Abubakar Kyari disclosed the plan on Wednesday, 18 November 2026, at the presentation of the 2026 Wet Season Agricultural Performance Survey Report in Abuja.

He said grains bought under the Bank of Agriculture's Guaranteed Minimum Price programme would soon be released to the market.

Fertiliser for farmers

The government also plans to distribute three million bags of fertiliser to farmers within the next month to boost production and improve food supply across the country.

Background

The Guaranteed Minimum Price scheme gives farmers an assured buyer for their produce.
