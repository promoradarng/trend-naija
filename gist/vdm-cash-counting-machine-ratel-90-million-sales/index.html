VeryDarkMan brings out cash counting machine after claiming record ₦90 million sales in one day

1 Nov 2026, 16:09 GMT

Social media activist and businessman VeryDarkMan (VDM) has once again got people talking, this time after bringing out a cash counting machine to count money reportedly received from the sale of his RATEL products.

Photos show him and a colleague surrounded by bundles of naira notes, with an open suitcase full of cash, stacks of money on cartons of RATEL power stations and a counting machine beside them.

A record day

According to VDM, the business recorded about ₦90 million in sales in one day, the biggest single-day figure since the products were launched.

Ready to show proof

VDM knows how Nigerians think. Once you mention ₦90 million in one day, some people will immediately start asking questions, while others will simply refuse to believe it.

To clear the doubts, he has promised to go a step further and make his bank account statement public so people can see the transactions for themselves. He appeared confident about the figures and made it clear he was ready to back up his claims with evidence.

A fast-growing brand

What makes this development interesting is how quickly the RATEL business appears to be gaining attention. From importing products from China to demonstrating how they perform in public, VDM has continued to attract customers and supporters.

However, the ₦90 million figure has yet to be independently verified, and sales revenue should not be confused with profit.

All is fair in war, but has VeryDarkMan gone too far with Davido?

9 Nov 2026, 17:57 GMT

Amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VeryDarkMan has reportedly brought up the deaths of the singer's late friends, a painful chapter long surrounded by unproven rumours. Criticism is fair, but should personal tragedy ever be used as ammunition?

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) does not seem to believe in fighting fairly. When he has a disagreement with someone, he appears willing to go to any length to make his point, even if it means bringing up some of the most painful moments in that person's life.

A painful chapter

Over the years, Davido has lost several close friends and associates in tragic circumstances. Their deaths set off all kinds of rumours and conspiracy theories online.

Some people even claimed the deaths were connected to rituals or sacrifices for fame and success. None of those claims was ever proven, and there is no credible evidence linking Davido to any of their deaths.

Imagine losing people who were very close to you, only to have strangers turn their deaths into accusations against you. Davido has lived with those memories for years. He has carried on with his music career, built his family and moved forward with his life, but moving forward does not mean the pain has disappeared.

Reopening old wounds

Now, amid his ongoing disagreement with Davido, VDM has reportedly brought those painful memories back into public discussion. This is where things appear to be getting out of hand.

You can criticise Davido's music, lifestyle, public statements or behaviour. You can disagree with him and challenge his opinions. That is perfectly normal. But bringing up the deaths of his close friends because of a disagreement raises serious questions.

What exactly is the intention? Is it to expose wrongdoing, to make a legitimate point, or simply to hurt someone where he is most vulnerable?

Where are the limits?

VDM has built a reputation for confronting people without holding back, and his supporters may argue he is only giving his opponents the same treatment they give others. But surely there should be limits.

Davido is not above criticism, and neither is VeryDarkMan. Both are public figures who should be held accountable for their actions. Using someone's personal tragedies as ammunition in a public quarrel, however, is something else entirely.

There are some experiences people never fully recover from. They may laugh and smile in public, but that does not mean they have forgotten. If winning an argument requires reopening another person's deepest wounds, perhaps the argument has already gone too far.

Over to you

People say all is fair in love and war, but does that mean we should abandon all compassion when we disagree with someone? Has VDM crossed the line with Davido, or is this just two public figures fighting without limits?

INEC warns against 'Rig and Die' slogan ahead of 2027 elections, raises concerns over electoral violence

9 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

INEC has raised serious concern over the growing use of the "Rig and Die" slogan by some political actors ahead of the 2027 elections, warning that such language could encourage violence. But the slogan also points to a deeper problem: Nigerians' fear that their votes will not count.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has expressed serious concern over the growing use of the controversial "Rig and Die" slogan by some political actors ahead of Nigeria's 2027 general elections.

A dangerous slogan

According to the electoral body, such language is dangerous and could encourage political violence, putting innocent Nigerians at risk as the country prepares for another major election.

Why it has come to this

The controversy also raises a question that cannot be ignored: why has Nigeria's electoral system reached a point where political actors feel the need to use such threatening language?

For years, Nigerians have complained about electoral malpractice, vote buying, intimidation, ballot box snatching and alleged manipulation of results. Many voters have grown frustrated, especially when they believe their votes do not count.

With the 2027 elections approaching, political tensions are already building.

Confidence in the process

INEC is right to warn against inflammatory statements that could endanger lives. But the commission must also recognise that public confidence in the electoral process remains a major concern.

Nigerians want elections where votes are counted honestly, results are transmitted transparently, and those caught manipulating the process face real consequences.

Political leaders, too, have a responsibility to avoid statements that could turn ordinary citizens into victims of electoral violence.

No election should cost an innocent Nigerian their life. At the same time, electoral offenders must never be allowed to operate without consequences.

Should INEC be more concerned about the slogan "Rig and Die", or about fixing the electoral weaknesses that make Nigerians fear rigging in the first place?
