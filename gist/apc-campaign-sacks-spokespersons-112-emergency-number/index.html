APC campaign council reportedly sacks spokespersons after embarrassing 112 emergency number incident on live TV

11 Nov 2026, 20:22 GMT

The All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council has reportedly removed some of its spokespersons following the controversy over a failed attempt to demonstrate Nigeria's 112 emergency number on live television.

Only three can now speak

According to reports, President Bola Tinubu's Chief of Staff, Femi Gbajabiamila, announced on Wednesday that the spokespersons earlier named by the campaign council had been removed.

He said only three officials are now authorised to speak on behalf of the council: Senator Abdulaziz Yari, the Director General; Minister of Solid Minerals Dele Alake; and Minister of Information Mohammed Idris.

What happened on live TV

It all started on Tuesday, when APC official Alwan Hassan appeared as a guest on a live television politics programme.

While discussing the achievements of the APC-led government, Hassan pointed to the 112 emergency number as an example of a working public service. He explained that Nigerians could dial it whenever they needed urgent help, whether in a medical emergency, a security threat or a road accident.

To prove his point, he challenged the host to dial 112 right there in the studio.

That was where things took an unexpected turn. The host tried the number, but the call failed to connect. He tried again, with the same result.

Hassan insisted the line was working and suggested network problems might have stopped the calls going through. His explanation did little to stop the criticism that followed.

The backlash

Clips of the interview spread quickly online, with Nigerians questioning the reliability of a service people are meant to depend on in life-threatening situations. Opposition figures also seized on the incident to challenge the government's claims about improved public services.

The real question

Is removing spokespersons really the answer, or should the government be more concerned about finding out why the emergency calls failed?

Someone calling 112 during an armed robbery, a serious accident or a medical emergency may not have the luxury of trying again and again until the network connects.

One failed demonstration does not prove the whole emergency system is broken. But it raises questions that deserve proper answers, and for many Nigerians, those answers matter far more than who is allowed to speak for a political campaign.

Kwara APC governorship rally in Offa reportedly turns violent over unpaid mobilisation funds

16 Nov 2026, 11:21 GMT

An APC governorship rally in Offa, Kwara State, reportedly descended into chaos after claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised. The incident has revived debate about paying crowds to attend political events.

Read more

A governorship campaign rally organised by the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Offa, Kwara State, has reportedly descended into chaos following claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised.

What reportedly happened

According to reports, the rally drew a large crowd of supporters to the streets of Offa. But tension rose when some of those who had turned up complained that they had not received the money they were allegedly promised for attending.

The situation reportedly turned violent as angry participants confronted organisers, disrupting the event.

The full details, including whether anyone was injured and how much money was involved, have not been independently confirmed, and the party has not publicly commented on the incident.

The business of crowds

The incident shines a light on a practice many Nigerians already know about: paying people, often called "mobilisation", to attend political rallies.

Across parties, campaign organisers frequently provide transport, food, branded caps and T-shirts, and sometimes cash, to fill venues and create the impression of massive support. When those promises are not kept, frustration can quickly boil over.

It also raises a deeper question. If people have to be paid to attend, how much do the crowds at political rallies really tell us about genuine support?

Over to you

Should people be paid to attend political rallies, or should campaigns rely only on supporters who come out of conviction?

Political violence: Jonathan sounds alarm, Senate asks INEC to disqualify violent candidates as APC and ADC trade blame

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that some campaigns are being run like cult groups, while the Senate urged police to probe recent attacks and INEC to disqualify candidates linked to violence. The APC accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, a claim the ADC rejected.

Read more

Concern over violence ahead of the 2027 elections dominated political discussion on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, with a former president, the Senate and the main parties all weighing in.

Jonathan's warning

Speaking at a peace and security conference organised by his foundation in Abuja, former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that rising political intolerance and violence could threaten the elections. He said some political groups were running their campaign teams like cult groups, and that Nigerians at home and abroad had expressed fears about possible crises after the polls. He also condemned thuggery and the use of young people as tools of violence.

The Senate's resolution

The Senate, acting on a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi (ADC, Bauchi Central), condemned recent attacks on political figures. These included the stoning of President Bola Tinubu's motorcade after Friday prayers in Abuja, reported attacks on NDC candidate Peter Obi in Edo State, a disrupted NDC procession in Asaba on 7 November that left one person dead, and an attack on ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar's convoy in Sokoto.

Lawmakers asked the Inspector-General of Police to carry out impartial investigations and prosecute those responsible, and urged INEC to apply the law disqualifying candidates linked to electoral violence. The motion was seconded by Senator Ali Ndume (APC, Borno South).

APC and ADC trade accusations

The APC presidential campaign council, through its spokesperson Dele Alake, accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, deepfakes and hired protests to damage President Tinubu's campaign, pointing to the convoy incident in Abuja.

The ADC campaign council, through Phrank Shaibu, dismissed the claim as a distraction from the government's economic record. It challenged the APC to provide evidence or withdraw the allegation, and asked why no one had been prosecuted if the ruling party had proof. The NDC's response was not included in the reports.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Tinubu asks Senate to decriminalise attempted suicide

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

President Tinubu has sent a bill to the Senate that would remove criminal penalties for attempted suicide and replace them with medical and psychological support. The National Mental Health Amendment Bill was read during plenary.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has reportedly asked the Senate to decriminalise attempted suicide and put in place medical and psychological support for survivors instead of punishment.

The bill

The proposal is contained in the National Mental Health Amendment Bill, 2026, which Senate President Godswill Akpabio read during plenary on Tuesday, 17 November 2026. It seeks to amend the National Mental Health Act of 2021 by removing criminal liability for attempted suicide, which currently attracts prison terms under Nigeria's Criminal and Penal Codes, and replacing it with a public health approach.

In his letter to the Senate, the President urged lawmakers to give the bill quick consideration, saying it was designed to decriminalise attempted suicide and support anyone who tries to take their own life.

Why it matters

If passed, the law would treat attempted suicide as a health issue requiring care and treatment rather than a crime.

Getting help

Anyone who is struggling or thinking about suicide is encouraged to speak to someone they trust or contact a health professional or local support service.

Olympic Eagles beat DR Congo 4-0 to overturn first-leg deficit

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Nigeria's U23 team beat DR Congo 4-0 in Ikenne to overturn a 2-0 first-leg loss and reach the final round of U23 qualifying. They will now face Zambia.

Read more

Nigeria's under-23 team, the Olympic Eagles, beat the Democratic Republic of Congo 4-0 at the Remo Stars Stadium in Ikenne to reach the final round of qualifying for the U23 Africa Cup of Nations.

The comeback

The win was all the more impressive because Nigeria had lost the first leg 2-0 in Lubumbashi four days earlier. Alex Oweilayefa opened the scoring in the eighth minute after the DR Congo goalkeeper spilled a powerful shot from Umar Abubakar.

The home side kept up the pressure, with Saleh Sadiq denied by a goal-line clearance before half-time and Abubakar missing a close-range chance, leaving the tie level on aggregate at the break. Nigeria pulled away in the second half to win 4-0 on the day.

What happens next

The Olympic Eagles will face Zambia in the final round of qualifying.

Bright spot

The result came on the same day the Super Eagles were held to a goalless draw by Tanzania in their AFCON 2027 qualifier.
