Kwankwaso joins TikTok as NDC launches its 2027 campaign council

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Former Kano State governor Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso, running mate to Peter Obi on the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) ticket, has joined TikTok as the 2027 campaign moves further onto social media.

What happened

Kwankwaso announced his arrival on the video platform in a post on his official X account on Wednesday, 28 October 2026. He said he would use TikTok to share verified information, live updates and exclusive content from his daily life, and closed the post with his campaign line that Nigeria will be okay.

Why it matters

TikTok has become one of the busiest spaces for political debate among young Nigerians, with supporters of all the major candidates producing videos, skits and live sessions every day. Several politicians have opened accounts in recent months to speak directly to younger voters. The NDC is expected to rely heavily on young people, who formed a large part of Obi's support base in 2023, while Kwankwaso's Kwankwasiyya movement has a strong following in the North.

Background

The announcement came on the same day the NDC inaugurated its presidential campaign council for the January 2027 elections. Kwankwaso, who was the NNPP presidential candidate in 2023, has teamed up with Obi on a joint ticket. The APC, ADC and other parties have also stepped up their online campaigns since the official start of campaigning.

How people reacted

Supporters quickly shared the account, while some critics joked that politicians only discover new apps during election season.

  • Kwankwaso says he will share verified information and live updates.
  • The NDC inaugurated its campaign council the same day.
  • Young voters are a key target for all the major parties.

Former Obidient coordinator Yunusa Tanko joins SDP, to lead Adebayo's campaign

11 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Yunusa Tanko, once Peter Obi's spokesman and national coordinator of the Obidient Movement, has been named national coordinator of the SDP presidential campaign council for candidate Adewole Adebayo. He says he informed Obi before accepting.

Read more

Yunusa Tanko, a former spokesman for Peter Obi and ex-national coordinator of the Obidient Movement, has joined the Social Democratic Party (SDP) campaign for the 2027 presidential election.

The new role

At the inauguration of the SDP Presidential Campaign Council in Abuja on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, Tanko was named its national coordinator. In his acceptance speech he thanked the party's leadership and pledged full commitment to its presidential candidate, Prince Adewole Adebayo. He said he had reached out to both Obi and Senator Seriake Dickson, national leader of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), before taking up the post, and described his relationship with Adebayo as dating back to the 2023 election cycle.

How he left Obi's camp

Tanko was removed as national coordinator of the Obidient Movement in October 2026 and briefly served as Obi's political adviser for Northern Nigeria. He resigned from that role on 30 October, saying he was returning to the National Consultative Front, which he co-chairs with Professor Pat Utomi. He took on that position in October after the death of former House of Representatives Speaker Ghali Na'Abba, who had co-chaired the group.

Background

Obi, the 2023 Labour Party candidate, is now the NDC presidential candidate with Rabiu Kwankwaso as his running mate. Adebayo was also the SDP's presidential candidate in 2023. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027. Obi's campaign had not publicly reacted to Tanko's new role at the time of writing.

ADC accuses Kaduna government of sealing Arewa House to block Atiku meeting

11 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The ADC says the Kaduna State government sealed Arewa House to stop Atiku Abubakar from holding a consultation with Northern stakeholders there on Thursday. The party blamed President Tinubu and the APC.

Read more

The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has accused the Kaduna State government of sealing Arewa House to prevent its presidential candidate, Atiku Abubakar, from meeting Northern stakeholders there.

What the ADC said

In a statement on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, issued by Phrank Shaibu, director of strategic communication for the ADC presidential campaign council, the party said Atiku's team had chosen Arewa House in Kaduna as the venue for a consultation on Thursday, 13 November, but that the state government sealed the building beforehand. Atiku himself said his campaign had proposed the venue and that the government sealed it the same day.

The party's complaint

The ADC described Arewa House as part of the shared heritage of Northern Nigeria and said using it to block a political gathering was an insult to the region. It held President Bola Tinubu and the All Progressives Congress (APC) responsible, saying a ruling party must not use government power to block opposition activities. There was no immediate public response from the Kaduna State government, which is led by the APC, to the allegation.

Background

Arewa House, the former residence of Sir Ahmadu Bello, the late Premier of the Northern Region, is a historic institution in Kaduna. Atiku is contesting the 2027 presidential election despite a court ruling that voided his nomination, which the ADC has appealed.

APC campaign council reportedly sacks spokespersons after embarrassing 112 emergency number incident on live TV

11 Nov 2026, 20:22 GMT

The APC Presidential Campaign Council has reportedly removed its spokespersons after APC official Alwan Hassan's attempt to demonstrate the 112 emergency number on live television failed twice. Only three officials are now authorised to speak for the council.

Read more

The All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council has reportedly removed some of its spokespersons following the controversy over a failed attempt to demonstrate Nigeria's 112 emergency number on live television.

Only three can now speak

According to reports, President Bola Tinubu's Chief of Staff, Femi Gbajabiamila, announced on Wednesday that the spokespersons earlier named by the campaign council had been removed.

He said only three officials are now authorised to speak on behalf of the council: Senator Abdulaziz Yari, the Director General; Minister of Solid Minerals Dele Alake; and Minister of Information Mohammed Idris.

What happened on live TV

It all started on Tuesday, when APC official Alwan Hassan appeared as a guest on a live television politics programme.

While discussing the achievements of the APC-led government, Hassan pointed to the 112 emergency number as an example of a working public service. He explained that Nigerians could dial it whenever they needed urgent help, whether in a medical emergency, a security threat or a road accident.

To prove his point, he challenged the host to dial 112 right there in the studio.

That was where things took an unexpected turn. The host tried the number, but the call failed to connect. He tried again, with the same result.

Hassan insisted the line was working and suggested network problems might have stopped the calls going through. His explanation did little to stop the criticism that followed.

The backlash

Clips of the interview spread quickly online, with Nigerians questioning the reliability of a service people are meant to depend on in life-threatening situations. Opposition figures also seized on the incident to challenge the government's claims about improved public services.

The real question

Is removing spokespersons really the answer, or should the government be more concerned about finding out why the emergency calls failed?

Someone calling 112 during an armed robbery, a serious accident or a medical emergency may not have the luxury of trying again and again until the network connects.

One failed demonstration does not prove the whole emergency system is broken. But it raises questions that deserve proper answers, and for many Nigerians, those answers matter far more than who is allowed to speak for a political campaign.

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso visit Yelewata community in Benue State, offer support to vulnerable children

11 Nov 2026, 19:53 GMT

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have visited Yelewata in Benue State, where Obi laid a wreath at the memorial to victims of the community's deadly 2025 attack. The two leaders donated ₦10 million for vulnerable children and promised a motorised borehole.

Read more

Peter Obi and Rabiu Kwankwaso have visited Yelewata community in Benue State, bringing some hope to residents who have endured years of hardship and neglect.

A moment of remembrance

Yelewata was the scene of a deadly attack in June 2025 that left many residents dead and shocked the nation. During the visit, Obi knelt to lay a wreath at the memorial bearing the names of the victims, as a large crowd looked on.

Donations and promises

The two political leaders promised to provide a motorised borehole to help address the community's water needs. They also donated ₦10 million to support vulnerable children, while Professor Shija contributed another ₦2 million towards the education of orphans.

The people speak

Beyond the donations, the people of Yelewata had their own stories to tell. They spoke about years of neglect, the difficulties they face every day and the lack of meaningful government support.

Imagine living for years without some of the basic things that should make life easier, while those elected to serve appear distant from your suffering. For residents, the visit was a chance to make their voices heard and draw attention to their situation.

Action, not just visits

Promises must be followed by action. The community needs lasting solutions, not just occasional visits.

Leadership should be about compassion, listening to the people and doing something practical to improve their lives.
