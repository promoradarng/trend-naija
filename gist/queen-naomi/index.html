Toyin Abraham's throwback TikTok post sparks talk of rivalry with Funke Akindele

31 Oct 2026, 09:42 GMT

Toyin Abraham has shared an old TikTok collaboration from her film 'OverSabi Aunty' and said she looks forward to more ahead of her December release 'Bestie'. Fans linked the post to Funke Akindele's new Facebook partnership for 'The Four', reviving talk of a rivalry.

Read more

Nollywood actress and filmmaker Toyin Abraham has once again found herself at the centre of talk about a rivalry with Funke Akindele, after a social media post that many fans read as a response to her colleague.

What she posted

On Friday, 30 October 2026, Toyin Abraham shared on Instagram a throwback clip of a TikTok collaboration she did while promoting her earlier film, 'OverSabi Aunty'. In the caption, she said she was looking forward to more collaborations ahead of her new film, 'Bestie', which is due in cinemas in December 2026.

Why fans linked it to Funke

The post came just days after Funke Akindele announced a partnership with Facebook for her new film, 'The Four'. Many fans interpreted Toyin's post as a subtle reminder that she had worked with a major platform first, although she did not mention Funke, and her intention has not been confirmed.

Background

The two actresses are among the most successful filmmakers in Nollywood, and fans have long compared their box office results. Toyin has also faced accusations of copying Funke's ideas, which she has denied. In the past she has spoken warmly about Funke, congratulating her and calling her the winner of their rivalry after one of Funke's films crossed ₦1 billion at the box office.

How people reacted

Reactions were divided. Critics questioned the timing of the post and accused her of seeking attention, while her supporters said she had every right to talk about her own past work. Funke Akindele has not commented.

AY Makun responds to backlash after missing Cardiff show, promises free return

30 Oct 2026, 11:49 GMT

Comedian AY Makun has defended himself after failing to appear at his AY Live show in Cardiff, which led to calls for him to be cancelled. He blamed repeated problems on the tour and promised a free return show, while saying ticket holders will still get refunds.

Read more

Comedian and filmmaker Ayo 'AY' Makun has spoken out after facing a wave of criticism for not turning up at his AY Live show in Cardiff, Wales.

What happened

Fans who bought tickets for the Cardiff show on Sunday were left angry when the comedian did not appear. Many criticised him on social media, and some called for him to be cancelled. AY had earlier apologised, saying fans' time, money and trust deserved respect, but the apology did not end the criticism.

His side of the story

In a long statement reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, AY said veterans in the industry had advised him to stay quiet, but he felt his audience deserved an explanation. He said he had spent years performing at events where payment promises were broken, sometimes paying fellow performers from his own pocket. He said a five-hour drive from Sheffield to Cardiff, after repeated bad experiences on the same tour, became his breaking point, but added that the fans who turned up had done nothing wrong. He also complained that negative stories about him get more attention than his achievements, and said calls for cancellation would not make him give up.

What he promised

AY said he would host a free return show in Cardiff, paid for by himself, once a venue and logistics are confirmed. He said this would not replace refunds owed to people who bought tickets for the original event.

Background

AY is one of Nigeria's best-known comedians and has run the AY Live brand for many years, as well as producing hit films. His account of the tour problems has not been confirmed by the organisers.

VeryDarkMan unveils RATEL Sensor Light, an affordable motion-activated light for Nigerian homes

31 Oct 2026, 14:32 GMT

VeryDarkMan has added a motion-activated sensor light to his growing RATEL brand, priced at ₦4,200 for the small size and ₦9,000 for the big size. The light switches on automatically when it detects movement, which could help households during power cuts and add a little security around the compound.

Read more

VeryDarkMan (VDM) has unveiled another product under his growing RATEL brand, and this time it is something many Nigerian households may find useful, especially with the country's unpredictable electricity supply.

The new product is the RATEL Sensor Light, a motion-activated light that comes on automatically when it detects movement.

How much it costs

According to VDM, the sensor light comes in two sizes:

- Small size: ₦4,200

- Big size: ₦9,000

Why it could be useful

Anyone living in Nigeria knows how frustrating the electricity situation can be. Imagine NEPA suddenly takes light at midnight and everywhere goes dark. You need to leave your bedroom, walk downstairs, check on your children or find your way outside to switch on the generator. Before you even find your torchlight, you have already knocked your leg against a chair.

This is where a sensor light could come in handy. It can be placed along staircases, corridors, bedroom entrances, wardrobes, storerooms and other dark corners of the house. Once it detects movement within its range, it switches on automatically, with no need to search for a wall switch in the dark.

A possible security benefit

The light could also help around entrances, gates and backyards. If somebody quietly approaches that area at night, the space around the sensor suddenly becomes bright.

Of course, a sensor light cannot replace proper home security, but it may help discourage unwanted visitors and make movement around a compound more noticeable.

VDM also explained that the product comes with different lighting colour options, including multicolour settings.

What buyers should check

At ₦4,200 for the smaller version, some households may find it affordable enough to install several units: one on the staircase, another in the corridor and perhaps two around the entrance. However, buyers should check the battery life, charging method, weather resistance and detection range before deciding where to install it.

A growing RATEL range

What stands out about this latest RATEL product is the idea behind it. VDM appears to be paying attention to the everyday inconveniences Nigerians face, especially those caused by poor electricity supply. From power stations to smaller household gadgets, he is gradually expanding his product range.

There is something worth appreciating about businesses that identify ordinary problems and offer affordable solutions. You don't always need to spend hundreds of thousands of naira to make your home more comfortable, and sometimes even a small gadget can save you the stress of walking around in darkness.

Imagine having four of these lights around your house. NEPA takes light, but as you move around, the lights come on automatically. Small enjoyment!

"2027 election has already been rigged": journalist Seun Okinbaloye raises alarm

31 Oct 2026, 14:12 GMT

Popular TV journalist Seun Okinbaloye has reportedly claimed that the 2027 general election has already been rigged, with reports linking his warning to concerns about flaws in the voter register. The claim has reopened the debate about whether INEC can deliver a credible poll.

Read more

With Nigeria's 2027 general elections still ahead, popular television journalist Seun Okinbaloye has raised a serious concern about the credibility of the electoral process.

What he said

In a statement now circulating widely online, Okinbaloye reportedly said the 2027 election has already been rigged. Reports link his warning to concerns about flaws in the national voter register.

The allegation raises troubling questions about Nigeria's democracy and about whether citizens will be able to freely choose their leaders.

Why it matters

The claim comes at a time when public confidence in Nigeria's electoral system remains a major issue, especially after the controversies that followed previous elections.

For millions of Nigerians, the concern goes beyond which political party wins or loses. The bigger question is whether the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) can guarantee a transparent, credible and fair election in 2027.

A claim, not proof

However, the allegation should not be mistaken for established evidence that the election has been manipulated. A claim this serious needs clear facts and proper investigation.

If Nigerians are already questioning the credibility of an election that has not yet taken place, INEC and the relevant authorities have a responsibility to address those concerns before voters head to the polls.

Democracy loses its meaning when citizens begin to believe that election results are decided before voting begins.
