Imagine if Pastor Ibiyeomie's massive Hand of God Auditorium were a factory employing thousands of Nigerians

6 Nov 2026, 15:54 GMT

When you look at the massive Hand of God Auditorium built by Pastor David Ibiyeomie, it is hard not to imagine something different.

What if a structure of that size, with all the money, planning, engineering and resources that went into it, were a massive factory producing goods for Nigerians and the international market?

Imagine the jobs

Just imagine the number of young Nigerians who could be employed there: engineers, technicians, machine operators, electricians, cleaners, security staff, accountants, drivers and thousands of other workers earning salaries and caring for their families.

Think about the businesses that would spring up around it. Food vendors, transport operators, suppliers and small business owners would all benefit from the economic activity.

And imagine if that factory produced the things Nigeria currently spends billions importing. It could strengthen the economy, encourage local production and create opportunities for future generations.

This is not an attack on the church

This is not an argument against Christianity, churches or places of worship. Everyone has the right to practise their religion, and religious organisations also provide jobs and contribute to society.

But Nigerians must begin to ask serious questions about our priorities as a nation.

A nation searching for jobs

Millions of young Nigerians are searching for work. Many graduates have certificates but nowhere to work. Some have spent years learning skills, yet there are few industries where they can put those skills to use.

Meanwhile, the country keeps celebrating enormous buildings without giving the same attention to factories, industries and other investments that could provide lasting employment.

Imagine if wealthy Nigerians, including religious leaders, invested more heavily in manufacturing alongside their religious projects. Imagine the difference it could make.

Prayer and production

A nation cannot pray its way into industrial development without also investing in production, skills, infrastructure and jobs.

Nigeria needs churches, yes. But it also desperately needs factories where young people can work, earn a decent living and build their future.

Over to you

If you had the money to build an auditorium as big as Pastor Ibiyeomie's Hand of God, would you build a church, or a factory that could employ thousands of Nigerians?

Spectranet to shut down services in Ibadan and Port Harcourt

26 Nov 2026, 09:41 GMT

Internet provider Spectranet says it will end its broadband services in Ibadan and Port Harcourt after a six-month notice period, citing rising operating costs. It will focus on Lagos and Abuja.

Read more

Internet service provider Spectranet Limited has announced that it will discontinue its broadband services in Ibadan and Port Harcourt after a six-month notice period.

What it means for users

The company said it would keep its network running and continue to provide customer support in both cities until the notice period ends, giving subscribers time to arrange alternative connections.

Why

Spectranet said higher operating costs had made it increasingly difficult to maintain its service standards in the two cities. It plans to concentrate its resources in Lagos and Abuja, where it says it will work on improving network performance.

Suspected bandits reportedly abducted nine people from two houses in Millennium City, Chikun, Kaduna State, late on Tuesday, later releasing three. Six remain in captivity.

Read more

Suspected bandits have reportedly abducted six residents in the Millennium City area of Chikun Local Government Area, Kaduna State.

What happened

The attackers stormed two houses at around midnight on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, shooting and breaking in before taking nine people away. Residents said they began hearing gunshots at about 11:30pm.

Three of those abducted, including an elderly woman and a mother with her toddler, were later released, leaving six people in captivity.

Residents' fears

A man whose wife is among those still held said he feared for her safety because she has difficulty walking, and appealed for help to rescue her.

Background

Kaduna has seen several kidnapping incidents in recent weeks, including a foiled attempt at a school in Zangon Kataf and the abduction of Boys' and Girls' Brigade members in Kachia/Kajuru, most of whom troops rescued.

CBN raises weekly dollar limit for BDCs from $150,000 to $1m

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has reportedly raised the weekly dollar purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators to $1 million to ease festive pressure on the naira. Banks have begun implementing the change.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has reportedly raised the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change (BDC) operators from $150,000 to $1 million, in a move aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the foreign exchange market.

How it will work

Sources said commercial banks had started contacting BDC operators to set up arrangements for the new limit. Banks will act only as intermediaries between BDCs and the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market, using a purchase tracking platform.

Background

The change is a major relaxation of restrictions the CBN introduced in February. It comes as the naira has weakened against the dollar in recent days and the country's external reserves have fallen. The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate at 23%.
