Imagine if Pastor Ibiyeomie's massive Hand of God Auditorium were a factory employing thousands of Nigerians

6 Nov 2026, 15:54 GMT

When you look at the massive Hand of God Auditorium built by Pastor David Ibiyeomie, it is hard not to imagine something different.

What if a structure of that size, with all the money, planning, engineering and resources that went into it, were a massive factory producing goods for Nigerians and the international market?

Imagine the jobs

Just imagine the number of young Nigerians who could be employed there: engineers, technicians, machine operators, electricians, cleaners, security staff, accountants, drivers and thousands of other workers earning salaries and caring for their families.

Think about the businesses that would spring up around it. Food vendors, transport operators, suppliers and small business owners would all benefit from the economic activity.

And imagine if that factory produced the things Nigeria currently spends billions importing. It could strengthen the economy, encourage local production and create opportunities for future generations.

This is not an attack on the church

This is not an argument against Christianity, churches or places of worship. Everyone has the right to practise their religion, and religious organisations also provide jobs and contribute to society.

But Nigerians must begin to ask serious questions about our priorities as a nation.

A nation searching for jobs

Millions of young Nigerians are searching for work. Many graduates have certificates but nowhere to work. Some have spent years learning skills, yet there are few industries where they can put those skills to use.

Meanwhile, the country keeps celebrating enormous buildings without giving the same attention to factories, industries and other investments that could provide lasting employment.

Imagine if wealthy Nigerians, including religious leaders, invested more heavily in manufacturing alongside their religious projects. Imagine the difference it could make.

Prayer and production

A nation cannot pray its way into industrial development without also investing in production, skills, infrastructure and jobs.

Nigeria needs churches, yes. But it also desperately needs factories where young people can work, earn a decent living and build their future.

Over to you

If you had the money to build an auditorium as big as Pastor Ibiyeomie's Hand of God, would you build a church, or a factory that could employ thousands of Nigerians?

IGP tells doctors: no police report needed to treat gunshot victims

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Inspector-General of Police Olatunji Disu has reminded doctors and hospitals that the law does not require a police report before treating gunshot victims. He urged health workers to put saving lives first.

Read more

The Inspector-General of Police, Olatunji Disu, has urged doctors and other health workers to treat gunshot victims immediately, without first demanding a police report.

What the IGP said

In a message posted on the Nigeria Police Force's official X account on Thursday, 19 November 2026, Disu stressed that the law does not require hospitals to obtain a police report before attending to people with gunshot wounds.

He appealed to doctors and health workers to prioritise saving lives instead of waiting for police documents before starting emergency treatment.

Help for the public

The IGP also encouraged members of the public not to hesitate to help injured people get to hospital.

Background

Nigeria has a law requiring hospitals to treat gunshot victims without first demanding a police report.

Polytechnic lecturers begin strike in Ogun, Oyo, Osun and Ekiti

20 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

ASUP Zone C has begun a strike in state-owned polytechnics and colleges of technology in four South-West states over unpaid academic allowances and arrears.

Read more

The Academic Staff Union of Polytechnics (ASUP), Zone C, has begun a strike in state-owned polytechnics and colleges of technology in four South-West states over unpaid academic allowances.

Where and when

The strike affects institutions in Ogun, Oyo, Osun and Ekiti States. The union said it took effect from Monday, 16 November 2026, after a 15-day ultimatum to the state governments expired.

The announcement was made at a press conference at The Polytechnic, Ibadan, on Thursday, 19 November.

The demands

The union wants immediate implementation of the Consolidated Academic Tools Allowance and the Earned Academic Allowance for academic staff, as well as payment of arrears dating back to 1 January 2026. It said the federal government had concluded the renegotiation of the union's agreement, but the state governments had yet to implement it.

The business community in Enugu State has been thrown into mourning following reports that billionaire businessman Chief Festus Oshaba-Onuh has died in a road accident.

What happened

Oshaba-Onuh, the chief executive of Ferotex Group and popularly known as Ferotex, reportedly died on the morning of Thursday, 19 November 2026, in a crash on the Enugu-Port Harcourt Expressway. He was said to be travelling back to Enugu at the time.

How the news spread

News of his death emerged after an Enugu-based social media influencer shared a video of the crash scene, showing a damaged SUV said to have been involved. The influencer described the death as a major loss to Enugu State and beyond.

Governor confirms

Enugu State Governor Peter Mbah confirmed the death in a social media post on Thursday, describing it as a grievous loss to the entire state and a painful personal blow. He described Oshaba-Onuh as a great son of the state. The businessman was known for his work in the construction sector.
