US corrections officer accused of inviting another man home, then calling him an intruder and firing gun when boyfriend returned

20 Nov 2026, 09:20 GMT

A 34-year-old woman in Michigan, United States, has been charged with six felonies after police say she invited another man to her home while her boyfriend was away, then reported him as an intruder when her partner returned unexpectedly.

The 7am phone call

According to prosecutors, Lasacia Gore, a corrections officer with Wayne County, called police at around 7am on Monday, claiming an unknown man had broken into her home in Eastpointe and was fighting with her boyfriend.

But investigators say they found that she had invited the man over herself, and had even left the door unlocked for him.

What police say happened

According to police, things turned violent when her boyfriend came home unexpectedly, found the visitor in the house and confronted him, leading to a fight.

Eastpointe Police Chief Corey Haines said Gore fired a gun while both men were wrestling on the kitchen floor. The bullet missed them and struck the wall above them.

"You told me to leave the door open"

Macomb County Prosecutor Pete Lucido said the visitor's account contradicted hers. "She's making a call saying she doesn't know him and this and that, and he says, 'What are you talking about? You're the one who told me to leave the door open and I'm coming in!'" he said.

"It's crazy. This could have been very tragic and somebody could've died," Lucido added.

Chief Haines also expressed concern that a corrections officer was allegedly involved in making a false police report and firing a weapon.

The charges

Gore faces six felony charges, including assault with a dangerous weapon, discharging a firearm at a building, filing a false felony report, lying to investigators and two firearm-related counts.

She pleaded not guilty at her arraignment on Wednesday. Her bond was set at $30,000, with a 10 per cent option. If released, she must stay at home under GPS monitoring, surrender her weapons and avoid contact with the complainant.

She is due back in court on 2 December for a probable cause conference. The charges are allegations and have not been proven in court.

Netflix removes Tyson Fury schizophrenia claim from documentary

27 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Netflix has removed references to schizophrenia from its Tyson Fury documentary after it emerged the boxer has not been medically diagnosed with the condition. The claim had come from his wife, Paris.

Read more

Netflix has removed references to schizophrenia from its upcoming documentary about boxer Tyson Fury, after it emerged that he has not been medically diagnosed with the condition.

What changed

In the original version of the 90-minute film, Fury's wife, Paris, said doctors had regarded him as a mild schizophrenic in the year after he beat Wladimir Klitschko in November 2015. Those references have now been taken out. The documentary premieres on Netflix on Friday, 27 November 2026.

Boxing board concerns

The British Boxing Board of Control, which is sanctioning Fury's fight with Anthony Joshua on 11 December, had raised concerns about his mental health after the original statements. Its rules bar boxers from fighting if they have certain psychological disorders.

Background

Fury struggled with depression, drug and alcohol problems after the Klitschko win and did not return to the ring for nearly three years. He has spoken openly about his mental health, including bipolar disorder. An earlier story on this site said he had been diagnosed with schizophrenia; that report has been corrected.

Trump calls for probe after US growth figure is revised upward

27 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

President Donald Trump has demanded an investigation after official figures revised US third-quarter growth up to 4.3%, claiming without evidence that the earlier figure was deliberately understated before the midterms.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has called for an immediate investigation into an upward revision of US economic growth figures.

The revision

On Wednesday, the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis revised annualised growth for the third quarter to 4.3%, up from its earlier estimate of 3.4% released on 29 October. Such revisions are a standard part of the process as more complete data becomes available.

Trump's claim

In a post on Truth Social on Thursday, 26 November 2026, Trump branded the earlier, pre-election figure fake and asked why the correct number was not released before the 3 November midterm elections. He claimed, without evidence, that the earlier figure had been deliberately understated.

Background

Trump's Republican Party suffered heavy losses in the midterms, with Democrats winning control of both the House and the Senate.

The pledge

Questions remain

US appeals court gives Trump new route to challenge hush money conviction

26 Nov 2026, 14:41 GMT

A US federal appeals court has revived Donald Trump's bid to move his New York hush money case into federal court, where he hopes to have it dismissed on presidential immunity grounds.

Read more

A US federal appeals court has given President Donald Trump a fresh opening to try to overturn his New York hush money conviction.

The ruling

In a short order issued on Monday, 23 November 2026, a three-judge panel of the 2nd US Circuit Court of Appeals sent the dispute back to a federal district court in Manhattan, instructing a judge to rule quickly on Trump's request to transfer the case to federal court.

The panel found that the previous judge had been wrong to conclude that moving a case to federal court after conviction was never possible.

What Trump wants

If the case is transferred, Trump intends to seek its dismissal on presidential immunity grounds. Any decision could later return to the 2nd Circuit.
