IK Ogbonna and Ego Nwosu reveal viral proposal was a promo for a wedding-themed event

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Nollywood actors IK Ogbonna and Ego Nwosu have reportedly cleared the air on their viral proposal video, confirming that they are not actually getting married.

The reveal

In a new video posted on Monday, 16 November 2026, the pair appeared together and explained that the proposal was part of a campaign for an event called Love at Julirose, billed for 15 December in Port Harcourt, Rivers State.

The event is described as a full wedding-themed experience, with a live band, bridesmaids and groomsmen, photographers and live performances. Tickets are on sale with different price levels and a dress code.

The playful banter

The two kept up the jokes in the new video, with IK saying he was still the groom and Ego replying that she was still the bride, making it clear the campaign was designed to create buzz.

How fans reacted

Many fans who had congratulated the pair earlier this month reacted with amusement and mock anger, with some jokingly asking for their congratulations back.

Media personality Denrele Edun has announced the death of his father, Edun Adekunle Alaba, sharing the news on what would have been the late patriarch's 85th birthday.

What he shared

Denrele said his father, who was born on 17 November 1941, died on Monday, 12 October 2026, after a brief illness, just weeks before the family had planned to celebrate his birthday in a big way.

Tribute

In a moving tribute, Denrele described his father as a man whose faith was part of his everyday life, not just his Sunday routine. He was a church elder, a warden and a bass singer in the choir of the Methodist church.

With his usual humour, Denrele recalled how his father insisted that he and his siblings join the church choir, even though their singing voices were limited.

What she shared

DSS says it found no evidence it planned to disrupt ADC campaign in Sokoto, asks Atiku to retract or provide proof

18 Nov 2026, 07:57 GMT

The Department of State Services says its internal investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off in Sokoto. It has asked Atiku Abubakar to retract or clarify his allegation, or provide evidence.

Read more

The Department of State Services (DSS) says its investigation found no evidence that it planned to recruit protesters to disrupt the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential campaign in Sokoto.

How it started

Earlier this month, former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar accused the leadership of the DSS of recruiting protesters to disrupt the ADC's presidential campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto.

The allegation drew wide attention, and the DSS responded by ordering an investigation into the claim.

The DSS findings

The agency now says its investigation found nothing to support the allegation that it planned to recruit protesters or disrupt the ADC campaign.

It has asked Atiku to either retract or clarify his claim, or to provide evidence that could support further investigation.

Not the final word

It is important to note that this is the DSS's own preliminary finding. It was not an independent investigation, which means the agency effectively investigated an allegation made against itself.

Atiku has not issued a fresh public response to the DSS findings.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections approaching, allegations that security agencies are being used against opposition parties are likely to keep coming up. Public trust depends on such claims being properly examined, whether they turn out to be true or not.
