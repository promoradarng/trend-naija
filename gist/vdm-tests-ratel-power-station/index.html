VeryDarkMan puts his Ratel Power Station to the test, shows off battery power as Blord rivalry continues

29 Oct 2026, 13:55 GMT

Popular Nigerian social media activist and businessman Martins Vincent Otse, better known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has once again got Nigerians talking after demonstrating the capabilities of his newly imported Ratel Power Station.

VeryDarkMan, who recently received a 40-foot container loaded with power stations and other gadgets from China, has begun testing the products to show potential customers what they can do.

One particular demonstration has caught the attention of many people.

In the test, the smallest model, rated at 1,200 watts, powered a television, a standing fan and a refrigerator for 5 hours 17 minutes. The TV drew 74W, the fan 50W and the fridge 150W, a total of 274W when all three were running.

And remember, this is just the smallest model in the range!

For a country like Nigeria, where unreliable electricity continues to affect homes, students, artisans and small businesses, a portable power station with such capabilities could be a useful alternative during power outages.

VDM has previously explained that his target customers include students, barbers, makeup artists and other Nigerians whose daily activities depend on electricity.

However, the demonstration has also added another chapter to the ongoing social media rivalry between VeryDarkMan and businessman Blord.

While Blord has continued promoting his own gadgets through videos and online publicity, supporters of VeryDarkMan are challenging him to demonstrate the actual performance of his products in a similar way.

And this is where the argument becomes interesting. Making promotional videos is one thing, but showing customers what a product can actually do is another.

VDM appears determined to let the performance of his products speak for itself, and his supporters are already celebrating his approach.

The box shows a battery capacity of 932Wh. A runtime of 5 hours 17 minutes fits with that, because a fridge's compressor switches on and off rather than running all the time, so the average load stays below the full 274W. Actual runtime will still vary with the appliances used and how they are used.

Nevertheless, the excitement surrounding the Ratel Power Station shows how much attention VeryDarkMan's latest business venture is attracting.

From activism and philanthropy to importing products from China, VDM is gradually expanding into commercial ventures. And as his supporters would say, VeryDarkMan is whoever he says he is!

Now, the big question is this: between VeryDarkMan's Ratel Power Station and Blord's gadgets, who is actually giving Nigerians better value for their money?

Let the products do the talking!

VeryDarkMan questions INEC chairman's photo with APC UK chapter chairman

Activist VeryDarkMan has questioned why INEC chairman Joash Amupitan was photographed with the APC's UK chapter chairman during his London trip, asking whether other parties' UK leaders were given similar access.

Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has raised questions about a photograph showing the chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) with a leader of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) in the UK.

What he said

In a post reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, VDM clarified that the man wearing an APC-branded cap in the photo was not INEC chairman Professor Joash Amupitan, but Tunde Doherty, chairman of the APC's UK chapter. He asked what the APC official was doing with the INEC chairman and whether the UK leaders of other parties, including the NDC, Labour Party, PDP and ADC, had been given the same opportunity to meet him. He went on to claim that INEC was compromised.

Background

Amupitan was in London this week to speak at Chatham House about preparations for the 2027 elections. Protesters confronted him outside the venue, accusing the commission of bias, and he pledged to reject any political appointment after leaving office to show his independence. INEC has also denied reports that he plans to resign. INEC had not responded to VDM's post at the time of writing, and the circumstances of the photo are not clear.

All 62 contacts of Nigeria's Ebola case complete monitoring with no spread, NCDC says

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The NCDC says all 62 people linked to Nigeria's single imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a type of Ebola, have completed 21 days of monitoring with no further transmission. It warns this is not yet a declaration that Nigeria is free of the disease.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says all 62 contacts linked to the country's imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, have completed their monitoring without any further spread.

The update

Speaking at a news conference in Abuja on Friday, 13 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said the last contact had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. He said 13 of the contacts developed fever or other symptoms during monitoring but tested negative for the disease. Seven other alerts reported over three weeks in Adamawa, Rivers, Borno, Lagos and Ekiti were investigated, and all suspected cases tested negative.

Not yet Ebola-free

Idris cautioned that the end of contact monitoring was not a formal declaration that Nigeria was free of the disease, and said surveillance must continue. He said any such declaration would depend on public health criteria and ongoing surveillance, especially as transmission continues in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Background

Earlier this week, the NCDC said 28 contacts were still under watch. The United States has temporarily barred travellers who have been in the DRC in the past 21 days. Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness or unexplained bleeding after travel to report to a health facility.

FG issues Ethiopia travel advisory after 16 Nigerians die transiting Addis Ababa

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The federal government has warned Nigerians with heart and lung conditions to seek medical clearance before travelling through Addis Ababa, after 16 Nigerians died in transit there in what officials link to the city's high altitude.

Read more

The federal government has issued a travel advisory warning Nigerians about the health risks of Ethiopia's high altitude, after the deaths of 16 Nigerians transiting through Addis Ababa.

The advisory

In a statement by Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson Oluwafemi Adeniyi, reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the government urged travellers with existing medical conditions, especially heart and respiratory problems, to be cautious and to obtain medical clearance before taking journeys with stopovers in the Ethiopian capital.

Why altitude matters

Addis Ababa sits about 2,355 metres above sea level, where lower air pressure reduces the oxygen available. The ministry noted that dry air, stronger sunlight and faster fluid loss can also cause problems, from headaches and breathlessness to serious conditions such as swelling of the brain. Healthy travellers usually adapt within a few days, but people with underlying illnesses face much higher risks.

Background

Addis Ababa is a major transit hub for Nigerian travellers. The ministry did not give details of the individual deaths.

ASUU warns officials against using union's name to endorse 2027 candidates

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Academic Staff Union of Universities says it will sanction any official who uses the union's name to back a candidate or party in 2027, stressing that it remains apolitical though members are free to vote.

Read more

The Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) has warned its members and officials not to use the union's name to endorse political candidates or parties ahead of the 2027 elections.

What ASUU said

Speaking at the union's 2026 Heroes' Day celebration in Abuja, reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, ASUU National President Professor Christopher Piwuna said individual lecturers keep their constitutional right to vote and take part in politics, but must not present their personal choices as the union's position. He said no member had permission to use ASUU's name to support any candidate or party, that the union remained apolitical, and that any official found compromising that stance by associating with parties or politicians would face disciplinary action.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
