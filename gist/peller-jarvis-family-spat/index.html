Cute Abiola's old friend says the comedian drifted away after fame

6 Nov 2026, 17:43 GMT

A TikToker who says he was once close to skit maker Cute Abiola has posted throwback videos complaining that their friendship faded after the comedian became successful. He insists it is not about money. Cute Abiola has not responded.

Read more

A TikToker who says he was once a close friend of skit maker The Cute Abiola has gone public with complaints that the comedian abandoned him after becoming famous.

What he posted

The man, who goes by the handle Mayordona Nolan, posted a short clip on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, showing himself and Cute Abiola together in a car. He described the two of them and another friend as a close trio who supported each other through hard times, and said that two of them had since found success while he remained in a difficult situation, and the friendship had faded. He posted another throwback video of the three friends the next day.

'Not about money'

After some viewers accused him of feeling entitled, he posted a long clarification insisting that his posts had nothing to do with money or expectations of financial help.

Background

Cute Abiola, a former member of the Nigerian Navy, is one of Nigeria's best-known skit makers. The complaints came in the same week that the comedian posted videos appealing to President Bola Tinubu over power cuts and economic hardship, saying he had sold one of his cars to settle debts.

How people reacted

Reactions were divided. Some said friends who shared hard times should make an effort to stay in touch, while others said success does not create an obligation to keep every old relationship and accused the man of entitlement. Cute Abiola had not publicly responded at the time of writing.

Cute Abiola says he sold one of his cars as he appeals to Tinubu over power and hardship

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

Comedian and skit maker The Cute Abiola has posted videos appealing to President Tinubu over poor electricity and the cost of living, saying his solar system had run down and that he recently sold one of his cars to settle debts.

Read more

Comedian and skit maker The Cute Abiola has joined Nigerians complaining about poor electricity supply and economic hardship, in videos addressed directly to President Bola Tinubu.

What he said

In one video shared online on Friday, 6 November 2026, the content creator said his solar power system had gone off because of heavy use during the long power outages in his area. He appealed to the President to help Nigerians, saying everywhere was hot, there was no light and the suffering was too much. In another video, he said he had just sold one of his cars to ease his financial situation, explaining that he owed people money and still needed funds to shoot new content. He added that he hoped things would not get to the point where he had to sell his house.

Background

Abiola, whose real name is Abdulgafar Ahmed, became popular for his comedy skits and is a former member of the Nigerian Navy. His complaints come as consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states report renewed power outages, which the federal government has blamed on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced hydropower generation. The government has also started paying a ₦50,000 monthly relief grant to selected households.

How people reacted

Many fans sympathised and said his videos reflected what millions of Nigerians are going through. Some critics said he had not praised the government when things were going well for him and was now blaming the President, while others said public figures should be free to speak about their struggles.

"If not for VeryDarkMan, I wouldn't be where I am today": Ratel Princess Jojooflele expresses gratitude from China

5 Nov 2026, 21:44 GMT

Social media personality Jojooflele, known as the Ratel Princess, has publicly thanked VeryDarkMan for changing her life, saying her following and opportunities grew after she met him. She spoke from China, a long way from the Nedu controversy that first brought her into the spotlight.

Read more

Popular social media personality Jojooflele, widely known as the Ratel Princess, has openly expressed her appreciation for VeryDarkMan (VDM), acknowledging the role he has played in changing her life.

"If not for VDM"

Speaking from China, Jojooflele reflected on how far she has come and how her association with VDM opened doors she probably never imagined would open so soon.

"If not for VDM, I wouldn't be where I am today," she said.

According to her, things took a completely different turn after she met VeryDarkMan. Her social media pages began attracting more attention, her followers increased, and opportunities started coming her way.

How it all started

What makes her story even more interesting is how it began. Many will remember the controversy involving Nedu, when Jojooflele found herself at the centre of public attention, with people discussing her personal life and questioning her actions.

For someone else, that kind of controversy could have been enough to damage their confidence or push them off social media completely.

Instead, Jojooflele turned the attention into something positive. Rather than letting the controversy define her future, she kept building her presence online and eventually became a familiar face among supporters of the Ratel Movement.

A lesson in gratitude

Today, she is in China, reflecting on her journey and publicly appreciating someone she believes contributed greatly to her success.

Jojooflele deserves credit for how she has handled her growing popularity. The right connections can open doors, but what you do with those opportunities matters just as much.

Her appreciation for VDM is also worth acknowledging. In a society where people sometimes forget those who supported them once they start to succeed, publicly recognising someone's contribution says a lot about gratitude.

Setbacks are not the end

Perhaps the biggest lesson here is never to be too quick to conclude that your present difficulties will decide your future. The situation people expect to destroy you can end up introducing you to opportunities you never knew existed.

What looks like a setback today may become part of the story you proudly tell tomorrow.

Wike and his Rainbow Coalition raise eyebrows as minister appears visibly angry at Rivers campaign ground

7 Nov 2026, 17:19 GMT

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike was reportedly seen checking how many people each of his aides had brought to the presidential campaign ground in Rivers State, looking far from pleased. The turnout has become a talking point, and so has the old question of paid crowds.

Read more

FCT Minister Nyesom Wike and his Rainbow Coalition campaign have got people talking again, this time after he appeared visibly angry at the presidential campaign ground in Rivers State.

Counting heads

Wike was reportedly seen checking how many people each of his aides had brought to the venue. Photos show the minister, in his black and red hat and a rainbow "Coalition" sash bearing the APC logo, deep in conversation with aides and looking far from amused.

One can only feel sorry for any aide who collected mobilisation money and failed to deliver the expected crowd.

The turnout question

The expectation may have been a stadium filled to capacity, but the turnout has now become a talking point on social media.

That brings us to Peter Obi, whose supporters often describe his campaign crowds as organic, with people attending out of personal conviction rather than financial inducement.

Can anyone fill a stadium for free?

Crowd mobilisation, from buses to refreshments to small payments, has long been a feature of Nigerian political rallies across parties. Large crowds make for impressive pictures, but they do not always reflect how people will vote.

So one question remains: can any Nigerian politician genuinely fill a stadium without spending money on crowd mobilisation?

Nigerian politics will never stop surprising us.
