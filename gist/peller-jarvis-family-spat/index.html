Social media activist VeryDarkMan (VDM) has continued his public exchange with singer Zlatan Ibile, daring him to openly deny claims that he secretly sent celebrities to plead with VDM over their ongoing disagreement.

"Just come out and say it's a lie"

Speaking partly in Pidgin, VDM said, in effect: "It's been over 24 hours since I dared Zlatan to come out and say I'm lying that he sent people to beg me. He acts like a hard guy in public, but does something else behind the scenes."

"Just come out and say it's a lie, simple! Let me call out the names of the celebrities you sent. If they deny it too, then you'll see what my next post will be."

He added that he was only holding back out of respect for Zlatan's parents: "It's because your mother and father are involved. We still respect parents here. If not, everyone would already know that you have no class."

Tough in public, soft in private?

VDM claims Zlatan is acting tough in public while making moves behind the scenes to settle the matter, and has threatened to name the celebrities he says were sent if Zlatan stays silent.

The feud began after a video of Davido singing a line from Zlatan's song INFERNO at Zlatan's wedding welcome party in Morocco, a lyric VDM believes was aimed at him. VDM then warned Zlatan that he would "learn the hard way".

Allegations, not facts

For now, these remain VeryDarkMan's claims. Zlatan has not responded publicly, and his silence does not confirm them.

"Pharaoh, let my people go!": Cubana Chief Priest replies Umahi, accuses him of giving Tinubu fake promises on South-East votes

9 Nov 2026, 11:14 GMT

Cubana Chief Priest has fired back at Works Minister David Umahi, nicknaming him "Pharaoh" and accusing him of making empty promises to President Tinubu about South-East votes. He also claimed Umahi is preparing his son to become Ebonyi governor, and mocked him with his own "money na water" catchphrase.

Read more

Socialite and businessman Cubana Chief Priest has replied Minister of Works David Umahi, a day after the minister mocked his famous "money na water" catchphrase and asked how many people he has given scholarships.

"Pharaoh, let my people go"

Borrowing from the Bible story of Moses and Pharaoh, Cubana Chief Priest gave the minister a new nickname, and accused him of making promises to President Bola Tinubu that he cannot deliver.

"Pharaoh, let my people go. Stop giving Tinubu fake promises on votes. What project are you using to convince South-East people to vote for him?" he said.

The governorship claim

He went further, claiming that Umahi is preparing his son to become governor of Ebonyi State after the current governor, Francis Nwifuru.

Speaking partly in Pidgin, he said, in effect: "You're busy preparing your son to be governor after Nwifuru. God won't let that happen. You want to use Ebonyi State to settle your child. Let the young man come and meet us on the street so we can teach him how to make 'money na water'."

This is Cubana Chief Priest's claim. Umahi has not publicly declared any plan for his son to contest the Ebonyi governorship, and he has not yet responded to this latest attack.

How it started

The exchange began weeks ago when Cubana Chief Priest described Umahi as the second-worst minister from the South-East. Umahi hit back by telling him he was "becoming very noisy", comparing him unfavourably with Dangote and Otedola, and asking, "How many people have you given scholarships?"

Now the socialite has turned the argument back to politics, questioning what Umahi has done to win South-East votes for the President.

Who is winning this war of words: the minister or the socialite? And is Cubana Chief Priest right to question what the South-East has to show for supporting Tinubu?

Trump admits immigration crackdown was 'too tough' after midterm defeat

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

President Donald Trump has said his administration's immigration crackdown was too tough and hurt Republicans in the midterm elections, particularly the deportation of people with stable jobs whose only offence was breaking immigration rules.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has acknowledged that his administration's immigration crackdown went too far, in a notable change of tone after his party's heavy losses in the midterm elections.

What he said

In an interview broadcast by the CBS programme '60 Minutes' on Sunday, 8 November 2026, Trump defended his record on border security and on removing dangerous criminals, but said he thought the crackdown had been too tough. He said the administration had suffered politically by deporting people who had stable jobs and had only committed immigration violations, and that this had hurt Republican candidates at the ballot box.

The election

Exit polls showed immigration was the third most important issue for voters, after the economy and the state of democracy. In traditionally conservative states such as Texas, a shift among Latino voters towards the Democrats contributed to their gains in Congress, where they are projected to control both the House of Representatives and the Senate.

Background

Mass deportations became a defining policy of Trump's second term, drawing protests and legal challenges. Only days ago he promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of the deportation programme, to White House chief of staff, so it is unclear how far policy will change in practice. Groups of Nigerians have been among those deported, including six who arrived in Lagos last week.

Nigeria seeks $500m World Bank loan for infrastructure financing platform

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The federal government has approached the World Bank for a $500 million loan to set up a platform to attract private investment into infrastructure, part of a wider $3.85 billion programme called INFRAGROW.

Read more

The federal government has asked the World Bank for a $500 million loan to set up a platform to attract private investment into Nigerian infrastructure.

The loan

According to a World Bank project document updated on 4 November 2026 and reported on Monday, 9 November, the facility is part of a broader $3.85 billion programme called the Nigeria Infrastructure Financing and De-Risking Platform for Growth and Jobs, or INFRAGROW. The Federal Ministry of Finance is listed as the borrower and the Nigeria Sovereign Investment Authority (NSIA) as the implementing agency. The aim is to reduce the risks that discourage private investors from funding infrastructure projects.

Why the bank says it is needed

The World Bank estimates that Nigeria has an infrastructure financing gap of about $100 billion a year, which holds back growth, competitiveness and job creation. It says the problem is not only a lack of money but also weak project preparation and difficulty turning proposals into commercially viable investments.

Background

The request comes as President Bola Tinubu also seeks Senate approval for about $7.7 billion in new external borrowing, and as government borrowing becomes a major campaign issue. NDC candidate Peter Obi has challenged the President to account for loans, while the Presidency says claims that it has borrowed ₦120 trillion are distorted. The World Bank reported last week that Nigeria's total external debt rose to about $145 billion in 2025.
