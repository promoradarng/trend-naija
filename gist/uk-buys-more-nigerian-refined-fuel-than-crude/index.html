UK now buys more refined fuel than crude oil from Nigeria, data shows

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

Nigeria's petroleum trade with the United Kingdom has shifted sharply towards refined fuel, according to UK government figures.

The figures

The UK's trade and investment factsheet, released on 6 November 2026 and reported this weekend, shows Britain bought £1.02 billion worth of refined petroleum products from Nigeria in the 12 months to June 2026. That was more than double the figure a year earlier and made up 53.5 per cent of all UK goods imports from Nigeria. Over the same period, UK purchases of Nigerian crude oil fell by 56.2 per cent to £550.3 million, meaning refined fuel exports exceeded crude shipments by about £467 million. Nigeria recorded a petroleum trade surplus of about £245 million with Britain.

Why it matters

The change reflects the growth of the Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Lagos, which can process 650,000 barrels a day and has been expanding its exports.

Background

The government has also announced plans for a new NNPC refinery in Edo State.

CBN and NNPC sign new agreement to tighten oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue

18 Nov 2026, 14:54 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement covering foreign-exchange inflows, remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing and more. It promises a more structured system, but the full agreement has not been released and no revenue targets have been set.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and NNPC Limited have signed a new agreement aimed at tightening oversight of Nigeria's oil revenue.

What the agreement covers

The agreement covers foreign-exchange inflows, the remittance of oil proceeds, energy financing, digital payments, regulatory compliance and cybersecurity.

Officials say it will replace the old practice of dealing with issues one transaction at a time with a more structured system, including joint committees and performance monitoring.

Why it matters

Oil remains Nigeria's biggest source of foreign exchange and a major part of government revenue. For years, questions have been raised about how much oil money actually reaches government accounts, and how quickly.

Stronger coordination between the central bank and the national oil company could, in theory, improve transparency, boost foreign-exchange supply and help stabilise the naira.

Promises versus results

It sounds promising, but Nigerians will need to see measurable results. The full agreement has not been made public, and no target has been given for additional revenue or foreign-exchange inflows.

Without published targets and regular public reporting, it will be difficult for citizens to judge whether the new arrangement is working.

The real test

The real test is simple: will more oil money reach government accounts, and will Nigerians be able to see it?

FG moves to start direct flights between Nigeria and Ireland

18 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo says Nigeria is working towards direct flights to Ireland, which could open a new route for local airlines such as Air Peace and Enugu Air.

Read more

Nigeria is taking steps to establish direct flights to Ireland, a move that could open a new international route for local airlines including Air Peace and Enugu Air.

What was said

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, disclosed the plan during a meeting with the Irish Ambassador to Nigeria, Lesley Ní Bhriain. He said Nigeria was ready to deepen aviation cooperation with Ireland, citing growing demand for travel between the two countries.

Why it matters

The proposed arrangement is expected to strengthen trade, tourism, education and business ties between the two countries, and make travel easier for Nigerians living in Ireland.

What happens next

Details such as the timeline for the first flights and which airlines will operate the route have not been announced.

Nigerian stock market loses ₦2.27 trillion in one day

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Investors lost about ₦2.27 trillion on the Nigerian Exchange on Tuesday as the All-Share Index fell 1.38%. Trading volume and deals also dropped.

Read more

The Nigerian stock market closed sharply lower on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, as sell-offs in major stocks wiped about ₦2.27 trillion off market value in a single trading session.

The numbers

Data from the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) showed the All-Share Index fell by 3,491.13 points, or 1.38%, to close at 249,318.65, down from 252,809.78 in the previous session. Total market capitalisation dropped by the same percentage, from ₦164.15 trillion to ₦161.88 trillion.

Trading activity

Activity also slowed. Investors traded 564.04 million shares, down 16.26% from 673.6 million in the previous session, while the number of deals fell by 18.91% to 30,992.

Bright spots

The losses were partly cushioned by buying interest in consumer goods and insurance stocks, with Cadbury Nigeria among the top gainers.

MTN launches MTN One TV streaming service with Nigerian original series

18 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

MTN has introduced MTN One TV, an ad-supported streaming platform to compete with Netflix and others. Its first original is a Nigerian series, 100 Ways to Stay Single in Lagos.

Read more

Telecommunications giant MTN has launched MTN One TV, an advertising-supported streaming service aimed at African audiences, as it takes on global platforms such as Netflix, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video and Apple TV.

The platform

The service is designed to give viewers access to local entertainment and original productions, and is expected to eventually include free-to-air television channels.

The first original

MTN has unveiled 100 Ways to Stay Single in Lagos, a Nigerian-produced series featuring actors from Nigeria, Ghana and South Africa. It is the platform's first original series.

Why it matters

The move signals MTN's ambition to become more than a mobile network provider, as competition for viewers in Africa's growing streaming market increases.
