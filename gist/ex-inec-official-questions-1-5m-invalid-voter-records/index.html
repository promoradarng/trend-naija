Former INEC director asks how 1.5 million questionable records got into the voter register

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

A former senior official of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has questioned how about 1.5 million questionable records found their way into the country's voter register.

What he said

Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, a former Director of Voter Education and Publicity at INEC, said in a television interview reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, that such problems should not happen given the safeguards the commission has put in place. He noted that the law on who can register is simple: a Nigerian citizen aged 18 or above who turns up in person to register. Because registration requires physical presence and the capture of fingerprints and photographs, he said, he could not understand how so many questionable entries had been made.

His questions for INEC

Osaze-Uzzi said the commission, led by its chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, had not explained what period its review of the register covered or how the problem entries were first detected. He said tools such as the Automated Fingerprint Identification System and artificial intelligence can help to remove duplicates, but that INEC should still give a clear account of how the records got into the database in the first place.

Background

INEC regularly cleans up the register to remove duplicate, underage and other invalid entries before elections, and the issue of the accuracy of the register has long been debated. The 2027 general elections are due in January, with the presidential poll on 16 January. INEC also urged young Nigerians this week to turn their online enthusiasm into votes, saying votes cannot be counted on social media.

Why it matters

A clean voter register is central to public confidence in elections. Civil society groups have urged INEC to publish more detail on the clean-up so that eligible voters are not wrongly removed.

President Bola Tinubu and his former close ally Rauf Aregbesola have met at least three times since their political falling-out, according to a senior figure in the ruling party.

What he said

Professor Sola Adeyeye, director of electioneering planning for the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential campaign in the South-West, said in an interview reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, that he knew the two men had met about three times since the rift began. He described them as former very close friends, said the hurt is deeper when people who care about each other feel betrayed, and suggested time could heal the relationship. He said he had personally tried to broker a reconciliation but did not give details.

Background

Aregbesola, a former Osun governor and minister, was one of Tinubu's closest political allies for decades before they fell out. He is now national secretary of the Mark-led faction of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), whose leadership was voided by a Federal High Court this month, a ruling the party has appealed. Aregbesola has not commented on Adeyeye's claim.

Representatives of former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and Oyo State governor Seyi Makinde have reportedly been meeting to discuss a possible alliance ahead of the 2027 presidential election.

The talks

According to reports on Sunday, 15 November 2026, sources from both camps say the prospect of an alliance is looking increasingly likely. An ADC leader in the South-West, speaking anonymously, described the discussions as fresh, purposeful and promising, and suggested an announcement would come before the 16 January election. Makinde said this week that talks with both Atiku and Obi remained open. ADC running mate Rotimi Amaechi also told a rally in Bauchi that the APM would win one South-West state.

Judgment details: why the court sacked the Mark-led ADC leadership

14 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The certified copy of the judgment that voided the David Mark-led ADC leadership shows the judge found a key resignation letter was fabricated and ruled that the party's executive committee could not appoint national officers without an election.

Read more

Details of the Federal High Court judgment that nullified the David Mark-led leadership of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) have emerged from the certified true copy of the ruling.

The resignation letter

Justice Peter Lifu, who delivered the judgment on 5 November 2026, examined three main issues. The first was a letter said to show that the party's deputy national chairman, Nafiu Bala Gombe, had resigned in May 2025, before the meeting that installed the new leadership. After comparing signatures, the judge found clear differences, described the letter as fictitious or fabricated and rejected it as evidence.

Powers of the executive committee

The ADC argued that its constitution allowed the National Executive Committee to dissolve the old leadership and appoint a new one. The judge accepted that parties may run their internal affairs, but held that this cannot override the Constitution and the Electoral Act, which require parties to elect their principal officers through periodic democratic elections. He ruled that the committee's administrative powers did not allow it to appoint national executives in place of an election.

No proof of a vote

The judge also found that the party could not show that members or delegates had been given the chance to vote for candidates of their choice, and said notifying INEC of a meeting was not the same as complying with the law.

Background

The judgment also voided the primary that produced Atiku Abubakar as the ADC's presidential candidate. The party has appealed, and INEC says it will obey the final court decision. Atiku and his running mate Rotimi Amaechi are continuing to campaign.
