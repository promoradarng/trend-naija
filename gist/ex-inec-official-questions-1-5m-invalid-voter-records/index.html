Former INEC director asks how 1.5 million questionable records got into the voter register

6 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

A former senior official of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has questioned how about 1.5 million questionable records found their way into the country's voter register.

What he said

Oluwole Osaze-Uzzi, a former Director of Voter Education and Publicity at INEC, said in a television interview reported on Friday, 6 November 2026, that such problems should not happen given the safeguards the commission has put in place. He noted that the law on who can register is simple: a Nigerian citizen aged 18 or above who turns up in person to register. Because registration requires physical presence and the capture of fingerprints and photographs, he said, he could not understand how so many questionable entries had been made.

His questions for INEC

Osaze-Uzzi said the commission, led by its chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, had not explained what period its review of the register covered or how the problem entries were first detected. He said tools such as the Automated Fingerprint Identification System and artificial intelligence can help to remove duplicates, but that INEC should still give a clear account of how the records got into the database in the first place.

Background

INEC regularly cleans up the register to remove duplicate, underage and other invalid entries before elections, and the issue of the accuracy of the register has long been debated. The 2027 general elections are due in January, with the presidential poll on 16 January. INEC also urged young Nigerians this week to turn their online enthusiasm into votes, saying votes cannot be counted on social media.

Why it matters

A clean voter register is central to public confidence in elections. Civil society groups have urged INEC to publish more detail on the clean-up so that eligible voters are not wrongly removed.

  • Osaze-Uzzi said it should not have been possible.
  • He wants INEC to explain how they got in.

Former All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential aspirant Adamu Garba has denied reports that he endorsed Peter Obi ahead of Nigeria's 2027 presidential election.

"The claim is false"

In a public statement dated 18 November 2026, Garba described the claim circulating online as false.

He said he has withdrawn from partisan politics and is now focusing on his business ventures and on Africa's digital economy.

His new focus

Garba named Crowwe, Tablars and Justitia SI among the projects currently receiving his attention.

Where did the story come from?

The origin of the endorsement claim remains unclear. No post, interview or public statement showing Garba endorsing Obi has been found.

What is clear is that Garba has publicly disputed the claim.

Peter Obi corrects claims that his Washington visit was an endorsement from the US Congress

18 Nov 2026, 16:43 GMT

Peter Obi says his recent trip to Washington involved talks on Nigeria's development, citizens' welfare and democracy, and that he neither sought nor announced any endorsement, after social media posts claimed the US Congress had backed him.

Read more

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi has corrected claims that his recent visit to Washington amounted to an endorsement from the United States Congress.

What Obi said

According to Obi, the trip involved discussions about Nigeria's development, the welfare of its citizens and the state of democracy. He said he neither sought nor announced any endorsement.

He said he met serving and former US lawmakers and other stakeholders during the visit.

How the confusion started

The confusion followed a viral video in which retired US Army Colonel Albert Short praised Obi.

Some social media posts presented those personal remarks as if they were official backing from the US Congress, and the claim spread quickly online.

What the facts show

There is no public evidence that the US Congress as an institution has endorsed Obi's 2027 presidential bid. Congress does not endorse candidates in other countries' elections.

A meeting with politicians, or kind words from an individual, is not the same as an endorsement from their institution.

Why it matters

With the 2027 elections drawing closer, claims about foreign support for candidates are likely to keep spreading on social media. Obi's correction is a reminder for supporters on all sides to check claims before sharing them.

The Coalition for Democracy, an alliance of opposition parties ahead of the 2027 elections, has reportedly postponed its second summit by one week.

The new date

The coalition's Central Leadership Committee announced on Friday, 20 November 2026, that the summit would now hold on Monday, 30 November, instead of Monday, 23 November.

Why it was moved

The committee said the request came from member parties whose presidential candidates and campaign teams are busy with intensive campaigns across the country. It said it was more important for every party, presidential candidate and national chairman to attend than to keep the original date.

Who is involved

The coalition brings together parties linked to ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar, NDC candidate Peter Obi and Oyo Governor Seyi Makinde. Member parties are expected to pause their nationwide campaigns to attend.
