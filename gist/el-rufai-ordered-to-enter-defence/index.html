Tinubu defends new loans, says debt should be judged by what it builds

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has defended his government's borrowing in a new essay, arguing that loans should be judged by the roads, power and ports they deliver. He listed major projects and economic figures and said Nigeria cannot reach a $1 trillion economy by 2030 on old infrastructure. Atiku Abubakar has again criticised the borrowing plan.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has responded to criticism of his government's borrowing plans, saying the real test of debt is what it produces rather than how large it is. His essay, titled 'From Reforms to Delivery', was released in Abuja on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and shared on X by his adviser on information and strategy, Bayo Onanuga.

What he said

Tinubu reportedly argued that the key question is not how much Nigeria borrows but what the country builds and leaves behind. He listed projects including the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway, the Sokoto–Badagry Superhighway, the Trans-Sahara Highway, upgrades to the electricity grid and the modernisation of ports in Lagos, as well as spending on health, education and skills. He said the coastal highway alone could cut logistics costs on the corridor by 15 to 20 per cent, add up to 2 per cent to GDP and create about 100,000 construction jobs.

The figures he cited

The President pointed to growth of 4.43 per cent in the second quarter, inflation of 14.63 per cent in September compared with about 21 per cent a year earlier, external reserves of about $55 billion and a tax-to-GDP ratio of 12.5 per cent. He credited the removal of the petrol subsidy, the unification of exchange rates and tax reforms. He said a $1 trillion economy by 2030 cannot run on infrastructure built for a much smaller economy, and named food affordability and energy prices among his priorities for the next phase.

The criticism

The essay follows the President's request to the Senate for approval to borrow about $20.5 billion and €1 billion. Atiku Abubakar, the ADC presidential candidate, said the request proved an earlier warning he gave, and has accused the government of piling up debt, which he says now stands at about ₦167 trillion. He has asked for details of how previous loans were spent.

What happens next

The Senate is yet to approve the new borrowing plan, and the debate over debt is expected to be a major theme of the 2027 campaign.

Sowore says armed thugs attacked and abducted him in Ado-Ekiti restaurant

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

AAC presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore has alleged that armed thugs attacked him inside a restaurant in Ado-Ekiti and kidnapped him. The claim, posted on his social media accounts on Friday, had not been confirmed by the police at the time of reporting.

Read more

Omoyele Sowore, the presidential candidate of the African Action Congress (AAC) for 2027, has raised the alarm that he was attacked and abducted by armed thugs in Ado-Ekiti, the capital of Ekiti State.

What he said

In a Facebook post on Friday, 30 October 2026, Sowore wrote that thugs had attacked him and his team inside a restaurant in Ado-Ekiti. A further post said he had been kidnapped by armed men and that his location was unknown. His campaign repeated the alarm on his official X account. It was not immediately clear how the attackers got into the restaurant, how many people were involved, or whether anyone with him was hurt.

Official response

At the time of reporting, neither the police nor the Ekiti State government had issued a statement confirming the incident, and several news outlets said they could not independently verify the claims. Further updates on his whereabouts are expected.

Background

Sowore, the founder of the online news platform SaharaReporters, has run for president before and is seeking the office again in 2027. He has been one of the loudest critics of the government in recent weeks, condemning the remand of actress and AAC governorship candidate Doris Ogala and claiming that President Bola Tinubu's trip to Saudi Arabia was for medical care, a claim the Presidency rejected. He has also accused the police of using the Cybercrimes Act to protect powerful people. The alleged attack comes amid rising tension and reports of violence at political events as the January 2027 elections draw closer.

How people reacted

Supporters called on the police and the Department of State Services to find him quickly, while others urged caution until the facts are confirmed.

Africa CDC warns continent is 'not winning' as Ebola cases pass 10,000

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

Africa CDC chief Jean Kaseya has reportedly warned that Africa is not winning the fight against Ebola, with more than 10,000 confirmed cases and nearly 5,000 deaths, mostly in the DR Congo. Nigeria has recorded one imported case, and 54 contacts are being monitored.

Read more

The head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC), Jean Kaseya, has warned that the continent is not winning the battle against the current Ebola outbreak, as confirmed cases passed 10,000.

The numbers

According to World Health Organization figures reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, there have been 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths across five countries. The Democratic Republic of Congo, where the outbreak was declared in May, accounts for almost all of them, with more than 10,100 cases across eight provinces. Uganda has recorded 20 confirmed cases and two deaths, Kenya nine cases and two deaths, while France and Nigeria have each recorded one imported case with no deaths.

Nigeria's case

Nigeria's case involved a person who had recently travelled to Uganda and tested positive on 20 October. The patient was evacuated to Switzerland for specialist care, and the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention is monitoring 54 contacts.

What was agreed

Speaking at an emergency meeting of African health ministers, Kaseya reportedly said that Ebola anywhere in Africa demands preparedness everywhere in Africa. Ministers agreed to improve readiness for imported cases, coordinate surveillance across borders and keep trade routes open while tightening health checks. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus.

Background

Kaseya recalled the 2014–2016 West Africa epidemic, which disrupted the education of millions of children, left about 23,000 children without a parent and cost close to $3 billion in lost economic output. Nigeria contained an outbreak in 2014 through rapid contact tracing, which is again being used now.

What it means

Health officials are urging Nigerians to report symptoms such as fever, weakness and bleeding promptly, and to follow advice on hand-washing and safe care of the sick.

INEC drops some governorship candidates from list, cites Supreme Court ruling

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

INEC has reportedly removed several governorship and state assembly candidates from its list for 2027, citing a Supreme Court ruling on party membership registers. Gombe PDP's Isa Pantami and Kano NDC's Abdulsalam Gwarzo were later cleared subject to court cases, while Ogun PDP's Ladi Adebutu was said not to have been.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has removed a number of governorship and state assembly candidates from its list for the 2027 elections, according to reports on Friday, 30 October 2026.

Why candidates were removed

INEC reportedly cited a Supreme Court judgment of 24 September 2026 that upheld provisions of the Electoral Act 2026 requiring parties to submit their membership registers before fielding candidates. The commission said it was bound by the Constitution to give effect to decisions of superior courts. It did not say how many candidates were affected in total.

Who is affected

Among those missing from the list were Isa Ali Pantami, the PDP governorship candidate in Gombe State, and Abdulsalam Gwarzo, the NDC candidate in Kano State. Both were reportedly later cleared to proceed, subject to the outcome of cases in court. However, sources said INEC did not extend the same clearance to Ladi Adebutu, the PDP governorship candidate in Ogun State. INEC is expected to issue a formal statement.

Background

INEC published its list of cleared governorship and state assembly candidates on 10 October, and parties that believed their candidates had been wrongly excluded had until 24 October to petition the commission. The 2027 primaries produced many disputes, and courts have already ordered changes to several candidacies, including the Imo West senatorial ticket.

What happens next

Affected parties are expected to go to court or press INEC for a review, and the final list could change again before the January elections.
