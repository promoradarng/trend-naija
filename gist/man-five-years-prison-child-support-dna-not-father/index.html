Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

Nine dead, 46 injured as overloaded truck crashes in Cross River

18 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

At least nine people died and 46 were injured when a truck carrying 85 people crashed near the Yahe Bridge in Cross River State. The FRSC blamed excessive speed.

Read more

At least nine people have died and 46 others were injured after a truck crashed near the Yahe Bridge area of Cross River State on Wednesday, 18 November 2026.

What happened

The Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) unit commander in Yahe, Pascal Anigbo, said the crash involved a DAF truck carrying 85 people as well as goods including palm oil, refrigerators and motorbikes.

He said the driver was speeding and lost control while trying to avoid a head-on collision with another truck coming from the opposite direction, causing the vehicle to veer off the road into the bush.

Casualties

According to the FRSC, 46 people were injured and taken to hospital, while 30 others escaped unhurt. Three bodies were taken to the mortuary after being confirmed dead by a doctor, while six others were collected by their families after being identified.

NIMC to let Nigerians start NIN registration on WhatsApp

18 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The National Identity Management Commission plans a WhatsApp platform for NIN pre-enrolment and booking appointments. It comes as NIN becomes compulsory for domestic flights from January.

Read more

Nigerians may soon be able to start their National Identification Number (NIN) registration through WhatsApp, as the National Identity Management Commission (NIMC) expands its digital services.

How it will work

The planned platform will allow people to complete pre-enrolment and book appointments for biometric capture before visiting a registration centre.

NIMC's Director-General, Abisoye Coker-Odusote, disclosed the plan at the 2026 International Students' Day celebration in Abuja. She said the commission chose WhatsApp because of how widely it is used by Nigerians.

Why it matters

The service is expected to reduce crowding at NIMC offices, cut waiting times and make registration easier, especially for students and young people.

Timing

The plan comes as the federal government has announced that passengers without a valid NIN will not be allowed on domestic flights from 1 January 2027. No launch date for the WhatsApp service was given.
