Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

Lagos offers 20% discount on food in 66 markets for the festive season

24 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Lagos State Government has relaunched its Ounje Eko food discount scheme, offering 20% off selected items in 66 markets. The markets will open on three Sundays: 29 November, 6 December and 20 December.

Read more

The Lagos State Government has relaunched its Ounje Eko food discount programme for the festive season, offering residents a 20% reduction on selected food items in 66 markets across the state.

When and where

The Commissioner for Agriculture and Food Systems, Abisola Olusanya, announced on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, that the scheme will run on three Sundays: 29 November, 6 December and 20 December. The markets will open from 9am to 4pm and cover all 57 local government and local council development areas, across the five IBILE divisions of Ikeja, Badagry, Ikorodu, Lagos Island and Epe.

Why it matters

The commissioner said the initiative was designed to ease pressure on household budgets as residents prepare for Christmas and the New Year. It comes amid concerns that many families have yet to feel real relief from high food prices, even though food inflation has slowed.

Canada resumes Express Entry draws, invites 1,999 for permanent residence

24 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Canada has resumed Express Entry draws, inviting 1,999 provincial nominees to apply for permanent residence on 23 November. It was the first draw under new ranking rules that reward Canadian work experience and high-wage job offers.

Read more

Canada has resumed its Express Entry draws after a short pause, issuing 1,999 Invitations to Apply (ITAs) for permanent residence to provincial nominees on 23 November 2026.

The draw

According to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada, candidates needed a minimum Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score of 673 to be invited, and must have created their profile before 31 July 2026.

New rules

It was the first draw since major changes to the CRS took effect on 3 November 2026. Candidates can now earn extra points through a new Labour Market Integration Factor, which rewards Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation.

Why it matters

Express Entry is one of Canada's main routes to permanent residence for skilled workers.
