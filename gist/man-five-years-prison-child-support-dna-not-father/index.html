Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

Sowore calls for legal cannabis, says Nigeria could earn up to ₦2.86 trillion a year

17 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

AAC presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore has urged the federal government to legalise, regulate and tax cannabis. He pointed to revenues in Canada and US states and projected Nigeria could earn between ₦715 billion and ₦2.86 trillion yearly.

Read more

African Action Congress (AAC) presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore has called on the federal government to legalise cannabis, arguing that Nigeria is missing out on billions of naira in revenue by keeping it illegal.

What he said

In a post on X on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, Sowore said the country should build a regulated cannabis industry instead of spending resources arresting people and destroying plantations. He said he has backed legalisation since 2018.

The figures

He pointed to regulated markets abroad, citing about CA$2.2 billion in government revenue from recreational cannabis in Canada in 2023-2024, around $1.067 billion in cannabis tax in California in 2024, and roughly $255.4 million in Colorado.

Using those examples, he projected that Nigeria could earn between $550 million and $2.2 billion a year, or about ₦715 billion to ₦2.86 trillion at an exchange rate of ₦1,300 to the dollar. He acknowledged that actual earnings would depend on how any such market is designed and run.

He also argued that legalisation could create jobs in farming, pharmaceutical production and industrial hemp, and support research and exports.

Background

Cannabis remains illegal in Nigeria, and the NDLEA regularly carries out raids on farms and arrests dealers. Sowore, a long-time activist, is running for president in 2027 on the AAC platform.

The change

Fulham reportedly want £50m for Calvin Bassey as Manchester United show interest

Fulham are reportedly asking for about £50 million for Super Eagles defender Calvin Bassey amid interest from Manchester United. A sale at that price would be a club record for Fulham.

Read more

Fulham are reportedly ready to demand around £50 million for Nigerian international defender Calvin Bassey in the January transfer window, as Manchester United weigh a move.

The interest

Manchester United are said to be genuinely interested in the 26-year-old, who plays mainly at centre-back but can also play at left-back, as they look to strengthen their defence for the second half of the season.

United reportedly believe a bid above £40 million would be needed to start talks, but Fulham are said to be holding out for closer to £50 million. That would beat the club's record sale, Aleksandar Mitrović's move to Al-Hilal in 2023.

Contract situation

Bassey's contract runs until summer 2027, with Fulham holding an option to extend it by a year. Talks on a new long-term deal have reportedly moved slowly, which could leave the club open to a big offer if nothing is agreed before January.

Background

Bassey, a former Ajax and Rangers defender, is a regular for the Super Eagles. He is reportedly open to a move to Old Trafford.

Abia government dissolves Enyimba management and technical crew

17 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Abia State Government has dissolved the management and technical crew of Enyimba FC with immediate effect, citing poor results and administrative problems. The sports ministry will lead a restructuring of the club.

Read more

The Abia State Government has dissolved the management and technical crew of Enyimba International FC with immediate effect, citing the club's poor performances and administrative problems.

What the government said

In a statement on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the state Commissioner for Information, Okey Kanu, said the decision was taken to address the club's recent disappointing results and structural challenges identified after a review of its affairs.

He said the government intends to fully restructure the club, restore its competitive identity and position it for lasting success in Nigerian and African football.

What happens next

The state Ministry of Sports and Youth Development has been asked to lead the restructuring, assess the club's management set-up and carry out administrative, technical and operational reforms aimed at restoring stability and accountability.

Background

Enyimba, based in Aba, are one of Nigeria's most successful clubs and twice won the CAF Champions League. They have struggled for form this season.
