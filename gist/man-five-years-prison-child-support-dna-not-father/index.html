Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

Nollywood legend Pete Edochie dies at 79

25 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Veteran actor Pete Edochie, famous for playing Okonkwo in Things Fall Apart, has died at the age of 79. His family says he died in Enugu early on Wednesday after a long battle with prostate cancer.

Read more

Veteran Nollywood actor Pete Edochie has died at the age of 79, his family has announced.

The announcement

In a statement shared on Instagram by his son, Leo Pete Edochie, on behalf of the family, the family said the actor died at 12:30am on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, in Enugu, after a prolonged battle with prostate cancer. The statement, titled 'An Iroko Has Fallen', described him as a beloved husband, father, brother and kinsman.

He is survived by his wife, Josephine Edochie, his children, including actors Yul and Linc Edochie, his siblings and other relatives. The family said funeral arrangements would be announced in due course.

His life and career

Pete Edochie hailed from Akamnato, Ifite Nteje, in Anambra State. He began his career in broadcasting in 1967 before becoming one of Nigeria's best-known actors. His major breakthrough came in 1987, when he played Okonkwo in the Nigerian Television Authority's adaptation of Chinua Achebe's Things Fall Apart, a role that brought him recognition far beyond Nigeria.

Known for his commanding voice and powerful screen presence, he became famous for portraying traditional patriarchs and kings in countless Nollywood films. He was honoured with the national award of Member of the Order of the Niger (MON).

His final weeks

In October, during a visit to Ghana, he spoke openly about his declining health, saying walking had become difficult, and prayed that he would not become a burden to those around him. Just this week, actress Toyin Abraham and her crew knelt to welcome him on her film set, and he had spoken publicly about his son Yul days earlier.

Tributes

News of his death has drawn an outpouring of grief from fans, many of whom described it as the end of an era for Nigerian film.

What he said

Speaking in a television interview in Jalingo, Taraba State, on Tuesday evening, 24 November 2026, after a town hall meeting, Obi is said to have explained that the movement, led by his running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso, has a long tradition of supporters attending political events with sticks. NDC boycotts IGP's security meeting, says its members are not safe

24 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The Nigeria Democratic Congress has reportedly refused to attend a security meeting called by the Inspector-General of Police, accusing security agencies of failing to protect its members from attacks. It blamed thugs it said were loyal to the APC.

Read more

The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has announced that it will boycott a security meeting convened by the Inspector-General of Police, citing attacks on its members ahead of the 2027 elections.

The party's reasons

In a statement on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, signed by its National Publicity Secretary, the party said it no longer felt safe under the Nigeria Police Force and would not lend legitimacy to a security process that had failed to protect its members.

The NDC accused security agencies of failing to prevent repeated attacks it alleged were carried out by thugs loyal to the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC). It pointed to incidents in Benue, Edo, Cross River, Enugu and Delta states, including what it described as an attempt to stop its presidential candidate, Peter Obi, from entering Benue State.

The Delta attack

The boycott follows Monday's deadly attack on the campaign team of the party's Delta Central senatorial candidate, Ovie Omo-Agege, in Ughelli North. The party alleged that security personnel were complicit. Police have since invited Omo-Agege and others for questioning as part of their investigation.

The other side

No response from the APC or the police to the NDC's allegations was included in the reports.
