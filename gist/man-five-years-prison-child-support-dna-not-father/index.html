Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

The House of Representatives has called on President Bola Tinubu to closely watch the security situation in Delta State, warning that a state of emergency may be needed if conditions keep getting worse.

The motion

The resolution on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, followed a motion by Representative Benedict Etanabene, who pointed to a series of politically motivated attacks in the state. Lawmakers expressed concern that the violence was spreading fear and threatening peace among communities.

Directive to the police

The House also directed the Inspector-General of Police to identify flashpoints across the state and set up a special team to investigate the attacks.

Background

The resolution comes two days after gunmen attacked an NDC campaign event led by former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege in Uduere, Ughelli North, killing several people. Police have invited Omo-Agege and others for questioning, and the Inspector-General has ordered an investigation. Delta Governor Sheriff Oborevwori has called for everyone involved to be arrested, whatever their party.

CBN raises weekly dollar limit for BDCs from $150,000 to $1m

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has reportedly raised the weekly dollar purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators to $1 million to ease festive pressure on the naira. Banks have begun implementing the change.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has reportedly raised the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change (BDC) operators from $150,000 to $1 million, in a move aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the foreign exchange market.

How it will work

Sources said commercial banks had started contacting BDC operators to set up arrangements for the new limit. Banks will act only as intermediaries between BDCs and the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market, using a purchase tracking platform.

Background

The change is a major relaxation of restrictions the CBN introduced in February. It comes as the naira has weakened against the dollar in recent days and the country's external reserves have fallen. The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate at 23%.
