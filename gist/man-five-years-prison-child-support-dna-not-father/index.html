Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

Court rejects Atiku's bid to add new evidence to suit against Tinubu's eligibility

20 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Abuja has dismissed an application by Atiku Abubakar to introduce fresh evidence into his suit challenging President Tinubu's eligibility for 2027. The judge said the constitutional 14-day window had passed.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Abuja has thrown out an application by ADC presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar to bring new evidence into his suit challenging President Bola Tinubu's eligibility to contest the 2027 election.

The ruling

Justice Inyang Ekwo held that Section 285(9) of the Constitution allows only 14 days for evidence to be filed in a pre-election case. He found that Atiku's request to amend his originating summons would effectively bring in fresh evidence after that period had expired, which would breach the Constitution.

What it means

The ruling is a setback for Atiku's legal challenge, although the main suit itself was not decided in the reports. Atiku's camp had not responded publicly to the ruling in the reports.

Background

Atiku is also involved in a separate legal battle over his ADC candidacy, after a court on 5 November voided the process that produced him as the party's candidate. INEC has said he will remain on its list until that dispute is settled.

Crypto firms given 30 November deadline to register for tax

20 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Nigerian Revenue Service has told cryptocurrency exchanges and other virtual asset providers serving Nigerians to register on its new tax portal by 30 November or face sanctions.

Read more

The Nigerian Revenue Service (NRS) has given cryptocurrency exchanges and other virtual asset service providers until 30 November 2026 to register on its new tax portal or risk regulatory sanctions.

Who is affected

The directive covers both local and foreign crypto businesses serving Nigerian customers, as the federal government moves to bring digital asset transactions under formal tax supervision.

The deadlines

According to a public notice, operators must complete their initial registration by 30 November and connect their systems to the portal by 31 December 2026.

Why it matters

The move is part of wider efforts to bring digital asset transactions under formal tax supervision.
