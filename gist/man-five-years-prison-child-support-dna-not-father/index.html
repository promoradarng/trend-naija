Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has alleged that its supporters were attacked in Benin City, Edo State, on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, as its presidential candidate Peter Obi arrived for a town hall meeting and other campaign events.

What the party says

According to the party, attackers who arrived in a pickup van opened fire on its campaign vehicles parked along Airport Road while supporters waited for Obi. In a statement, the NDC's National Publicity Secretary said the attackers also assaulted people in the crowd and destroyed buses, and described the attack as unprovoked. The party blamed thugs it said were linked to the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC).

The other side

No response from the APC or the police to the allegation was included in the reports.

Background

The incident comes two days after gunmen attacked an NDC campaign event in Delta State, killing several people, and on the same day the House of Representatives warned that a state of emergency might be needed in Delta if violence continues. Supreme Court affirms Ogundipe as ADC governorship candidate in Ogun

25 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Supreme Court has recognised Dr Biodun Collins Ogundipe as the ADC's governorship candidate in Ogun State, overturning lower court rulings in favour of Jimi Lawal. It held that the party's own primary guidelines decide the outcome.

Read more

The Supreme Court has affirmed Dr Biodun Collins Ogundipe as the African Democratic Congress (ADC) governorship candidate in Ogun State for the 2027 elections.

The ruling

The apex court set aside earlier judgments of the Federal High Court and the Court of Appeal, which had recognised Jimi Lawal as the party's rightful candidate. A key issue was which body had the authority to collate and declare results in the ADC's primary. The court upheld the party's three-tier primary guidelines, set by its National Working Committee, as the binding framework.

Reaction

Ogundipe welcomed the judgment with humility and gratitude, saying it was consistent with the Supreme Court's long-held position that political parties have the power to run their own primaries without outside interference.

Background

This week, lower courts also voided the candidacies of the NDC's Ovie Omo-Agege in Delta Central and the APC's candidate for Bayelsa West.
