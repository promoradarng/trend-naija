Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

Fountain of Life Church removes Jimmy Odukoya as senior pastor

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The board of trustees of The Fountain of Life Church in Lagos has reportedly removed Jimmy Odukoya as senior pastor with immediate effect. It said his appointment did not reflect the succession wishes of his late father and founder, Taiwo Odukoya.

Read more

The Fountain of Life Church in Ilupeju, Lagos, has reportedly removed Jimmy Odukoya as its senior pastor, with the board of trustees saying his appointment conflicted with the succession plans left by the church's late founder, Taiwo Odukoya.

What the board said

In a statement on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the board is said to have explained that it reviewed the church's constitution alongside recordings of the founder's wishes made before his death in 2023. It concluded that Jimmy's tenure did not reflect those wishes and said he was no longer recognised as senior pastor, effective immediately.

The board reportedly stressed that the decision was not a personal judgement against him but a governance matter, describing it as necessary to uphold the church's constitution and preserve the succession arrangements set up by the founder. It said the decision was not taken lightly.

Background

Jimmy Odukoya, an actor and pastor, took over leadership of the church after his father's death. Earlier this month, on 1 November, he remarried and presented his new wife to the congregation, in a ceremony at which Pastor Tunde Bakare officiated the blessing.

How people reacted

The news quickly became a talking point online, with many people revisiting a cryptic post he shared days before the announcement, and a video of the late founder that is said to show the role he intended for his son.

What happens next

There was no public response from Jimmy Odukoya in the reports, and the board did not say who would lead the church.

No NIN, no flight: FG sets 1 January deadline for domestic air passengers

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Passengers without a valid National Identification Number will be refused boarding on domestic flights from 1 January 2027. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo announced the rule while unveiling a new passenger verification system.

Read more

The federal government has announced that passengers without a valid National Identification Number (NIN) will not be allowed to board domestic flights from 1 January 2027.

The new system

Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development Festus Keyamo announced the policy while unveiling the Verified Passenger Authentication System (VPASS), a digital platform that links passengers' identities directly to their NIN records.

Keyamo said the system will help authorities confirm genuine passengers, prevent unauthorised access to restricted airport areas and make sure airline passenger lists are accurate.

Why it is being introduced

The minister pointed to irregularities in ticket sales, including bulk buying and reselling of tickets under different identities, as problems the government wants to stop. He said tying bookings to verified identity records would make it harder for anyone to travel using false details.

What airlines must do

Keyamo directed the Nigerian Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) to ensure that domestic airlines connect to the platform before 31 December 2026, ahead of the January deadline.

What it means for travellers

Anyone planning to fly within Nigeria in the new year will need to make sure their NIN is valid and matches the details they use to book tickets.

MTN reopens talks to buy South Africa's Telkom

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

MTN Group has confirmed early talks to buy rival Telkom, four years after an earlier attempt collapsed. Telkom shares jumped 18% on the news.

Read more

MTN Group, Africa's largest mobile operator by subscribers, has reportedly reopened talks to acquire South African rival Telkom.

What happened

The two companies confirmed preliminary discussions on Monday, 16 November 2026, four years after an earlier round of negotiations ended without a deal. MTN said it was considering a cash offer, possibly with an option for Telkom shareholders to take MTN shares instead, but stressed the talks were at an early stage and might not lead to an agreement.

Market reaction

Telkom's shares rose about 18% to their highest level in more than seven years, while MTN's shares fell 1.7%. Telkom's enterprise value was put at about 38 billion rand, or around $2.2 billion, while MTN's market value stood at about 432 billion rand.

Why it matters

A deal would add Telkom's mobile customers to MTN's South African business, which could push MTN ahead of Vodacom as the biggest mobile operator in South Africa by subscribers.
