Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

WWE has announced the death of wrestler Bronson Reed, whose real name was Jermaine Haley, at the age of 38.

The announcement

In a statement on its website on Friday, 27 November 2026, WWE said Reed had died earlier that day from medical complications, but did not give further details. The company described him as a larger-than-life figure, a devoted family man and a beloved colleague, and sent its condolences to his pregnant wife, Paige, and their daughter.

Earlier injury

His death came about three weeks after he was seriously injured at WWE's Crown Jewel event in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, on 7 November. During a tag team title match, Nigerian-born wrestler Oba Femi threw him off the stage through a stack of tables. Reed reportedly suffered broken ribs and a lacerated kidney and spent more than a week in hospital in Saudi Arabia. WWE has not said whether his death was linked to the injury.

His career

Born in Adelaide, Australia, Reed joined WWE in 2019. He won the NXT North American Championship and the 2024 André the Giant Memorial Battle Royal. Wrestlers and fans around the world have paid tribute.

INEC holds nationwide mock accreditation in 17,656 polling units

INEC is conducting a mock voter accreditation and result transmission exercise across 17,656 polling units in all 36 states and the FCT on Saturday. It is testing its upgraded BVAS machines and security arrangements.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) is conducting a nationwide mock voter accreditation and result transmission exercise on Saturday, 28 November 2026.

The exercise

The test is taking place at 17,656 polling units across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory. It is designed to test INEC's upgraded Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS), the result transmission process and election security arrangements under simulated election-day conditions. Sample ballot papers are being used.

Why it matters

The exercise comes ahead of the presidential and National Assembly elections on 16 January 2027. INEC has faced criticism in past elections over delays in uploading results, and this week the commission named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence.

Lagos adjusts

Lagos State cancelled its monthly environmental sanitation exercise, which had been scheduled for the same morning, so that residents could take part.

VeryDarkMan says he will register his own Ratel party and fund it himself

28 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Activist VeryDarkMan has announced plans to register the Ratel Political Party before 2032, saying he will pay the registration costs from his business earnings. He said the movement will start with grassroots offices.

Read more

Social media activist Martins Vincent Otse, popularly known as VeryDarkMan (VDM), has announced plans to register his own political party, the Ratel Political Party, before 2032.

Paying his own way

In a video, VDM said he would fund the project with his own earnings from his businesses, rather than relying on supporters. He said he had heard the registration fee was over ₦50 million and that he would raise it. INEC's party registration guidelines set a non-refundable administrative fee of ₦50 million, and payment does not guarantee approval.

Grassroots first

VDM said the Ratel movement would begin by focusing on grassroots positions, such as local government chairmen and councillors, rather than seats in the National Assembly.

Background

'Ratel' is the name VDM uses for his followers. This week he also urged opposition leaders Peter Obi, Atiku Abubakar and Seyi Makinde to unite under one party against President Tinubu.
