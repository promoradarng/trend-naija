Man reportedly spends five years in prison over child support, only to discover through DNA test that he is not the father

5 Nov 2026, 11:01 GMT

Imagine spending five years behind bars for failing to pay child support, only to come out and discover that the child you were punished for not supporting is not even yours.

That is the heartbreaking story being told online about a man identified as Mr Mensar.

Five years behind bars

According to the account circulating online, Mr Mensar was imprisoned for failing to meet his child support obligations. For five years, he reportedly lived with the consequences of a responsibility he believed was his.

The mother's admission

What makes the story even more disturbing is that the child's mother knew all along that Mr Mensar was not the biological father of her son.

She allowed him to face imprisonment without revealing the truth, and the account claims she remained in contact with the child's real biological father throughout the ordeal. When questioned by the judge, she accepted responsibility.

The DNA test

After serving his sentence, Mr Mensar reportedly demanded a DNA test to establish whether the child was biologically his. According to the account, the result confirmed his worst fear: he was not the father.

Five years gone. Five years he can never get back, all over a paternity question that could have been settled much earlier.

Questions that need answers

Serious questions must now be asked about the mother's conduct and about how the child support case was handled.

How does a man end up spending five years in prison over a child who is not biologically his? What happens to the years he lost? And if someone deliberately concealed the child's paternity, should there be legal consequences?

The full court records and DNA findings have not been made public.

Still, the story raises an important issue about paternity disputes, child support enforcement and fairness in the justice system.

Over to you

If a woman knowingly allows a man to go to prison over child support while hiding the fact that he is not the father, should she face criminal charges and be made to compensate him for the years he lost?

Delta man presumed dead after accident reportedly wakes up in mortuary

A man who was presumed dead after an accident in Delta State has reportedly woken up in a mortuary.

What happened

According to reports on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the man was involved in a motorcycle accident near Evwreni and was taken to a mortuary after being presumed dead. He is said to have later woken up there. A video shared online shows residents celebrating his survival. Voices in the video say that when he woke up, the first thing he asked for was a cigarette and a lighter.

Celebrations

Residents rubbed white powder on their faces and bodies, a traditional sign of celebration often used at a child's birth, and also applied it to the survivor. Others shared snacks with passers-by to mark the occasion, while some urged him to go for a proper medical check-up.

Background

It is not clear who declared the man dead or whether he was examined by a doctor before being taken to the mortuary. The authorities had not commented on the incident at the time of writing.

All 62 contacts of Nigeria's Ebola case complete monitoring with no spread, NCDC says

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The NCDC says all 62 people linked to Nigeria's single imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a type of Ebola, have completed 21 days of monitoring with no further transmission. It warns this is not yet a declaration that Nigeria is free of the disease.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says all 62 contacts linked to the country's imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, have completed their monitoring without any further spread.

The update

Speaking at a news conference in Abuja on Friday, 13 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said the last contact had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. He said 13 of the contacts developed fever or other symptoms during monitoring but tested negative for the disease. Seven other alerts reported over three weeks in Adamawa, Rivers, Borno, Lagos and Ekiti were investigated, and all suspected cases tested negative.

Not yet Ebola-free

Idris cautioned that the end of contact monitoring was not a formal declaration that Nigeria was free of the disease, and said surveillance must continue. He said any such declaration would depend on public health criteria and ongoing surveillance, especially as transmission continues in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Background

Earlier this week, the NCDC said 28 contacts were still under watch. The United States has temporarily barred travellers who have been in the DRC in the past 21 days. Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness or unexplained bleeding after travel to report to a health facility.

FG issues Ethiopia travel advisory after 16 Nigerians die transiting Addis Ababa

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The federal government has warned Nigerians with heart and lung conditions to seek medical clearance before travelling through Addis Ababa, after 16 Nigerians died in transit there in what officials link to the city's high altitude.

Read more

The federal government has issued a travel advisory warning Nigerians about the health risks of Ethiopia's high altitude, after the deaths of 16 Nigerians transiting through Addis Ababa.

The advisory

In a statement by Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson Oluwafemi Adeniyi, reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the government urged travellers with existing medical conditions, especially heart and respiratory problems, to be cautious and to obtain medical clearance before taking journeys with stopovers in the Ethiopian capital.

Why altitude matters

Addis Ababa sits about 2,355 metres above sea level, where lower air pressure reduces the oxygen available. The ministry noted that dry air, stronger sunlight and faster fluid loss can also cause problems, from headaches and breathlessness to serious conditions such as swelling of the brain. Healthy travellers usually adapt within a few days, but people with underlying illnesses face much higher risks.

Background

Addis Ababa is a major transit hub for Nigerian travellers. The ministry did not give details of the individual deaths.

ASUU warns officials against using union's name to endorse 2027 candidates

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Academic Staff Union of Universities says it will sanction any official who uses the union's name to back a candidate or party in 2027, stressing that it remains apolitical though members are free to vote.

Read more

The Academic Staff Union of Universities (ASUU) has warned its members and officials not to use the union's name to endorse political candidates or parties ahead of the 2027 elections.

What ASUU said

Speaking at the union's 2026 Heroes' Day celebration in Abuja, reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, ASUU National President Professor Christopher Piwuna said individual lecturers keep their constitutional right to vote and take part in politics, but must not present their personal choices as the union's position. He said no member had permission to use ASUU's name to support any candidate or party, that the union remained apolitical, and that any official found compromising that stance by associating with parties or politicians would face disciplinary action.

Background

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
