Political violence: Jonathan sounds alarm, Senate asks INEC to disqualify violent candidates as APC and ADC trade blame

17 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Concern over violence ahead of the 2027 elections dominated political discussion on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, with a former president, the Senate and the main parties all weighing in.

Jonathan's warning

Speaking at a peace and security conference organised by his foundation in Abuja, former President Goodluck Jonathan warned that rising political intolerance and violence could threaten the elections. He said some political groups were running their campaign teams like cult groups, and that Nigerians at home and abroad had expressed fears about possible crises after the polls. He also condemned thuggery and the use of young people as tools of violence.

The Senate's resolution

The Senate, acting on a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi (ADC, Bauchi Central), condemned recent attacks on political figures. These included the stoning of President Bola Tinubu's motorcade after Friday prayers in Abuja, reported attacks on NDC candidate Peter Obi in Edo State, a disrupted NDC procession in Asaba on 7 November that left one person dead, and an attack on ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar's convoy in Sokoto.

Lawmakers asked the Inspector-General of Police to carry out impartial investigations and prosecute those responsible, and urged INEC to apply the law disqualifying candidates linked to electoral violence. The motion was seconded by Senator Ali Ndume (APC, Borno South).

APC and ADC trade accusations

The APC presidential campaign council, through its spokesperson Dele Alake, accused the ADC and NDC of sponsoring violence, deepfakes and hired protests to damage President Tinubu's campaign, pointing to the convoy incident in Abuja.

The ADC campaign council, through Phrank Shaibu, dismissed the claim as a distraction from the government's economic record. It challenged the APC to provide evidence or withdraw the allegation, and asked why no one had been prosecuted if the ruling party had proof. The NDC's response was not included in the reports.

The chants

The APC statement also blamed the opposition for youths chanting "Tinubu Must Go" and the Hausa phrase "Ba mayi", meaning roughly "we are not supporting you", at some of the ruling party's rallies.

What happens next

The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

  • Jonathan says some campaigns are run like cult groups.
  • The Senate wants INEC to disqualify candidates linked to violence.
  • The ADC rejects APC claims that it sponsors attacks.

WWE star Bronson Reed dies at 38

28 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

WWE has announced the death of wrestler Bronson Reed, real name Jermaine Haley, at the age of 38 from medical complications. He had been seriously injured at a WWE event in Saudi Arabia three weeks earlier.

Read more

WWE has announced the death of wrestler Bronson Reed, whose real name was Jermaine Haley, at the age of 38.

The announcement

In a statement on its website on Friday, 27 November 2026, WWE said Reed had died earlier that day from medical complications, but did not give further details. The company described him as a larger-than-life figure, a devoted family man and a beloved colleague, and sent its condolences to his pregnant wife, Paige, and their daughter.

Earlier injury

His death came about three weeks after he was seriously injured at WWE's Crown Jewel event in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, on 7 November. During a tag team title match, Nigerian-born wrestler Oba Femi threw him off the stage through a stack of tables. Reed reportedly suffered broken ribs and a lacerated kidney and spent more than a week in hospital in Saudi Arabia. WWE has not said whether his death was linked to the injury.

His career

Born in Adelaide, Australia, Reed joined WWE in 2019. He won the NXT North American Championship and the 2024 André the Giant Memorial Battle Royal. Wrestlers and fans around the world have paid tribute.

INEC holds nationwide mock accreditation in 17,656 polling units

28 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

INEC is conducting a mock voter accreditation and result transmission exercise across 17,656 polling units in all 36 states and the FCT on Saturday. It is testing its upgraded BVAS machines and security arrangements.

Read more

The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) is conducting a nationwide mock voter accreditation and result transmission exercise on Saturday, 28 November 2026.

The exercise

The test is taking place at 17,656 polling units across the 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory. It is designed to test INEC's upgraded Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS), the result transmission process and election security arrangements under simulated election-day conditions. Sample ballot papers are being used.

Why it matters

The exercise comes ahead of the presidential and National Assembly elections on 16 January 2027. INEC has faced criticism in past elections over delays in uploading results, and this week the commission named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence.

Lagos adjusts

Lagos State cancelled its monthly environmental sanitation exercise, which had been scheduled for the same morning, so that residents could take part.
