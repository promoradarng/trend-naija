Africa CDC warns continent is 'not winning' as Ebola cases pass 10,000

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC), Jean Kaseya, has warned that the continent is not winning the battle against the current Ebola outbreak, as confirmed cases passed 10,000.

The numbers

According to World Health Organization figures reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, there have been 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths across five countries. The Democratic Republic of Congo, where the outbreak was declared in May, accounts for almost all of them, with more than 10,100 cases across eight provinces. Uganda has recorded 20 confirmed cases and two deaths, Kenya nine cases and two deaths, while France and Nigeria have each recorded one imported case with no deaths.

Nigeria's case

Nigeria's case involved a person who had recently travelled to Uganda and tested positive on 20 October. The patient was evacuated to Switzerland for specialist care, and the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention is monitoring 54 contacts.

What was agreed

Speaking at an emergency meeting of African health ministers, Kaseya reportedly said that Ebola anywhere in Africa demands preparedness everywhere in Africa. Ministers agreed to improve readiness for imported cases, coordinate surveillance across borders and keep trade routes open while tightening health checks. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus.

Background

Kaseya recalled the 2014–2016 West Africa epidemic, which disrupted the education of millions of children, left about 23,000 children without a parent and cost close to $3 billion in lost economic output. Nigeria contained an outbreak in 2014 through rapid contact tracing, which is again being used now.

What it means

Health officials are urging Nigerians to report symptoms such as fever, weakness and bleeding promptly, and to follow advice on hand-washing and safe care of the sick.

  • There are 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths, mostly in the DR Congo.
  • Nigeria has one imported case; 54 contacts are being monitored.
  • Ministers agreed to step up border surveillance.

Sweden returns 39 Benin Bronzes to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted

13 Nov 2026, 15:50 GMT

Sweden has handed back 39 Benin artefacts taken during the British invasion of Benin in 1897, at a ceremony in Lagos. Six of the objects will stay in Sweden on a 10-year loan.

Read more

Sweden has returned 39 Benin Bronzes and other artefacts to Nigeria, 129 years after they were looted from the Benin Kingdom.

The handover

Representatives of Sweden's National Museums of World Culture formally handed over the objects at a ceremony in Lagos on Thursday, 12 November 2026. They will be received by Nigeria's National Commission for Museums and Monuments. The collection includes cultural objects known as Emwin Arre. Six of the 39 artefacts will remain in Sweden under a 10-year loan arrangement.

Why it matters

The objects were among thousands taken by British forces who attacked and burned Benin City in 1897 before bringing the kingdom under British rule. Michael Barrett, curator for Africa at Sweden's National Museums of World Culture, described the return as an important step in addressing colonial-era looting of cultural property.

Background

Nigeria has been campaigning for decades for the return of the Benin Bronzes, which are held in museums and private collections around the world. The Netherlands returned 119 objects in 2025, and Germany handed over 20 Benin Bronzes in 2022. Many more remain abroad.

Four UNN students die after off-campus party in Nsukka, six in hospital

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka has confirmed that four students died after a party held to celebrate a friend's final exams, after allegedly consuming a mix of alcohol and drugs. Six others are in hospital, and the university has declared three days of mourning.

Read more

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka (UNN) has confirmed that four of its students have died after an off-campus party in Nsukka, Enugu State.

What happened

Speaking to journalists on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Vice-Chancellor Professor Simon Ortuanya said 10 students were involved in the incident at the party on Wednesday, 11 November, which had been organised to celebrate a friend finishing his final-year examinations. He said the four who died allegedly consumed substances believed to be a mixture of alcohol and drugs. The other six are receiving treatment in different hospitals. Earlier reports had put the death toll as high as seven.

University responds

The Vice-Chancellor described the deaths as one too many and declared three days of mourning in honour of the students. He also corrected reports that all the students were from the Faculty of Pharmaceutical Sciences, saying they came from different departments. The exact substances involved have not been officially identified, and it is not clear whether the police have opened an investigation.

Police investigate

The Enugu State Police Command said on Thursday evening that it had opened an investigation into the deaths. It said the four victims, two men and two women, fell ill after allegedly drinking alcoholic beverages at a gathering at a hostel in Nsukka and were confirmed dead on arrival at hospital. Police said the drinks had been recovered from the scene and the bodies taken to a mortuary for autopsy.

Background

Drug and alcohol misuse among young people has become a growing public health concern in Nigeria.

Cooking gas now up to ₦1,800 per kg in Lagos, Ogun and Oyo

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Retail cooking gas prices have reportedly jumped from about ₦1,200 to ₦1,800 per kilogramme in parts of Lagos, Ogun and Oyo within a month, after depot prices rose by nearly 32 per cent. Some households say they are turning to charcoal.

Read more

Households in Lagos, Ogun and Oyo states are paying much more for cooking gas after a sharp rise in prices over the past month.

The new prices

According to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026, retail prices of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) at several outlets rose from around ₦1,200 to ₦1,800 per kilogramme. In parts of Ogun State, including Magboro, Mowe and Ibafo, some residents said they were paying as much as ₦1,900 per kg. In Oyo State, prices reportedly range from about ₦1,450 per kg at direct outlets to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,650 at neighbourhood retailers.

Why prices rose

The increase follows a reported 31.8 per cent rise in depot prices, from ₦1,100 to ₦1,450 per kg, which has pushed up costs along the supply chain.

Impact on households

Some households say they are considering returning to charcoal because they can no longer afford gas.

Background

The latest increase comes even as petrol prices have eased slightly, with Dangote Refinery reportedly cutting its petrol price this week.

Court remands journalist Daniel Ojukwu in Abuja prison over cybercrime charges

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu has been remanded in prison in Abuja on a 12-count cybercrime charge linked to his reports about a presidential aide, pending a bail hearing. The decision has renewed concerns about press freedom.

Read more

A court in Abuja has ordered that investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu be held in prison pending a hearing on his bail application.

The charges

Ojukwu, who works for the Foundation for Investigative Journalism, was arraigned on a 12-count charge of allegedly breaching Nigeria's Cybercrimes Act, and the court ordered his remand on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The charges relate to reports he published about Adejoke Orelope-Adefulire, who was senior special assistant on the Sustainable Development Goals under former President Muhammadu Buhari and now holds the same role under President Bola Tinubu. The charges have not been tested at trial.

Press freedom concerns

The decision drew widespread criticism from media freedom advocates, who have long argued that the cybercrimes law is used to silence journalists. According to the Committee to Protect Journalists, at least 29 journalists have been prosecuted under the law since it was enacted in 2015. Nigeria is ranked 112th out of 180 countries in the 2026 World Press Freedom Index compiled by Reporters Without Borders.

Background

Ojukwu was previously detained by police for several days in 2024 over a report on the same official, a case that also drew national and international criticism.
