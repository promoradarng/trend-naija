Africa CDC warns continent is 'not winning' as Ebola cases pass 10,000

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC), Jean Kaseya, has warned that the continent is not winning the battle against the current Ebola outbreak, as confirmed cases passed 10,000.

The numbers

According to World Health Organization figures reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, there have been 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths across five countries. The Democratic Republic of Congo, where the outbreak was declared in May, accounts for almost all of them, with more than 10,100 cases across eight provinces. Uganda has recorded 20 confirmed cases and two deaths, Kenya nine cases and two deaths, while France and Nigeria have each recorded one imported case with no deaths.

Nigeria's case

Nigeria's case involved a person who had recently travelled to Uganda and tested positive on 20 October. The patient was evacuated to Switzerland for specialist care, and the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention is monitoring 54 contacts.

What was agreed

Speaking at an emergency meeting of African health ministers, Kaseya reportedly said that Ebola anywhere in Africa demands preparedness everywhere in Africa. Ministers agreed to improve readiness for imported cases, coordinate surveillance across borders and keep trade routes open while tightening health checks. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus.

Background

Kaseya recalled the 2014–2016 West Africa epidemic, which disrupted the education of millions of children, left about 23,000 children without a parent and cost close to $3 billion in lost economic output. Nigeria contained an outbreak in 2014 through rapid contact tracing, which is again being used now.

What it means

Health officials are urging Nigerians to report symptoms such as fever, weakness and bleeding promptly, and to follow advice on hand-washing and safe care of the sick.

  • There are 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths, mostly in the DR Congo.
  • Nigeria has one imported case; 54 contacts are being monitored.
  • Ministers agreed to step up border surveillance.

Air Peace offloads passenger who allegedly threatened his anger could 'burn down' plane

10 Nov 2026, 17:47 GMT

An Air Peace flight to Asaba returned to the ramp after a passenger who refused to stop taking photos allegedly told crew his anger could 'burn down' the plane, the NCAA says. He was removed and later said he had no bad intentions.

Read more

An Air Peace flight to Asaba, Delta State, returned to the ramp before take-off after a passenger allegedly threatened the crew.

What happened

According to the Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority's director of public affairs and consumer protection, Michael Achimugu, who described the incident on X on Monday, 9 November 2026, the trouble began when the passenger refused repeated requests from cabin crew to stop taking photographs and put his device away. During the dispute, he allegedly said his anger could 'burn down' the plane. The crew reported the threat to the captain, who ordered the aircraft back to the ramp at 3.57pm, and the passenger was offloaded. His travelling companion also chose to leave the plane.

The passenger's explanation

Achimugu said the passenger cooperated during the offloading. The man later told authorities that his frustration had built up after he missed an earlier flight, and that he had no intention of causing harm.

Background

Under aviation rules, passengers must follow the instructions of cabin crew, and threats of any kind on board are treated seriously.

Fact check: No evidence UK university has banned CAS letters for Osun and Imo students

10 Nov 2026, 17:47 GMT

A viral claim that the University of Greenwich will stop issuing study confirmation letters to applicants from Osun and Imo states could not be verified, a fact-check found. The university has raised deposit requirements for all Nigerian applicants.

Read more

A viral claim that a UK university will stop issuing Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) letters to students from Osun and Imo states has not been confirmed.

The claim

The founder of an immigration consultancy posted on social media that the University of Greenwich would stop issuing CAS letters, which international students need to apply for a UK study visa, to applicants from the two states. The post was viewed more than 54,000 times.

The check

According to a fact-check reported on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, nothing in the university's public materials points to a restriction targeting applicants from Osun or Imo. The university has responded to concerns about visa refusals by raising deposit requirements for all Nigerian applicants, but that does not amount to a state-specific ban. Prospective students were advised to contact the university directly through its official channels.

Background

The UK remains one of the most popular destinations for Nigerian students, but tougher rules on dependants and rising visa refusals have made the process harder in recent years.

Kano governor launches ₦50,000 monthly grant for 5,200 women

10 Nov 2026, 14:43 GMT

Kano governor Abba Kabir Yusuf has launched a scheme paying ₦50,000 a month to each of 5,200 women across the state, urging beneficiaries to use the money to start or grow businesses.

Read more

Kano State governor Abba Kabir Yusuf has launched a monthly grant scheme for women in the state.

The scheme

The governor unveiled the programme at the Open Theatre in Government House, Kano, on Monday, 9 November 2026. Under the scheme, 5,200 women across the state will each receive ₦50,000 a month. The governor's chief press secretary, Mustapha Muhammad, said in a statement on Tuesday that the aim is to give women the means to start or expand businesses and reduce their dependence on others for income.

The governor's message

Yusuf urged the women to put the money into productive ventures rather than consumption, and said his government had put safeguards in place to ensure the funds reach the intended beneficiaries. He said transparency, fairness and accountability would guide the programme.

Background

The scheme comes as the federal government rolls out its own ₦50,000 monthly relief payments to selected households under the National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention.

FRSC says 19,236 Nigerians died in road crashes since 2023

10 Nov 2026, 09:45 GMT

The FRSC says 19,236 people died and 118,720 were injured in 37,789 road crashes across Nigeria between January 2023 and September 2026, with speeding and reckless driving the biggest killers.

Read more

The Federal Road Safety Corps (FRSC) says 19,236 Nigerians were killed in road crashes between January 2023 and September 2026.

The figures

The FRSC's Abia State Sector Commander, Ngozi Ezeoma, told journalists on Monday, 9 November 2026, that there were 37,789 road crashes in the period, in which 118,720 people were injured. She said the figures came from the FRSC's Road Traffic Database Management System, and that speeding and human recklessness remain the biggest killers on Nigerian roads.

Remembrance day

She spoke ahead of the World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, which the United Nations marks on the third Sunday of November. She said the command would visit crash victims in selected hospitals as part of the commemoration.

Background

Last week, actor Kunle Afod complained about the slow emergency response to a crash on the Lagos–Ibadan Expressway, although the Ogun State Government disputed his account.
