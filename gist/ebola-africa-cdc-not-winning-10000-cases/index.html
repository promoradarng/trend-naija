Africa CDC warns continent is 'not winning' as Ebola cases pass 10,000

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

The head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC), Jean Kaseya, has warned that the continent is not winning the battle against the current Ebola outbreak, as confirmed cases passed 10,000.

The numbers

According to World Health Organization figures reported on Friday, 30 October 2026, there have been 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths across five countries. The Democratic Republic of Congo, where the outbreak was declared in May, accounts for almost all of them, with more than 10,100 cases across eight provinces. Uganda has recorded 20 confirmed cases and two deaths, Kenya nine cases and two deaths, while France and Nigeria have each recorded one imported case with no deaths.

Nigeria's case

Nigeria's case involved a person who had recently travelled to Uganda and tested positive on 20 October. The patient was evacuated to Switzerland for specialist care, and the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention is monitoring 54 contacts.

What was agreed

Speaking at an emergency meeting of African health ministers, Kaseya reportedly said that Ebola anywhere in Africa demands preparedness everywhere in Africa. Ministers agreed to improve readiness for imported cases, coordinate surveillance across borders and keep trade routes open while tightening health checks. The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus.

Background

Kaseya recalled the 2014–2016 West Africa epidemic, which disrupted the education of millions of children, left about 23,000 children without a parent and cost close to $3 billion in lost economic output. Nigeria contained an outbreak in 2014 through rapid contact tracing, which is again being used now.

What it means

Health officials are urging Nigerians to report symptoms such as fever, weakness and bleeding promptly, and to follow advice on hand-washing and safe care of the sick.

  • There are 10,172 confirmed cases and 4,932 deaths, mostly in the DR Congo.
  • Nigeria has one imported case; 54 contacts are being monitored.
  • Ministers agreed to step up border surveillance.

Edo consumer committee uncovers fake kpomo hub in Benin market

9 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The Edo State Consumer Protection Committee says it has uncovered a hub in New Benin Market where fake, chemically treated kpomo (cow skin) was being prepared and sold. Two women were arrested.

Read more

The Edo State Consumer Protection Committee says it has uncovered a site in Benin City where fake and chemically treated kpomo, the popular cow-skin delicacy, was being produced and sold.

What happened

The committee's chairman, Osaro Iyamu, said officials acted on a tip-off in the early hours of Monday, 9 November 2026, and raided a spot near Efehi Street in New Benin Market. He said two women were arrested with the products, while others ran away, and that some market women later admitted to the practice. The two suspects were handed to the police for further investigation.

The danger

Chemically treated food products can pose serious health risks, and fake kpomo has been a recurring concern in Nigerian markets.

Background

Kpomo is widely eaten across Nigeria in soups and stews. Regulators have previously urged consumers to buy food from trusted sources.

A house in Kano State has caught fire, with the incident being linked to its solar power installation.

Flames on the roof

Photos from the scene show thick smoke rising from the roof of a two-storey building, with flames breaking through beside a row of solar panels. A man can be seen climbing up to an upper-floor balcony as a crowd of neighbours gathers below, some filming on their phones and others watching in shock.

A growing safety concern

The incident raises serious concerns about the safety of solar installations, especially as more Nigerians turn to solar energy because of unreliable electricity supply.

Poor wiring, faulty batteries, overloaded inverters and improper installation can all create fire hazards. Experts often advise homeowners to use qualified installers, buy certified equipment and have their systems checked regularly.

Amnesty International condemns Zamfara killings, says burning of 12 family members alive exposes failure of Nigerian authorities

8 Nov 2026, 21:28 GMT

Amnesty International has condemned the reported killing of 12 members of one family in Zamfara State, who were said to have been burned alive, calling it another sign of the authorities' failure to protect communities from armed attacks.

Read more

Amnesty International has strongly condemned the reported killing of 12 members of a family in Zamfara State, describing the horrifying incident as another painful reminder of the Nigerian authorities' failure to protect innocent citizens from violent attacks.

An entire family wiped out

Imagine an entire family wiped out in such a cruel way. Twelve human beings, reportedly burned alive. People who had their own dreams, responsibilities and hopes for tomorrow, only to have their lives cut short in circumstances no family should ever experience.

The tragedy has once again raised serious questions about the worsening security situation in parts of northern Nigeria, where many families live under the constant threat of armed attacks, kidnappings and killings.

A failure to protect

For Amnesty International, the incident reflects a much bigger problem: the continued inability of the authorities to guarantee the safety of communities that have suffered attack after attack.

For years, residents of Zamfara and neighbouring states have endured violence from armed groups. Villages have been raided, homes destroyed, residents abducted and families forced to abandon communities where they have lived for generations.

Despite repeated assurances from government officials and security agencies, reports of fresh atrocities keep emerging.

Every citizen deserves protection

The Nigerian government has a constitutional duty to protect lives and property. That duty extends to every citizen, whether they live in Abuja, Lagos, Zamfara or a remote village far from the attention of the national media.

The victims deserve justice, and their surviving relatives deserve more than another round of official condolences. There must be a thorough investigation, those responsible must be brought to justice, and the authorities must take meaningful steps to prevent similar tragedies.

A country cannot claim to be making progress when families are being burned alive in their homes and communities are left wondering who will be attacked next.

Six Nigerians deported from the US arrive in Lagos

8 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

The National Emergency Management Agency says it received six Nigerians deported from the United States at Lagos airport on 4 November. The five men and one woman were profiled by immigration officers and given resettlement support.

Read more

Six Nigerians deported from the United States have arrived in Lagos, the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) says.

What happened

In a statement on its X account on Sunday, 8 November 2026, NEMA said the five men and one woman, all adults, landed at the cargo terminal of Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Ikeja at about 10.13pm on Wednesday, 4 November, aboard a small chartered aircraft. NEMA's Lagos office received them in partnership with the US Consulate, the Federal Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Nigeria Immigration Service and an aviation services company. Immigration officers documented and profiled each returnee before clearing them, and NEMA said it coordinated support to help them resettle.

Background

The deportations are part of the United States' wider crackdown on undocumented migrants under President Donald Trump, whose administration has carried out mass removals, often on chartered flights. Groups of Nigerians have been returned on several occasions over the past year. The agency did not give the reasons for the individual deportations. Trump this week named Stephen Miller, the architect of his deportation programme, as White House chief of staff.

What happens next

NEMA said the federal government would continue to work with other agencies to manage the return of Nigerians from abroad in an orderly and dignified way.
