Paraguay's Santiago Peña impeached and placed under house arrest

7 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has been removed from office by the Senate and placed under house arrest over the collapse of Ueno Bank.

What happened

On Friday, 6 November 2026, all 44 senators present, including members of Peña's own Colorado Party, voted to remove him; one senator was absent. He was arrested minutes after the vote, and Vice-President Pedro Alliana was sworn in as interim president, expected to serve until 2028. Peña had offered to resign on Thursday, but senators went ahead with the impeachment, which strips him of the immunity normally enjoyed by former presidents. Senate President Basilio Núñez said Peña had been found guilty of dereliction of duty.

The allegations

The impeachment charges included conflicts of interest, manipulation of regulatory oversight, diversion of pension funds and involvement in a cover-up involving the central bank. Peña, who until last year held shares in the company that owned Ueno Bank, has denied wrongdoing. Prosecutors said he was due to be questioned.

Background

The collapse of Ueno Bank left large numbers of Paraguayans unable to access their savings and pensions, triggering a political crisis. Peña, an economist and former finance minister, took office in August 2023. Earlier this week, he apologised to Paraguayans in a video message.

What happens next

Investigators are expected to continue their inquiries into the bank's collapse, while depositors wait to learn whether they will recover their money.

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu becomes New Jersey's lieutenant governor

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu has been appointed lieutenant governor of the US state of New Jersey, becoming the first immigrant to hold the post. She will also serve as Secretary of State.

Read more

Shirley U. Emehelu, a lawyer born in Nigeria, has been appointed lieutenant governor of New Jersey in the United States.

The appointment

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill appointed Emehelu on Monday, 9 November 2026, making her the state's fifth lieutenant governor and the first immigrant to hold the office. She was sworn in at the Statehouse in Trenton by the state's Chief Justice, Stuart Rabner. The appointment also means that, for the first time in New Jersey's history, women hold the state's two highest executive offices at the same time. Emehelu will also serve as Secretary of State, a role that covers government administration and oversight of elections.

Why she was chosen

Sherrill described Emehelu as an experienced public servant and pointed to her record prosecuting financial crimes, protecting consumers, defending civil rights and tackling government waste. The governor also praised her leadership as acting Secretary of State, including overseeing the state's recent elections.

Her Nigerian roots

Emehelu, who moved from Nigeria to the United States as a child, said she was proud of what the appointment means for Black women, immigrants and people who share her Nigerian heritage, and expressed hope that it would inspire young people to serve. She said she would work with the governor on the cost of living and economic opportunity.

Prison Break and Mike Hammer actor Stacy Keach dies at 85

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

American actor Stacy Keach, known for playing detective Mike Hammer and for his role in Prison Break, has died at 85 after a long battle with heart disease, his wife said.

Read more

American actor Stacy Keach has died at the age of 85.

What happened

Keach reportedly died on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, at his home in Poland after a long battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure. His wife, Małgosia Tomassi Keach, announced his death and described him as a brave man who stayed strong despite his health problems.

His career

Keach began acting on Broadway in the 1960s before moving into films in the following decade, and drew attention starring alongside Faye Dunaway in the 1971 film 'Doc'. He became best known for playing private investigator Mike Hammer in the television series 'Mickey Spillane's Mike Hammer' and 'The New Mike Hammer'. Younger audiences knew him as the prison warden in the hit series 'Prison Break'.

Salvadoran troops reportedly launch deadly operation against Haitian gang, leaving 20 dead

11 Nov 2026, 12:50 GMT

At least 20 people have reportedly been killed in an operation involving troops from El Salvador against suspected gang members in Haiti, where armed groups have taken control of large areas and forced thousands from their homes.

At least 20 people have reportedly been killed in an operation involving Salvadoran troops targeting suspected gang members in Haiti.

A country held hostage

The reported incident comes as Haiti continues to struggle with widespread gang violence, kidnappings and insecurity, particularly in areas where armed groups have taken control of communities and forced thousands of residents to flee.

For years, ordinary Haitians have lived under the constant threat of armed gangs, with many families unable to go about their daily lives without fear.

Foreign forces on the ground

Soldiers from El Salvador have been in Haiti since early 2025 as part of an internationally backed mission to help the Haitian police fight the gangs, alongside forces from other countries.

The reported operation has raised fresh questions about the growing role of foreign security forces in efforts to restore order in the troubled Caribbean nation.

What we don't know

The circumstances of the reported deaths, including the identities of those killed and the exact role played by the Salvadoran troops, have not been independently confirmed.

US bars travellers who have been in DR Congo in the last 21 days over Ebola

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The US CDC has temporarily barred anyone who has been in the Democratic Republic of Congo within the past 21 days, including US citizens and transit passengers, from boarding flights to the United States because of Ebola.

Read more

The United States has imposed a temporary travel restriction on people who have recently been in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) because of Ebola.

The rule

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) said anyone who has been in the DRC in the past 21 days may not board a flight to the United States, according to reports on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. The rule applies to American citizens as well as foreigners, and covers passengers whose aircraft stopped at a DRC airport even if they did not leave the plane. Affected travellers must wait until 21 days have passed since they left the DRC. The US Embassy in Kinshasa announced the measure on social media.

Why 21 days

The period matches the maximum incubation time of the Ebola virus.

Background

The restriction comes amid an Ebola outbreak in Central Africa. Nigeria is currently monitoring contacts of one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, and says no further cases have been confirmed.
