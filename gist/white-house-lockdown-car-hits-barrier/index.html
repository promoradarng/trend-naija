White House briefly locked down after car crashes into barrier near Lafayette Park

10 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The White House was briefly placed on lockdown on Monday, 9 November 2026, after a car crashed into a security barrier near the complex.

What happened

Shortly before 10am, a silver sedan mounted the pavement and hit a sign at the northern end of Lafayette Park, directly opposite the White House, according to reports. Witnesses said the driver got out and became involved in a physical confrontation with Secret Service agents and National Guard troops. Two people were arrested. The press area was locked down, reporters were ordered indoors, and roads around the park were closed. President Donald Trump was in the White House at the time. Officials said the driver was combative but was quickly arrested.

Background

The incident came days after the Secret Service arrested an armed man near the White House who allegedly said voices had told him to kill the President. Officials have said the White House has seen a rise in security incidents, many linked to mental health problems. No motive for Monday's crash was given, and there were no reports of serious injuries.

Biden says he would not have sought re-election had he known he had cancer

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Former US President Joe Biden says he would not have run for a second term if he had known about his cancer, and has played down the importance of the 2024 debate that led to his exit from the race.

Read more

Former US President Joe Biden says he would not have run for a second term if he had known he had cancer.

What he said

In an interview ahead of the release of his memoir, 'Promise Me, America', reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Biden said he had felt unwell before his diagnosis but did not know he was ill while he was campaigning. He also played down his June 2024 debate against Donald Trump, saying everyone makes mistakes in debates and that, in his view, it was not very consequential. He said he had expected there to be another debate.

Background

Biden's poor debate performance triggered intense pressure from fellow Democrats and led him to withdraw from the 2024 race and endorse Vice-President Kamala Harris, 107 days before the election. Harris went on to lose to Trump. About four months after leaving the White House, Biden was diagnosed with an aggressive form of prostate cancer.

Indonesia's vice-president Gibran resigns after court voids his school certificate

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Indonesian Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has reportedly handed his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto, after the Supreme Court ruled that a high-school equivalence certificate he used to qualify for office was invalid.

Read more

Indonesia's Vice-President, Gibran Rakabuming Raka, has resigned, according to an aide who said the letter was handed directly to President Prabowo Subianto on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. It was not immediately clear whether the President had accepted it.

The court ruling

The resignation follows a ruling by Indonesia's Supreme Court that declared invalid a high-school equivalence certificate Gibran used during the 2024 election campaign. Indonesian law requires anyone running for vice-president to have completed senior high school. Gibran, who studied in Singapore and Australia, had relied on a local equivalence document and argued that his university education was enough proof. The court reportedly found no evidence of a foreign qualification that could legally replace a senior high school certificate, and pointed to an earlier Constitutional Court ruling that reached the same conclusion. On Tuesday he had asked for a chance to challenge the ruling.

Political pressure

The ruling prompted swift political action, with more than 100 lawmakers reportedly backing moves against him.

Background

Gibran is the eldest son of former President Joko Widodo.

Russia's ambassador to the US, Alexander Darchiev, dies at 66

11 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Alexander Darchiev, Russia's ambassador to the United States since early 2025, has died in Moscow at the age of 66 from an unspecified chronic illness, the Russian Foreign Ministry says.

Read more

Alexander Darchiev, Russia's ambassador to the United States, has died at the age of 66.

What happened

The Russian Foreign Ministry announced his death on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said he died in Moscow from an unspecified chronic illness. The ministry praised him as an outstanding diplomat and an expert on the United States, and said details of a memorial service would be released later.

His career

Darchiev became ambassador in Washington in early 2025, succeeding Anatoly Antonov. Before that, he headed the Foreign Ministry's North America department, served as Russia's ambassador to Canada and worked as a senior diplomat at the embassy in Washington. In February 2025, he led Russia's delegation at talks with the United States in Istanbul.

Background

In 2017, Russia's UN envoy Vitaly Churkin also died suddenly in New York.

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu becomes New Jersey's lieutenant governor

11 Nov 2026, 17:46 GMT

Nigerian-born lawyer Shirley Emehelu has been appointed lieutenant governor of the US state of New Jersey, becoming the first immigrant to hold the post. She will also serve as Secretary of State.

Read more

Shirley U. Emehelu, a lawyer born in Nigeria, has been appointed lieutenant governor of New Jersey in the United States.

The appointment

New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill appointed Emehelu on Monday, 9 November 2026, making her the state's fifth lieutenant governor and the first immigrant to hold the office. She was sworn in at the Statehouse in Trenton by the state's Chief Justice, Stuart Rabner. The appointment also means that, for the first time in New Jersey's history, women hold the state's two highest executive offices at the same time. Emehelu will also serve as Secretary of State, a role that covers government administration and oversight of elections.

Why she was chosen

Sherrill described Emehelu as an experienced public servant and pointed to her record prosecuting financial crimes, protecting consumers, defending civil rights and tackling government waste. The governor also praised her leadership as acting Secretary of State, including overseeing the state's recent elections.

Her Nigerian roots

Emehelu, who moved from Nigeria to the United States as a child, said she was proud of what the appointment means for Black women, immigrants and people who share her Nigerian heritage, and expressed hope that it would inspire young people to serve. She said she would work with the governor on the cost of living and economic opportunity.
