Moment multi-storey building under construction collapses in Nairobi's Eastleigh

23 Oct 2026, 19:21 BST

A multi-storey building still under construction came crashing down in the Eastleigh area of Nairobi, Kenya, early on Friday afternoon, sparking a frantic rescue operation amid fears that several people were trapped under the rubble. The structure stood along Yusuf Haji Avenue, next to Day to Day Supermarket on 9th Street, in one of the city's busiest commercial hubs. Reports of its height vary, with some outlets putting it at 18 floors and Nairobi police commander Issa Mohamed saying it was about 10. Videos from the scene show slabs of concrete falling and a thick cloud of dust rolling down the street as people ran for safety. Kenya Red Cross teams, Nairobi County emergency crews and police joined the search, and the county said excavators had been brought in to clear debris and reach anyone trapped. Police fired tear gas to push back crowds of onlookers and stop the looting of nearby shops. Inspector General of Police Douglas Kanja reportedly confirmed that one person had been pulled out alive, and the search continued into the night. The cause of the collapse is not yet known. It comes less than a week after another building collapse on Moi Avenue in Nairobi's central business district.

  • Police fire tear gas to clear onlookers and stop looting
  • Second Nairobi building collapse in under a week

Trump calls Brazil's presidential election 'a disgrace' as Flavio Bolsonaro claims victory from the US

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

US President Donald Trump has reportedly described Brazil's presidential election as a disgrace and declined to say whether he recognises Lula da Silva's victory. His comments came a day after Lula's rival, Flavio Bolsonaro, arrived in the United States and declared himself the rightful leader.

Read more

US President Donald Trump has reportedly called Brazil's presidential election a disgrace, raising tensions with Latin America's largest country.

What he said

Asked by reporters on Thursday, 29 October 2026, whether he accepted the victory of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, Trump reportedly described the election as a disgrace and an embarrassment to Brazilians, but said he did not want to say more at that time. He did not say whether he recognised Lula as Brazil's president.

Background

Lula, a veteran left-wing leader, faced Flavio Bolsonaro, the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, in the election. Flavio Bolsonaro, an ally of Trump, has claimed he was the true winner. He reportedly travelled to the United States the day before Trump's remarks, after going into exile from Brazil, and declared himself the rightful leader. Earlier this week, a Brazilian court had banned him from leaving the country, according to reports at the time.

Why it matters

Trump's comments reportedly go further than his earlier remarks on the vote and are likely to strain relations between Washington and Brasília. The United States and Brazil are major trading partners, and relations were already tense after Trump imposed tariffs on Brazilian goods and criticised the prosecution of Jair Bolsonaro.

What happens next

Brazil's government has not yet responded publicly to the latest comments, and it is not clear whether the United States will formally question the result.

Spain mourns as death toll in Toledo high-speed train crash reportedly rises to 69

29 Oct 2026, 15:04 GMT

Spain has begun three days of national mourning after a high-speed train reportedly crashed into a stationary train near Urda in Toledo province on Wednesday. The regional leader of Castilla-La Mancha says 69 people have died, and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has promised an investigation.

Read more

Spain began three days of national mourning on Thursday, 29 October 2026, after a high-speed train reportedly ran into the back of a stationary train near the town of Urda in the central province of Toledo on Wednesday. The regional leader of Castilla-La Mancha, Emiliano García-Page, said the death toll had risen to 69 after another body was found overnight.

What happened

According to reports, the moving train struck the stationary one from behind, and three carriages came off the tracks. On Thursday morning, rescue teams with sniffer dogs were still searching the wreckage, while cranes lifted carriages and police officers and soldiers combed the site. The number of people injured has not been confirmed in the reports seen so far, and the cause of the collision has not been announced.

What the government said

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez visited the scene and promised a full investigation as well as safety improvements on the railways. He reportedly said Spain must strengthen train safety to win back public trust in high-speed rail, and said it was unacceptable for the country to suffer two high-speed accidents in a single year.

Background

The crash is Spain's second deadly high-speed rail disaster of 2026. In January, a train derailed near Adamuz in Andalusia and collided with a train coming the other way, killing dozens of people the first deadly accident on a Spanish high-speed train since the network opened in 1992. Spain has one of the largest high-speed rail systems in the world, and both disasters have raised hard questions about maintenance and signalling.

What happens next

Investigators are expected to examine the signalling and the movements of both trains in the hours before the crash. Further updates on the victims and the injured are expected as families are informed.

Makinde takes campaign to Enugu despite warnings about Obi's stronghold, questions Tinubu's Saudi trip

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

Oyo governor Seyi Makinde, who is running for president on the APM ticket, told a town hall in Enugu that his team was almost talked out of campaigning in the South East because it is seen as Peter Obi's territory. He also asked the Presidency to explain the real purpose of President Tinubu's trip to Saudi Arabia.

Read more

Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde, the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) for 2027, has taken his campaign to the South East, telling supporters in Enugu that his team was almost discouraged from coming to a region widely seen as Peter Obi's base. On the same day, he raised questions about President Bola Tinubu's trip to Saudi Arabia.

What he said in Enugu

Speaking at an APM town hall meeting in Enugu on Thursday, 29 October 2026, Makinde said people had warned his team against campaigning in the South East because the zone was regarded as belonging completely to Obi, the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate. He said he decided to bring his message there anyway and urged Nigerians to reject politics that divides people along ethnic and tribal lines.

His question about Tinubu's trip

In a video shared the same day by an Oyo affairs account on X, Makinde said he had read on social media that the President was travelling to Saudi Arabia to pray for Nigeria. He remarked that prayers can be answered from within Nigeria too, and asked the Presidency to tell Nigerians the true situation. He did not say what he believed the situation to be.

How people reacted

Reactions online were divided. Some users backed his call for openness, while others accused him of politicising a religious trip, and one asked whether he was wishing the President ill because of the election. The Presidency has said the President is performing Umrah, and an aide has rejected claims that the trip is for medical treatment.

Background

Obi won most of the South East in the 2023 presidential election and remains very popular there. Makinde, who is serving his second and final term as Oyo governor, is one of several candidates now trying to build support across regions. Tinubu left Abuja for Makkah this week, and Atiku Abubakar and activist Omoyele Sowore have also questioned the trip.

Tinubu defends new loans, says debt should be judged by what it builds

29 Oct 2026, 17:46 GMT

President Bola Tinubu has defended his government's borrowing in a new essay, arguing that loans should be judged by the roads, power and ports they deliver. He listed major projects and economic figures and said Nigeria cannot reach a $1 trillion economy by 2030 on old infrastructure. Atiku Abubakar has again criticised the borrowing plan.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has responded to criticism of his government's borrowing plans, saying the real test of debt is what it produces rather than how large it is. His essay, titled 'From Reforms to Delivery', was released in Abuja on Thursday, 29 October 2026, and shared on X by his adviser on information and strategy, Bayo Onanuga.

What he said

Tinubu reportedly argued that the key question is not how much Nigeria borrows but what the country builds and leaves behind. He listed projects including the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway, the Sokoto–Badagry Superhighway, the Trans-Sahara Highway, upgrades to the electricity grid and the modernisation of ports in Lagos, as well as spending on health, education and skills. He said the coastal highway alone could cut logistics costs on the corridor by 15 to 20 per cent, add up to 2 per cent to GDP and create about 100,000 construction jobs.

The figures he cited

The President pointed to growth of 4.43 per cent in the second quarter, inflation of 14.63 per cent in September compared with about 21 per cent a year earlier, external reserves of about $55 billion and a tax-to-GDP ratio of 12.5 per cent. He credited the removal of the petrol subsidy, the unification of exchange rates and tax reforms. He said a $1 trillion economy by 2030 cannot run on infrastructure built for a much smaller economy, and named food affordability and energy prices among his priorities for the next phase.

The criticism

The essay follows the President's request to the Senate for approval to borrow about $20.5 billion and €1 billion. Atiku Abubakar, the ADC presidential candidate, said the request proved an earlier warning he gave, and has accused the government of piling up debt, which he says now stands at about ₦167 trillion. He has asked for details of how previous loans were spent.

What happens next

The Senate is yet to approve the new borrowing plan, and the debate over debt is expected to be a major theme of the 2027 campaign.
