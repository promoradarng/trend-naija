Obi promises to go into the bush himself to rescue kidnap victims if elected

31 Oct 2026, 06:42 GMT

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has reportedly promised to personally lead efforts to rescue kidnap victims if he is elected in 2027.

What he said

Speaking at a campaign rally in Bayelsa State on Friday, 30 October 2026, Obi said that if anyone kidnapped schoolchildren or anyone else under his government, he would go into the bush himself. He said he had recently travelled by road through several states, including Enugu, Kogi, Nasarawa and Ebonyi, and that Nigeria's roads must be made safe. He said his government would start by uniting the country so that no region or group felt left out.

Other promises

Obi reportedly pledged that public money would be managed openly, that an NDC government would work with states to make primary education free, and that it would aim to generate, transmit and distribute at least 10,000 megawatts of electricity. He claimed current supply is far lower.

Criticism of Tinubu

Obi criticised President Bola Tinubu for not visiting Bayelsa, where oil was first discovered in Nigeria, and repeated his claim that the President spends a large part of the year abroad. The Presidency has previously rejected such claims, and had not responded to the latest remarks at the time of the report.

Background

Obi has held a series of town halls and rallies this week, including in Nasarawa, where he said election riggers would be jailed, and in Ebonyi. Insecurity, including kidnappings on highways and in schools, remains one of the biggest issues for voters ahead of the January 2027 elections.

  • He promised free primary education and at least 10,000MW of power.
  • He criticised Tinubu for not visiting Bayelsa.

Kwara APC governorship rally in Offa reportedly turns violent over unpaid mobilisation funds

16 Nov 2026, 11:21 GMT

An APC governorship rally in Offa, Kwara State, reportedly descended into chaos after claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised. The incident has revived debate about paying crowds to attend political events.

Read more

A governorship campaign rally organised by the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Offa, Kwara State, has reportedly descended into chaos following claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised.

What reportedly happened

According to reports, the rally drew a large crowd of supporters to the streets of Offa. But tension rose when some of those who had turned up complained that they had not received the money they were allegedly promised for attending.

The situation reportedly turned violent as angry participants confronted organisers, disrupting the event.

The full details, including whether anyone was injured and how much money was involved, have not been independently confirmed, and the party has not publicly commented on the incident.

The business of crowds

The incident shines a light on a practice many Nigerians already know about: paying people, often called "mobilisation", to attend political rallies.

Across parties, campaign organisers frequently provide transport, food, branded caps and T-shirts, and sometimes cash, to fill venues and create the impression of massive support. When those promises are not kept, frustration can quickly boil over.

It also raises a deeper question. If people have to be paid to attend, how much do the crowds at political rallies really tell us about genuine support?

Tinubu announces home loans of up to ₦100m at 9.75% interest over 20 years

17 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

President Tinubu says eligible Nigerians can get home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed 9.75% interest rate, repayable over 20 years. Buyers must pay at least 10% of the property's value upfront.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has announced that eligible Nigerians can now access home loans of up to ₦100 million at a fixed interest rate of 9.75%, repayable over 20 years.

How it works

In a post on his X account on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, the President reportedly said the loans are available through the ₦1 trillion MOFI Real Estate Investment Fund (MREIF). Applicants must pay a deposit of at least 10% of the property's value to qualify.

Using a ₦10 million home as an example, he said the buyer would pay ₦1 million upfront and then about ₦85,400 a month over the 20 years.

Extra options

He added that workers in the Contributory Pension Scheme can use up to 25% of their retirement savings towards the deposit, while married couples can apply jointly to qualify for bigger loans.

What happens next

Full eligibility rules and the application process were not detailed in the reports.

Defence minister says US is giving Nigeria satellite intelligence to hunt terrorist leaders

17 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Defence Minister Christopher Musa says the United States is providing satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help Nigeria's military track militant leaders before the 2027 elections. He said troops have killed several commanders in recent operations.

Read more

Nigeria's Minister of Defence, Christopher Musa, has reportedly said the United States is providing the Nigerian military with satellite imagery, intelligence and training to help locate militant leaders ahead of the 2027 general elections.

What the minister said

Speaking to journalists on Monday, 16 November 2026, Musa said troops had killed a number of bandit and terrorist commanders in overnight operations across the north-west and north-east. He described the operations as happening almost daily and said they were backed by much better intelligence.

He said the partnership with Washington had improved the military's ability to find and strike targets.

More equipment

The minister also said Nigeria is expecting more helicopters from the United States and Italy to support operations against insurgents in the north-east and armed bandits in the north-west.

Enugu Assembly passes bill limiting rent increases

17 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Enugu State House of Assembly has passed a Landlord and Tenant Amendment Bill that reportedly allows rent increases only after four years, with the increase capped. A lawmaker said it would cut what residents pay to secure accommodation by over 60%.

Read more

The Enugu State House of Assembly has passed the Landlord and Tenant Amendment Bill, which is meant to protect tenants from sudden and steep rent increases.

What happened

The bill passed its third reading on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, after lawmakers considered a report by a joint committee drawn from the House committees on housing, public petitions, ethics and the judiciary.

The committee chairman, Okey Mbah, who represents Nkanu East, said the report followed wide consultation with stakeholders and that the law would end the exploitation of tenants. He said it would cut more than 60% of what residents typically pay when securing accommodation in the state.

What the bill says

Under the bill, rent can reportedly only be raised after four years, and any increase is capped at 2%.

What happens next

The bill is expected to go to the governor for assent before it becomes law.
