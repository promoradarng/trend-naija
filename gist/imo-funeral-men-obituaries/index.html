"8 out of every 10 obituaries in the Southeast are men!" Woman raises alarm at Imo funeral over pressure on Igbo men

26 Oct 2026, 05:02 GMT

A woman reportedly lost her cool during a funeral ceremony in Imo State as she raised serious concerns about what she believes is an alarming number of men dying prematurely across Ala Igbo.

According to her, eight out of every ten obituaries she comes across in the Southeast are those of men. Although this figure has not been independently verified, her emotional outburst has raised an important conversation about the struggles many men face in silence.

She argued that financial responsibilities, marital problems, emotional stress, family expectations and the constant pressure to provide are gradually wearing many men down.

In her view, some men are carrying burdens that nobody seems interested in discussing. They are expected to pay the bills, provide for their wives and children, support extended family members and remain strong regardless of what they are going through.

She also accused some wives of making matters worse through constant domestic conflict, excessive financial demands and a lack of emotional support.

However, it would be unfair to blame women generally for men's deaths without credible evidence. Health conditions, economic hardship, lifestyle factors and access to medical care must also be considered.

But there is a serious question here that deserves attention.

When a man is struggling financially, who checks on his mental health? When he is emotionally exhausted, who listens to him? When he can no longer meet everyone's expectations, does his family support him or make him feel like a failure?

In many homes, men are expected to be strong even when everything around them is falling apart.

Marriage should be a place of peace and mutual support, not another source of unbearable pressure for either partner.

If this woman's observation reflects a wider problem, then Igbo communities need to examine the health, welfare and life expectancy of their men more seriously.

Bandits abduct six residents in Kaduna's Millennium City

25 Nov 2026, 20:42 GMT

Suspected bandits reportedly abducted nine people from two houses in Millennium City, Chikun, Kaduna State, late on Tuesday, later releasing three. Six remain in captivity.

Suspected bandits have reportedly abducted six residents in the Millennium City area of Chikun Local Government Area, Kaduna State.

What happened

The attackers stormed two houses at around midnight on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, shooting and breaking in before taking nine people away. Residents said they began hearing gunshots at about 11:30pm.

Three of those abducted, including an elderly woman and a mother with her toddler, were later released, leaving six people in captivity.

Residents' fears

A man whose wife is among those still held said he feared for her safety because she has difficulty walking, and appealed for help to rescue her.

Background

Kaduna has seen several kidnapping incidents in recent weeks, including a foiled attempt at a school in Zangon Kataf and the abduction of Boys' and Girls' Brigade members in Kachia/Kajuru, most of whom troops rescued.

CBN raises weekly dollar limit for BDCs from $150,000 to $1m

25 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has reportedly raised the weekly dollar purchase limit for Bureau De Change operators to $1 million to ease festive pressure on the naira. Banks have begun implementing the change.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has reportedly raised the weekly dollar cash purchase limit for Bureau De Change (BDC) operators from $150,000 to $1 million, in a move aimed at easing seasonal pressure on the foreign exchange market.

How it will work

Sources said commercial banks had started contacting BDC operators to set up arrangements for the new limit. Banks will act only as intermediaries between BDCs and the official Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market, using a purchase tracking platform.

Background

The change is a major relaxation of restrictions the CBN introduced in February. It comes as the naira has weakened against the dollar in recent days and the country's external reserves have fallen. The CBN this week kept its benchmark interest rate at 23%.

The United States Embassy in Abuja and the Consulate General in Lagos will close for two days this week as the US marks its Thanksgiving holiday.

The US Mission in Nigeria confirmed on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, that both offices will be shut on Thursday, 26 November, and Friday, 27 November.
