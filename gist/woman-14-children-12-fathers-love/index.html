A live television debate took an awkward turn on Tuesday night, 10 November 2026, when Nigeria's national emergency number failed to connect after a ruling party official insisted it worked.

What happened

Speaking on a Channels Television programme to highlight the government's achievements, Alwan Hassan, a member of the APC presidential campaign council, said the 112 emergency response system had been upgraded and worked reliably nationwide. To prove his point, he dared the anchor, Seun Okinbaloye, to call the number live on air. The anchor accepted, dialled 112 on his phone, but the call did not go through.

Background

The 112 toll-free number is meant to connect people to police, fire, ambulance and other emergency services across Nigeria. Emergency response became a talking point last week when actor Kunle Afod complained about the response to a crash on the Lagos–Ibadan Expressway.

Ebola: 28 contacts still under watch in Nigeria, 34 cleared, says NCDC

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The NCDC says 28 people linked to Nigeria's single imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a type of Ebola, remain under surveillance, while 34 others have been cleared after 21 days of monitoring. No further cases have been confirmed.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says 28 people linked to the country's imported Ebola case are still being monitored.

The update

In its national health security update released on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said 34 other contacts had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. As of 9 November, Nigeria had confirmed only one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, with no further confirmed cases.

The response

Idris said the response involves the Federal Ministry of Health, the FCT Administration, state authorities and development partners, with contact tracing, investigation of reported cases, laboratory testing, infection prevention and control, safe patient care and stronger screening at entry points.

Background

Ebola is a severe and often deadly viral disease spread through contact with the body fluids of infected people. The 21-day monitoring period reflects the maximum incubation time of the virus. The case in Nigeria is linked to an outbreak in Central Africa; the United States has just barred travellers who have been in the Democratic Republic of Congo in the past 21 days. Nigeria contained an Ebola outbreak in 2014.

Advice

Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness, vomiting or unexplained bleeding, especially after travel or contact with a suspected case, to report to a health facility immediately.

US bars travellers who have been in DR Congo in the last 21 days over Ebola

11 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

The US CDC has temporarily barred anyone who has been in the Democratic Republic of Congo within the past 21 days, including US citizens and transit passengers, from boarding flights to the United States because of Ebola.

Read more

The United States has imposed a temporary travel restriction on people who have recently been in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) because of Ebola.

The rule

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) said anyone who has been in the DRC in the past 21 days may not board a flight to the United States, according to reports on Wednesday, 11 November 2026. The rule applies to American citizens as well as foreigners, and covers passengers whose aircraft stopped at a DRC airport even if they did not leave the plane. Affected travellers must wait until 21 days have passed since they left the DRC. The US Embassy in Kinshasa announced the measure on social media.

Why 21 days

The period matches the maximum incubation time of the Ebola virus.

Background

The restriction comes amid an Ebola outbreak in Central Africa. Nigeria is currently monitoring contacts of one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, and says no further cases have been confirmed.
