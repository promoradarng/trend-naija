Four Nigerian-Americans win seats in US midterm elections

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Four Nigerian-Americans reportedly won state and local races in the US midterms, including Will Jawando as Montgomery County executive in Maryland and Onitsha-born Junior Ezeonu in the Texas House. A fifth, Georgia's Segun Adeyina, was leading in an uncalled race.

Read more

Nigerian-Americans have recorded a string of wins in the United States midterm elections held on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, according to reports.

Who won

Four Nigerian-Americans reportedly won state and local offices in Maryland, Texas, Washington DC and Pennsylvania. In Maryland, Democrat Will Jawando, whose father is Nigerian, defeated Republican Esther Wells to become Montgomery County executive, winning about 78 per cent of the vote with most ballots counted. He previously served on the Montgomery County Council. In Texas, 27-year-old Democrat Junior Ezeonu won the District 101 seat in the state House of Representatives. Born in Onitsha, Anambra State, he moved to Texas at the age of two, was elected to the Grand Prairie City Council in 2021 and became the city's youngest mayor pro tempore. He had defeated the incumbent in the Democratic primary and faced no Republican opponent. In Washington DC, Democrat Oye Owolewa won an at-large seat on the District of Columbia Council.

Still counting

A fifth Nigerian-American, Georgia state Representative Segun Adeyina, was reportedly leading in his race, which had not yet been called.

Background

The wins came on a night when Democrats were projected to take control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate. Nigerian-Americans are one of the most highly educated immigrant groups in the United States, and a growing number have entered politics at local, state and national level in recent years.

How people reacted

Many Nigerians at home and abroad celebrated the results online, describing them as a proud moment for the diaspora.

First Muslim senator and first Black woman governor among US midterm history-makers

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Abdul El-Sayed has reportedly become the first Muslim US senator after winning in Michigan, Keisha Lance Bottoms is the first Black woman elected governor of a US state, and Gina Hinojosa is set to be Texas's first Latina governor after defeating Greg Abbott.

Read more

The 2026 US midterm elections produced several historic firsts, according to results and projections reported on Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

First Muslim senator

Democrat Abdul El-Sayed reportedly defeated Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race, becoming the first Muslim member of the US Senate. A former Detroit health director, he ran on a progressive platform including Medicare for All and higher taxes on billionaires, and won the seat left by retiring Democrat Gary Peters. His campaign survived a bitter contest in which a Democratic rival he beat in the primary endorsed his Republican opponent.

First Black woman governor

In Georgia, former Atlanta mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms, 56, defeated Republican businessman Rick Jackson, becoming the first Black woman elected governor of any US state and Georgia's first female governor. Her victory reportedly ends 24 years of Republican control of the office. Atlanta-born Nigerian singer Davido shared the news on his Instagram Story.

Texas turns blue

In Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa reportedly defeated Republican governor Greg Abbott, who was seeking a fourth term, by about 53.5 per cent to 46.5 per cent. When she takes office in January 2027, the 52-year-old will be the state's first Latina governor and its first Democratic governor since Ann Richards left office in 1995.

Background

The results came as Democrats were projected to win control of both the House and the Senate, in what many analysts described as a rebuke of President Donald Trump's second-term agenda amid concern over the economy, tariffs and the conflict with Iran.

Nollywood actor Adams Kehinde, popularly known as Lege Miami, became emotional at a campaign event for President Bola Tinubu's re-election on Tuesday, 3 November 2026.

What happened

According to reports, the actor organised a rally attended by transport union leader Musiliu Akinsanya, known as MC Oluomo, and other personalities. In a video shared online, he broke down in tears while thanking those who had supported the event financially and taken time out of their busy schedules to attend.

The Eniola Badmus moment

In another clip that went viral the same day, actress Eniola Badmus, who also backs the President, appeared to ignore Lege Miami at a pro-Tinubu gathering. She reportedly took a seat across the room after walking in, and kept her distance even after he made a light-hearted attempt to ease the tension. Background

Several Nollywood stars and entertainers have openly declared support for candidates ahead of the January 2027 elections. Eniola Badmus has been a visible supporter of the President. Supporters of other candidates, including Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar, have also held events with entertainers in recent weeks.

Fans were divided over the tears, with some saying they were genuine and others mocking the display. Many joked about the frosty moment with Eniola Badmus, asking why the two always seem to be at odds.

Actor Uche Odoputa to hold empty-casket funeral for father killed after 2022 kidnapping

4 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Actor Uche Odoputa says his family will hold a funeral with an empty casket for his father, who was abducted in Imo State in 2022 and killed despite a ransom payment. He urged people carrying hidden grief to seek support.

Read more

Nollywood actor Uche Odoputa has announced that his family will hold a funeral for his late father without a body to bury, more than four years after his death.

What he said

In an emotional video posted on Instagram on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, the actor said the family would gather in a few weeks to honour his father, Mr Kenneth Chukwumezie Odoputa, whom those close to him called 'Honestman'. He said they have no body to lay to rest, but have his life to remember and his dignity to honour.

What happened to his father

According to reports, the actor's father, who was born on 10 August 1950, was kidnapped by gunmen in Imo State in May 2022 and was killed despite a ransom being paid. He died on 6 May 2022. His remains were never recovered.

A message on grief

Odoputa also used the video to encourage people who are carrying hidden grief to seek help from someone they trust, saying no one should carry such pain alone.

Background

Kidnapping for ransom has affected many families across Nigeria, including in the South East, where armed groups have attacked travellers and residents in recent years. Odoputa is known for his roles in Nollywood films. Ebonyi State's governor this week sent a bill to make kidnapping punishable by death.

How people reacted

Colleagues and fans sent condolences and prayers, praising the actor for speaking openly about his loss.
