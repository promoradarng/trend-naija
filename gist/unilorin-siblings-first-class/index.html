Obasanjo says debt is worse than leprosy, calls Tinubu's borrowing remark 'nonsense'

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

Former President Olusegun Obasanjo has reportedly hit back at President Tinubu's past remark that borrowing is not leprosy, saying debt destroys generations while leprosy destroys only one person. He spoke at a forum in Abeokuta, a day after Tinubu defended his government's new loans.

Read more

Former President Olusegun Obasanjo has criticised President Bola Tinubu's attitude to government borrowing, reportedly describing the President's past comparison of debt to leprosy as nonsense.

What he said

Speaking at a public forum in Abeokuta, Ogun State, on Friday, 30 October 2026, Obasanjo said debt was worse than leprosy because leprosy destroys only one person, while debt destroys generations. He was responding to a remark Tinubu made in April, when the President said borrowing was not leprosy and that the country simply had to work hard to repay its loans.

Other points

Obasanjo reportedly recalled that when he started his farm 47 years ago, one naira was worth more than a dollar, compared with about ₦1,500 to the dollar today, and asked what Nigeria had done with that time. He questioned whether the country's leaders understood its needs, criticised Nigeria's reliance on the World Bank and International Monetary Fund, and said Nigeria was advised not to subsidise its farmers while rich countries support theirs. He also warned against relying too heavily on foreign-trained economists whose advice may not suit Nigeria. The forum was focused on using artificial intelligence to solve grassroots problems.

Background

Obasanjo, who led Nigeria from 1999 to 2007, is credited by supporters with securing relief from much of the country's foreign debt in 2005. His comments came a day after Tinubu published an essay defending his government's new external loans, arguing that debt should be judged by what it builds and pointing to falling inflation and rising reserves. The President has asked the Senate to approve new borrowing of about $20.5 billion and €1 billion, which opposition figures including Atiku Abubakar have criticised.

What happens next

The Presidency had not responded to Obasanjo's remarks at the time of the report, and the debate over debt is likely to feature strongly in the 2027 campaign.

FG announces ₦50,000 monthly cash relief for vulnerable households

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

The Federal Government has announced a ₦50,000 monthly cash transfer for vulnerable households under a new scheme called NERSI. Officials say beneficiaries do not need to apply or pay anyone, and will receive a text message asking for consent before payment.

Read more

The Federal Government has unveiled a new monthly cash transfer of ₦50,000 for vulnerable Nigerian households, as part of efforts to ease the pressure of high living costs.

What was announced

The Minister of Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Reduction, Dr Bernard Doro, announced the National Emergency Relief and Stabilisation Intervention (NERSI) in Abuja on Friday, 30 October 2026, describing it as a presidential initiative. He said the programme would reach millions of Nigerians, although the exact number of beneficiaries and how long the scheme will last were not given. The amount could be reviewed over time in line with the cost of living.

How it will work

Officials reportedly said beneficiaries do not need to apply, pay any fee or register through agents. Payments will be made through licensed digital financial service providers. Each beneficiary will first receive a text message asking for their consent before money is sent to their bank account or mobile wallet. The ministry warned people never to share their PIN or Bank Verification Number with anyone, and provided an official helpline for complaints.

Background

The announcement follows the government's recent approval of a ₦70,000 monthly transport allowance for federal workers, which labour unions are divided over, and comes as opposition parties attack the government over the cost of living ahead of the January 2027 elections. Previous cash transfer schemes have faced complaints about who was selected and about fraudsters pretending to register people.

What to watch out for

Nigerians are being urged to be wary of anyone asking for money or personal details to 'enrol' them in the scheme, as officials say no such registration is needed.

Pope Leo XIV casts mail-in ballot in US midterm elections

30 Oct 2026, 20:49 GMT

The Vatican says Pope Leo XIV has voted by mail in the US midterm elections in his home state of Illinois, believed to be the first time a pope has voted in an American election. The Vatican said he believes citizens have a right and duty to take part in democracy.

Read more

Pope Leo XIV, the first American pope, has voted in the United States midterm elections, the Vatican has confirmed.

What happened

The Holy See press office said on Friday, 30 October 2026, that the Pope had cast a mail-in ballot in Illinois, where he was born. It said he had exercised his right to vote as a US citizen whenever he was eligible, and that he believes citizens have both a right and a duty to take part in democratic processes. His ballot is expected to include races for the US Senate, the governorship of Illinois and the House of Representatives. He is believed to be the first pope ever to vote in an American election.

Background

The Pope was born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago and holds both American and Peruvian citizenship, having spent many years as a missionary and bishop in Peru. He was elected pope in May 2025 after the death of Pope Francis. Public records show he voted regularly in Illinois before becoming pope, including in the 2024 presidential election. The Vatican did not say who he voted for.

Why it matters

The US midterm elections in November will decide control of Congress, and the Pope's decision to vote has drawn wide attention because of his role as a global religious leader. Supporters praised him for setting an example of civic duty, while others questioned whether a pope should take part in a national election.

What happens next

The Pope is expected to continue his public schedule, including a planned visit to South America.
