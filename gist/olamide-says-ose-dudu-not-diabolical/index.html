Olamide defends Yoruba culture, says black soap is not diabolical

4 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

YBNL boss Olamide Adedeji, popularly known as Olamide, has spoken in defence of Yoruba culture and tradition.

What he said

At a private screening of 'Ose Dudu', a new film produced by his friend, actress Sotayo Gaga, on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Olamide was asked about the traditional black soap of the same name, which some people associate with spiritual or diabolical practices. In a video from the event, he said Yoruba culture and tradition are not diabolical and should not be demonised, and that people should be proud of who they are and where they come from. He said he would not hesitate to use the soap if someone offered it to him.

Background

Ose dudu, or black soap, is widely used across West Africa for skin care and is made from natural ingredients such as plantain skins, cocoa pods and palm oil. In some contexts it is also prepared with herbs for traditional spiritual purposes, which has led to suspicion among some religious people. Olamide is one of Nigeria's most influential rappers and record label owners, and has launched the careers of several stars.

How people reacted

Many fans praised Olamide for standing up for African heritage, while others said they would still be careful with traditional products associated with spiritual rites.

  • He spoke at a screening of Sotayo Gaga's film 'Ose Dudu'.
  • Fans praised him for defending African heritage.

Skales says Burna Boy once asked Davido for space on his private jet

4 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Singer Skales says Burna Boy once asked Davido to let them travel on his private jet to a show in Yola where they performed their song 'Temper'. He said the jet was so full that one person sat on the toilet seat.

Read more

Singer Skales has shared a story from the early days of his career involving two of Nigeria's biggest stars, Burna Boy and Davido.

What he said

In comments that spread on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, Skales said he and Burna Boy were booked to perform their collaboration 'Temper' at a show in Yola. According to him, Burna Boy contacted Davido and asked if they could travel on his private jet, and Davido agreed. Because the plane was already full, Davido reportedly asked one of his team members to stay behind and fly the next day. Skales said the jet was still so crowded that one person had to sit on the toilet seat during the flight.

A lucky break

Skales reportedly said the Yola trip led to an important connection for him with the international act Major Lazer, which helped his career.

Background

'Temper', released in 2014, was one of Skales's biggest hits and featured Burna Boy. Burna Boy and Davido have had a complicated relationship over the years, with periods of rivalry among their fans, although they have also shown respect for each other in public. Both are now global stars, with Burna Boy winning a Grammy in 2021 and Davido receiving multiple Grammy nominations.

How people reacted

Fans enjoyed the throwback, with some joking about the toilet seat and others saying it showed that the stars once helped each other.

Former Osun governor Oyinlola says his endorsement of Tinubu 'has expired'

5 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Former Osun governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has reportedly withdrawn his 2025 endorsement of President Tinubu for 2027, saying it was a political move to resolve Osun's local government crisis that did not work.

Read more

Former Osun State governor Olagunsoye Oyinlola has said his endorsement of President Bola Tinubu for the 2027 election is no longer valid.

What he said

Asked in a television interview, reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026, whether he still stood by the endorsement, Oyinlola reportedly replied that it had expired. He explained that he and other Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) stakeholders in Osun endorsed the President on 20 July 2025 as a strategic move, because of problems in the state that they believed only the President could solve, including missing local government funds and a dispute over who should occupy the local council offices. He said the endorsement was meant to help resolve those issues, but the solution had not come.

Background

Oyinlola governed Osun State from 2003 to 2010. Osun has been at the centre of a long-running dispute over control of its local governments, involving the PDP-led state government of Governor Ademola Adeleke and APC-backed council officials, with court rulings and disagreements over the release of council funds. The PDP itself is divided, with some members backing Tinubu, others joining opposition coalitions, and a court battle over its national leadership.

Why it matters

The withdrawal is a setback for the President's campaign in Osun, a swing state in the South West. The APC says it remains confident of winning the state, while opposition parties hope to benefit from discontent there.

What happens next

Oyinlola did not say which candidate, if any, he would now support.
