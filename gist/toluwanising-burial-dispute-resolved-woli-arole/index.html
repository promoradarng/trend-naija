Toluwanising burial row resolved as widow and father agree, Woli Arole calls for calm

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

The dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried appears to have been settled, according to one of his closest colleagues.

The agreement

In an Instagram video shared on Sunday, 1 November 2026, singer Lanre Alewilese said the widow, Damilola Olayemi, and her father-in-law had agreed that the singer should be buried at his house in Ibadan, Oyo State. He described the disagreement as a misunderstanding and asked Nigerians to let the family settle the matter in peace. He also appealed to people to stop attacking the widow, describing her as a young woman going through a very difficult time, and said members of the singer's band could vouch for her character.

Woli Arole speaks

On Monday, 2 November, comedian-turned-pastor Bayegun Oluwatoyin, known as Woli Arole, posted a video in which he condemned people seeking social media attention from the tragedy. Quoting Proverbs 31:8 on speaking up for those who cannot speak for themselves, he described Toluwanising as a rare talent, and questioned why some of those now talking about him had shown little interest in his music while he was alive. He urged the public to support the widow and children, and said no dispute could take away the music the singer left behind. His comments came after unproven claims made online by a preacher based in Canada named him and other gospel figures in connection with the death.

Background

Toluwanising, 32, died suddenly on 14 October 2026, and a night of tributes was held for him in Ibadan on 27 October. His father had insisted that he be buried at the family residence or in his hometown near his late mother, and relatives had claimed that the widow preferred a cemetery. His younger brother later defended the widow, as did media personality Nancy Umeh.

Police broker peace deal

On Tuesday, 3 November, the Oyo State Police Command said the singer's wife, Olayemi Elizabeth Damilola, his father, Muniru Olayemi, and some of his friends and associates had signed a peace undertaking at the command headquarters in Eleyele, Ibadan. The agreement followed an intervention by the Commissioner of Police, Abimbola Olugbenga, and the parties agreed to stop public exchanges about the dispute.

What happens next

A burial date has not yet been announced.

CAS restores Senegal as AFCON 2025 champions, overturning CAF ruling

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Court of Arbitration for Sport has confirmed Senegal as winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. It overturned in full the CAF Appeal Board decision that had handed the title to Morocco.

Read more

Senegal are once again Africa's champions after the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) overturned the decision that had stripped them of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations title.

The verdict

CAS delivered its ruling on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, reversing in full the decision of CAF's Appeal Board, which had awarded the title to hosts Morocco. With the verdict, Senegal are officially recognised as the 2025 AFCON champions.

How it started

Senegal beat Morocco 1-0 after extra time in the final at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat on 18 January 2026, with Pape Gueye scoring the only goal. During the match, Senegal's players walked off the pitch for about 15 minutes in protest at a late penalty awarded to Morocco, which was later saved.

The Royal Moroccan Football Federation then took legal action, and on 17 March CAF's Appeal Board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match, awarding Morocco a 3-0 win and the trophy. The Senegalese Football Federation refused to accept the decision and appealed to CAS.

What it means

The ruling ends months of legal uncertainty over the continent's top football title. Before the verdict, CAF President Patrice Motsepe had said the body would accept whatever CAS decided.

Court sacks Omo-Agege as NDC senatorial candidate for Delta Central

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

A Federal High Court in Warri has voided Ovie Omo-Agege's nomination as the NDC candidate for Delta Central and ordered INEC to remove his name. The judgment came a day after gunmen attacked his campaign.

Read more

A Federal High Court in Warri, Delta State, has nullified the nomination of former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege as the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) candidate for Delta Central Senatorial District in the 2027 elections.

The judgment

Delivering judgment on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, Justice Hyeladzira Ajiya Nganjiwa ruled that the NDC failed to comply with mandatory provisions of the Electoral Act in nominating Omo-Agege. The court directed INEC to remove his name from the list of senatorial candidates and barred him from presenting himself as the party's candidate.

Why the court ruled against him

The suit was filed by Professor Alexander Osondu Akpodiete, who challenged Omo-Agege's nomination, his resignation from the APC and his eligibility to take part in the NDC primary. The court held that the NDC did not submit its membership register to INEC as required, and that Omo-Agege was still an APC member when he took part in the party's senatorial primary on 18 May 2026. The judge first dismissed five preliminary objections raised by Omo-Agege, the NDC and INEC.

Background

The ruling came a day after gunmen attacked Omo-Agege's campaign event in Uduere, Ughelli North, killing several people. The senator escaped unhurt.

CBN keeps interest rate at 23% despite slowing inflation

24 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has held its benchmark interest rate at 23% at the last policy meeting of the year. Analysts had warned that election-year spending could put fresh pressure on prices.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 23%, maintaining a tight policy stance even though inflation has continued to slow.

The decision

Announcing the outcome in Abuja on Tuesday, 24 November 2026, after the Monetary Policy Committee's final two-day meeting of the year, CBN Governor Olayemi Cardoso said members voted unanimously to hold the Monetary Policy Rate at 23%.

The committee also left the corridor around the rate unchanged, and kept the cash reserve requirement for commercial banks at 45% and for merchant banks at 16%.

Why it held

The decision reflects caution amid renewed pressure on the naira and uncertainty at home and abroad. Analysts had said the recent drop in inflation might not be enough to justify a cut, warning that spending ahead of the 2027 elections could increase money in circulation and push prices up again.

What it means

Borrowing costs for businesses and households are expected to remain high for now.
