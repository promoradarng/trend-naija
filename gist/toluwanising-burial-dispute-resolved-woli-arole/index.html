Toluwanising burial row resolved as widow and father agree, Woli Arole calls for calm

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

The dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried appears to have been settled, according to one of his closest colleagues.

The agreement

In an Instagram video shared on Sunday, 1 November 2026, singer Lanre Alewilese said the widow, Damilola Olayemi, and her father-in-law had agreed that the singer should be buried at his house in Ibadan, Oyo State. He described the disagreement as a misunderstanding and asked Nigerians to let the family settle the matter in peace. He also appealed to people to stop attacking the widow, describing her as a young woman going through a very difficult time, and said members of the singer's band could vouch for her character.

Woli Arole speaks

On Monday, 2 November, comedian-turned-pastor Bayegun Oluwatoyin, known as Woli Arole, posted a video in which he condemned people seeking social media attention from the tragedy. Quoting Proverbs 31:8 on speaking up for those who cannot speak for themselves, he described Toluwanising as a rare talent, and questioned why some of those now talking about him had shown little interest in his music while he was alive. He urged the public to support the widow and children, and said no dispute could take away the music the singer left behind. His comments came after unproven claims made online by a preacher based in Canada named him and other gospel figures in connection with the death.

Background

Toluwanising, 32, died suddenly on 14 October 2026, and a night of tributes was held for him in Ibadan on 27 October. His father had insisted that he be buried at the family residence or in his hometown near his late mother, and relatives had claimed that the widow preferred a cemetery. His younger brother later defended the widow, as did media personality Nancy Umeh.

Police broker peace deal

On Tuesday, 3 November, the Oyo State Police Command said the singer's wife, Olayemi Elizabeth Damilola, his father, Muniru Olayemi, and some of his friends and associates had signed a peace undertaking at the command headquarters in Eleyele, Ibadan. The agreement followed an intervention by the Commissioner of Police, Abimbola Olugbenga, and the parties agreed to stop public exchanges about the dispute.

What happens next

A burial date has not yet been announced.

  • Woli Arole urged people to stop using the death for clout.
  • No burial date has been announced.

Ghanaian promoter upset after Davido reportedly performs under 30 minutes

16 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

A Ghanaian hotel owner who hosted 'The Davido Experience' in Manso Akwasiso was filmed looking upset after the singer reportedly performed for less than 30 minutes, sparking debate about the booking.

Read more

Davido is at the centre of a debate in Ghana after reportedly performing for less than half an hour at a show there.

What happened

The singer was the headline act at 'The Davido Experience' on Sunday, 15 November 2026, an event marking the third anniversary of a hotel in Manso Akwasiso owned by businessman Great Osama, whose real name is Kwaku Abonee. A video shared online appeared to show the businessman visibly upset after the short set, and another clip showed him apparently urging that the gates be kept shut as Davido prepared to leave.

The fee

Some reports claimed the booking fee was around $600,000, but neither Davido, his management nor the organiser has confirmed the figure, and the terms of the agreement, including the agreed performance time, have not been made public.

How people reacted

Reactions online were mixed, with some questioning the deal and others defending the singer. Davido had not publicly commented at the time of writing. He was also filmed warmly greeting Flavour in Ghana, a moment that sparked its own online debate.

Kwara APC governorship rally in Offa reportedly turns violent over unpaid mobilisation funds

16 Nov 2026, 11:21 GMT

An APC governorship rally in Offa, Kwara State, reportedly descended into chaos after claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised. The incident has revived debate about paying crowds to attend political events.

Read more

A governorship campaign rally organised by the All Progressives Congress (APC) in Offa, Kwara State, has reportedly descended into chaos following claims that some participants were not paid the mobilisation allowances they had been promised.

What reportedly happened

According to reports, the rally drew a large crowd of supporters to the streets of Offa. But tension rose when some of those who had turned up complained that they had not received the money they were allegedly promised for attending.

The situation reportedly turned violent as angry participants confronted organisers, disrupting the event.

The full details, including whether anyone was injured and how much money was involved, have not been independently confirmed, and the party has not publicly commented on the incident.

The business of crowds

The incident shines a light on a practice many Nigerians already know about: paying people, often called "mobilisation", to attend political rallies.

Across parties, campaign organisers frequently provide transport, food, branded caps and T-shirts, and sometimes cash, to fill venues and create the impression of massive support. When those promises are not kept, frustration can quickly boil over.

It also raises a deeper question. If people have to be paid to attend, how much do the crowds at political rallies really tell us about genuine support?

Over to you

Should people be paid to attend political rallies, or should campaigns rely only on supporters who come out of conviction?

Tinubu campaign unveils five-year defence plan: 28,000 soldiers, 50,000 police

16 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

The APC presidential campaign says President Tinubu has launched a five-year defence plan, with approval to recruit 28,000 more soldiers and 50,000 police officers, new army divisions and training depots.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu's campaign has outlined a five-year strategic defence plan it says will guide efforts to tackle insecurity.

What was announced

In a statement on Sunday, 15 November 2026, APC presidential campaign spokesperson Dele Alake said the plan brings together defence priorities, operational needs, capability development and spending to respond to threats including terrorism, banditry and kidnapping. The campaign said the government had approved the recruitment of 28,000 additional soldiers and 50,000 new police officers, created four new army divisions and approved two new army depots in Osogbo, Osun State, and Amasiri-Edda, Ebonyi State, each able to train up to 6,000 recruits at a time. It also pointed to a 700-unit barracks project in Ikere-Ekiti.

Claims of progress

The campaign claimed that military operations over the past three years had killed more than 15,000 insurgents and bandits, and that over 130,000 Boko Haram fighters and their dependants had surrendered under the Operation Safe Corridor rehabilitation programme. These figures could not be independently verified.

Background

Insecurity is one of the main issues in the 2027 campaign. ADC candidate Atiku Abubakar this week cited tracker data showing 638 people were killed in October, and recent attacks in Zamfara and Kano have heightened concern. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Obi hits back at Tinubu over Anambra erosion jibe, launches 'Record of Service' series

16 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Peter Obi has rejected President Tinubu's claim at a rally that he abandoned Anambra during its erosion crisis, saying he will publish a ten-day series on his record as governor and that 'facts outlast allegations'.

Read more

Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has responded to President Bola Tinubu's criticism of his record as governor of Anambra State.

Tinubu's claim

At a recent campaign rally, the President accused Obi of failing to tackle erosion in Anambra while governor, contrasting it with what he described as his own decisive action on coastal problems in Lagos. He also praised Akwa Ibom governor Umo Eno for tackling erosion in his state.

Obi's response

Writing from the United States, where he said he had arrived for engagements in Washington DC, Obi rejected the accusation and said he would launch a ten-day series called 'Record of Service', documenting his interventions and achievements as governor. He said opponents who were uneasy about their own records were scrutinising his past, and that while no leader achieves every goal, his dedication was never in doubt. He said facts outlast allegations and records weigh more than rhetoric.

Background

The exchange is one of the most direct confrontations between the two candidates so far. Obi's record in Anambra has also been questioned by others this month, including musician Seun Kuti. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.
