Toluwanising burial row resolved as widow and father agree, Woli Arole calls for calm

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

The dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried appears to have been settled, according to one of his closest colleagues.

The agreement

In an Instagram video shared on Sunday, 1 November 2026, singer Lanre Alewilese said the widow, Damilola Olayemi, and her father-in-law had agreed that the singer should be buried at his house in Ibadan, Oyo State. He described the disagreement as a misunderstanding and asked Nigerians to let the family settle the matter in peace. He also appealed to people to stop attacking the widow, describing her as a young woman going through a very difficult time, and said members of the singer's band could vouch for her character.

Woli Arole speaks

On Monday, 2 November, comedian-turned-pastor Bayegun Oluwatoyin, known as Woli Arole, posted a video in which he condemned people seeking social media attention from the tragedy. Quoting Proverbs 31:8 on speaking up for those who cannot speak for themselves, he described Toluwanising as a rare talent, and questioned why some of those now talking about him had shown little interest in his music while he was alive. He urged the public to support the widow and children, and said no dispute could take away the music the singer left behind. His comments came after unproven claims made online by a preacher based in Canada named him and other gospel figures in connection with the death.

Background

Toluwanising, 32, died suddenly on 14 October 2026, and a night of tributes was held for him in Ibadan on 27 October. His father had insisted that he be buried at the family residence or in his hometown near his late mother, and relatives had claimed that the widow preferred a cemetery. His younger brother later defended the widow, as did media personality Nancy Umeh.

Police broker peace deal

On Tuesday, 3 November, the Oyo State Police Command said the singer's wife, Olayemi Elizabeth Damilola, his father, Muniru Olayemi, and some of his friends and associates had signed a peace undertaking at the command headquarters in Eleyele, Ibadan. The agreement followed an intervention by the Commissioner of Police, Abimbola Olugbenga, and the parties agreed to stop public exchanges about the dispute.

What happens next

A burial date has not yet been announced.

  • Woli Arole urged people to stop using the death for clout.
  • No burial date has been announced.

Goodluck Jonathan calls for review of Nigeria's democracy and institutions

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a broad review of Nigeria's democratic structures, how politicians are recruited and the state of public institutions, saying the country owes a debt to its founding nationalists.

Read more

Former President Goodluck Jonathan has called for a wide-ranging review of Nigeria's democratic structures, political recruitment and public institutions.

What he said

Chairing a colloquium at the Nigerian Institute of International Affairs in Lagos to mark the 162nd birth anniversary of nationalist Herbert Macaulay, reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Jonathan said the country's founding nationalists did not merely fight to end colonial rule, but imagined a sovereign, united and prosperous Nigeria where every citizen would enjoy equal rights, dignity and opportunity. He said independence was also meant to bring economic freedom, industrialisation, quality education, healthcare and social justice.

Honesty about failures

While acknowledging progress in some areas, Jonathan said true patriotism requires honesty about the country's failures. He pointed to leadership failures and weak institutions, and urged a review of how the country's democracy works and how leaders emerge.

Background

Jonathan, who governed from 2010 to 2015, is widely remembered for conceding defeat to Muhammadu Buhari in the 2015 election, a first for a sitting Nigerian president. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Yoruba Muslim clerics' league says it will not endorse any 2027 candidate

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland says it will not back any politician or party in the 2027 elections, insisting its role is to pray for Nigeria and guide the people, not to campaign.

Read more

The League of Imams and Alfas in Yorubaland has declared that it will not endorse any politician or party ahead of the 2027 elections.

What the clerics said

At a gathering in Ibadan on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the chairman of the league's board of trustees, Sheikh Muhammad Habibullah Adam-Abdullahi El-Ilory, said the league had neither the capacity nor the intention to name a preferred candidate for any office. He said Islamic clerics are not politicians and their duty does not include campaigning for any individual or party. League president Sheikh Jamiu Kewulere said imams must stay focused on religious guidance.

Background

The statement came after Oyo governor and APM presidential candidate Seyi Makinde made a donation to the league. The lecturers' union ASUU also said this week that it would not endorse any candidate. The presidential election is scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Jordan sentences three men to death over deadly church attack

15 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

A Jordanian court has sentenced three men to death for a gun attack on a Greek Orthodox church funeral in October that killed nine people and wounded 25.

Read more

A court in Jordan has sentenced three men to death by hanging for a deadly attack on a church in the south of the country.

The verdict

The State Security Court on Sunday, 15 November 2026, found the men, two brothers and a relative by marriage, guilty of terrorist attacks resulting in death, weapons offences, conspiracy to commit terrorist acts and promoting the ideas of a terrorist organisation, according to a statement from the Jordanian military.

The attack

Gunmen killed nine people and wounded 25 when they stormed a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Hammoud village, in the Karak governorate, on 23 October 2026. The three suspects fled after the attack and were arrested three days later.

Background

The attack shocked Jordan, a Muslim-majority country where Christians make up around three per cent of the population.
