Toluwanising burial row resolved as widow and father agree, Woli Arole calls for calm

2 Nov 2026, 11:45 GMT

The dispute over where gospel singer Toluwanising should be buried appears to have been settled, according to one of his closest colleagues.

The agreement

In an Instagram video shared on Sunday, 1 November 2026, singer Lanre Alewilese said the widow, Damilola Olayemi, and her father-in-law had agreed that the singer should be buried at his house in Ibadan, Oyo State. He described the disagreement as a misunderstanding and asked Nigerians to let the family settle the matter in peace. He also appealed to people to stop attacking the widow, describing her as a young woman going through a very difficult time, and said members of the singer's band could vouch for her character.

Woli Arole speaks

On Monday, 2 November, comedian-turned-pastor Bayegun Oluwatoyin, known as Woli Arole, posted a video in which he condemned people seeking social media attention from the tragedy. Quoting Proverbs 31:8 on speaking up for those who cannot speak for themselves, he described Toluwanising as a rare talent, and questioned why some of those now talking about him had shown little interest in his music while he was alive. He urged the public to support the widow and children, and said no dispute could take away the music the singer left behind. His comments came after unproven claims made online by a preacher based in Canada named him and other gospel figures in connection with the death.

Background

Toluwanising, 32, died suddenly on 14 October 2026, and a night of tributes was held for him in Ibadan on 27 October. His father had insisted that he be buried at the family residence or in his hometown near his late mother, and relatives had claimed that the widow preferred a cemetery. His younger brother later defended the widow, as did media personality Nancy Umeh.

Police broker peace deal

On Tuesday, 3 November, the Oyo State Police Command said the singer's wife, Olayemi Elizabeth Damilola, his father, Muniru Olayemi, and some of his friends and associates had signed a peace undertaking at the command headquarters in Eleyele, Ibadan. The agreement followed an intervention by the Commissioner of Police, Abimbola Olugbenga, and the parties agreed to stop public exchanges about the dispute.

What happens next

A burial date has not yet been announced.

"Her own papa?!": Rudeboy reacts in disbelief as young lady blames mother for marrying a broke man

5 Nov 2026, 18:08 GMT

Singer Paul Okoye, popularly known as Rudeboy, has expressed shock over a viral video of a young lady who blamed her mother for marrying a man who was not financially successful: her own father. The clip has sparked a wider debate about money, marriage and what children expect of their parents.

Read more

Nigerian singer Paul Okoye, popularly known as Rudeboy, has expressed shock over a viral video of a young lady who reportedly blamed her mother for marrying a man who was not financially successful.

Rudeboy's reaction

Reacting to the video on his Instagram story, Rudeboy could hardly believe what he was hearing. "Imagine a daughter blaming her own mother for marrying down, as in marrying a broke man! As in her father! Her own papa!" he wrote.

It is easy to see why the statement caught his attention. Imagine growing up, looking at your parents, and deciding your mother made a terrible mistake simply because your father was not rich.

Social media reacts

The young lady's remarks have stirred plenty of reactions, with many Nigerians asking how someone could speak so openly about her own father's financial situation.

Some believe her comments reflect a growing obsession with money and luxury among young people. Others argue that children who grow up in financial hardship may develop resentment towards their parents, especially when they see other families living more comfortably.

Some social media users have even joked that they will save the video and wait to see the kind of wealthy man she eventually marries. Since she believes her mother chose poorly, they say, the public will be interested to see whether she makes a better choice when her own time comes.

One thing about social media is that people rarely forget statements like this. Years from now, someone may dig up the same video and remind her of what she said about her parents.

The other side

Nobody chooses the circumstances they are born into, and wanting a better life is perfectly understandable. But does that justify publicly blaming a parent for not marrying someone wealthy?

Marriage itself is unpredictable. A man can be comfortable today and lose everything tomorrow, while another can start with almost nothing and become successful years later.

That is why Rudeboy's reaction has become part of a much bigger conversation about money, marriage, parental sacrifice and the expectations children place on their parents.

Over to you

Patoranking contrasts peaceful US midterms with Nigerian elections

5 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

Singer Patoranking has praised the peaceful conduct of the US midterm elections, noting that nobody died and no ballot boxes were snatched, and said Nigerians have normalised bad behaviour in their elections.

Read more

Reggae-dancehall singer Patoranking has compared the recent US midterm elections with elections in Nigeria.

What he said

In a post on X, reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026, the singer noted that nobody died during the American polls and there were no reports of ballot box snatching. He said Nigeria's problems have become so bad that citizens now accept bad behaviour as normal.

Background

The US midterm elections on 3 November saw the Democrats projected to take control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate, with several historic firsts, including the first Muslim US senator. Midterms are held halfway through a president's four-year term and decide all 435 House seats and about a third of the Senate, as well as many state governorships. Nigeria's past elections have often been marred by violence and allegations of rigging, and attacks on campaign events have already been reported ahead of the January 2027 polls, including on Peter Obi's convoy in Calabar this week.

How people reacted

Many fans agreed with the singer and called for peaceful and credible elections in 2027, while others pointed out that the US also has problems with its elections.

Bandits demand over ₦100 million for abducted Niger Islamiyya students, teacher says

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

The teacher of the Islamiyya students abducted from his farm in Rafi, Niger State, says the kidnappers have demanded more than ₦100 million for their release. He said families have had little official help and called for military action.

Read more

The kidnappers of a group of Islamiyya students abducted in Niger State have demanded more than ₦100 million for their release, according to their teacher.

What the teacher said

Malam Aminu Bako, whose students were taken while helping him harvest maize on his farm in Rafi Local Government Area, said on Thursday, 5 November 2026, that the bandits had contacted village leaders with the ransom demand. He said 15 children were abducted, and called for urgent military action to rescue them. According to him, only the acting chairman of the local government and the divisional police officer had visited since the abduction, and families were relying on local vigilantes to track the kidnappers.

Where the children may be

Bako said information gathered by vigilantes suggests the children are still in Niger State, held in an isolated building guarded by heavily armed men, possibly without enough food. He said he was under intense pressure from worried parents.

Background

The students were abducted on 31 October from a farm in Tungan-Bako village near Pandogari. Police at the time said 14 students were taken and that a joint security operation had been launched. Rafi and surrounding areas have suffered repeated attacks by armed groups, and kidnapping for ransom remains widespread in north-west and north-central Nigeria. Paying ransom is illegal in Nigeria, although families often feel they have no choice.

What happens next

Families and community leaders are calling on the federal and state governments to step up efforts to rescue the children.

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña resigns over bank collapse scandal

5 Nov 2026, 20:43 GMT

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned after the lower house voted to begin impeachment proceedings over the collapse of a bank he was once linked to as a shareholder. He apologised to Paraguayans.

Read more

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned amid a political crisis caused by the collapse of a bank he was previously linked to.

What happened

In a video message posted on X, Peña said he was stepping down to protect the country's stability and democratic institutions, a day after opposition lawmakers requested his impeachment and the lower house of Congress voted to begin proceedings, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. He apologised to the people if he had failed them at any point, saying it was never his intention to betray their trust.

The bank collapse

The crisis began when Ueno Bank collapsed and froze its operations, reportedly leaving large numbers of customers unable to access their accounts. Lawmakers accused Peña of conflicts of interest, pointing to his past shareholding in the company that owned the bank.

Background

Peña, an economist and former finance minister, took office in August 2023 as a member of the long-dominant Colorado Party. Under Paraguay's constitution, the vice-president takes over when a president leaves office before the end of the term.

What happens next

Attention now turns to who will lead the country for the rest of the term, and to investigations into the bank's collapse and whether depositors will recover their money.
