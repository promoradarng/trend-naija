8 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Ekiti politician Opeyemi Falegan has publicly apologised to Nollywood actress Nkechi Blessing Sunday, years after their much-discussed break-up.

What happened

The apology came during a TikTok Live session on Saturday, 7 November 2026, in which the former couple spoke face to face online about their 2022 separation. Nkechi made it clear that she wanted a genuine apology, reminding Falegan that remarks he made about her during a social media live session years ago were still circulating and being used against her. Falegan's response

Falegan apologised and said he had grown since their split, urging both of them to let the past go. He also spoke about what he described as emotional unavailability and temperament issues on both sides that made the relationship difficult.

Background

Nkechi Blessing is a popular actress and producer known for her outspoken personality on social media. Her relationship with Falegan was widely publicised before it ended in 2022 amid public exchanges online. The actress was also recently in the news after confronting comedian Carter Efe and his girlfriend.

Fans were divided, with some praising the two for making peace and others questioning why Nkechi chose to revisit the matter in public.

Presidential election turnout has fallen from 69% to 27% since 2003

28 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has dropped from a Fourth Republic high of about 69% in 2003 to under 27% in 2023, according to figures compiled by a statistics platform. The decline has continued for five straight elections.

Read more

Voter turnout in Nigeria's presidential elections has fallen sharply over the past two decades, according to figures shared by a statistics platform this week.

The numbers

Measured as total votes cast as a share of registered voters, turnout reached a Fourth Republic high of 69.08% in 2003. It then fell to 57.49% in 2007 and 53.68% in 2011, and continued to decline in each election after that, reaching just 26.71% in 2023. That is a drop of more than 42 percentage points in 20 years.

Why it matters

The figures come as parties and INEC step up efforts to get Nigerians to collect their Permanent Voter Cards and vote in the 16 January 2027 elections. INEC is also running a nationwide mock accreditation exercise on Saturday to test its systems.

Background

Concerns about insecurity, logistics and confidence in the electoral process have often been cited as reasons for low turnout. INEC this week named nine states as hotspots for campaign violence.
