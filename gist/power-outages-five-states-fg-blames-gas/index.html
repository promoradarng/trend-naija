Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Public frustration

The outages have drawn complaints from well-known Nigerians. On Friday, comedian The Cute Abiola posted a video addressed to President Bola Tinubu, saying his solar system had run down after days of poor supply, that everywhere was hot and that the suffering was too much.

Actress Kate Henshaw also posted a video criticising the minister, saying power in her area lasts only minutes before going off and demanding accountability for spending on the sector.

Grid operator explains

In a statement on Thursday, 5 November, the Nigerian Independent System Operator (NISO), which manages the national grid, blamed the drop in supply on inadequate gas deliveries to thermal power plants and lower water levels at hydroelectric stations. It said this had forced it to cut the power allocated to distribution companies, leading to more load shedding, and that the impact varies from one region to another.

Minister gives timeline

In a further statement on Friday, 6 November, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said three problems had combined to cut supply: planned maintenance at the Egbin power station, limited gas supply to some thermal plants, and sabotage of key transmission lines, which he said had been especially damaging in Lagos because it blocked routes engineers would normally use to redirect power. He said damaged generating units would be brought back in phases, with the first expected back within 48 to 72 hours, and that work was under way with gas suppliers and on repairs. Singer Spyro also said on Saturday that when he visited an Eko Electricity Distribution Company office to complain, he found staff fanning themselves because of the heat.

Agencies trade blame

By the weekend, agencies in the power sector were contradicting one another. NNPC's gas infrastructure subsidiary said in a statement dated Friday that its pipeline network had no constraints preventing gas from reaching power plants, rejecting suggestions that gas supply was to blame, though it said volumes actually taken up by individual plants and maintenance were separate issues. The Transmission Company of Nigeria also accused Ikeja Electric of misleading customers by blaming outages on 3 and 4 November on grid instability, saying its records showed no equipment failure or line trip, and that lower generation and problems within Ikeja Electric's own network were largely responsible.

Supply restored in many areas

On Monday, 9 November, the Minister of Power said electricity had been restored to many of the affected areas in Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Kwara and the FCT, with work continuing on the rest. He said the vandalised Benin–Egbin 330kV line had been repaired, raising supply to the Lagos area to over 1,000 megawatts, back to the level before the disruption.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.

  • The minister blamed gas shortages and low hydro output.
  • The government says there was no grid collapse.

Zamfara police rescue 19 women and children after 22 days with bandits

16 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Zamfara police say they have rescued 19 women and children who had been held by bandits for 22 days, after a joint operation with the DSS and local hunters near Gusau. All were found unharmed.

Read more

Police in Zamfara State say they have rescued 19 women and children who had spent 22 days in the hands of bandits.

The rescue

In a statement, police spokesperson DSP Yazid Abubakar said the victims had been abducted from Tungar Haki village in Gusau Local Government Area and taken to a bandits' hideout near Kungurmi village. They were rescued on Saturday, 14 November 2026, in a coordinated operation involving the police anti-kidnapping unit, officers attached to Government House, the Department of State Services and local hunters, following intelligence about their location.

Reunited

The police said all 19 victims were found unharmed and have been reunited with their families.

Background

Zamfara is one of the states worst affected by banditry and kidnapping for ransom. On Sunday, bandits killed a senior aide to the state governor during an attack in Kaura Namoda and reportedly abducted several worshippers.

NNPC gets land title for new Oredo refinery in Edo State

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

NNPC has received a Certificate of Occupancy for its proposed Oredo Refinery and Petrochemical Company in Edo State, a step towards building a new refinery to boost local fuel production.

Read more

The Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC) has taken a step towards building a new refinery in Edo State.

The approval

NNPC's Executive Vice President for Downstream, Mumuni Dagazau, received the Certificate of Occupancy for the proposed Oredo Refinery and Petrochemical Company Limited during a courtesy visit to Governor Monday Okpebholo at the Edo State Government House in Benin City on Friday, 13 November 2026. He thanked the governor for providing the land and documents, and said the project would help increase local refining capacity, reduce dependence on imported fuel and strengthen energy security.

Background

Nigeria's refining landscape has been transformed by the 650,000-barrel-per-day Dangote Refinery in Lagos, whose fuel exports to the United Kingdom have now overtaken crude oil sales, according to UK trade figures. No cost, capacity or timeline for the Oredo refinery was announced.

UK now buys more refined fuel than crude oil from Nigeria, data shows

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

UK trade figures show Britain bought £1.02 billion of refined petroleum products from Nigeria in the year to June 2026, more than double the year before, overtaking crude oil imports, as Dangote Refinery expands exports.

Read more

Nigeria's petroleum trade with the United Kingdom has shifted sharply towards refined fuel, according to UK government figures.

The figures

The UK's trade and investment factsheet, released on 6 November 2026 and reported this weekend, shows Britain bought £1.02 billion worth of refined petroleum products from Nigeria in the 12 months to June 2026. That was more than double the figure a year earlier and made up 53.5 per cent of all UK goods imports from Nigeria. Over the same period, UK purchases of Nigerian crude oil fell by 56.2 per cent to £550.3 million, meaning refined fuel exports exceeded crude shipments by about £467 million. Nigeria recorded a petroleum trade surplus of about £245 million with Britain.

Why it matters

The change reflects the growth of the Dangote Petroleum Refinery in Lagos, which can process 650,000 barrels a day and has been expanding its exports.

Background

The government has also announced plans for a new NNPC refinery in Edo State.

FG plans 24-hour electricity pilot for Lagos, Ogun and Abuja

15 Nov 2026, 17:41 GMT

Power Minister Joseph Tegbe says the Lagos–Ogun axis and the Abuja distribution area have been chosen for a pilot scheme to provide 24-hour electricity, but no start date has been set.

Read more

The federal government says it plans to pilot round-the-clock electricity supply in parts of Lagos, Ogun and Abuja.

What the minister said

Speaking at a luncheon with senior media editors in Lagos on Friday, 13 November 2026, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said the Lagos–Ogun axis and the Abuja electricity distribution franchise area had been selected for the proposed 24-hour supply pilot. He said the areas account for more than half of Nigeria's electricity demand, and that the scheme would focus on economic centres where reliable power could boost industry and reduce dependence on costly generators.

Conditions

The minister said implementation would depend on technical readiness and commercial viability, and no start date was announced.

Background

NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi this week promised 10,000 megawatts within four years, while a cleric called on the minister to resign over the power situation. Another state has separately announced a target of 24-hour power by 2029.
