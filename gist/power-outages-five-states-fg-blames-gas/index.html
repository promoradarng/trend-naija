Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Public frustration

The outages have drawn complaints from well-known Nigerians. On Friday, comedian The Cute Abiola posted a video addressed to President Bola Tinubu, saying his solar system had run down after days of poor supply, that everywhere was hot and that the suffering was too much.

Actress Kate Henshaw also posted a video criticising the minister, saying power in her area lasts only minutes before going off and demanding accountability for spending on the sector.

Grid operator explains

In a statement on Thursday, 5 November, the Nigerian Independent System Operator (NISO), which manages the national grid, blamed the drop in supply on inadequate gas deliveries to thermal power plants and lower water levels at hydroelectric stations. It said this had forced it to cut the power allocated to distribution companies, leading to more load shedding, and that the impact varies from one region to another.

Minister gives timeline

In a further statement on Friday, 6 November, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said three problems had combined to cut supply: planned maintenance at the Egbin power station, limited gas supply to some thermal plants, and sabotage of key transmission lines, which he said had been especially damaging in Lagos because it blocked routes engineers would normally use to redirect power. He said damaged generating units would be brought back in phases, with the first expected back within 48 to 72 hours, and that work was under way with gas suppliers and on repairs. Singer Spyro also said on Saturday that when he visited an Eko Electricity Distribution Company office to complain, he found staff fanning themselves because of the heat.

Agencies trade blame

By the weekend, agencies in the power sector were contradicting one another. NNPC's gas infrastructure subsidiary said in a statement dated Friday that its pipeline network had no constraints preventing gas from reaching power plants, rejecting suggestions that gas supply was to blame, though it said volumes actually taken up by individual plants and maintenance were separate issues. The Transmission Company of Nigeria also accused Ikeja Electric of misleading customers by blaming outages on 3 and 4 November on grid instability, saying its records showed no equipment failure or line trip, and that lower generation and problems within Ikeja Electric's own network were largely responsible.

Supply restored in many areas

On Monday, 9 November, the Minister of Power said electricity had been restored to many of the affected areas in Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Kwara and the FCT, with work continuing on the rest. He said the vandalised Benin–Egbin 330kV line had been repaired, raising supply to the Lagos area to over 1,000 megawatts, back to the level before the disruption.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.

World Bank says 17.6 million Nigerians have benefited from NG-CARES programme

17 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The World Bank says more than 17.6 million Nigerians have benefited from the NG-CARES programme. It comes as the federal government continues its separate ₦50,000 relief grant.

Read more

The World Bank has reportedly said that more than 17.6 million Nigerians have benefited from the Nigeria Community Action Recovery and Economic Stimulus (NG-CARES) programme.

What was said

The figure was given by the programme's World Bank task team leader, Dr Lire Ersado, at a technical meeting in Abuja on additional financing for the scheme.

He explained that NG-CARES was first set up to soften the economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, but has since grown to respond to other emergencies, including the 2022 floods. It supports social protection, food security, farming, small businesses and the strengthening of institutions.

The ₦50,000 grant

The update comes as the federal government continues a separate ₦50,000 relief grant for vulnerable Nigerians, part of wider efforts to help low-income households and small businesses cope with the rising cost of living.

Confusion at Fountain of Life as church rejects reported removal of Jimmy Odukoya

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

A group of trustees announced that Jimmy Odukoya had been removed as senior pastor of The Fountain of Life Church, citing the late founder's succession wishes. The church and Odukoya have since rejected the announcement, saying it came from a faction acting outside the church's constitution.

Read more

There is confusion at The Fountain of Life Church in Ilupeju, Lagos, after an announcement that Jimmy Odukoya had been removed as senior pastor was quickly rejected by the church and by Odukoya himself.

The removal announcement

On Tuesday, 17 November 2026, a statement presented as coming from the church's board of trustees said Jimmy's tenure did not reflect the succession wishes of his late father and the church's founder, Taiwo Odukoya, who died in 2023. It said the board had reviewed the church's constitution and recordings of the founder's wishes, and that Jimmy was no longer recognised as senior pastor with immediate effect. The statement described the decision as a governance matter and not a personal judgement.

The church's response

Within hours, a statement posted on the church's official Instagram page and on Jimmy Odukoya's page rejected the announcement as having no constitutional weight. It said the announcement did not come from a properly constituted meeting of the Board of Trustees, but from a faction calling itself "Concerned Members of the Board".

The church said the individuals behind the faction had been formally asked to step down from the board on 14 March 2026 over what it described as repeated violations of the church's constitution and founding principles, and that the letter had been delivered to and acknowledged by them.

Background

Jimmy Odukoya, an actor and pastor, took over leadership of the church after his father's death. On 1 November he remarried and presented his new wife to the congregation. A video of the late founder said to show the role he intended for his son has also been circulating online amid the dispute.

What happens next

It is not yet clear how the leadership dispute will be resolved. The faction behind the removal announcement had not responded publicly to the church's statement in the reports.

No NIN, no flight: FG sets 1 January deadline for domestic air passengers

17 Nov 2026, 09:42 GMT

Passengers without a valid National Identification Number will be refused boarding on domestic flights from 1 January 2027. Aviation Minister Festus Keyamo announced the rule while unveiling a new passenger verification system.

Read more

The federal government has announced that passengers without a valid National Identification Number (NIN) will not be allowed to board domestic flights from 1 January 2027.

The new system

Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development Festus Keyamo announced the policy while unveiling the Verified Passenger Authentication System (VPASS), a digital platform that links passengers' identities directly to their NIN records.

Keyamo said the system will help authorities confirm genuine passengers, prevent unauthorised access to restricted airport areas and make sure airline passenger lists are accurate.

Why it is being introduced

The minister pointed to irregularities in ticket sales, including bulk buying and reselling of tickets under different identities, as problems the government wants to stop. He said tying bookings to verified identity records would make it harder for anyone to travel using false details.

What airlines must do

Keyamo directed the Nigerian Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) to ensure that domestic airlines connect to the platform before 31 December 2026, ahead of the January deadline.

What it means for travellers

Anyone planning to fly within Nigeria in the new year will need to make sure their NIN is valid and matches the details they use to book tickets.

FG orders ministries and agencies to freeze staff numbers for 2027

17 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The federal government has reportedly told ministries, departments and agencies to keep their workforce at current levels in 2027. Agencies that ignore the new reporting rules risk losing budget provisions.

Read more

The federal government has reportedly directed all ministries, departments and agencies (MDAs) to keep their workforce at current levels for the 2027 fiscal year.

The circular

The directive is contained in a circular dated 9 November 2026 from the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation, titled the 2027 Workforce Budget Call Circular. It was addressed to ministers, permanent secretaries, service chiefs and heads of federal institutions.

The new rules

Under the circular, MDAs may not create new departments, units or other structures without clearance from the Head of Service. Every workforce budget proposal must include the institution's approved organisational structure, and no vacancies may be created in structures that have not been officially approved.

The government is said to have singled out posts on Grade Levels 15 to 17, the senior ranks of the federal civil service, warning agencies against creating unauthorised vacancies at those levels. Agencies that can show a genuine staffing need must back their requests with evidence.

Penalties

Institutions that fail to comply with the new reporting requirements reportedly risk having their budget provisions cut off.
