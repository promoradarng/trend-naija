Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Public frustration

The outages have drawn complaints from well-known Nigerians. On Friday, comedian The Cute Abiola posted a video addressed to President Bola Tinubu, saying his solar system had run down after days of poor supply, that everywhere was hot and that the suffering was too much.

Actress Kate Henshaw also posted a video criticising the minister, saying power in her area lasts only minutes before going off and demanding accountability for spending on the sector.

Grid operator explains

In a statement on Thursday, 5 November, the Nigerian Independent System Operator (NISO), which manages the national grid, blamed the drop in supply on inadequate gas deliveries to thermal power plants and lower water levels at hydroelectric stations. It said this had forced it to cut the power allocated to distribution companies, leading to more load shedding, and that the impact varies from one region to another.

Minister gives timeline

In a further statement on Friday, 6 November, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said three problems had combined to cut supply: planned maintenance at the Egbin power station, limited gas supply to some thermal plants, and sabotage of key transmission lines, which he said had been especially damaging in Lagos because it blocked routes engineers would normally use to redirect power. He said damaged generating units would be brought back in phases, with the first expected back within 48 to 72 hours, and that work was under way with gas suppliers and on repairs. Singer Spyro also said on Saturday that when he visited an Eko Electricity Distribution Company office to complain, he found staff fanning themselves because of the heat.

Agencies trade blame

By the weekend, agencies in the power sector were contradicting one another. NNPC's gas infrastructure subsidiary said in a statement dated Friday that its pipeline network had no constraints preventing gas from reaching power plants, rejecting suggestions that gas supply was to blame, though it said volumes actually taken up by individual plants and maintenance were separate issues. The Transmission Company of Nigeria also accused Ikeja Electric of misleading customers by blaming outages on 3 and 4 November on grid instability, saying its records showed no equipment failure or line trip, and that lower generation and problems within Ikeja Electric's own network were largely responsible.

Supply restored in many areas

On Monday, 9 November, the Minister of Power said electricity had been restored to many of the affected areas in Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Kwara and the FCT, with work continuing on the rest. He said the vandalised Benin–Egbin 330kV line had been repaired, raising supply to the Lagos area to over 1,000 megawatts, back to the level before the disruption.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.

  • The government says there was no grid collapse.

Troops rescue 18 of 22 children abducted in Kaduna, search on for four

23 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Soldiers have rescued 18 children who were abducted by suspected terrorists in Kachia/Kajuru, Kaduna State, early on Sunday. The army says the search for the remaining four continues.

Read more

The Nigerian Army says its troops have rescued 18 children who were abducted by suspected terrorists in Kaduna State early on Sunday, 22 November 2026, and are searching for four others.

What happened

According to the army, armed men intercepted 22 members of the Boys' and Girls' Brigade in the Mother-Cat community, in the Kachia/Kajuru area of the state, while they were on an early-morning group road walk, and took them away.

The rescue

The army's acting director of public relations, Colonel Appolonia Anele, said troops of 1 Division operating under Operation FANSAN YAMMA moved immediately after receiving a distress call and pursued the abductors. Troops advanced towards a riverside area, where some of the children were found after the fleeing attackers abandoned them. In all, 18 were rescued.

What happens next

The army said operations were continuing to rescue the remaining four children. The attack comes days after troops foiled a suspected kidnap attempt at a school in another part of Kaduna State.

Correctional officers probed after two death-row inmates escape in Ilorin

23 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

About 20 correctional officers are reportedly under investigation after two death-row inmates escaped from the Oke-Kura custodial centre in Ilorin. The service has confirmed the probe but not the number of officers involved.

Read more

About 20 officers of the Nigerian Correctional Service are reportedly under investigation after two death-row inmates escaped from the Medium Security Custodial Centre at Oke-Kura, Ilorin, Kwara State, last Wednesday.

The probe

Those affected are said to include members of the armed squad and duty officers responsible for supervising inmates around the clock. Sources described the breach as a coordinated escape attempt involving seven inmates, two of whom got away.

What the service said

The Kwara State Command of the service confirmed on Sunday, 22 November 2026, that an investigation had begun on the orders of the Controller-General of Corrections. Its spokesperson said the probe would be thorough, but declined to confirm how many officers had been questioned, saying it would be premature while the investigation was ongoing.

Gunmen reportedly set secondary school on fire in Plateau's Bokkos

22 Nov 2026, 20:41 GMT

Suspected gunmen reportedly set fire to Government Secondary School, Masharkut, in Bokkos, Plateau State, on Sunday afternoon. No casualties were reported, and local officials said the attackers had not been identified.

Read more

Suspected gunmen reportedly set fire to Government Secondary School, Masharkut, in the Butura District of Bokkos Local Government Area, Plateau State, on Sunday, 22 November 2026, causing panic among residents.

What happened

According to a community member, the attack began at about 1pm. Residents noticed smoke rising from the school shortly after returning from church, and realised about 30 minutes later that the building was on fire.

Official response

The chairman of Bokkos Local Government Area confirmed that the council had received reports of the fire. He said the circumstances and the identity of those responsible had not yet been established, and that the damage to the school building was the authorities' immediate concern.

Security agencies had not commented at the time of the reports, and no casualties were mentioned.

Background

Bokkos and other parts of Plateau State have suffered repeated attacks on rural communities in recent years.

Read more

A 70-year-old woman has reportedly been rescued alive after falling into a 60-foot-deep well at Oworonshoki, in the Kosofe Local Government Area of Lagos State.

The rescue

Firefighters from the Oworonshoki Fire Station responded to a distress call in the early hours of Sunday, 22 November 2026, and brought the woman out of the well alive.

What officials said

The Controller-General of the Lagos State Fire and Rescue Service, Margaret Adeseye, confirmed the incident. She said the woman was stabilised after the rescue and handed over to her family, and praised the firefighters for responding quickly.

Unanswered questions

How the woman came to fall into the well was not disclosed.
