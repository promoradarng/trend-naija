Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Public frustration

The outages have drawn complaints from well-known Nigerians. On Friday, comedian The Cute Abiola posted a video addressed to President Bola Tinubu, saying his solar system had run down after days of poor supply, that everywhere was hot and that the suffering was too much.

Actress Kate Henshaw also posted a video criticising the minister, saying power in her area lasts only minutes before going off and demanding accountability for spending on the sector.

Grid operator explains

In a statement on Thursday, 5 November, the Nigerian Independent System Operator (NISO), which manages the national grid, blamed the drop in supply on inadequate gas deliveries to thermal power plants and lower water levels at hydroelectric stations. It said this had forced it to cut the power allocated to distribution companies, leading to more load shedding, and that the impact varies from one region to another.

Minister gives timeline

In a further statement on Friday, 6 November, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said three problems had combined to cut supply: planned maintenance at the Egbin power station, limited gas supply to some thermal plants, and sabotage of key transmission lines, which he said had been especially damaging in Lagos because it blocked routes engineers would normally use to redirect power. He said damaged generating units would be brought back in phases, with the first expected back within 48 to 72 hours, and that work was under way with gas suppliers and on repairs. Singer Spyro also said on Saturday that when he visited an Eko Electricity Distribution Company office to complain, he found staff fanning themselves because of the heat.

Agencies trade blame

By the weekend, agencies in the power sector were contradicting one another. NNPC's gas infrastructure subsidiary said in a statement dated Friday that its pipeline network had no constraints preventing gas from reaching power plants, rejecting suggestions that gas supply was to blame, though it said volumes actually taken up by individual plants and maintenance were separate issues. The Transmission Company of Nigeria also accused Ikeja Electric of misleading customers by blaming outages on 3 and 4 November on grid instability, saying its records showed no equipment failure or line trip, and that lower generation and problems within Ikeja Electric's own network were largely responsible.

Supply restored in many areas

On Monday, 9 November, the Minister of Power said electricity had been restored to many of the affected areas in Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Kwara and the FCT, with work continuing on the rest. He said the vandalised Benin–Egbin 330kV line had been repaired, raising supply to the Lagos area to over 1,000 megawatts, back to the level before the disruption.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.

  • Outages are reported in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo.
  • The minister blamed gas shortages and low hydro output.
  • The government says there was no grid collapse.

Court remands journalist Daniel Ojukwu in Abuja prison over cybercrime charges

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

Investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu has been remanded in prison in Abuja on a 12-count cybercrime charge linked to his reports about a presidential aide, pending a bail hearing. The decision has renewed concerns about press freedom.

Read more

A court in Abuja has ordered that investigative journalist Daniel Ojukwu be held in prison pending a hearing on his bail application.

The charges

Ojukwu, who works for the Foundation for Investigative Journalism, was arraigned on a 12-count charge of allegedly breaching Nigeria's Cybercrimes Act, and the court ordered his remand on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. The charges relate to reports he published about Adejoke Orelope-Adefulire, who was senior special assistant on the Sustainable Development Goals under former President Muhammadu Buhari and now holds the same role under President Bola Tinubu. The charges have not been tested at trial.

Press freedom concerns

The decision drew widespread criticism from media freedom advocates, who have long argued that the cybercrimes law is used to silence journalists. According to the Committee to Protect Journalists, at least 29 journalists have been prosecuted under the law since it was enacted in 2015. Nigeria is ranked 112th out of 180 countries in the 2026 World Press Freedom Index compiled by Reporters Without Borders.

Background

Ojukwu was previously detained by police for several days in 2024 over a report on the same official, a case that also drew national and international criticism.

FG reopens free CAC business registration for 250,000 small businesses

12 Nov 2026, 06:42 GMT

The federal government has reopened its free business name registration programme through SMEDAN, targeting 250,000 nano, micro and small businesses, after earlier problems with the application portal.

Read more

The federal government has reopened its programme offering free registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) to small business owners.

The programme

The scheme, run through the Small and Medium Enterprises Development Agency of Nigeria (SMEDAN), targets 250,000 nano, micro and small enterprises across the country, according to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026. It covers the cost of business name registration, which is one of the barriers that keeps many small businesses informal.

Who can apply

The programme is aimed at owners of small businesses not yet registered with the CAC, including traders, artisans, retailers and service providers. Business owners already registered with SMEDAN but not with the CAC may also qualify, subject to the agency's requirements.

Why it reopened

The portal was reopened after applicants earlier reported difficulties accessing it, which disrupted the application process.

Background

The reopening comes as the government also rolls out a ₦50,000 cash grant to vulnerable households.

Tinubu launches ₦52,500 minimum monthly pension for 173,209 retirees

11 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

President Tinubu has activated a guaranteed minimum pension of ₦52,500 a month for 173,209 retirees under the Contributory Pension Scheme, a provision that has been in law since 2004 but was never enforced. He also launched free basic healthcare for low-income pensioners.

Read more

Retirees under Nigeria's Contributory Pension Scheme will now receive at least ₦52,500 a month, after President Bola Tinubu activated a minimum pension guarantee in Abuja.

What was announced

At the Presidential Pension Security Initiatives ceremony at the State House on Wednesday, 11 November 2026, the President, represented by Senate President Godswill Akpabio, declared the guarantee active for 173,209 qualifying retirees. He described it as a floor beneath Nigerian retirees and urged future governments to raise it but never remove it. The Director-General of the National Pension Commission (PenCom), Omolola Oloworaran, confirmed the figure and said more than 65 per cent of private-sector retirees currently earn less than the new minimum.

Who benefits

According to PenCom, 54 per cent of the qualifying retirees come from the private sector, 34 per cent from federal service and 12 per cent from state employment. More than ₦400bn in the Pension Protection Fund will finance the guarantee.

Healthcare and arrears

The President also launched PenCare, a healthcare plan that will give low-income pensioners free access to essential health services, with access cards to be distributed in the coming months. He said the government had issued a ₦758bn bond to clear pension arrears and approved a ₦32,000 monthly adjustment for affected federal pensioners.

Background

The minimum pension was first written into the Pension Reform Act of 2004 and kept in the 2014 law, but no government had put it into effect until now.
