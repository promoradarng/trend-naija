Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Public frustration

The outages have drawn complaints from well-known Nigerians. On Friday, comedian The Cute Abiola posted a video addressed to President Bola Tinubu, saying his solar system had run down after days of poor supply, that everywhere was hot and that the suffering was too much.

Actress Kate Henshaw also posted a video criticising the minister, saying power in her area lasts only minutes before going off and demanding accountability for spending on the sector.

Grid operator explains

In a statement on Thursday, 5 November, the Nigerian Independent System Operator (NISO), which manages the national grid, blamed the drop in supply on inadequate gas deliveries to thermal power plants and lower water levels at hydroelectric stations. It said this had forced it to cut the power allocated to distribution companies, leading to more load shedding, and that the impact varies from one region to another.

Minister gives timeline

In a further statement on Friday, 6 November, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said three problems had combined to cut supply: planned maintenance at the Egbin power station, limited gas supply to some thermal plants, and sabotage of key transmission lines, which he said had been especially damaging in Lagos because it blocked routes engineers would normally use to redirect power. He said damaged generating units would be brought back in phases, with the first expected back within 48 to 72 hours, and that work was under way with gas suppliers and on repairs. Singer Spyro also said on Saturday that when he visited an Eko Electricity Distribution Company office to complain, he found staff fanning themselves because of the heat.

Agencies trade blame

By the weekend, agencies in the power sector were contradicting one another. NNPC's gas infrastructure subsidiary said in a statement dated Friday that its pipeline network had no constraints preventing gas from reaching power plants, rejecting suggestions that gas supply was to blame, though it said volumes actually taken up by individual plants and maintenance were separate issues. The Transmission Company of Nigeria also accused Ikeja Electric of misleading customers by blaming outages on 3 and 4 November on grid instability, saying its records showed no equipment failure or line trip, and that lower generation and problems within Ikeja Electric's own network were largely responsible.

Supply restored in many areas

On Monday, 9 November, the Minister of Power said electricity had been restored to many of the affected areas in Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Kwara and the FCT, with work continuing on the rest. He said the vandalised Benin–Egbin 330kV line had been repaired, raising supply to the Lagos area to over 1,000 megawatts, back to the level before the disruption.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.

Lagos says construction of Epe International Airport will start in 2027

15 Nov 2026, 11:41 GMT

Lagos State says work on the proposed Epe International Airport will begin in 2027, with Governor Sanwo-Olu announcing the timeline at an APC campaign event in Epe and his deputy saying preliminary work is well advanced.

Read more

The Lagos State Government plans to begin construction of the proposed Epe International Airport in 2027.

The announcement

According to a statement by the Lagos chapter of the All Progressives Congress (APC), reported on Sunday, 15 November 2026, Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu announced the timeline at an APC governorship campaign event in Epe. His deputy, Dr Obafemi Hamzat, said significant progress had been made on the documentation and groundwork needed for the project.

What it could mean

Lagos APC spokesman Seye Oladejo described the airport as a major infrastructure investment that could turn Epe into an important aviation, industrial and commercial hub, and said it would create opportunities for residents and businesses along the Lekki–Epe corridor.

Background

Lagos is currently served by Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Ikeja, which handles most of Nigeria's international air traffic. The Lekki–Epe corridor has seen rapid development in recent years, including the Dangote Refinery and the Lekki Deep Sea Port. No cost or funding details were given in the latest announcement.

All 62 contacts of Nigeria's Ebola case complete monitoring with no spread, NCDC says

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The NCDC says all 62 people linked to Nigeria's single imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a type of Ebola, have completed 21 days of monitoring with no further transmission. It warns this is not yet a declaration that Nigeria is free of the disease.

Read more

The Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC) says all 62 contacts linked to the country's imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a species of Ebola, have completed their monitoring without any further spread.

The update

Speaking at a news conference in Abuja on Friday, 13 November 2026, NCDC Director-General Dr Jide Idris said the last contact had been discharged after completing the mandatory 21-day monitoring period. He said 13 of the contacts developed fever or other symptoms during monitoring but tested negative for the disease. Seven other alerts reported over three weeks in Adamawa, Rivers, Borno, Lagos and Ekiti were investigated, and all suspected cases tested negative.

Not yet Ebola-free

Idris cautioned that the end of contact monitoring was not a formal declaration that Nigeria was free of the disease, and said surveillance must continue. He said any such declaration would depend on public health criteria and ongoing surveillance, especially as transmission continues in the Democratic Republic of Congo.

Background

Earlier this week, the NCDC said 28 contacts were still under watch. The United States has temporarily barred travellers who have been in the DRC in the past 21 days. Health authorities urge anyone with symptoms such as fever, weakness or unexplained bleeding after travel to report to a health facility.

FG issues Ethiopia travel advisory after 16 Nigerians die transiting Addis Ababa

15 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The federal government has warned Nigerians with heart and lung conditions to seek medical clearance before travelling through Addis Ababa, after 16 Nigerians died in transit there in what officials link to the city's high altitude.

Read more

The federal government has issued a travel advisory warning Nigerians about the health risks of Ethiopia's high altitude, after the deaths of 16 Nigerians transiting through Addis Ababa.

The advisory

In a statement by Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson Oluwafemi Adeniyi, reported on Saturday, 14 November 2026, the government urged travellers with existing medical conditions, especially heart and respiratory problems, to be cautious and to obtain medical clearance before taking journeys with stopovers in the Ethiopian capital.

Why altitude matters

Addis Ababa sits about 2,355 metres above sea level, where lower air pressure reduces the oxygen available. The ministry noted that dry air, stronger sunlight and faster fluid loss can also cause problems, from headaches and breathlessness to serious conditions such as swelling of the brain. Healthy travellers usually adapt within a few days, but people with underlying illnesses face much higher risks.

Background

Addis Ababa is a major transit hub for Nigerian travellers. The ministry did not give details of the individual deaths.
