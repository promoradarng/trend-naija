Power outages hit Lagos, Abuja and three other states as government blames gas shortages

6 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

Electricity consumers in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo states are facing renewed power outages, prompting the federal government to explain the situation.

What the minister said

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe reportedly blamed the drop in supply on gas shortages, maintenance problems and reduced generation from hydropower plants because of low water levels, according to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026. The government said there had been no major collapse of the national grid. It also said it had cleared 44 containers of equipment that had been stranded at the ports, which are needed to improve supply.

Impact on customers

The outages have caused particular anger among Band A customers, who pay the highest tariffs in return for at least 20 hours of electricity a day. Ikeja Electric this week issued seven apologies in nine days for failing to meet that promise. Many households and businesses have had to spend more on fuel for generators.

Public frustration

The outages have drawn complaints from well-known Nigerians. On Friday, comedian The Cute Abiola posted a video addressed to President Bola Tinubu, saying his solar system had run down after days of poor supply, that everywhere was hot and that the suffering was too much.

Actress Kate Henshaw also posted a video criticising the minister, saying power in her area lasts only minutes before going off and demanding accountability for spending on the sector.

Grid operator explains

In a statement on Thursday, 5 November, the Nigerian Independent System Operator (NISO), which manages the national grid, blamed the drop in supply on inadequate gas deliveries to thermal power plants and lower water levels at hydroelectric stations. It said this had forced it to cut the power allocated to distribution companies, leading to more load shedding, and that the impact varies from one region to another.

Minister gives timeline

In a further statement on Friday, 6 November, Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe said three problems had combined to cut supply: planned maintenance at the Egbin power station, limited gas supply to some thermal plants, and sabotage of key transmission lines, which he said had been especially damaging in Lagos because it blocked routes engineers would normally use to redirect power. He said damaged generating units would be brought back in phases, with the first expected back within 48 to 72 hours, and that work was under way with gas suppliers and on repairs. Singer Spyro also said on Saturday that when he visited an Eko Electricity Distribution Company office to complain, he found staff fanning themselves because of the heat.

Agencies trade blame

By the weekend, agencies in the power sector were contradicting one another. NNPC's gas infrastructure subsidiary said in a statement dated Friday that its pipeline network had no constraints preventing gas from reaching power plants, rejecting suggestions that gas supply was to blame, though it said volumes actually taken up by individual plants and maintenance were separate issues. The Transmission Company of Nigeria also accused Ikeja Electric of misleading customers by blaming outages on 3 and 4 November on grid instability, saying its records showed no equipment failure or line trip, and that lower generation and problems within Ikeja Electric's own network were largely responsible.

Supply restored in many areas

On Monday, 9 November, the Minister of Power said electricity had been restored to many of the affected areas in Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Kwara and the FCT, with work continuing on the rest. He said the vandalised Benin–Egbin 330kV line had been repaired, raising supply to the Lagos area to over 1,000 megawatts, back to the level before the disruption.

Background

Nigeria's electricity supply has long been unreliable, with generation often far below demand for a population of more than 200 million. Gas supply problems, vandalism of pipelines and weak transmission infrastructure are frequent causes. The government says it is working to expand generation and transmission capacity, while critics, including opposition candidates, say progress has been too slow. Electricity is expected to be a major issue in the 2027 campaign.

What happens next

Consumers are waiting for a clear timeline from the government and distribution companies on when supply will improve.

  • Outages are reported in Lagos, Abuja, Ogun, Kwara and Oyo.
  • The minister blamed gas shortages and low hydro output.
  • The government says there was no grid collapse.

Senior civil servants demand ₦300,000 minimum wage and state police

13 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

The Association of Senior Civil Servants of Nigeria has called for the minimum wage to be raised from ₦70,000 to ₦300,000, prompt payment of gratuities and the creation of state police.

Read more

Nigeria's senior civil servants have called for a new national minimum wage of ₦300,000.

The demands

At a National Executive Council meeting of the Association of Senior Civil Servants of Nigeria (ASCSN) in Abuja, reported on Thursday, 12 November 2026, union president Shehu Mohammed said the current ₦70,000 minimum wage had steadily lost value amid economic pressures and could no longer meet workers' basic needs. The union also demanded prompt payment of outstanding gratuities and the creation of state police, saying insecurity continues to put workers and their families at risk.

Collaboration call

Senior lawyer Femi Falana addressed delegates under the theme 'From Confrontation to Collaboration', arguing that lasting progress between labour and government depends on how the authorities treat workers.

Background

The ₦70,000 minimum wage was signed into law in 2024 after months of negotiations and strike threats by organised labour. The federal government had not responded to the new demand at the time of writing.

Four UNN students die after off-campus party in Nsukka, six in hospital

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka has confirmed that four students died after a party held to celebrate a friend's final exams, after allegedly consuming a mix of alcohol and drugs. Six others are in hospital, and the university has declared three days of mourning.

Read more

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka (UNN) has confirmed that four of its students have died after an off-campus party in Nsukka, Enugu State.

What happened

Speaking to journalists on Thursday, 12 November 2026, Vice-Chancellor Professor Simon Ortuanya said 10 students were involved in the incident at the party on Wednesday, 11 November, which had been organised to celebrate a friend finishing his final-year examinations. He said the four who died allegedly consumed substances believed to be a mixture of alcohol and drugs. The other six are receiving treatment in different hospitals. Earlier reports had put the death toll as high as seven.

University responds

The Vice-Chancellor described the deaths as one too many and declared three days of mourning in honour of the students. He also corrected reports that all the students were from the Faculty of Pharmaceutical Sciences, saying they came from different departments. The exact substances involved have not been officially identified, and it is not clear whether the police have opened an investigation.

Police investigate

The Enugu State Police Command said on Thursday evening that it had opened an investigation into the deaths. It said the four victims, two men and two women, fell ill after allegedly drinking alcoholic beverages at a gathering at a hostel in Nsukka and were confirmed dead on arrival at hospital. Police said the drinks had been recovered from the scene and the bodies taken to a mortuary for autopsy.

Background

Drug and alcohol misuse among young people has become a growing public health concern in Nigeria.

Petrol prices ease as MRS cuts pump price to ₦1,399 and depots lower rates

12 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

MRS filling stations in Abuja have reportedly cut petrol from ₦1,408 to ₦1,399 per litre, while some depot owners have reduced wholesale prices to as low as ₦1,215, following lower crude oil prices.

Read more

Motorists may see some relief at the pump as petrol prices begin to fall.

The cuts

A manager at an MRS filling station in Abuja told reporters on Thursday, 12 November 2026, that the company had reduced its pump price from ₦1,408 to ₦1,399 per litre, a cut of ₦9. It is not yet clear whether the new price applies across the company's whole network. Some depot owners who buy from Dangote Refinery have also reportedly lowered their wholesale prices from as high as ₦1,290 to ₦1,215 per litre.

Why prices are falling

The reductions follow a fall in global crude oil prices, which recently dropped to their lowest level since August, and Dangote Refinery's switch to a pricing system linked to international petrol prices, which reportedly cut its loading price to ₦1,250 per litre.

Background

MRS is a major retail partner of Dangote Refinery. The industry regulator has also given the refinery and marketers a deadline of 9 December to comply with a new pricing framework. Meanwhile, cooking gas prices have risen sharply in some states.

Cooking gas now up to ₦1,800 per kg in Lagos, Ogun and Oyo

12 Nov 2026, 09:43 GMT

Retail cooking gas prices have reportedly jumped from about ₦1,200 to ₦1,800 per kilogramme in parts of Lagos, Ogun and Oyo within a month, after depot prices rose by nearly 32 per cent. Some households say they are turning to charcoal.

Read more

Households in Lagos, Ogun and Oyo states are paying much more for cooking gas after a sharp rise in prices over the past month.

The new prices

According to reports on Thursday, 12 November 2026, retail prices of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) at several outlets rose from around ₦1,200 to ₦1,800 per kilogramme. In parts of Ogun State, including Magboro, Mowe and Ibafo, some residents said they were paying as much as ₦1,900 per kg. In Oyo State, prices reportedly range from about ₦1,450 per kg at direct outlets to between ₦1,500 and ₦1,650 at neighbourhood retailers.

Why prices rose

The increase follows a reported 31.8 per cent rise in depot prices, from ₦1,100 to ₦1,450 per kg, which has pushed up costs along the supply chain.

Impact on households

Some households say they are considering returning to charcoal because they can no longer afford gas.

Background

The latest increase comes even as petrol prices have eased slightly, with Dangote Refinery reportedly cutting its petrol price this week.
