Cooking gas depot prices jump by up to 16% in three days

3 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Nigerians are facing higher cooking gas costs after depot prices at major suppliers in Lagos rose sharply in just three days, according to a price review published on Monday, 2 November 2026.

The new prices

All five monitored Lagos depots reportedly raised their prices between 30 October and 2 November, with increases of ₦140 to ₦200 per kilogram. Navgas recorded the steepest rise, from ₦1,250 to ₦1,450 per kg, an increase of 16 per cent. 11Plc and Nipco both moved from ₦1,225 to ₦1,390 per kg, while Techno Oil and Rain Oil went from ₦1,200 to ₦1,370 per kg. Dangote's depot price was the lowest among those monitored, at ₦1,050 per kg.

What it means for households

Depot prices feed directly into what retailers charge for refilling cylinders, so consumers are likely to pay more in the coming days. A 12.5kg cylinder, the size used by many families, could cost roughly ₦2,000 more to refill at these depot rates. Clean cooking advocates warned that rising gas costs are pushing some households back to firewood and charcoal, which are cheaper but cause indoor air pollution and deforestation.

Background

Cooking gas prices in Nigeria have been volatile over the past two years, affected by exchange rates, supply problems and international prices. The government has encouraged wider use of liquefied petroleum gas as a cleaner alternative to kerosene and firewood. The Dangote refinery has also become a major supplier, and its prices have often been lower than those of other depots.

What happens next

Marketers say prices will depend on supply and global market conditions in the coming weeks.

  • Navgas is highest at ₦1,450 per kg; Dangote lowest at ₦1,050.
  • Advocates warn some families are returning to firewood.

Tinubu to inaugurate sections of Kaduna–Kano and Kano–Maradi railways on 3 December

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

President Tinubu will inaugurate completed sections of the Kaduna–Kano and Kano–Katsina–Maradi railway projects on 3 December, according to his campaign council. It says the Kaduna–Kano line is about 90% complete.

Read more

President Bola Tinubu has fixed 3 December 2026 for the inauguration of completed sections of two major railway projects in northern Nigeria.

The projects

According to a statement on Sunday, 8 November 2026, by Dele Alake, spokesman of the APC Presidential Campaign Council, the 203-kilometre Kaduna–Kano Standard Gauge Railway is about 90 per cent complete, while the 400-kilometre Kano–Katsina–Maradi line, which will link Nigeria with the Republic of Niger, is about 70 per cent complete. The 190-kilometre Jaji-to-Kano segment of the Kaduna–Kano line and the 250-kilometre Dawanu–Kano–Kazaure–Daura–Katsina stretch of the Kano–Maradi line are due to be inaugurated on 3 December.

Progress claims

The statement said that when Tinubu took office in 2023, the Kano–Maradi project was barely 5 per cent complete and the Kaduna–Kano line under 30 per cent. It said a federal inspection team led by the Permanent Secretary of the Federal Ministry of Transportation, Funso Adebiyi, recently assessed both corridors and carried out a test run. The figures come from the President's campaign and have not been independently verified.

Background

The Kaduna–Kano line is part of the planned Lagos–Kano standard gauge railway, which already runs from Lagos to Ibadan and from Abuja to Kaduna. The Kano–Maradi project was started under former President Muhammadu Buhari.

Why it matters

The inauguration will come just weeks before the 16 January 2027 presidential election, and infrastructure is a key part of the President's re-election campaign.

Lagos seals Surulere church over noise pollution after resident's complaint

8 Nov 2026, 17:42 GMT

The Lagos State environment ministry has sealed All Christians Gathering Ministry in Surulere on Sunday morning after a resident complained online about noise. Commissioner Tokunbo Wahab said it will stay closed until further notice.

Read more

The Lagos State Government has sealed a church in Surulere over noise pollution.

What happened

The Commissioner for the Environment and Water Resources, Tokunbo Wahab, said on his X account on Sunday, 8 November 2026, that All Christians Gathering Ministry, located off Clegg Street in Surulere, had been shut and would remain closed until further notice. He said the action followed a complaint from a resident on X about noise from the church, and that officials investigated and confirmed the complaint was valid, finding the church in breach of environmental regulations. The sealing took place on Sunday morning.

No exceptions

Wahab said the government would not soften its position on such violations, whether the offender is a religious body, a business or anyone else.

Background

The Lagos State Environmental Protection Agency has sealed many churches, mosques, hotels, clubs and event centres in recent years over noise pollution, often after complaints on social media. Wahab is known for responding publicly to residents' complaints online. Just this week, another Lagos resident cried out to the commissioner over noise from a church at midnight.

Bandits reportedly kill 12 members of one family in Zamfara village attack

8 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Twelve members of the same family were reportedly killed when bandits set fire to the building where they had taken refuge during an attack on Gidan Taba village in Maradun LGA, Zamfara State. Other residents were said to have been kidnapped.

Read more

Twelve members of one family have reportedly been killed in an attack by bandits on a village in Zamfara State.

What happened

The attack took place late on Saturday, 7 November 2026, in Gidan Taba village, Dosara Birnin Kaya ward, in Maradun Local Government Area, according to residents. A resident said the head of the family had gathered his relatives into a single room to try to stop them from being kidnapped. When the attackers found the family and they refused to come out, the bandits reportedly set the building on fire, and all 12 people inside died. An unknown number of other residents were reportedly abducted during the raid. The victims were buried according to Islamic rites on Sunday morning.

Official response

The police and the Zamfara State Government had not publicly commented on the attack at the time of writing.

Background

Zamfara is one of the states worst affected by banditry in Nigeria's North-West, with armed groups carrying out killings, mass kidnappings and attacks on farming communities. Last week, bandits ambushed a bus carrying police officers on the Sokoto–Gusau road, reportedly killing and abducting some of them. Security forces have carried out repeated operations in the state's forests, but attacks on villages continue.

CBN sets ₦15bn capital rule for dollar-backed stablecoins in new crypto rules

8 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

The Central Bank of Nigeria has set minimum capital of ₦10 billion for naira-backed stablecoin issuers and ₦15 billion for those offering dollar or other foreign-currency tokens, under new rules for crypto operators. Other crypto service providers will need ₦1 billion.

Read more

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has introduced tougher rules for cryptocurrency and stablecoin businesses, including minimum capital requirements of up to ₦15 billion.

The new requirements

Under the regulations, reported on Sunday, 8 November 2026, issuers of naira-backed stablecoins must have at least ₦10 billion in paid-up capital, while operators offering tokens backed by foreign currencies, such as dollar-pegged stablecoins, will need ₦15 billion. Other virtual asset service providers, including digital wallet, payment, transfer and custody businesses, must have at least ₦1 billion. The CBN said it may demand more capital from larger or riskier operators.

Licensing and foreign firms

The rules make it illegal for individuals or companies to issue, exchange, distribute or facilitate regulated virtual assets in Nigeria without CBN approval. Foreign operators targeting Nigerian customers must incorporate and obtain licences locally, regardless of approvals elsewhere. Companies already licensed by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) will face extra capital requirements if they offer services under the CBN's oversight, while digital assets classed as securities will remain under SEC supervision. The rules also set standards for reserve management and consumer protection.

Background

Nigeria has one of the world's largest communities of cryptocurrency users. The CBN banned banks from dealing with crypto firms in 2021 before lifting the restriction in 2023, and the SEC has since moved to register and regulate crypto exchanges. Stablecoins, which are designed to keep a steady value linked to a currency such as the dollar, are widely used by Nigerians for savings and payments.
