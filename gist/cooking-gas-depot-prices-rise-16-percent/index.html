Cooking gas depot prices jump by up to 16% in three days

3 Nov 2026, 11:42 GMT

Nigerians are facing higher cooking gas costs after depot prices at major suppliers in Lagos rose sharply in just three days, according to a price review published on Monday, 2 November 2026.

The new prices

All five monitored Lagos depots reportedly raised their prices between 30 October and 2 November, with increases of ₦140 to ₦200 per kilogram. Navgas recorded the steepest rise, from ₦1,250 to ₦1,450 per kg, an increase of 16 per cent. 11Plc and Nipco both moved from ₦1,225 to ₦1,390 per kg, while Techno Oil and Rain Oil went from ₦1,200 to ₦1,370 per kg. Dangote's depot price was the lowest among those monitored, at ₦1,050 per kg.

What it means for households

Depot prices feed directly into what retailers charge for refilling cylinders, so consumers are likely to pay more in the coming days. A 12.5kg cylinder, the size used by many families, could cost roughly ₦2,000 more to refill at these depot rates. Clean cooking advocates warned that rising gas costs are pushing some households back to firewood and charcoal, which are cheaper but cause indoor air pollution and deforestation.

Background

Cooking gas prices in Nigeria have been volatile over the past two years, affected by exchange rates, supply problems and international prices. The government has encouraged wider use of liquefied petroleum gas as a cleaner alternative to kerosene and firewood. The Dangote refinery has also become a major supplier, and its prices have often been lower than those of other depots.

What happens next

Marketers say prices will depend on supply and global market conditions in the coming weeks.

  • Navgas is highest at ₦1,450 per kg; Dangote lowest at ₦1,050.
  • Advocates warn some families are returning to firewood.

Christmas airfares to South-East and South-South jump by up to 208%

5 Nov 2026, 14:42 GMT

Domestic economy tickets for flights around 20 and 21 December on routes from Lagos and Abuja to South-East and South-South cities have reportedly risen to between ₦196,000 and ₦337,500, increases of up to 208%. A Lagos–Benin ticket now costs about ₦310,500.

Read more

Nigerians planning to fly home for Christmas and the New Year face sharply higher fares, according to booking data reported on Thursday, 5 November 2026.

The new prices

Economy tickets for flights departing on 20 and 21 December on routes linking Lagos and Abuja to cities in the South-East and South-South have reportedly risen to between ₦196,000 and ₦337,500, with some fares up by as much as 208 per cent. One airline was reportedly charging about ₦310,500 for a one-way Lagos–Benin flight, up from about ₦120,000. Air Peace, Aero Contractors and United Nigeria Airlines were among the carriers with the steepest increases.

Why fares rise

Demand for flights to the South-East and South-South surges every December as many people travel home for the holidays. Airlines say high costs of aviation fuel, foreign exchange for maintenance and leasing, and limited aircraft numbers push up fares. Senate President Godswill Akpabio said this week that even politicians are struggling to find aircraft to hire.

Background

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) previously accused some domestic airlines of fixing prices during the 2025 festive season. Airlines denied wrongdoing and blamed operating costs. Many travellers choose road travel instead, despite security concerns on some highways.

What it means

Consumer groups are urging regulators to monitor fares, while travel experts advise booking early to avoid the highest prices.

NNPC cuts petrol price to ₦1,325 in Abuja and ₦1,355 in Lagos

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

NNPC retail stations in Abuja are now reportedly selling petrol at ₦1,325 per litre, down from as much as ₦1,405, while Lagos stations cut prices by ₦5 to ₦1,355. The cuts follow a pricing review by the Dangote refinery.

Read more

Motorists in Abuja and Lagos are paying a little less for petrol after the Nigerian National Petroleum Company (NNPC) cut prices at its filling stations.

The new prices

Checks on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, reportedly showed that NNPC stations in Abuja were selling petrol at ₦1,325 per litre, down from between ₦1,370 and ₦1,405, a reduction of ₦45 to ₦80 per litre. Stations along the Kubwa Expressway and in Gwarinpa were among those already selling at the new price. In Lagos, NNPC stations cut their price by ₦5 to ₦1,355 per litre.

Why prices fell

The cuts followed a pricing review by the Dangote refinery and competition among depots, some of which have been selling below Dangote's ₦1,300 per litre gantry price. Depot prices across Lagos, Port Harcourt, Calabar and Warri showed mixed movements.

Background

Petrol prices have been set by the market since the subsidy was removed in 2023, and the Dangote refinery now plays a leading role in local supply. Prices remain far higher than before the subsidy removal, and fuel costs are a major factor in transport and food prices. Global oil prices have also been affected this week by tension between the United States and Iran.

What it means

Analysts say other marketers may follow NNPC's lead, but warn that prices could rise again if crude prices or the exchange rate move.

Ikeja Electric apologises seven times in nine days over poor supply to Band A customers

5 Nov 2026, 11:43 GMT

Ikeja Electric has reportedly issued seven public apologies between 27 October and 4 November for failing to give Band A customers the promised 20 hours of electricity a day, citing grid instability and network faults.

Read more

Electricity distribution company Ikeja Electric has come under criticism after issuing seven public apologies in nine days over poor power supply in parts of Lagos.

What happened

According to reports on Thursday, 5 November 2026, the company published the apologies on its official X account between 27 October and 4 November, acknowledging that it had failed to provide the minimum 20 hours of daily electricity promised to customers on Band A feeders. It blamed grid instability, network faults, transformer limitations and maintenance work.

Why it matters

Band A customers pay the highest tariffs in exchange for a promise of at least 20 hours of power a day. Under regulatory rules, distribution companies that fail to meet the promised supply for a set period can be required to move affected feeders to a lower band and cheaper tariff. Customers have also complained of spending more on fuel for generators during outages.

Background

Nigeria's national grid has suffered frequent disruptions, and power supply remains one of the biggest complaints among households and businesses. This week, broadcaster Rufai Oseni said states lack the capacity to run their own electricity markets, while opposition candidates have criticised the government's record on power. The government says it is expanding generation and transmission capacity.

What happens next

Customers are calling on the regulator to enforce compensation and downgrade rules if supply does not improve.

Reps bill would let regulators shut hospitals that refuse emergency patients

5 Nov 2026, 06:43 GMT

A bill to amend the National Health Act has passed second reading in the House of Representatives. It would require hospitals to begin triage within 15 minutes and stabilise emergency patients within 30 minutes, or risk losing their licences.

Read more

A bill that would allow regulators to suspend or revoke the licences of hospitals that refuse or delay emergency treatment has passed its second reading in the House of Representatives.

What the bill says

The bill, sponsored by House spokesman Akin Rotimi and debated on Wednesday, 4 November 2026, seeks to amend the National Health Act 2014 by setting mandatory timelines for emergency care. Under the proposal, facilities would have to begin assessing a patient within 15 minutes of arrival and provide basic stabilisation within 30 minutes. Where a life-threatening condition is identified, staff would have to start lifesaving treatment immediately, and hospitals with a free bed would have to assign it to an emergency patient.

Why it is needed

Section 20 of the existing Act already bans health workers and facilities from refusing emergency treatment, with a fine of ₦100,000, up to six months in prison, or both. However, some hospitals have continued to turn emergency patients away or demand payment before treatment. Rotimi said delays, administrative hurdles, upfront payment demands and poor referrals were still causing avoidable complications and deaths, and that the amendment would make clear who is responsible and how quickly they must act.

Background

There have been many reports in Nigeria of accident and gunshot victims, pregnant women and others dying after being refused care because they could not pay a deposit or provide a police report. The government has previously said hospitals must treat gunshot victims without waiting for police clearance.

Senate version

A similar bill sponsored by Senator Abdulaziz Yar'Adua is before the Senate. It proposes up to six months in prison, a fine of 300,000 naira, or both, for hospitals and health workers that fail to provide emergency care. It would also require hospitals to assess patients within 15 minutes, begin stabilisation within 30 minutes, provide a bed within two hours where available, and dispatch an ambulance or other transport within 30 minutes when a referral is needed. The medical and nursing councils would be asked to set up a national emergency care curriculum.

What happens next

The bill will now go to committee for further work before returning to the House for third reading.
