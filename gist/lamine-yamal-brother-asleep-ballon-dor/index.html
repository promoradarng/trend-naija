Samson Adamu becomes first Nigerian to be confirmed as CAF General Secretary

30 Oct 2026, 14:43 GMT

CAF has reportedly confirmed Samson Adamu, 40, as its substantive General Secretary, making him the first Nigerian to hold the post since the body was founded in 1957. He had served in an interim role since March.

Read more

Nigerian football administrator Samson Adamu has been confirmed as General Secretary of the Confederation of African Football (CAF), the first Nigerian to hold the position.

What happened

CAF's executive committee reportedly approved his appointment as substantive General Secretary, according to reports on Friday, 30 October 2026. Adamu had held the role on an interim basis since March 2026, after his predecessor, Veron Mosengo-Omba, resigned. The same executive committee meeting also voted to return the Africa Cup of Nations to a two-year cycle.

Who is Samson Adamu

The 40-year-old previously served as CAF's Director of Competitions, where he was involved in organising the Africa Cup of Nations and the CAF Champions League. He holds a FIFA Master's degree in sports management, law and humanities. He began his career in football administration by founding the Copa Lagos beach soccer tournament in the early 2010s.

What the job involves

As General Secretary, Adamu will run CAF's day-to-day administration, oversee legal matters and carry out decisions taken by the executive committee. His first big task will be preparations for AFCON 2027, which will be co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda.

How people reacted

Nigerian football fans and officials celebrated the appointment as a proud moment for the country, which has produced many top players but rarely held top administrative jobs in African football.

CAF scraps four-year plan, AFCON returns to every two years

30 Oct 2026, 09:49 GMT

CAF has reversed its decision to hold the Africa Cup of Nations every four years and restored the traditional two-year cycle. President Patrice Motsepe says the move protects African football's interests, and AFCON 2027 in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda goes ahead.

Read more

The Africa Cup of Nations will once again be played every two years after the Confederation of African Football (CAF) reversed its earlier decision to move the tournament to a four-year cycle.

What happened

CAF's Executive Committee reportedly approved the change unanimously on Thursday, 29 October 2026, after months of discussion. CAF President Patrice Motsepe announced the decision in a statement on the body's website.

Why CAF changed course

Motsepe said the decision was taken to advance and protect the interests of African football, its 54 member associations and African players at clubs abroad. CAF had planned to introduce an Africa Nations League to fill the gap between tournaments, but it was reportedly concerned about how long the new competition would take to deliver the expected money and development benefits. Returning to a two-year AFCON was seen as the safer option in the short term.

What comes next

AFCON 2027 will be co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. Motsepe said he was confident the three countries would deliver a successful competition. He described AFCON as more than a football tournament, calling it a celebration of African cultures that brings together people on the continent and in the diaspora.

Background

AFCON has been held every two years for most of its history, which has sometimes caused friction with European clubs that lose key players in the middle of their seasons. The move to a four-year cycle had been welcomed by some clubs but criticised by many fans and smaller federations, who rely on tournament income.

How fans reacted

Many African football fans, including Nigerians, welcomed the decision, saying more frequent AFCONs give the Super Eagles and other national teams more chances to compete for the title.
